Teaching work · 2022 — present

The work, scored like the letters

Three pieces of work, told the way the research says a portfolio should tell them: what was asked, what I decided, what happened. The outcomes are about teaching — completion, corrections, an archive that adds up. None of them is a trading result, because my students' accounts are theirs, and using them as advertising would be exactly the practice this site exists against.

Case file 01 · Course design

Risk first: rebuilding the beginner course

What it is
Redesign of Reading Charts Properly, the flagship course
Year
2023
Format
6 live online sessions · cohorts of 12
Status
Running in this form since autumn 2023

The brief

The first run of the flagship course, in spring 2023, taught the way most chart courses teach: four weeks of candle patterns, then risk and sizing in week five, almost as an appendix. The students were engaged, the sessions scored well — and their practice journals told a different story. Reviewed at the end of the course, the journals showed the same failure in eleven of twelve: position sizes chosen by feel ("a grand's worth", "half my usual"), with invalidation levels written down after the trade, if at all. The patterns they had learned were not the problem. The order was.

The decisions

I rebuilt the course in the order the losses arrive. Weeks one and two are now risk and position sizing — the calculator on the home page is lesson 01, unchanged — and no student sees a pattern until their risk budget and invalidation habit are written down. Patterns moved to week four, taught as descriptions rather than signals. Every session ends with a sizing drill marked against the student's own journal, and cohorts were capped at twelve so I can actually read twelve journals a week. Two pattern lessons were cut entirely to make room; nobody has asked for them back.

The outcome

Completion went from 5 of 12 in the first run to 10 of 12 in the rebuilt autumn run, and has stayed at 9 or better of 12 since. Every finisher leaves with a written risk plan — that is now the course's definition of finished. The honest limit, printed here because it belongs here: I can verify that students size positions the way they were taught. I cannot verify what it did to their accounts, I do not ask, and any teacher who quotes students' profits as a course outcome is selling something other than teaching.

Case file 02 · The book

The Chart Workbook: 120 charts, none of them real

What it is
A printed workbook of annotated teaching charts
Year
2024 · second printing 2025
Format
120 charts · exercises with worked answers
Status
In print · errata page maintained

The brief

Students kept photographing the annotated charts from sessions and asking for them as a book. A workbook was the obvious artefact — and an obvious trap: a book of real charts, chosen after the fact, quietly teaches cherry-picking. Every example would be one where the reading "worked", because nobody publishes the other kind.

The decisions

Every instrument in the book is fictional — invented indices, invented companies, price series constructed for the book — so no chart can be mistaken for a tip and no example is a real market's highlight reel. Roughly a third of the worked examples are readings that failed: the invalidation was hit, the scenario did not play out, and the exercise is to find the moment the plan said to stop. An editor with a numerate brief re-computed every open, high, low and close figure in the book before print; three arithmetic slips were caught at proof stage, which is why the rule exists.

The outcome

Published in spring 2024; a second printing followed in 2025 with corrections folded in. The errata page — linked from the book's first pages and kept current — lists 3 corrections to date: two transposed figures and one mislabelled wick. The workbook is now the spine of the flagship course, and the fictional-instrument rule has become the practice's rule for everything: if an example is real, it goes in the Letter's scored archive instead, where being wrong is recorded rather than edited away.

Case file 03 · The letter

The Method Letter archive: every example, scored in public

What it is
A weekly method letter with a complete public archive
Years
2022 — present
Format
Weekly email · one worked example per letter
Status
Publishing · 148 letters to November 2026

The brief

Market commentary usually works like this: publish a view, mention it again if it comes right, and let the rest sink. I wanted the opposite artefact — a letter whose examples could not sink. If the practice teaches that a chart reading is a description with an invalidation level, the letter should be forced to live by it.

The decisions

Every letter works exactly one chart example, in full: the reading, the invalidation level, the sizing arithmetic — using real instruments this time, because the archive is the accountability mechanism. Each example is scored 20 trading days later against the scenario it described, by a rule written in advance: played out if price followed the scenario before touching invalidation; did not if invalidation was touched first; inconclusive if neither happened in 20 days. Scores are appended to the original letter, never edited into it. Corrections to letters are issued as numbered corrections in the archive — 4 to date.

The outcome

148 letters published between January 2022 and November 2026. Of the 141 examples old enough to be scored:

Played out as described6345%

Read that table the way I teach students to read it: a worked example that "plays out" 45% of the time is not a signal service with a 45% strike rate, because the examples were chosen to teach, not to trade — some were chosen because the reading was finely balanced. What the table proves is narrower and, I think, worth more: the archive is complete, the rule was fixed in advance, and the failures are still on the page. A scored archive of teaching examples is not a performance record, and the letter is not advice.
