About · Method · Honesty

Twelve years of algorithms.
Then the teaching.

The short version

I spent 2010 to 2022 as a quant analyst and algorithmic trader at two London proprietary trading firms — building execution models and statistical arbitrage systems, and watching, from inside, exactly how money is made and lost by people with far better tools than any retail trader will ever own. The lesson that stayed with me was not a strategy. It was that the professionals' real edge was boring: position sizes fixed by rule, invalidation decided in advance, every decision journalled and reviewed. Their discipline was a system. Retail traders are usually taught the opposite order — patterns first, excitement first — and the published loss statistics are what that order produces.

I left in 2022 and set up Candlestick Pulse in Bristol to teach the boring part properly: risk arithmetic, chart anatomy, method, review. My background is an MSc in financial mathematics and twelve years of production trading systems; my teaching material is checked the way my code was — every figure re-derived, every example scored, errata published. I am not authorised to give financial advice, and I do not. Education is not a loophole around that line; it is a different job, and this page exists to keep the two separate.

The working page

How the teaching runs

The curriculum order is the method. It has not changed since the 2023 rebuild, and it is printed here so a prospective student can hold me to it.

  1. 1 · Risk before everything

    Risk budgets, invalidation, the sizing division, and the three ways a planned loss grows in real trading: spreads, slippage, gaps. Students write their risk plan in week one and are marked against it all course.

  2. 2 · Anatomy before patterns

    What a candle records — open, high, low, close over a stated period — and what it cannot record. Shapes are taught as descriptions with definitions you can measure, like the hammer's two-to-one wick ratio, never as omens.

  3. 3 · Method before markets

    Building a complete, written method from the parts: where you look, what counts as a setup, where it is wrong, what it costs when it is. Students assemble theirs from their own risk plan, not from a template of mine.

  4. 4 · Review before more content

    The final sessions are journal review: the class reads real practice journals (their own, anonymised with permission) and scores decisions against the written plan — not against whether the trade won. A good decision that lost is marked good. That sentence is the course.

The practicalities

  • Sessions: live online, 90 minutes (flagship) or 2 hours (risk course), recorded for enrolled students only.
  • Office hour: one hour a week during each cohort, first come, questions about the material — not about anyone's live positions.
  • Journals: submitted weekly during courses; I read and annotate every one. This is why cohorts stop at twelve.
  • Materials: the Chart Workbook is included in the flagship fee; the risk course includes its worksheets. No other purchases are required, and I take no commission on anything a student buys elsewhere.

The honesty page

What this practice does not do

Most trading education is sold with implication — screenshots of other people's wins, urgency, a lifestyle. This page is the opposite artefact: the refusals, in print, so they can be quoted back at me.

No financial advice

The rule
I am not authorised to advise on investments and nothing here — course, coaching, letter — is advice or a personal recommendation. If you need advice, you need a regulated adviser; I can explain what one does, and that is the limit.

No signals, no tips, no groups

The rule
There is no alert service, no "inner circle", no channel where trades are called. The Method Letter works examples for teaching and scores them; it never tells a reader to buy or sell anything.

No managed money

The rule
I never trade anyone else's account, hold client funds, or take a share of anyone's profits. Fees for teaching are the only income of this practice. A teacher paid from your wins has stopped being a teacher.

No broker money

The rule
No introducer fees, no affiliate links, no sponsored platforms. If I mention a tool in a session it is because I use it, and I say so. Broker marketing is how most "free" trading education is paid for; this practice charges its students instead, openly.

Examples are artefacts

The rule
Course charts use fictional instruments so nothing reads as a tip. Real examples live only in the Letter archive, where each is scored by a rule fixed in advance — played out, did not, inconclusive — and the failures stay published. Past examples, scored or not, are not a performance record and predict nothing.

Engagement terms

Terms, in plain print

  • Payment: course fees in full at booking; coaching blocks in advance; the Letter monthly or yearly, cancel any time — the archive stays public either way.
  • Refunds: full refund on any course before session two begins; after that, pro-rata for the sessions not yet run. Coaching blocks are refundable for unused sessions within six months of purchase.
  • Places: cohort places are confirmed in booking order and are not transferable after session one, because the journal work is personal.
  • Conduct: sessions are for the enrolled student. Anyone using a session to promote a signal service, a broker or a coin will be removed without refund — it has happened twice, and the rule is why the room stays honest.
  • Complaints: to me first, in writing; if I cannot resolve it, the booking terms name the independent route. Education is unregulated, which is exactly why these terms are written down.
