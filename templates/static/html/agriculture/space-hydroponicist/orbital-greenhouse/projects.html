CASE FILE 01 · OG-01

Salad for a crew that isn't in space — yet

The ask

A habitat-simulation programme — four crew, eight weeks sealed in an analogue station on Earth, rehearsing the routines a Mars transit crew would live by — needed fresh salad on the table every day. The crop module was 4 m² of bench inside the habitat, on a hard power cap: the lamps had to share a budget with everything else keeping the crew alive, simulated or not.

What was done

The module ran the power-capped recipe from Table 2: 220 µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹ × 12 h = DLI 9.5. That is below butterhead lettuce's comfort band of 12–17, and the plan said so on page one rather than discovering it in week three. Two decisions followed from the arithmetic. First, a cultivar chosen for low-light tolerance over headline yield. Second, the cycle was planned at 36 days, not 32, with sowing staggered — one tray every four days — so harvest became a rhythm instead of an event. Basil filled the bench edges, where its band (12–18) was also short but its value per gram forgave it.

The outcome

Fresh salad on 54 of 56 mission days, at 11% under the power cap. The two missed days were a failed circulation pump, logged as an equipment failure with the crop loss recorded against it — not quietly reclassified as a crop problem, and not hidden. The programme's own report adopted the staggered-sowing calendar for its next simulation, which is the outcome a consultant is actually for.

CASE FILE 02 · AUDIT + PROGRAMME

The farm that was over-lighting its lettuce

The ask

A commercial vertical farm growing butterhead lettuce had two problems that turned out to be one: electricity costs per kilogram were squeezing the margin, and the inner leaves kept showing tip burn — the scorched margins that make a head unsellable. The farm's instinct was more cooling and a nutrient change. They commissioned the audit first, which is the order that saves money.

What was done

A week of quantum-sensor logging at canopy height — at the leaf, not at the lamp. The recipe on paper was 300 µmol × 18 h, and the canopy agreed: measured DLI 19.4 mol, a full 2.4 mol above the top of lettuce's 12–17 band. The plants were being given more light than the band allows; the surplus came out as heat, haste and tip burn, because a leaf growing faster than its calcium supply arrives will scorch at the margins. The recipe was rebuilt to 250 µmol × 16 h = DLI 14.4 — hours trimmed and intensity trimmed together — and nutrient EC held steady so only one variable moved.

The outcome

Cycle length unchanged at 32 days — the crop had never needed the extra light to grow at speed. Tip burn gone within two cycles. Light energy per kilogram fell from 8.9 to 6.6 kWh/kg, a 26% cut that matches the photon reduction almost exactly, as it should: the arithmetic and the meter told the same story. The nutrient change was never made. It was never needed.

“She took light away from a farm that sells light-grown lettuce, and the lettuce got better and the bill got smaller. The audit paid for itself before the programme invoice arrived.”

Jeroen Maas · Head Grower, commercial vertical farm, South Holland (farm name withheld under NDA)

CASE FILE 03 · OG-05

The strawberry trial we stopped

The ask

A grower supplying local shops wanted year-round strawberries under lights — a genuinely attractive idea in a country that imports most of its winter fruit. They commissioned a twelve-week trial before committing to a full installation: an everbearing cultivar, gutter system, target DLI 18, costed honestly per kilogram from day one.

What was done

The trial grew well, and the log refuses to pretend otherwise. Yield tracked within 8% of the planning figure; fruit sugar (Brix) averaged 9.1, respectable for a lit crop; the plants were healthy. But the meter is also part of the crop. At the trial's electricity tariff, the energy cost alone came to €31 per kilogram of fruit — against a wholesale price of €9–12 per kilogram for the imported fruit it would compete with. No plausible recipe change closes a gap of that shape; it is not a tuning problem, it is a physics-and-tariffs problem.

The outcome

The trial was stopped at week 6 of 12, with the client's agreement, and the report said why in numbers they could check. The remaining trial fee was not billed. The client redirected the installation budget into leafy greens on the same benches — a crop whose arithmetic works at their tariff — and the strawberry entry stays on the register, marked stopped, because a log that hides its stopped trials will eventually hide something that matters.
