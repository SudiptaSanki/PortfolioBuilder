Nightwater / Lab

The lab

A cold room, a seawater tap and a very steady timer.

Nightwater is a three-person marine culture lab on the pier at Oban. We grow the organisms, build the reactors and keep them alive afterwards, which is the part most installations forget to budget for.

Why Oban

The lab is on the pier because the water is the raw material. Seawater is drawn from 8 m down, filtered to 0.2 µm and sterilised before it goes near a culture. The temperature outside does most of the cooling, which keeps the energy cost of a 20 °C room honest in a way it would not be in a city.

Iona Reyes founded Nightwater in 2021 after a decade in harmful algal bloom monitoring. The work was mostly counting cells and explaining why a bay had turned green. Starting a lab that grows the same organisms on purpose was a way to work with them instead of against them.

The first installation was a single 2 L flask in a bookshop window in Fort William. It was visible for three weeks, then the shop changed its lighting and the culture crashed. That lesson — the room decides whether the organism survives — is why every commission now starts with a light and temperature survey.

The algae do not care about the design. They care about the light, the salt and the timer. Dr Iona Reyes, founder

How the lab runs

  • Sterile technique throughout. Autoclaved glassware, 0.2 µm filtered seawater, and a bunsen at the bench.
  • Subculture every four weeks. One quarter of each culture into fresh medium, so the line never ages out.
  • Counting, not guessing. Cell counts under a stereo microscope before every dispatch.
  • No antibiotics. If a culture contaminates, it is autoclaved and the line is restarted from a sealed stock.
  • Sealed reactors on site. No discharge to the sea, ever. Spent medium is sterilised and goes to trade waste.

Biosecurity

Pyrocystis fusiformis is a non-toxic marine dinoflagellate, but it is not native to Scottish coastal water in the densities we grow. Every reactor is a closed system with a sealed headspace, and every waste culture is autoclaved before disposal. We do not sell cultures for release into open water, and we do not ship to addresses without an indoor installation plan.

02 / WHAT WE HAVE LEARNED

Four things that decide whether an installation glows.

01

The room light

Ambient light after dark suppresses the flash. A reactor in a bright room will look dim until the room lights actually drop.

survey first
02

The timer

Twelve hours on, twelve off, every day. A drifting timer desynchronises the culture and the glow fades.

astronomic clock
03

The stirring

Too little and the cells settle; too much and they are stressed and dim. 30–50 rpm is the useful band.

30–50 rpm
04

The maintenance

A reactor is a living system. Someone has to count cells and change medium, or it will fail within a season.

monthly
