01 / THE ORGANISM

A single cell, 500 microns across, that answers a shake with light.

Pyrocystis fusiformis is a marine dinoflagellate. By day it photosynthesises; after dark, a mechanical disturbance triggers a blue flash from luciferin and luciferase. The flash is a defence: startle a copepod and it lets go.

The cell is a flattened teardrop with two flagella and a cell wall of cellulose plates. In culture it drifts rather than swims, and it divides roughly once a day when light and nutrients allow. The light it emits peaks at about 475 nm, in the blue-green where seawater is clearest, which is not a coincidence: the flash is meant to be seen.

What makes it farmable is the circadian control. Bioluminescence only appears a couple of hours into the dark phase, and a culture that is stimulated repeatedly will exhaust its luciferin and stop flashing until it has had 30–40 minutes of darkness to recover. A good installation is therefore designed around the recovery, not against it.

You cannot make the algae glow. You can only keep them well enough that they still want to. Dr Iona Reyes, founder

03 / METHOD

From a 50 ml flask to a lit wall.

Every installation begins in the lab, not on site. Scaling a culture too fast is how installations arrive dim or dead.

01

Culture

Sterile seawater, f/2 nutrients, 12:12 light. Subcultured every four weeks into fresh medium.

4 weeks
02

Scale

50 ml to 2 L to 20 L in a stepwise dilution, never more than a factor of five at a time.

6–8 weeks
03

Install

Reactors are built, run for two weeks on site, then stocked. Light, temperature and stirring are commissioned with them.

2 weeks
04

Keep

Fortnightly checks for the first three months, then monthly: cell count, salinity, light output, and a partial medium change.

ongoing

04 / INSTALLATION

Forty litres, six reactors, one dark cycle.

The Salt House on Skye wanted a wall that came alive after service. We built a six-reactor array behind the bar, timed so the glow begins two hours after the kitchen lights go down.

THE SALT HOUSE · ISLE OF SKYE · 2025

The long glow

A restaurant with a dark bar and a west window. The array is lit from 08:00 to 20:00 by a filtered LED bar; after 22:00 the room lights drop and the reactors are stimulated by a slow pump pulse every 20 minutes. Diners see the flash travel up each tube.
