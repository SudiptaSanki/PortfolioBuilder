Case 01 — Remix

Night Freight: a club remix in six days, from one vocal stem

A label’s lead remixer dropped out a week before a release window. What arrived in the studio was a deadline, a dry vocal stem — and nothing else.

Client

Label — Kanal Records

Tempo / Key

132 BPM · G♯ min

Turnaround

6 days (rush rate)

Source material

Vocal stem only

Delivered

Day 5 · Club + radio edits

The brief

The original was a 118 BPM album track in A major. The label needed a peak-time club version for the single package, the vocal had to stay recognisable, and the master deadline was fixed by a vinyl cut already booked. No instrumental stems existed — the original producer’s project files were unavailable.

The decisions

The vocal was re-harmonised from A major into G♯ minor — a semitone shift small enough to pitch without artefacts, dark enough to carry a new bassline. Everything under it was rebuilt: a rolling 16th-note bass locked to the vocal’s rhythm, chords re-voiced as short stabs so the voice keeps the middle of the record, and a 16-bar break that is only voice and a filtered chord — the cheapest way to make the second drop feel earned. Arrangement was locked on day two; the remaining days were sound and mix only.

The outcome

Delivered on day five, a day early, with club and radio edits plus a full stem set. The label’s resident opened with it for a month of club nights, and the remix — not the original — became the version serviced to DJs. The A&R quote on the Session page is from this job.

Case 02 — Original production

Low Ceiling: the drop that didn’t work, rebuilt

The first version sounded finished in the studio and emptied the floor in a club test. This is the case study I show clients who ask why the demo stage matters.

Client

Self-release

Tempo / Key

126 BPM · F min

Versions

2 (v1 rejected)

Tested on

2 club PAs

Outcome

Single · 2025

The brief

An original for my own catalogue: a 126 BPM record for a 300-cap room, written to sit in the first hour of a peak-time set. The brief I set myself was restraint — one bass idea, one lead idea, nothing else competing.

The decisions

Version one broke that brief: the drop arrived with kick, bass, lead and a chord stab all at once, and on a club system the lead masked the bass — the record felt loud and weightless at the same time. The rebuild removed the lead from the first eight bars of each drop entirely, moved the bass pattern from straight eighths to off-beat sixteenths so the kick owns the downbeat, and let the lead enter only once the groove was established. Nothing else changed — same sounds, same mix, different order.

The outcome

Version two held the floor at the same test night and became the most-requested track in the register. The lesson is printed in my process: arrangement is decided at demo stage, on paper, before sound design — because this record’s problem was never its sounds.

Case 03 — Co-write

Glasshouse: a track built under someone else’s melody

A vocalist arrived with a finished chorus — melody, lyric, phrasing — recorded over a piano sketch in the wrong key for her voice. The job was to build the record without touching the song.

Client

Independent vocalist

Tempo / Key

122 BPM · A min

Sessions

4 days, in person

Split

Agreed in writing, day 1

Outcome

Single · 2026

The brief

Keep the chorus exactly as written — it was the best thing she had — and produce a 122 BPM club-leaning single around it. Verses were open: she wanted space to half-sing, half-speak. Publishing split and credits were agreed in writing on the first day, before any recording.

The decisions

The piano sketch sat in C major; her voice sits better lower, so the track was built in A minor and the chorus melody re-pitched a minor third down — close enough to keep its shape, far enough to sit in her strongest register. Chords under the chorus were kept deliberately plain (two chords, no extensions) so the melody carries the harmony, and the arrangement leaves the 1–3 kHz band almost empty outside the vocal — nothing in the track competes with the voice. The drop quotes the chorus chords instead of introducing a new idea.

The outcome

Released as her lead single in 2026, with the instrumental serviced alongside for DJ use. She has since booked two further productions — repeat work is the metric I trust more than streams.
