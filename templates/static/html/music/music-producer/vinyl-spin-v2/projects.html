Case files — three entries from the log

The work, filed the way it happened.

Each file is kept the way the room keeps everything: the brief as it arrived, the decisions as they were made, and the outcome with its figures attached — including the file where the outcome was a rejection.

Case file 01 · Entry № 044

Low Water — re-sequenced for the format

Artist The Feldspar Line Format 12″ LP · 10 tracks · 38:23 Year 2025 Status Shipped · no re-cut

The brief

A finished album, mixed and mastered for streaming, with the running order set by the streaming release: the loudest, widest song — Big Weather, five minutes of stereo bass — closing Side A, and the two sides unbalanced by over two minutes. The band's instruction was simple: press it as it is.

The decisions

The side sheet said otherwise, and the sheet was shown to the band before anyone argued. As delivered, Side A ran 19:21 — Band II money — with the widest bass on the record sitting in the inner grooves, the seat where distortion arrives first. The re-sequence moved Big Weather to third on the side, where the groove is still wide, brought Salt & Copper up to close the A side at full level, and let the B side carry the length instead: 20:37 of the album's quieter material, where the Band II trade costs least.

The outcome

Side A cut at full level; the B side's trade stated to the band in advance and accepted in writing. First test pressing approved — the only round needed. The record is the working example on this site's side sheet, loaded in its shipped sequence.

Case file 02 · Entry № 038

Glasshouse Hymns — the test pressing that failed

Artist Ana Ruiz Quartet Format 12″ LP · 8 tracks Year 2024 Status First pressing rejected · re-cut approved
Rejected

The brief

A jazz quartet record with a bright, close-miked vocal — produced elsewhere, brought to this room for mastering supervision, cutting and pressing liaison. The master arrived approved by the label, by email, the week before the cutting session.

What the test pressing said

Sibilance tearing on the last two tracks of each side — the vocal's esses breaking up exactly where the groove is tightest. The fault was not the cut. It was baked into the supplied master: the vocal chain had been printed hot, and no cutting engineer can remove distortion that is already in the file; they can only choose where it shows. The pressing was rejected in writing, with the fault named at the level of the practice: this room had passed a master it had only heard on headphones in an office, not on the room system. That step is in the method now, in ink, because of this file.

The outcome

The master went back for de-essing at source — two days of studio time, the label's cost, stated here because it happened — and the record was re-cut. The second test pressing was approved. The delay was three weeks; the plant figures are printed in full, because a rejection that hides its price teaches nothing.

Case file 03 · Entry № 031

Achterhoek Songs — a run sized by its audience

Artist Maren Vos (self-release) Format 12″ LP · 9 tracks Year 2023 Status 500 pressed · sold out in 7 months · repressed 300

The brief

A folk singer with no label, a finished album, and a savings account. Her first instinct was 1,000 records — the unit price is kindest there — and her streaming numbers, quoted hopefully, suggested she might be right. Streaming numbers are not a pressing quantity, and the log does not accept them as one.

The decisions

The run was sized from what could be counted: a mailing list of 640 names she could actually email, two years of gig-room sales written down, and the break-even sheet printed on this page. At 500 records the pressing paid for itself at 123 sales — a number her counted audience could plausibly reach — while the fixed £895 was spent once and the stampers stayed hers. The 1,000-run would have lowered the break-even per record and doubled the money at risk to do it.

The outcome

Break-even passed in the second month. The run sold out in seven. The repress of 300 cost £1,470 plus delivery — pressing only, because the stampers already existed and were already hers. That is the whole argument for owning your metalwork, made in one invoice.

The room

Vermeulen Cutting Room is the practice of Saskia Vermeulen, a fictional producer created for this template. The method, the physics and the arithmetic are stated at real practice level; the clients, records and figures are illustrative.

Sound

This site makes no sound, deliberately. The honest demonstration of what vinyl does to music is a record on a turntable; a synthesised imitation of crackle would be the fake warmth this room exists to refuse. The stub's hover sounds were removed, not rebuilt.
