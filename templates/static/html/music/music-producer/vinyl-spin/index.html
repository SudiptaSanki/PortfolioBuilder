A record is a compromise you can hear. Every side of vinyl has a fixed area, and the pitch of the groove decides how much music fits into it. Cut wider to keep the bass loud and the side gets shorter. Cut tighter to fit the last chorus and the top end starts to sand off near the label, where the stylus is moving slowest.

Most of the work is choosing that compromise with the artist in the room, not after the test pressing arrives. I cut attended where I can, and I send a level report with every lacquer: pitch, running time, peak level, and where the tracing loss lands.

Amara Osei · cutting engineer

01 / Cutting bench

What the side will accept

Set up a side the way you would brief it. The bench works the same arithmetic the lathe does: groove area divided by pitch gives running time, radius gives linear velocity, velocity gives tracing loss, and the saturation stage gives harmonics. Spacing on the drawing is exaggerated twelve times so the grooves stay visible.

03 / The chain

From file to stamper

  1. 01

    Reference master

    You send a 24-bit master with 3 dB of peak headroom, or the unmastered mix if you would rather decide in the room. I listen on three systems before touching a fader.

  2. 02

    Cut, attended or unattended

    De-essing, high-pass, programme-dependent pitch. I cut a reference lacquer, play it back on the same turntable, and note every place the groove is doing something the digital file does not.

  3. 03

    Lacquer to metal

    The lacquer is silvered, plated into a nickel father, then a mother, then the stamper that goes into the press. Each step loses a little top end; the cut is set slightly bright to land where it should.

  4. 04

    Test pressing

    Two copies arrive before the run. I check run-in, run-out, locked groove, and the inner-groove top end on a cartridge with a 0.3 × 0.7 mil stylus, then sign it off or ask for a recut.

What arrives with the lacquer

  • Pitch map and running time per side
  • Peak and loudness report (dBTP, LUFS)
  • Rumble filter and de-esser settings
  • Tracing-loss estimate at the label
  • Runout matrix and catalogue number

A recut is free if the fault is mine. If it is a change of mix after the lacquer is cut, it is a new lacquer.
