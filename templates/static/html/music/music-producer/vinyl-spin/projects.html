Selected cuts

SS-041

Eelco Voss — Kade

Four tracks recorded to 1-inch tape in a shed outside Ghent, then mixed in the box. The kick has a 42 Hz fundamental with a long tail, which is the one thing vinyl genuinely dislikes: every extra dB of low end costs pitch, and pitch costs running time. A 45 cut would have given the kick more room but taken four minutes off each side, so we stayed at 33⅓: 170 lpi for the two loud tracks, 175 for the two quiet ones, and the bass kept its weight at 17:20 and 16:55.

The first lacquer had a sibilance peak at 7.8 kHz on the vocal in track three. A 2 dB de-ess and a small change of pitch placement fixed it. Test pressing approved on the second attempt, no recut.

SS-038

Tess Anning Trio — Hollow Body

Recorded live to two-track in the Sint-Hippolytus church in Delft, no overdubs. The master arrived at −18 LUFS with 6 dB of peak headroom, which is a gift: it meant the whole side could be cut at 195 lpi and the piano kept its decay instead of being compressed into the groove wall. One 38 Hz filter to take out the heating rumble, and 2 dB of tape saturation only on the last chorus where the drummer leans in.

This is the record I use when someone asks what an unattended session sounds like. Nothing was decided in a hurry.

1 × 12″ · 33⅓
195 lpi · −3.0 dBTP
20:20 · 1 side
300 copies · Arnhem
Sep 2025
SS-036

De Service — Postbus 51

Two minutes a side, seven inches, 45 rpm, and a mix that had been mastered loud before it reached me. There is nowhere to hide on a seven: the music band is 32 mm wide, so 100 lpi at 45 gives you just over two minutes before the groove runs into the label. We cut it hot and short, pulled 1.5 dB out of the cymbals so the stylus would track the choruses, and it still reads as the loudest thing in the room.

The runout carries a message for the plant that I will not repeat here. It is in the metal.

1 × 7″ · 45 rpm
100 lpi · −0.8 dBTP
2:04 · 1 side
1,000 copies · Arnhem
Jun 2025
