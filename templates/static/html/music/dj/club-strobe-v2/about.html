Sheet 03 — The Resident

Anna Sobczak, playing as SABLE.

The first room was a sixty-capacity bar basement in Poznań in 2017 — a borrowed controller, a house crowd that came for the bar, and a slot nobody else wanted: the first two hours. It turned out to be the best training a DJ can get. An empty room teaches tempo discipline; nobody learns to build a floor by starting on a full one.

The route ran the way it usually does when it is honest: bar slots to warm-up slots, warm-ups to guest dates, guest dates to a first headline in the home room — Fabryka, Poznań, 2022 — and then Berlin in 2023, starting again at the front of the night. The stage name came with the Berlin move; the real name stays on the site because contracts, invoices and trust all run under it. The residency at Raum 9 began in March 2025, nine dates agreed in print before the first was played, and renewed for 2026 — the season is told in File 02.

The specialism, if the word fits, is the front of the night: warm-up sets built to a specified handover tempo, and residency nights with a memory. The opinion that comes with it: most rooms do not have a headliner problem, they have a first-two-hours problem.

The working page

How a booking runs

Five steps, the same for a warm-up in Berlin and a headline abroad. The running order is agreed before the night, not negotiated in the booth.

T − 14 d

The advance sheetOne page: slot and set times, the full running order with every name on it, handover tempo where one is owed, fee and travel as printed on this site, and the rider. If it is not on the advance, it is not agreed.

T − 7 d

Tech checkThe booth is confirmed against the rider — players, mixer, monitor on its own feed. Substitutions are agreed now, while they are still cheap.

T − 2 h

SoundcheckLevels set with the room empty and the limiter in circuit. The handover tempo and the changeover are walked through with the DJ on either side of the slot.

The night

Room-readingThe plan is a ceiling and a floor, not a script: tempo steps are held or released by what the floor is doing, checked every fifteen minutes. The running order does not move without the booker being told.

T + 1 d

The handover noteA short written note to the room: what was played, what the room did, what to change next time. It is how Night 5 of the residency stayed in the record instead of being quietly forgotten.

The honesty page

Four things this site will not do

H.1 — The warm-up's job

A warm-up DJ's job is the headliner's entrance, not their own highlight. If my set is the part of the night people describe afterwards, I have taken something that was not mine.

H.2 — Photographs

A photograph of a crowd is not a review. Every room photographs full at the peak; the peak is ten minutes of a five-hour night. This site prints sheets, dates and one named booker's words instead.

H.3 — Numbers

No play counts, no follower counts, no "international DJ". Every gig printed here carries a room, a city and a date, and the residency is claimed only with its run of dates beside it.

H.4 — The quiet night

Rooms have quiet nights — heat, weather, a bigger night across town. They stay in the record (File 02, Night 5) because a booker planning a season needs the whole distribution, not the highlight reel.

Engagement terms

The rider and the lines, in print

Booth rider (booth class, as stated)

Two CDJ-3000-class players and a DJM-A9-class mixer, serviced and linked; a booth monitor on its own feed with its own level control; a booth fan; drinking water. That is the whole rider — no brands beyond class, no dressing-room list.

Not provided — printed so it is never negotiated at midnight

Pyrotechnics: none, at any price. My own PA beyond booth class: not carried — the room's system is the room's responsibility. Unannounced back-to-back guests: guests are agreed on the advance sheet or they do not play. Sets past the room's printed limit: the last record time on the sheet is final.

Lighting and strobes

I ask every room to run strobes in short, announced bursts and never as a default state, and to print the lighting policy where the room's audience can read it before the night. This website follows its own request: nothing here flashes, pulses or strobes, in any section, at any size.

Hearing

The booth is the loudest workplace in the building. I wear moulded plugs at −15 dB for every set, and I book rooms that run a limiter at about 103 dB(A) at the floor's loudest point and say so in their own listings. Promoters: if your room cannot state its limit, that conversation happens before the date is agreed, not during it.

Money and changes

Fees are the printed table on the home page; a booking is held on a 30% deposit against the advance sheet, balance on the night. Cancellation by either side at 14 days or more returns the deposit in full; inside 14 days it is held against a rebooked date within six months. If a slot moves, the sheet is reprinted and every name on it is told — including mine.
