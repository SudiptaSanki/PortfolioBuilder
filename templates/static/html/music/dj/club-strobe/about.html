How it started

The bar was called Sete and it held forty people on a good night. The owner, Sr. Almeida, did not believe in DJs. He believed in a record player behind the bar and someone with enough sense to change the record before it ended. I was nineteen, working the door, and I started bringing my own records because his were all Dire Straits.

He let me play on Sundays, on the condition that I never spoke into a microphone and never played the same record twice in a night. I worked there for three years. Everything I know about reading a room comes from watching forty people decide whether to stay for one more drink while I had my back turned to them.

The best compliment I have received is that nobody noticed the transition.

What the set is

Long. I play four to six hours when the room allows it, starting around 110 and ending wherever the night goes. The first ninety minutes are for the staff and the people who arrived early; the next two hours are the actual work. If I am doing it right, the room does not notice the tempo climbing until it is already up.

Half vinyl, half unreleased material from friends. I do not play requests before 2 a.m. and I do not play them at all if they do not fit — but I will write the name down and tell you honestly whether it would have worked.

Records, and why I still carry them

Vinyl is not a moral position for me. It is a constraint that keeps me listening: you cannot see the waveform, so you learn the record with your ears. I carry about sixty records to a five-hour set and I know all of them well enough to know what the third minute does.

That said, half my sets are digital now, because a lot of the music I want to play only exists as a file from someone’s hard drive. I use a pair of CDJs and a rotary mixer when the booth has one, and I bring my own needles either way.

Radio and the rest

Radio Quântica gave me a monthly hour in 2022 and has not asked me to change it since. I treat it as the opposite of a club set: one listener, probably at home, no obligation to keep anyone in a room. The show has no sponsors and no ads, and I would like to keep it that way.

I also run a small night called Meia-Noite four times a year with two friends, in a room that holds 150 people. No headliners, no flyers with faces on them, five euros at the door. It has broken even twice.
