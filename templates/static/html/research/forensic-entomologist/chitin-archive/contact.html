Three notes decide whether the insects can speak

The time the sample was taken, the temperature of the place it came from, and where on the body it was. If you have those three, write them on the label before the container is closed. Everything in the report is built on them.

Do not

Do not put live larvae in a sealed bag, do not fix them in formalin, and do not clear the scene of insects before the sample is taken. A body that has been washed and bagged has lost most of its clock.

Method note

Where the sample comes from changes the answer

A collection made only from the head and neck describes the moment the flies found an exposed surface. A collection made from the torso, the groin and the underside describes a different, later colonisation. The two can differ by days in the same body, and a report that pools them without saying so is wrong in a way nobody notices until cross-examination.

The same is true of the room. A radiator, a south-facing window and a fridge door each leave a mark in the temperature record, and a mean of the whole flat hides all three. If the scene has a logger, send the raw file rather than the summary; if it does not, tell me the heating type, the window aspect and whether the body was covered.

Finally, send the label with the sample, not after it. A container of larvae that arrives with a case number and no temperature is a container I can identify and nothing more.
