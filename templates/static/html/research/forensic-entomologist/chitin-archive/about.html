The laboratory

A report is a chain of small, boring certainties

Forensic entomology gets its reputation from the courtroom, but the case is made in the cabinet: which species, which instar, which temperature, which assumption. I write reports that a defence barrister can take apart line by line, because that is the only kind worth writing.

01 — Training

From a museum drawer to a mortuary table

I started with beetles, not bodies. My master's was in forensic science at the University of Amsterdam, with a thesis on dermestid beetles in museum storage, and the doctorate at Wageningen was on calliphorid development under fluctuating temperatures, which is the question that decides most real cases and the one least served by the published tables.

The published tables are built at constant temperatures, usually on a laboratory strain fed on liver. A body in a flat is neither. My doctoral work ran cohorts through sine-wave temperature cycles with the same mean as a constant treatment and found development times diverging by up to 18 %, which is not a rounding error in a case where the defendant has an alibi for a four-day window.

After the doctorate, two years at the Netherlands Forensic Institute as a trainee reporter, then registration with the NRGD, the Dutch register of court experts. I have written around 120 reports and given evidence in 31 hearings, including four where my interval was the only scientific evidence in the case.

02 — Method

What actually happens to your sample

Identification

Morphology first: spiracle slits, cephalopharyngeal skeleton, the shape of the posterior spiracles, the banding on the puparium. Then DNA. I sequence COI from two individuals per sample and compare against a local reference library I maintain with the NFI, because a barcode matched to a database built in another country is a weaker claim than it looks.

Staging

Length, dry mass, and the characters that mark an instar boundary. Weight is unreliable once larvae have left the food source, so the post-feeding stage is staged on behaviour and on the crop and integument rather than size. Every measurement is repeated by a second person; single-observer staging is a known source of drift.

Temperature

The scene supplies a temperature series or it does not. If it does, I fit it to the developmental model hour by hour rather than averaging it, because an average hides the part of the day that matters. If it does not, the report says so and gives a range wide enough to be honest, usually from a nearby weather station corrected for indoors versus outdoors.

Rearing

A subsample of live larvae is reared at 20 °C on the tissue they came from, and the time to pupariation is compared with the model. That single check catches local strain differences, which are real and which no table can carry.

03 — Limits

Sentences I will not say in court

“The time of death was…” There is a window, built from a minimum and a maximum, and it is stated as a window. A point estimate is a story, not a measurement.

“The insects prove…” Insects are consistent with an interval. They do not prove a place, a person or an intention, and the moment a report starts using that verb the science has left the room.

“Indoors, at 21 °C, as the thermostat shows.” A thermostat setting is not an air temperature, and a flat with a south-facing window is not the same flat at six in the evening and six in the morning. I have withdrawn one interval already because I trusted a household setting; I would rather do that in a second report than in cross-examination.

“The larvae were on the body the whole time.” Larvae move. They leave the body to pupariate, they cross a room, they end up under a rug. Where they were found is a fact about the search, not necessarily about the death.

Turnaround

What a case costs in time

Stage

Identification, 24 h

Species and instar from preserved material, with a short preliminary note for the investigating officer. No interval yet.

Stage

Full report, 5–8 days

Temperature reconstruction, rearing check, barcoding where the species is ambiguous, and a written interval with its assumptions listed.

Stage

Court, whenever listed

Evidence in person, a meeting with counsel beforehand, and a written response to any counter-report within the timetable the court sets.
