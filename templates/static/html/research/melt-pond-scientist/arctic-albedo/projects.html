2020 · MOSAiC leg 4

Pond survey on the Transpolar Drift

Floe 4, 81.8° N 8.6° E → 82.4° N 15.1° E. Eleven weeks, one floe, 1,240 ponds.

The main floe drifted with the Transpolar Drift from late June to mid-September. We flew a fixed-wing drone on a 400 m grid every third day, processed the images to a 3 cm surface model, and measured pond depth at 60 fixed points with a graduated rod and a conductivity probe. The point of the campaign was to get pond fraction and pond depth on the same floe for a whole melt season — something earlier surveys had never managed.

The survey showed that pond area and pond depth peak at different times. Area peaks first, when the snow has gone and the surface is still impermeable; depth keeps growing for another two weeks. Models that carry a single "pond fraction" state miss that lag.

2015 — 2024 · Qaanaaq

A decade of fast-ice albedo

Inglefield Bredning, 77.5° N 69.2° W. One mast, one transect, every April to July.

The fast ice in Inglefield Bredning is stable enough to instrument the same way each year. A 3 m mast with up- and down-facing spectrometers sits on the same 200 m transect, and the same observer (now two) walks the same 12 points. The record is short by climate standards but long enough to see the melt season move.

The step after 2018 tracks a change in spring snow depth rather than a change in summer weather. Thinner snow means less insulation and earlier bare ice, which is why the site now measures snow depth at 50 points rather than 12.

2023 · Fram Strait

What drains a pond

Two floes north of 79° N. 86 ponds tracked for nine days with depth loggers.

Ponds do not simply evaporate. They drain through cracks and seal again, and the timing of that drainage sets how long a floe stays dark. We instrumented 86 ponds with pressure loggers on the ice lid and a camera on a mast, and followed a warm spell in late July.

Ponds deeper than 0.31 m were twice as likely to drain as shallow ones, because the water pressure at the base is enough to open a hairline crack. That threshold is now used in the pond model's drainage scheme, replacing a fixed porosity.

2018 — 2024 · Archive

Spectral albedo library

3,140 spectra from snow, bare ice, white ice and ponds, 350–2,500 nm.

Broadband albedo hides what is happening at different wavelengths. A pond is dark in the near-infrared but not in the blue-green; snow is bright everywhere but less so as grain size grows. The library was built to give radiative-transfer models measured input spectra rather than assumed ones.

The library is public, with each spectrum tagged by surface type, grain size, solar zenith and instrument. It has been used by four modelling groups and is the basis of the pond albedo parameterisation in CICE 6.5.

2022 — 2025 · Model work

Evaluating pond fraction in CMIP6

Eleven CMIP6 models, four survey datasets, one question: does the pond fraction match?

CMIP6 sea-ice models carry a prognostic pond scheme, but most are tuned to a handful of old surveys. We compared their June–August pond fraction against our drone surveys and the MOSAiC record. The spread was larger than the seasonal cycle in some models.

The models that matched the peak fraction still got the peak date wrong by two weeks, which matters more for the energy budget than the annual mean. The evaluation is now part of the Sea Ice Model Intercomparison Project's pond diagnostic set.
