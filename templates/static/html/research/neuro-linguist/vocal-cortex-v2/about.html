The practice · Who, how, and on what terms

A practice built out of other people's near-misses.

Dr Hana Kobayashi trained as a computational neurolinguist — a doctorate on how readers and listeners build meaning word by word, followed by postdoctoral years in two laboratories on two different kinds of language data.

The practice exists because of a pattern she kept meeting from inside those labs: studies rarely fail at the statistics. They fail earlier and quieter — in a stimulus list nobody audited, a pilot nobody tried to break, a response window chosen because it was the software's default. Analysis cannot rescue a question that was never cleanly asked; it can only describe the mess precisely.

Since 2019 the practice has worked the other end of the pipeline: design first, stimuli second, pre-registration before data, and analysis that reports what arrived. It works inside client laboratories — on site for setup and piloting weeks, remotely for analysis — because the work belongs to the laboratory, not to the consultant. Forty-one studies in, the founding observation has not changed: the cheapest place to fix a study is on paper, and the second cheapest is in the pilot.

Training, stated as it would be on a grant application: doctorate in computational neurolinguistics; postdoctoral research in behavioural and EEG laboratories; analysis practice in mixed-effects modelling and ERP pipelines; pre-registration and registered-report drafting since 2019.

§ 01

How an engagement runs

Five stages, in order. The order is the method.

  1. 1

    Design review

    The protocol is read against the claim it is meant to support — not against how studies in the area are usually run. Confounds, defaults and unexamined inheritances from earlier studies are listed in writing, with the cost of fixing each one now versus later.

  2. 2

    Piloting, with a gate

    The pilot is run to break the study, not to preview a pleasing result. Pilot data are audited — stimulus properties, timing logs, exclusion counts — and the main study proceeds only if the design survives its own pilot. If it does not, the redesign is the deliverable, as in study VC-2023-014.

  3. 3

    Pre-registration

    Hypotheses, sampling plan, exclusions and the primary analysis are written down and timestamped before main data collection. Not as bureaucracy: as the document that lets everyone, including the team itself, tell the difference between a prediction and a discovery.

  4. 4

    Analysis, to the plan first

    The registered analysis runs first and is reported whatever it shows. Anything beyond it is run openly and labelled exploratory — in the manuscript and in the code, not just in conversation. Pipelines are delivered as runnable scripts with counts at every exclusion stage.

  5. 5

    Write-up support

    Methods sections written so another laboratory could repeat the study from the page alone; results reported at their real size; null results written up as results. The laboratory's name goes on the paper. The practice's does not — authorship follows the institution's own criteria, and support work is acknowledged, not authored, unless the contribution genuinely earns it.

§ 02

The honesty page

Printed here so it does not have to be negotiated per enquiry.

Claims this practice will not make

  • “The brain's language centre.” There isn't one. Language runs on a distributed network whose parts are shared with other jobs — that is the interesting finding, not a slogan problem.
  • Left-brain / right-brain people. A horoscope with electrodes. Refused in print, in talks and in client reports.
  • “Brain-based” therapy, training or diagnosis. Not offered, not implied, not referred onward as a quiet favour. Clinical questions belong to clinicians.
  • Reading thoughts from a scan. An activation map is a statistical map — a statement about averages and thresholds — not a photograph of a thought, and it will not be described as one.

Principles that cost money, kept anyway

A null result is a result. Three of this practice's write-ups report null findings, and they are listed in the scoreboard on the protocol page next to everything else — not in a smaller font.

The smaller true effect wins. Given a choice between a large effect that lives in the stimulus list and a small one that survives the audit, this practice hands you the small one, every time. It is the one still standing at replication.

Exploratory means exploratory. An analysis invented after seeing the data can generate the next study. It cannot testify about this one, and it will not be dressed up to do so.

The participant's hour is real. Sessions are planned on paper — the planner on the protocol page is the actual tool — so that nobody sits in a booth for a session that was never going to fit its own breaks.

§ 03

Engagement terms

The short version. The contract version is exchanged before work starts.

  • Research contracts onlyWork is undertaken for institutions — universities, research clinics, funded collaborations — under a written research-support agreement. No work is undertaken for individuals, and no clinical service of any kind is offered or implied.
  • Ethics approval is yoursEthical approval for any study is obtained by the client institution, from its own committee, before data collection begins. This practice works to the approved protocol; it does not hold approvals of its own and cannot lend one.
  • Data stays homeParticipant data remain on the client institution's systems. Analysis is performed on the institution's infrastructure or on de-identified exports handled under the institution's data agreement. Nothing is retained after handover beyond the delivered scripts and reports.
  • PaymentInstitutional purchase order against the quoted scope; day-rate analysis work is invoiced monthly in arrears. Participant payments are made by the institution directly and never pass through this practice.
  • CancellationBooked days cancelled with ten working days' notice are not charged. Work already done is invoiced as done — including, especially, the audit that tells you not to run the study.
