Protocol VC–GEN · Support for language & brain studies

Language is a network, not a postcode.

I design language experiments, build and norm their stimulus sets, and analyse the data — for university laboratories and research clinics that would rather find the flaw in a study before the reviewers, the funders or the participants do.

Scope — stated plainly

Research support only. This practice offers no therapy, no diagnosis and no clinical assessment, and it does not read brains. What it offers is cleaner questions: designs that survive their pilot, stimuli that do not smuggle in the effect, and analyses that report what the data actually said.

Most pilots are run to confirm the grant application. I run them to break the study while breaking it is still cheap. — Dr Hana Kobayashi, on piloting

§ 01

The session planner

Three printed rules do all the work. The arithmetic is shown line by line — check it.

Planning rules — printed, not hidden in the code

  1. R1 · Trial time. One trial = stimulus presentation + response window + inter-trial interval. Nothing else is charged per trial.
  2. R2 · Breaks. A 90-second break follows every completed block of 40 trials — except after the final block, when the participant is finished.
  3. R3 · Setup. 10 minutes (600 s) per session for instructions, a practice block and equipment checks, before the first counted trial.

§ 03

Fees, and the study estimator

Rates are printed. The estimator uses exactly these numbers — nothing is added at the end.

What moves the price

  • 01Participant numbers and session counts. The participant-payment line grows with both, and so does analysis time. On most studies this is the largest mover — which is why the estimator asks for them first-class, not as a footnote.
  • 02Modality. EEG/ERP analysis is slower per dataset than behavioural data. fMRI support is quoted per study after a written scoping exchange, because the honest price depends on the pipeline the host lab already runs.
  • 03Stimulus media. Recorded speech has to be recorded by someone. A voice artist and studio time are passed through at cost and itemised on the quote — never bundled into the day rate.
  • 04Deadline. A pre-registration needed before a grant deadline is still written at the day rate. Urgency changes the queue, not the arithmetic.

Basis

Design and stimulus work is quoted as a fixed scope per study once the protocol has been read. Analysis support is booked by the day. Universities pay by purchase order against an institutional contract; the practice never bills participants, and participant payments are handled by the host institution.

§ 04

One study, told straight

The full case file — brief, decisions, outcome — is on the studies page.

A psycholinguistics laboratory piloted a priming study and found a gratifyingly large effect. Before the main grant budget was committed, the pilot came here for a design review. The audit found the effect living in the word list: the primed targets were, by accident of selection, systematically higher-frequency words than the controls. The lists were rebuilt — matched on frequency, length and neighbourhood density — and the study ran again at its real, smaller size.

“Hana found in two weeks what our pilot was politely hiding: the effect was in the word list, not in the design. The rebuilt study cost less than the one we nearly ran.”

Prof. Daniel Okafor — Principal Investigator, psycholinguistics laboratory · on study VC-2023-014
