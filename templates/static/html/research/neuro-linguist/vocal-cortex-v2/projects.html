Case file · Three engagements, told in full

Studies are arguments. These are three of them.

Every engagement is written up the same way: the brief as it arrived, the decisions that were actually taken, and the outcome as it actually was — including the study whose honest outcome was that there was no effect. Client laboratories are described by type, not named: their data and their drafts are theirs.

The effect that belonged to the word list

STUDY VC-2023-014
Client
University psycholinguistics laboratory
Year
2023
Methods
Behavioural — lexical decision
Engagement
Design review + stimulus rebuild
Deliverable
Audited lists, rebuilt stimulus set, pre-registration
Status
Complete — main study run and reported by the lab

The brief

A laboratory studying morphological priming had piloted its design with twelve participants and found an effect large enough to build a grant application around. The PI, to her credit, asked for an independent design review before the main budget — 48 participants, two sessions each — was committed.

The decisions

The audit read the stimulus list before it read the results. The primed targets turned out to be systematically higher-frequency words than the unprimed controls — an accident of how the list had been assembled — and word frequency is exactly the variable a lexical-decision task is most sensitive to. The lists were rebuilt from scratch, matched item-by-item on frequency, length and neighbourhood density, and a pilot gate was pre-registered: the main study would only run if the rebuilt pilot survived its own audit.

The outcome

The effect was real, and roughly half the size the first pilot had promised. The main study ran on the rebuilt lists and reported the smaller number — which is the number the laboratory can defend in a viva, a review and a replication. The redesign was the deliverable; it cost two weeks and saved a two-session study from measuring its own word list.

“Hana found in two weeks what our pilot was politely hiding: the effect was in the word list, not in the design. The rebuilt study cost less than the one we nearly ran.”

Prof. Daniel Okafor — Principal Investigator, psycholinguistics laboratory · on this study

A null result, written up as one

STUDY VC-2024-007
Client
University–clinic research team
Year
2024
Methods
EEG / ERP — sentence processing
Engagement
Analysis rescue, mid-study
Deliverable
Rebuilt pipeline + full write-up of the null
Status
Complete — manuscript with the client team

The brief

An EEG study of agreement processing had collected its data when the team's analyst left the institution. The raw files, a half-finished preprocessing script and a funder deadline arrived together. The ask was simple to state: find out what the data say, in time to report it.

The decisions

The pipeline was rebuilt from the raw recordings rather than repaired, so that every step could be defended: filtering, artefact rejection and exclusions were documented with counts at each stage, not described in adjectives. The pre-specified analysis was run exactly as registered. One further analysis was run and labelled, in the write-up and in the code, as exploratory.

The outcome

The pre-specified effect was not there. That is the finding, and it was written up as one: a full report of a well-run study with a null result, the pipeline archived alongside it, and the exploratory analysis presented as a hypothesis for a future study — not as the answer to this one. A null result from a clean pipeline is worth more to the field than a positive one from a pipeline nobody can re-run.

Two laboratories, one protocol

STUDY VC-2022-021
Client
Two university laboratories, two countries
Year
2022
Methods
Behavioural — auditory word recognition
Engagement
Replication design & harmonisation
Deliverable
Shared protocol, shared stimulus set, shared analysis script
Status
Complete — joint report published by the two labs

The brief

Two laboratories wanted to replicate a published auditory word-recognition effect — together, with one protocol, so that whatever they found would be one finding and not two arguments. Neither lab wanted to run “the other lab's study”; both wanted a study they could each defend at home.

The decisions

The stimuli were re-recorded by a single speaker for both sites, so the audio files are literally identical. One protocol document and one analysis script were version-controlled and frozen before either lab collected a participant. The differences that could not be harmonised — the two participant populations' language backgrounds — were logged in the protocol as known site differences, not smoothed over in the write-up.

The outcome

Stated carefully, because that is the only honest way to state it: the effect held in the first laboratory at close to its published size, and was attenuated in the second. The joint report attributes the difference to the documented difference in participant language background — a result about the effect's boundary conditions, not a verdict that one laboratory “failed”. Both labs signed the same report.
