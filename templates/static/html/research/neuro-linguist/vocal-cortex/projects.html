S-01 · 2023—2024

Vowel categories and the N400

48 participants · 12-step F1 continuum · /i/ to /e/

We synthesised a 12-step continuum between /i/ and /e/ by moving F1 in equal steps while holding F2 and F3 fixed. Participants heard each token in a carrier sentence and pressed a button to classify it. The psychometric boundary fell between steps 6 and 7. The N400 amplitude, measured at 350–450 ms, followed the category boundary rather than the acoustic step: trials on either side of the boundary differed by 2.9 µV, while steps of the same acoustic size within a category did not.

The effect replicated across two language groups, but the boundary itself moved by about 40 Hz between them, which is why the dialect of the listener is now a factor in every model we fit.

S-02 · 2022—2024

Repair, not surprise

64 participants · garden-path sentences · with and without a plausible continuation

The P600 has been explained as a response to syntactic surprise, to reanalysis, or to both. We separated them by building garden-path sentences that either could or could not be repaired at the disambiguating word. Surprise was matched across conditions; repairability was not.

The result supports the reanalysis account: the P600 is the cost of building a new structure, not the cost of being surprised. It also predicts that a sentence which is surprising but easy to repair should produce a small P600, which is what we found in a follow-up with 40 participants.

S-03 · 2021—2023

The replication that failed

120 participants · direct replication of Halvorsen & Lind (2020)

Our 2020 paper reported that two hours of vowel category training shifted the N400 boundary by 22 ms. It was a small study — 32 participants — and the effect was just under the threshold we had pre-registered. We ran it again at four times the sample, with the same stimuli and a pre-registered analysis plan.

Table S-03 — Original versus replication
Measure2020 (n = 32)2023 (n = 120)Status
N400 boundary shift22 ms4 msnot replicated
Behavioural shift18 ms16 msreplicated
Effect size (d)0.510.11attenuated
Posterior probability0.930.41inconclusive

The behavioural effect held: training does move the category boundary. The neural effect did not. Our best explanation is that the original N400 difference was driven by a small number of participants with very noisy data, and that our artefact rejection at the time was too lenient. The lab now uses a fixed, blinded rejection pipeline for every study.

The original paper carries a published correction pointing to this one. We left both on the site because a replication that fails is more informative than a claim that is never checked.

S-04 · 2022—2023

Speakers converge in forty seconds

36 dyads · map task · 8 minutes of dialogue

Pairs described routes on a map to each other while we tracked F2 in their vowels. Convergence — one speaker moving toward the other's F2 — began within 41 seconds on average, well before either reported that the conversation was going well. The amount of convergence predicted later agreement on word choice, not the reverse.

Convergence was asymmetric in most pairs: one speaker moved about twice as far as the other. The direction did not predict who led the task, which argues against a simple accommodation account.

S-05 · 2020—2022

Prediction in a second language

86 participants · Norwegian–English bilinguals · cloze-based prediction

Highly predictable sentence endings produce a smaller N400 than unpredictable ones, which is usually read as evidence for prediction. We asked whether bilinguals predict in their second language to the same degree, and whether the answer depends on proficiency or on exposure.
