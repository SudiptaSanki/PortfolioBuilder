Study № 34 · 2024

The crossover that wasn't near

Client
Deep-tech optimisation company
Line
Q.01 · Advantage assessment
Duration
5 weeks · study days
Deliverable
Resource estimate + crossover analysis
Status
Closed · conclusion published with consent
The brief

A routing-software company had a deck claiming quantum advantage for its logistics optimisation "at around 200 qubits", and a funding round opening in eight weeks. The ask was not whether the algorithm was elegant — it was — but whether the claim would survive a technically literate investor's questions.

Decisions inside the work

The estimate was run at the client's actual instance sizes, not asymptotic ones, and against the best classical heuristic their own engineers used — not against a textbook straw man. Assumptions were printed on page two: two-qubit gate error 10⁻³, a stated surface-code overhead model, and the client's real constraint that a routing answer is worth money for about ninety seconds.

The outcome

At every problem size the client will plausibly reach this decade, the classical heuristic wins — on time, on cost, and it is not close. Under the study's printed assumptions, the crossover sits near 10⁶ physical qubits, roughly three orders of magnitude beyond the deck's figure. The advantage claim was withdrawn from the deck before the round; what replaced it was the crossover analysis itself, which is a more interesting thing to show an investor. The company raised on the strength of its classical product. The study's conclusion, verbatim: not yet — and the missing factor is not qubits, it is error rate at scale.

Study № 29 · 2023

The assumption doing all the work

Client
European quantum-hardware consortium
Line
Q.02 · Error-model review
Duration
7 weeks · review days
Deliverable
Restated roadmap, assumptions as milestones
Status
Closed · roadmap adopted by the board
The brief

A consortium's roadmap promised fault-tolerant machines by a named year, and the board wanted an outside referee before committing the next funding tranche. The document was impressive: forty pages, measured data in the early milestones, confident dates in the later ones.

Decisions inside the work

Every number in the roadmap was traced to its source and labelled: measured, modelled, or assumed. Thirty-nine of forty-one load-bearing numbers had a source. The date did not come from any of them. It came from number forty: an assumption, nowhere stated in the document, that two-qubit gate error rates would improve tenfold on a smooth schedule — with no mechanism, no owner, and no measurement that would announce its failure early.

The outcome

The roadmap was restated with the assumption promoted to a printed milestone with a measurable trigger: the dates became conditions. "When sustained two-qubit error below X is demonstrated across Y devices, the following becomes schedulable." The board adopted the restated version and funded the tranche against the conditions. Nothing in the consortium's hardware programme changed; everything in its promises did.

"Vera found the assumption our roadmap was standing on. It was load-bearing, and it was not written anywhere in the document. The restated roadmap is shorter, duller, and the first version our board has actually believed."

— Programme director, the consortium (name and institution withheld under the engagement's terms; a reference is available to funders on request)
Study № 41 · 2025

A negative result, reported

Client
University foundations group
Line
Q.04 · Foundations collaboration
Duration
One semester · study days
Deliverable
Null-result write-up + analysis code
Status
Closed · write-up public
The brief

A university group had preliminary data suggesting an improved contextuality witness — a genuinely interesting claim, if it held. They brought the practice in before running the full experiment, which is the correct order and rarer than it should be.

Decisions inside the work

The analysis was pre-registered before the full data existed: the witness, the estimator, the exclusion rules, the threshold for calling it an effect. The re-analysis was run blinded, with condition labels held by a group member who did no fitting. These are boring decisions. They are also the whole difference between a result and a rumour.

The outcome

Under the pre-registered analysis, the effect was not there. The preliminary signal traced to a selection effect in the early, hand-picked runs — exactly the failure mode pre-registration exists to catch. The collaboration wrote it up as a null result, with the analysis code released, including the section explaining what the preliminary data had actually shown. The group's next grant application cited the null result as preliminary work, which is what it was. A negative result reported cleanly is a result; the file is closed and the notebook entry is not embarrassed.
