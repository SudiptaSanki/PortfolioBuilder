Dephasing is not decay

An excited atom that emits a photon loses energy: that is T1, and it is irreversible in the obvious way. An atom that is merely watched loses coherence without losing energy. The populations stay where they were; the off-diagonal terms of the density matrix shrink. In the sphere above, the vector tips toward the equator and then shortens along that direction.

The distinction matters experimentally because the two processes have different signatures. Energy decay removes contrast and changes the count rate; pure dephasing removes contrast and leaves the count rate alone. If your fringes fade and your singles rate is flat, the environment is watching but not taking.

Note 1

For a two-level system the coherence is ρ₀₁, and the visibility of a fringe is 2|ρ₀₁|. A visibility of 1 means a pure superposition; 0 means a classical mixture of paths.

The fringe is a measurement, not a picture

In the double-slit panel, each bright band is a place where the two path amplitudes add. The moment any physical record distinguishes the paths — a polariser, a stray photon, a detector that does not fire — the bands fill in. The pattern does not fade gradually because the apparatus is imperfect; it fades because the paths are no longer indistinguishable.

Englert’s relation makes this quantitative: V² + D² ≤ 1, where V is fringe visibility and D is the distinguishability of the paths. You can have a perfect fringe or perfect knowledge, never both. Most of our lab time goes into pushing D down without losing photons.

Note 2

Englert, Phys. Rev. Lett. 77, 2154 (1996). The bound is saturated by an ideal which-path detector; real detectors sit inside it.

What we actually measure

Our interferometer is a fibre Mach–Zehnder with a 4 m path imbalance and a piezo stretcher in one arm. We count coincidences between two superconducting nanowire detectors at 810 nm and sweep the piezo through 2π. A run takes eleven minutes and produces about 40,000 counts, which is enough to fit a visibility to ±0.4%.

The hard part is not the optics; it is the 4 m of fibre that is not in the cryostat. Thermal drift in that spool moves the phase faster than the piezo can compensate, so the whole bench sits on a floating slab in a room held at 21 ± 0.2 °C.

Note 3

The 4 m imbalance is deliberate: it lets us insert a delay line and ask whether coherence survives a longer path, which is the whole point of the phonon-bath experiment.

Current questions

  • Q1

    Does a mechanical phonon bath retain a record of a photon that passed through, and for how long?

  • Q2

    Can we use a second photon as a which-path detector with a controlled, calibrated D?

  • Q3

    What is the visibility floor set by the detector timing jitter rather than by the state?

Open

Two of the three are doctoral projects. The third is the question every referee asks, and I would like an answer that is not an estimate.
