SAMPLE FILES · KESSLER LOW-TEMPERATURE LABORATORY

Three files, told the way they measured.

A characterisation lab’s portfolio is its reports. These three files are the ones clients have agreed — or their NDAs allow — to have described in public: one where the honest number hurt, one hanging in a museum with its margins on the wall, and one where the material was innocent and the magnet was guilty. Files are anonymised to client level where NDAs require it; the underlying data belong to the client and are never published by the lab.

SAMPLE FILE 01

The datasheet that wasn’t

BELOW DATASHEET — REPORTED AS MEASURED
SAMPLENb₃Sn wire, one production batch, 14 lengths
CLIENTSuperconducting wire manufacturer (name under NDA)
MEASUREMENTJc at 12 T, 4.2 K · four-probe transport · 3 cooldowns on the worst length
OUTCOMEMean Jc 18% below the published datasheet; batch re-graded by the client

THE BRIEF

A wire manufacturer was qualifying a new production batch of Nb₃Sn against its own published datasheet, ahead of a magnet contract that specified the datasheet grade. They asked for an independent characterisation — and, to their credit, they asked for the distribution, not a pass/fail stamp.

THE DECISIONS

All fourteen lengths in the batch were measured, not a sample of three: batch spread is exactly what a datasheet average conceals. The worst-performing length was taken through three full cooldowns to exclude a mounting or contact artefact before anyone was allowed to conclude anything. The report carried the full distribution, the worst length, the uncertainty budget and the cooldown log — and the headline figure was the mean against the datasheet, stated plainly: 18% below, at 12 T and 4.2 K.

THE OUTCOME

The client re-graded the batch, supplied the magnet contract at the grade the wire actually met, and changed its own datasheet procedure to quote a batch minimum alongside the mean. The lab’s fee was the standard rate card, unchanged by the direction of the result — which is the entire point of a rate card. This file is the laboratory’s reference for what characterisation is for: the number is the deliverable, whether or not it is the number anyone wanted.

“Kessler’s report cost us a product grade and saved us a recall. The distribution was in the report, not just the average — eighteen percent below our own datasheet at 12 tesla, and she showed her cooldowns. We re-graded the batch.”

SAMPLE FILE 02

A puck that stays up, with the maths on the wall

IN SERVICE — MARGINS PRINTED ON THE LABEL
CLIENTScience centre, Amsterdam region
SYSTEMREBCO puck, Ø 45 mm, field-cooled over an NdFeB track
MEASURED MARGIN9.6 N holding force at the 8 mm working gap — 2.3× the puck-and-cradle weight
SERVICE TERMSLiquid-nitrogen top-up roughly every 35 minutes of demonstration; refill log kept in public view

THE BRIEF

A science centre wanted a levitation exhibit “like the videos” — a puck floating, spinning, apparently free. The videos, mostly, are field-cooled flux pinning presented as antigravity. The centre agreed to have the real phenomenon instead, on one condition from the lab: the exhibit text would tell the truth about what visitors were seeing.

THE DECISIONS

The puck is field-cooled in position and pinned: locked to the field geometry, stable sideways and vertically, which is more interesting than floating — visitors can push it, tilt it, and feel it push back. The holding force was measured on the lab’s load-cell rig at the exhibit’s working gap and the margin printed on the label: 9.6 N against a 4.1 N load. The label also prints the refill reality — a nitrogen top-up about every 35 minutes of demonstration — and the centre keeps the refill log on the wall beside the exhibit, initialled by whichever staff member topped it up.

THE OUTCOME

Two years in service, run daily by the centre’s own staff after a half-day handover. The exhibit text the lab wrote for it ends: “This is flux pinning, not antigravity. The puck is locked to an invisible track — and the lock is the discovery.” It remains the lab’s favourite kind of deliverable: a demonstrator that demonstrates the actual physics.

SAMPLE FILE 03

Geometry, not material

FORCE ×2.1 — SAME DISC, NEW ARRAY
CLIENTUniversity–industry research group
SAMPLEBulk REBCO (YBCO) disc, Ø 50 mm, melt-grown
FINDINGForce capped by the client’s magnet-array gradient, not by the disc’s Jc
OUTCOMEArray redesigned; measured force up 2.1× with the identical disc

THE BRIEF

A research group had grown a bulk YBCO disc they believed in, and their levitation rig said otherwise: the measured force was well below what their critical-current numbers promised. They commissioned a flux-pinning study expecting a materials verdict — which pinning centres were missing, and what to change in the growth.

THE DECISIONS

The disc was measured twice: over the client’s own magnet array, and over the lab’s reference array, under identical field-cooling. Over the reference array the disc performed to its Jc — the material was innocent. The client’s array spread its field too evenly across the disc’s face: pinning force lives on the field gradient, and their gradient was shallow where the disc needed it steep. The study’s deliverable changed mid-flight, with the client’s agreement, from a growth report to an array redesign.

THE OUTCOME

A segmented array with a steeper gradient over the disc face took the measured force up by a factor of 2.1 — same disc, same cooldown protocol, new geometry. The group’s subsequent paper reports array geometry alongside material parameters in its methods, which is the study’s durable result: in levitation, the magnet is half the material science, and any force figure quoted without the array that produced it is only half a measurement.

A note on what is not here. Replication files (S.05) are never described in public while a claim is live, and most manufacturers’ files stay at the level of File 01: anonymised, with the data held by the client. If your material cannot survive being written about this plainly, it will not survive the measurement either — which is rather the point of booking one.
