I read forests from what the resin caught

The Cretaceous was a greenhouse with forests at both poles and no ice caps. Amber and fossil leaves are two independent records of that world, and my work is mostly about making them agree.

I grew up in Trieste, where the limestone is full of rudist shells, and I went to university expecting to study dinosaurs. A first-year course on fossil plants changed that: the plants were the part of the Cretaceous that could actually be counted, measured and compared across sites.

Amber came later. A museum internship in Vitoria-Gasteiz put me in front of a drawer of 400 unlabelled pieces, and I spent a summer identifying them. That drawer is still the core of the collection, and the census we published in 2022 doubled the deposit's known diversity.

My reputation, such as it is, comes from combining the two records. The animals in the amber tell you who was there; the stomata in the leaves tell you what the air was like. Put them together and you get a forest with a CO₂ value attached, which is more useful than either alone.

I am cautious about the numbers. Stomatal proxies carry a factor-of-two uncertainty and depend on the calibration species, so every reconstruction I publish comes with a range and, where possible, a second, independent proxy.

Teaching and training

Amber taphonomy — a master's module, six weeks. Students describe a real specimen, produce a plate drawing and defend an identification against the collection's catalogue.

Field course: Cretaceous Iberia — two weeks in Álava and Teruel each June. Mapping, collecting, and the ethics of sampling a finite deposit.

Workshops — synchrotron imaging for palaeontologists, run with ALBA twice a year.

Service

Associate editor, Review of Palaeobotany and Palynology (2022–). Reviewer for Nature Communications, Palaeontology and Cretaceous Research.

Member of the Spanish palaeontological heritage committee. I also answer identification queries from amateur collectors, which is how several of the best pieces in the collection were found.
