A drop of resin falls on a forest floor, traps what is small enough to be caught, and hardens into a window that opens 113 million years later. I read that window: what lived in the Cretaceous understorey, and what the leaves of the same forest say about the air it breathed.

01

The archive is small, and that is the point

Amber preserves soft tissue, behaviour and whole micro-habitats that bones never do. It also preserves them selectively: only resin-producing forests, only organisms small enough to be caught, only the chemistry that survives burial.

What resin catches

Insects, spiders, pollen, spores, plant hairs, feathers, and the occasional small vertebrate. A single piece can hold a food web: a wasp carrying pollen, a mite riding the wasp, fungal hyphae on the mite.

61 arthropod families recorded

What it misses

Large animals, aquatic organisms, and anything that lived above the resin-producing trees. The assemblage is a biased sample of a forest, and every reconstruction has to say so.

bias correction: 3 taphonomic filters

Why leaves matter

Amber gives the animals; fossil leaves give the climate. The same formation yields conifer shoots whose stomatal density records the CO₂ of the atmosphere they grew in, which lets the animals be placed in a quantified world.

stomatal counts: 14,600

“The amber is not a time capsule. It is a filter that happened to keep the small things, and a good reconstruction starts by admitting what the filter removed.”

Stomatal CO₂ proxy and inclusion inspector

04

How a 4 mm fossil is read

01 · surface

Light microscopy

Polished faces photographed under transmitted, reflected and polarised light. The inclusion is drawn at the microscope before any scan, because the drawing records uncertainty that the scan hides.

02 · volume

Synchrotron micro-CT

Propagation-phase imaging at ALBA BL20, 0.6 µm voxel. The scan reveals internal structures — ovipositors, mouthparts, pollen — that the polished surface does not show.

03 · chemistry

FTIR and pyrolysis

Infrared spectroscopy identifies the resin's botanical source and its diagenetic path. Pyrolysis-GC-MS confirms whether the amber is Class Ib, as most Álava material is.

04 · climate

Stomatal counts

Leaf fragments cleared, mounted and counted at 400×. Ten fields per leaf, three leaves per horizon, and a calibration curve for each plant group.
