Low-frequency radio · 21-cm cosmology

The universe is mostly hydrogen, and most of it is too cold to see

I spend nights with the Halcyon Array, sixty-four crossed dipoles in the Karoo, counting the cold hydrogen that sits between galaxies and timing pulsars to a few hundred nanoseconds. Two measurements, one instrument, a great deal of careful bookkeeping.

Two numbers decide whether the night was worth it

Move the array or the gas, and the fringes move with them. Change the channel, and a pulsar arrives late by an amount that is a promise about how much ionised gas it passed through.

Sonification

Opt-in

A 5.7 ms rotation is a 175 Hz tone. The array stores it as a click train; here it is a filtered drone with an 11 Hz comb on top, so you can hear the pulse rate rather than read it.

Audio off

Synthesised in the browser with two oscillators and a noise buffer. No audio files, no autoplay, no cookies. Headphones at low volume; the transient is sharp on purpose.
