Programme list

Five programmes, one array, and a queue that never quite empties

Each entry lists what the measurement is for, how many hours it has taken, and the honest state of it. Two are producing papers; one is still a prototype; one exists mostly so the others can be believed.

01

21-cm cosmology · Principal investigator

Intensity mapping at redshift 0.2–0.6

A drift-scan survey of 1,420 square degrees, cross-correlated with optical galaxy catalogues so the cosmological signal survives foreground removal. The current limit is a 0.9 mK noise floor per 4 MHz channel after 320 hours.

Band
950–1250 MHz
Integration
320 h, 41 nights
Noise floor
0.9 mK / 4 MHz
Status
Second data release

02

Pulsar timing · Lead modeller

Timing six millisecond pulsars to 310 ns

A five-year campaign on six southern millisecond pulsars, run at 0.7 and 1.4 GHz on alternate nights. The 310 ns residual rms is limited by the solar wind model, not the instrument, which is a good problem to have and a hard one to solve.

Sources
6 MSPs
Residual rms
310 ns
Cadence
2 per fortnight
Status
Ongoing to 2027

03

Instrumentation · Co-investigator

A receiver that survives the Karoo summer

The 2024 upgrade replaced indium-phosphide low-noise amplifiers with a GaAs design and a passive thermal strap. System temperature dropped from 78 K to 41 K at 1.1 GHz, which is worth more than any analysis trick we have tried.

Band
0.9–1.6 GHz
Tsys
78 K → 41 K
Deployed
64/64 stations
Status
Commissioned 2024

04

Methods · Maintainer

Flagging a mobile tower eleven kilometres away

An open-source RFI flagging stack built on visibility statistics rather than threshold cuts. It removes 0.7% of data and leaves the Epoch of Reionisation-style 21-cm window usable at 180 MHz, where the site is otherwise unworkable after dark.

Method
SumThreshold + GP
Data removed
0.7%
Runtime
11 min / night
Status
Open repository

05

Public data · Lead

Teaching set: a pulsar you can time yourself

Twelve hours of raw voltages on a bright millisecond pulsar, released with a notebook that folds, dedisperses and fits a timing solution. Built for a third-year course and now used in four countries, mostly by people who have never touched a correlator.
