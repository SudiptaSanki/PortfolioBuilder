LOG 02

The instrument

FIRST PASS · NOT A PIPELINE

Neutral hydrogen — most of the ordinary matter you will ever point a dish at — emits and absorbs at a rest frequency of 1420.4 MHz, the 21 cm line. Motion shifts the line: away from us, to lower frequency; towards us, to higher. This converter is the first pass I run on any candidate line, with the arithmetic printed in full so you can check it the way a referee would.

Check it by hand — worked example

A line is observed at fobs = 1419.0 MHz. Then:

Δf 1420.4 − 1419.0 = +1.4 MHz
Δf ÷ f₀ 1.4 ÷ 1420.4 = +0.000986
v 299,792 × 0.000986 = +295.5 km/s, receding

Press the “Worked example” preset above and the converter must agree, digit for digit. If it ever doesn’t, the converter is wrong — not the arithmetic.

Sign convention: observed below the rest frequency means the source is receding, and the velocity is written positive — redshift. Observed above it means approaching, negative — blueshift.

Convention note: this page uses the radio convention, defined on frequency, as printed above — the working convention of radio spectroscopy. An optical convention, defined on wavelength, also exists and returns slightly different numbers for the same shift. Pipelines state which they used. So do I.

First pass only: the relation is non-relativistic, with no barycentric correction and no cosmological terms. That is honest arithmetic for Galactic work and for triage. A paper gets the full treatment — see About & method.

What moves the price

Data volume. A 40 TB archive with missing calibration logs is not a 40 GB dataset with tidy ones; reduction is priced on the data as it is, not as the proposal imagined it.

Deadlines set by telescope time. Allocation rounds and observing windows wait for nobody. Work that must land before a fixed proposal deadline is scheduled first and priced as such.

Number of facilities. Making three telescopes’ data agree is a different job from reducing one instrument well.

Telescope time is never in my invoice. It is allocated by peer-reviewed proposal to the strength of the science case. My fee is for making the case, the plan and the data worthy of it.

Case study 01 · the credibility one

The signal that wasn’t

A spectral-line survey found a narrow candidate line near 1419 MHz, exactly where a discovery would want to live. I was brought in to verify it before anyone wrote a press release. The line was real in the data. It was also fixed in the telescope’s frame while the sky drifted past it — the classic signature of something made on Earth, not in space. It resolved as an intermodulation product of a strong out-of-band transmitter, and the survey published it as an interference note instead of a discovery.

That paper is the one I show first. A practice that will kill its own headline is a practice you can trust with yours.

“Priya cost us a headline and saved us a retraction. The interference note she wrote is now the most-cited methods reference our group has.”

Dr Priya Nair — principal investigator of the survey in Case Study 01
