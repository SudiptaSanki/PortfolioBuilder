LOG 07

Who is keeping this log

DR MARA ELLISON

I came to radio astronomy through a physics degree and stayed for the data. My doctorate was in spectral-line surveys — hydrogen, mostly, the 1420.4 MHz line this site is built around — and I spent eight years as a staff scientist at a national observatory, where the work is equal parts observing, calibration, and explaining to a time-allocation committee why your field deserves another night.

Since 2019 I have worked independently as Spectrum Log. The pattern of the work surprised me at first: groups rarely lack telescope time or talent. They lack a spare pair of expert hands at the three moments that decide whether a programme becomes a paper — the proposal, the reduction, and the week a candidate signal has to be believed or buried. Those are the three moments I sell.

I work from Cheshire, within sight, on a clear day, of the dish in the photograph. Radio data, though, is just files: my clients are on four continents, and the observing runs I support happen while I am asleep as often as not. The log is kept in UTC. So am I, mostly.

Credentials, briefly: PhD in radio astronomy; eight years observatory staff scientist; co-author on survey papers I am happy to list on request; fluent in the standard reduction ecosystems and in the dialects of three archives’ retired formats — a smaller and more useful skill than it sounds, as Case Study 02 shows.

LOG 08

How an engagement runs

FIVE ENTRIES, IN ORDER
01

Scoping call & data audit

We look at the data as it actually is — volume, format, calibration state, documentation — before anyone quotes anything. Half of all surprises in this trade are visible in the first hour, if someone looks.

02

Proposal-cycle support

If telescope time is needed, the work is scheduled backwards from the allocation round: science case, technical justification, observing plan, and the sensitivity arithmetic done properly — the full treatment, not the converter on the home page.

03

Calibration & flagging

Bandpass, gain and flux calibration against standard calibrators, then interference excision in two passes: automated flagging for the known shapes of trouble, manual inspection for the rest. Every excision is logged with its reason. Data is never deleted silently — flagged, documented, reversible.

04

Imaging & cubes

Continuum images and spectral cubes produced with the weighting and cleaning choices stated in the delivery notes, and the sensitivity actually achieved reported alongside the sensitivity that was promised.

05

Delivery you can re-run

Versioned data, the scripts that produced every figure, and notes written for the stranger who opens the archive in nine years. That stranger is the real client of every delivery.

LOG 09

The honesty page

READ BEFORE THE FEES, IDEALLY

A detection is not a discovery

A line in a spectrum is a claim about a receiver as much as about the sky. Until a candidate survives independent re-reduction, a frame-of-reference test and, ideally, a second facility, it is a detection — and my reports will call it one. The 1400–1427 MHz band is internationally protected for radio astronomy, and interference still gets in: out-of-band signals, harmonics, intermodulation. Assuming your signal is celestial is the one error I am hired to prevent.

The converter is a first pass

The converter on the home page uses the non-relativistic radio convention and stops there: no barycentric correction for Earth’s motion, no relativistic relation for high-redshift sources, where the full formula — not the schoolbook one — applies. It is the right arithmetic for triage and for Galactic work. Anything destined for print gets the complete treatment, and the method section says which conventions were used.

Your data stays yours

I work on copies; originals never leave your archive. Nothing I reduce is published, presented or reused by me without written agreement, and I ask for acknowledgement rather than authorship unless we agree otherwise in advance. If I find something in your data that you would not want found, you hear it from me first, in writing, before anyone else hears it at all.

LOG 10

Engagement terms

THE SMALL PRINT, LARGE
  • BasisDay rates as printed on the home page (Log 03), minimum engagement two days; a fixed quote follows the scoping call and is honoured unless the data turns out to be materially different from its description — in which case we talk before anyone spends anything.
  • SchedulingProposal-round deadlines are booked in the order enquiries arrive. Two analysis slots per quarter are held for verification work, because candidates do not schedule themselves politely.
  • DeliverablesCalibrated data or cubes, reduction scripts, delivery notes, and a methods paragraph written for your paper. Raw working files are retained for twelve months, then deleted and the deletion confirmed.
  • ConflictsI will not verify a candidate signal for a group while also advising a competing claim on the same signal. If a conflict appears mid-engagement, I say so and withdraw from one side.
  • PaymentInvoiced monthly in arrears for long engagements, on delivery for short ones. Institutes’ purchase-order processes are familiar and painless; I have filled in the forms before.
