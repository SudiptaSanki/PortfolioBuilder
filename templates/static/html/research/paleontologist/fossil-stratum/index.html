STRATUM 01

The Strata Explorer

538.8 MA — PRESENT

A fossil is only as old as the bed it came out of, and the bed is dated by the fossils it holds — that circular-sounding bargain is held together by index fossils: species so widespread, so short-lived and so recognisable that finding one pins the rock to a slice of time. Work down the column — youngest at the top, as it lies in the ground in reverse — and read what each period hands a preparator: its index fossils, its typical rock, and the work that rock demands.

PeriodAge (Ma)

Jurassic

Mesozoic Era · “Age of Reptiles”
Age range
201.4 — 145.0 million years ago (56.4 million years)
Index fossils
Ammonites — a new fauna every few hundred thousand years, which is what makes them the finest clock in the column. Belemnite guards and the oyster Gryphaea support them in marine beds.
Typical rock
Blue Lias limestone and shale on this coast; mudstones, sandstones and oolitic limestones inland.
Preparation
Hard limestone nodules: air scribe for bulk, needle under the microscope for the last millimetre. Pyritised specimens from the shale need humidity control for life — pyrite decay is the slow enemy, not the scribe.

Ages are the International Chronostratigraphic Chart values (ICS, 2023 revision), in millions of years before present. Boundaries that matter to this bench are marked where they fall: the K–Pg boundary at 66.0 Ma closes the Cretaceous; the Permian–Triassic boundary at 251.9 Ma is the largest extinction in the record.

A typical single specimen costs £400–£2,500 in preparation. Anything larger is quoted after assessment, in writing, with the hours broken down by stage — the same breakdown the case files on the Cases page show.

What moves the price

  • Matrix hardness. The single biggest factor. Soft shale gives up its fossil at roughly 15 cm of specimen per bench hour; hard sandstone and ironstone at nearer 4 cm. The estimator below uses exactly these rates.
  • Fragility & consolidation. Bone that powders at a touch is consolidated before, during and after prep — each cycle adds hours that no one can shortcut safely.
  • Pyrite. Pyritised specimens need isolation and humidity-controlled storage advice; the prep is faster, the aftercare is not optional.
  • Size & jacketing. Anything over 120 cm, or still in a field jacket, is assessed before it is quoted. Jackets come off slowly, on purpose.
  • Deadline. Exhibition deadlines are met by re-sequencing the queue, not by rushing a scribe. Rush work is priced as such, or declined.

THE MODEL, IN PRINT: hours = 2 (assessment & documentation) + length ÷ rate, where the rate is 15 cm/hour in soft matrix, 8 cm/hour in limestone, 4.5 cm/hour in hard sandstone or ironstone. The band runs from 0.85× to 1.25× the central figure, rounded; prices are the band hours × £45, rounded to the nearest £5. Specimens over 120 cm are quoted after assessment only — the band above is indicative for them, never a quote.
