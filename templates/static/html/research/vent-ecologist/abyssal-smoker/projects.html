F-01

Lucky Strike

A seamount at the ridge axis, 1,685 m, with a lava lake at the summit and vents around its rim. The fluid is basalt-hosted, 324 °C, pH 3.1, with 8.4 mmol/kg of sulphide. The field is the best-studied in the Azores and the only one with a continuous 12-year image series.

The community is dominated by Bathymodiolus azoricus in dense beds, with Rimicaris exoculata in patches where the flow is stronger and alvinellids on the hottest chimney faces. Our transects show the mussel bed advancing about 30 cm a year along one chimney and retreating along another, which is why the field is monitored rather than mapped once.

What I would do differently: the first six years of imagery were shot without a scale bar in frame. Every area estimate from that period carries a 20% error that no amount of annotation can remove.

F-02

Rainbow

An ultramafic-hosted field on the Rainbow massif, off-axis, 2,300 m. The fluid is the hottest and most acidic we sample in the region: 365 °C, pH 2.8, 12.6 mmol/kg of sulphide and a methane concentration an order of magnitude above Lucky Strike, because the water is reacting with mantle peridotite rather than basalt.

The fauna reflects the chemistry. Mussels are scarce; the chimney faces are covered with shrimp at 1,800 per square metre, and the anemones and alvinellids are the only large animals in the hottest zones. The sulphide is there, but the fluid is so acidic that carbonate shells are not viable on the active chimney, which is part of why the mussel beds are absent.

What I would do differently: we deployed loggers without a recovery line in 2024 and lost two of six when the chimney collapsed. A 40 m line costs nothing and would have saved the season's temperature record.

F-03

Menez Gwen

The shallowest field in the region at 850 m, on a small volcano north of Lucky Strike. The fluid is cooler, 281 °C, and the sulphide is lower at 5.1 mmol/kg, which makes the field a natural experiment: same ridge, same fauna pool, less energy. The mussel beds are denser but the individual mussels are smaller, and the shrimp are almost absent.

Because it is shallow, it is also the field most exposed to a future change in the water column: a warm anomaly at 850 m reaches the vent field directly, where at 2,300 m it does not. We have a temperature logger on the seafloor there that has run since 2019 and shows a 0.4 °C seasonal signal.

What I would do differently: the first imagery was shot in a single day. Community change at this field happens on a scale of months, and a single day is a snapshot of the weather, not the field.

F-04

Saldanha

A basalt-hosted field at 2,200 m on the ridge axis south of the Azores, discovered in 2013 and mapped by us in 2021. The fluid is 302 °C and pH 3.0, and the fauna is a mixture of Lucky Strike and Rainbow elements: mussels, alvinellids, and a shrimp population that is genetically closer to the Rainbow animals than to Lucky Strike, 400 km away.

The connectivity result is the important one. Larvae in this region travel on the Azores Current for weeks, and the fields are arranged along it like beads. Saldanha is the southern bead, and its shrimp arrive from the north.

What I would do differently: the discovery cruise was a geology cruise, and we had one day of biology. One day produced a species list and no community map.

F-05

Eiffel Tower spire

A single 22 m chimney at Lucky Strike, named for its shape and sampled every year since 2014. It is the most photogenic structure in the field and the most instructive: the top is hot and colonised by alvinellids, the middle is the mussel zone, and the base is in diffuse flow with a microbial mat. One structure holds the whole gradient.

In 2022 the spire lost its top three metres in a collapse. The alvinellids were gone within a year; the mussels below were unaffected. Recovery at the top has been slow, which tells us that the hot-edge community is the fragile one, not the dense mussel bed that looks more vulnerable.

What I would do differently: we have no imagery of the collapse itself. A camera on a frame, running for a year, would have caught it.
