Campaigns — 2015 to 2026

Five campaigns, four of which lost days to weather

Each entry gives the question, the measurement, and the number that came out of it. Where a campaign failed to produce the intended result, the failure is stated first, because that is usually the more useful sentence.

01 / 2024

Droplete size spectra over the Bjørnholt ridge

A six-week ground-based campaign with a forward-scattering probe on a ridge 340 m above the fjord, sampling orographic cloud as it crossed. The intended result was a clean relationship between updraft speed and droplet number; the actual result was that updraft speed at the ridge is poorly correlated with droplet number because the cloud has already been processed by the time it arrives.

Useful by-product: 214 droplet spectra, of which 31 % are bimodal, which is a much higher fraction than the literature assumes for marine stratocumulus.

02 / 2020—21

Ship tracks and the sulphur cap

The 2020 fuel-sulphur limit cut ship emissions sharply and without warning, which made it the largest unintentional aerosol experiment in the northern hemisphere. We tracked 1,140 ship tracks in satellite imagery and compared their cloud optical depth before and after the change, controlling for meteorological regime by matching sea surface temperature and lower-tropospheric stability.

Optical depth in tracks fell by 12 % within eighteen months. Two-thirds of the original track sample had to be discarded because the plume could not be separated from natural variability; that filtering is the paper.

03 / 2023

Winter stratus in the high Arctic

Three weeks at 78°N in February, measuring CCN and cloud droplets in a boundary layer that was, for eleven consecutive days, colder than −20 °C and therefore mostly ice. The liquid-cloud instrument spent most of the campaign measuring a phase it was not designed for.

What survived was a small, high-quality set of 46 liquid segments inside mixed-phase cloud, showing CCN concentrations an order of magnitude lower than summer and droplet effective radii correspondingly larger. The campaign is a cautionary tale about season selection.

04 / 2019

An intercomparison of four CCN counters

Four instruments, one chamber, one aerosol source, two weeks. The counters agreed within 8 % in number at 0.4 % supersaturation and within 25 % at 0.1 %, where the calibration is most sensitive to the column temperature profile.

The outcome is a correction table now used by three groups and a strong argument for measuring the column profile rather than trusting the set point.

05 / 2025

An open teaching dataset

One campaign's raw data, cleaned and documented, released with a notebook that runs the closure from size distribution to droplet number. Built for a master's course and now used in six countries, mostly by people who have never seen a CCN counter.

The dataset includes the failures: two days when the inlet iced, one when the counter lost its set point, and the afternoon a technician tripped over the sample line.
