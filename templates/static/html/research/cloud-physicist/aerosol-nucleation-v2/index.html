The activation explorer

Instrument: teaching model M1
Chamber loading N = 1,000 cm⁻³

A cloud condensation nucleus (CCN) counter holds air at a set supersaturation — vapour slightly beyond saturation — and counts what fraction of the particles activate into droplets. That fraction, measured across a range of supersaturations, is an activation spectrum: the basic product of this practice. The explorer below runs the same idea in miniature.

Teaching model

This is a teaching model, not a research result. It applies one printed rule — f = (S − S₀) × k, capped at 1 and floored at 0 — using the thresholds and slopes in Table M1. Real activation follows κ-Köhler theory across a whole size distribution; no single line describes the atmosphere. The model's job is to make the shape of the phenomenon legible: thresholds exist, eagerness differs, and soot is reluctant.

Sulfate — the aged, acidic workhorse of continental air — needs a firmer push than sea salt: nothing below its threshold, then a steady climb. Much of the aerosol over land behaves roughly like this class, which is why its spectrum is the one modellers ask for first.
