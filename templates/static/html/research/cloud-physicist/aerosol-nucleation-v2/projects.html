CASE FILE 01 · AN-01

The Smoke Season Spectra

The ask

A modelling consortium was parameterising biomass-burning aerosol with a borrowed spectrum from a different fuel and a different decade. They needed activation spectra for their own source class — fresh smoke and smoke aged a day — measured on one calibration chain.

What was done

Twelve chamber days. Two source classes generated and aged under stated conditions; spectra taken at six supersaturations from 0.1% to 1.0%; filtered-air blanks bracketed every burn day, and each spectrum was repeated on a second day before it entered the record.

The outcome

Spectra delivered as a parameterisation range with its repeat scatter, not a single line. The aged class activated more readily than the fresh — consistent with coating uptake — and the memo states exactly how far that conclusion reaches and where it stops.

CASE FILE 02 · AN-02

Half the Sky

The ask

Profile cloud condensation nuclei below and inside cloud across a frontal system: eight flights over three weeks, feeding a seasonal comparison the programme had announced in its own plan.

What was done

Four of the eight flights were lost — two to weather, one to aircraft unserviceability, one to airspace. The four that flew completed their profile legs in full, on the campaign's own calibration record, and the lost days were charged at the standby terms agreed before the campaign began.

The outcome

The vertical-profile conclusion held and was delivered with its dataset. The seasonal comparison was withdrawn — in the report, in writing: four flights cannot carry a seasonal claim, and the report says so on its first page. Analysis days were billed only against flights flown.

CASE FILE 03 · AN-03

Two Counters, One Number

The ask

Two CCN counters at a mountain observatory disagreed by roughly 30% at low supersaturation, and two teams were publishing from the same air. The site needed one number both teams would sign — or an honest statement that none existed.

What was done

Five days co-located on one air stream. Inlets swapped, flows audited, the calibration chain checked end to end against a reference salt. The disagreement narrowed as each suspect was isolated, and was traced to a flow calibration drift in one counter, worst exactly where the teams disagreed most.

The outcome

A reconciliation memo both teams signed, and a corrected joint dataset. The site's long record was re-flagged for the drift period — the unglamorous part of the job, done anyway, because a quiet correction is still a correction.
