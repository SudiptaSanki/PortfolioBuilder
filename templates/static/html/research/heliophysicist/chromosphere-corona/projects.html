Campaigns 2022–2026

Eight campaigns, one of which was mostly wrong

Each entry gives the region or the observing window, the instrument and the number that came out. The forecasting work is included with its scores, because a space weather page that only shows the physics is hiding the part that has a public consequence.

Showing 8 of 8 campaigns

  1. AR 3664

    The May 2024 storm

    One region, five X-class flares in a week, and a G5 geomagnetic storm on 10 May that put aurora over Mexico. I issued four forecasts in that window. Three were right to within six hours; the fourth called the arrival twelve hours early because I fitted the drag parameter to the faster event two days before. The lesson is in the parameter, not the model.

    G54 forecasts1 miss
  2. LOOP-7

    Kink oscillations in a fan loop

    Eleven loops in a single active region, observed with AIA 171 for four hours at 12-second cadence. Periods from 140 to 380 seconds, damping times of two to four periods. The inferred field strength agrees with the magnetogram extrapolation within 20 %, which for this method is a result rather than a nuisance.

    AIA 17111 loops20 % agreement
  3. FCST-25

    A year of forecasts, scored

    Every M-class call in 2025 with its outcome: 214 forecasts, 152 hits, 62 misses, and 73 false alarms. Hit rate 0.71, false-alarm rate 0.34, base rate 0.48. The base rate is the number that makes the other two readable, and it is the one usually left out of the abstract.

    214 calls0.71 hit0.34 FAR
  4. CME-23

    The cannibal CME of November 2023

    Two eruptions four hours apart, the second overtaking the first and arriving with a shock that no single-CME model predicted. The arrival was eleven hours early and the Dst undershoot was 60 % deeper than forecast. This is the case that made the interaction term a standing item in every forecast I write.

    2 CMEs11 h earlyinteraction
  5. XRS-24

    A flare catalogue cross-check

    Three catalogues of X-class flares over solar cycle 25, compared event by event. They disagree on 7 % of events, mostly the ones near the M/X boundary and the ones from regions partly behind the limb. The disagreement is a calibration issue and it matters for anyone computing a rate.

    3 catalogues7 % disagreement
  6. AR 3872

    The confined flare that looked eruptive

    A long-duration M8 with everything that suggests an eruption: rising post-flare loops, a dimming, a coronal wave. No CME. The coronagraph was unambiguous and the magnetogram showed a strongly anchored arcade. It is the cleanest example I have of why the flare class is not a proxy for ejecta.

    M8.2no ejectaanchored
  7. FCST-24

    Two days of false alarms

    In August 2024 I issued M-class warnings for six consecutive windows and got nothing. The region was complex and stable, which is a combination the complexity metric scores as dangerous and the persistence metric scores as quiet. The post-mortem is published and the metric is now weighted differently, which is the only honest way to lose.

    6 windows0 eventspublished
  8. ICME-22

    The arrival that nobody called

    A slow CME, 380 km/s at the coronagraph, that arrived after 68 hours with a southward field and produced a moderate storm. It was below every warning threshold and it still mattered to a satellite operator. Slow events are under-forecast because the drag models were tuned on the fast ones.

    380 km/s68 hunder-forecast
