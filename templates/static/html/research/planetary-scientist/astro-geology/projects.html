S-01 · 2019—2024

Mare Tranquillitatis chronometry

LROC NAC · 8,600 craters · 3 units

Three lava flows in southern Mare Tranquillitatis have distinct albedo and titanium content, which makes them easy to separate on the image. We counted every crater above 100 m in each unit and fitted separate isochrons. The flows differ by about 250 million years, which is smaller than the formal uncertainty of a single count but resolvable because the counts share the same production function.

Table S-01 — Counts and fitted ages
UnitCraters >100 mDensity (per km²)Model age± 1σ
A (high-Ti)3,2400.413.35 Ga0.18

The caveat: the production function is calibrated on the same kind of surface, so the relative ages are more trustworthy than the absolute ones. A 250-million-year offset is robust; the absolute 3.1 Ga is not.

S-02 · 2022—2024

Jezero crater floor mineralogy

CRISM · Perseverance ground truth · 12 cubes

Orbital spectra of the Jezero floor showed a mix of olivine, carbonate and hydrated silica, but the spatial resolution blurred the contacts. Perseverance's instruments gave the ground truth at centimetre scale. We used the rover data to test which orbital unmixing actually survives contact with the ground.

The comparison changed our band-depth threshold: two of the "hydrated silica" pixels were sulfate mixtures, and one carbonate detection was a spectral artefact of dust coating. The map is more conservative now.

S-03 · 2021—2023

Highland plagioclase

M³ · 1,240 spectra · 6 massifs

The lunar highlands are the original anorthosite crust, and their spectra are bright and nearly featureless because plagioclase has only a weak absorption band. We mapped the 1.25 µm feature across six massifs and found that its depth correlates with the crater population age: older surfaces are more weathered and the band is weaker.

This is the kind of result that complicates a mineral map: the same rock can look different depending on how long it has been exposed. We now publish a weathering correction with every highland abundance map.

S-04 · 2020—2023

Europa's chaos terrain

Galileo SSI · 2,100 craters · 4 regions

Europa has almost no craters, which is the strongest evidence that its surface is young. The chaos terrain — blocks of ice rafted in a darker matrix — is younger still. Counting what few craters exist across four regions gives a surface age of 40–90 million years, with the caveat that the production function is extrapolated from the Moon and the impactor population at Jupiter is different.

S-05 · 2019—2022

Lonar as a spectral analog

Field spectra · 280 samples · Deccan basalt

Lonar is a 1.8 km impact crater in Deccan basalt, and its ejecta is fresh enough to be a good analog for Martian low-albedo surfaces. We measured 280 samples with a field spectrometer and characterised each by XRD and thin section, producing a library where every spectrum has mineralogy attached.

The library is public and has been used by three teams to calibrate CRISM unmixing. The mismatch in the visible slope is the space-weathering term, which is now a free parameter in our inversions.
