Sheet 03 · The Geologist

Petra Lindqvist — trained on Earth, employed off it

Sixteen years of planetary science: a geology degree, a doctorate built on orbiter data, instrument-team science support, and field seasons in the three deserts that teach the most about the one nobody can visit.

The story

Petra Lindqvist trained as a field geologist first — hammer, hand lens, and a mapping season in the Scottish Highlands where the weather grades your work daily. The doctorate moved the field site: a thesis on volcanic terrain mapped entirely from orbiter imaging and laser altimetry, and the discovery that mapping a place you cannot walk is the same discipline with the excuses removed — every unit boundary has to be argued from evidence, because nobody is going out to check.

Then came a decade of mission science support: geological mapping for instrument teams, landing-site working groups, and the long meetings where a map becomes a decision. Areoid was founded in Reykjavík in 2019 for a practical reason — Iceland is the cheapest Mars analogue with an airport — and a principled one: a practice that sells inference should live where the ground truth is a short drive away and constantly embarrassing.

The analogue seasons are the practice's spine: six expeditions across Iceland, the Atacama and Utah, each one commissioned to test a specific orbital reading. Two of the six overturned the interpretation they were sent to confirm. Both reports were published in full. A practice that only confirms is a printing service.

How an engagement runs

Data first. Work begins from the primary orbital data — high-resolution imaging, laser-altimetry topography, imaging spectroscopy — never from another team's finished map. Existing maps are read afterwards, as claims to be tested.

Mapping practice. Map units are defined by stated criteria before mapping starts: what observation, at what threshold, puts a boundary here. Every boundary in the delivered sheet can be traced to the observation that placed it, and the criteria document travels with the map.

Peer review. Every study is red-teamed inside the practice before delivery, and the standard report package reserves time for the client's own science review. Disagreements that survive review are recorded in the report's appendix — not resolved by seniority, and never deleted.

Deliverables. The map or report, the criteria document, the working archive (counts, measurements, rejected interpretations), and a schedule printed on both clocks — sols for the surface plan, Earth dates for the people.

The honesty page

An orbital map is an inference stack. Photons became pixels, pixels became units, units became a history. Each layer adds judgement. The delivered product shows the layers, so a reviewer can stop at the one they distrust.

An analogue is a comparison, not an equivalence. The Atacama can falsify a reading of Mars; it cannot verify one. Earth has weather, biology and a thicker sky, and every analogue conclusion on this site's studies is phrased as what it is: evidence about a process, not proof about a place.

A crater-count age is a range with a model inside it. The count is data; the age depends on a production function and a chronology model, and honest work prints both the range and the dependence. Anyone who quotes you a single-year age for an unvisited surface is rounding their uncertainty, not yours.

Geology informs; it does not certify. This practice will tell a programme exactly what the terrain evidence supports. It will not declare a landing site “safe” — that word belongs to engineers, systems and margins, and a geologist who uses it is selling comfort.

Engagement Terms — Printed

  • Studies are scoped in writing before work starts: question, data, deliverable, deadline, fee — from the printed rate table on sheet S.01.
  • Embargoed or proprietary mission data is handled under the client's NDA; the working archive is returned or destroyed as the client directs.
  • Conclusions are not negotiable as part of the fee. A client may decline to publish a finding; they may not purchase a different one.
  • Analogue fieldwork follows the host country's permits and the practice's field safety plan; a season can be cancelled by weather or ground conditions, and the report says so when it happens.
  • Availability: one analogue season per year; assessment and dating studies booked against mission phase windows.
