Method · core log 02

An age scale is an argument, not a measurement

Depth is easy. Depth is a tape measure and a log sheet. Everything after that — the year of a layer, the temperature behind an isotope value, the velocity under a stake — is inference, and the whole craft is in making the inference visible enough that someone else can disagree with it.

01 — Dating

Counting lines you can barely see

In the upper core the annual layers are visible: a winter layer of dust and a summer layer of clearer ice, a few centimetres thick. Two people count them independently, then reconcile the differences, which are usually at the same three depths. Below the point where the layers thin past the resolution of the line scan, counting stops and the age scale becomes a flow model tied to dated horizons.

Those horizons are the anchors: volcanic sulphate from known eruptions, the 1963 tritium peak, the 1259 Samalas dust band, and in the deepest ice, the Laschamp geomagnetic excursion. Each one has its own uncertainty, and the ensemble of age scales published for a core usually differs by more than any single one admits.

The brittle zone

Between roughly 700 and 1,400 m the ice is under enough stress to fracture as it is cut. Core comes up in pieces, the line scan has gaps, and the gas record is contaminated by air that entered the cracks. Everyone knows this, everyone works around it, and every published record that spans that interval has a note about it in the supplement.

02 — Analysis

Continuous flow, one centimetre at a time

The core is melted from the bottom of a small heated plate, and the meltwater is split into streams that feed an isotope ratio mass spectrometer, a dust counter, a conductivity cell and an ion chromatograph. The result is a record at millimetre to centimetre resolution with all the proxies on the same depth axis, which is the entire advantage: a dust peak and an isotope dip at the same depth are the same event.

Water isotopes give temperature, with a slope near 1.5 °C per mil over Greenland and a spatial correction that matters more than most papers admit. Deuterium excess separates the temperature of condensation from the humidity of the source ocean. Dust and calcium give circulation and aridity. Black carbon gives fire, and it is the proxy that most often turns out to be contamination from the drilling fluid.

Air

The bubbles are crushed in a separate line and the gas is measured for CO₂, CH₄ and the isotopes of carbon. The gas is younger than the ice, by 200 years in the Holocene and by several thousand near the bed, and getting that offset wrong is the classic way to publish a spurious lead–lag relationship.

03 — Dynamics

The same ice, moving

Velocity comes from three places: stakes re-surveyed each season, phase-sensitive radar measuring the internal layers, and GPS on the surface. They agree to within about 10 % on a good day, which sounds poor until you compare it with the spread between two flow models of the same glacier.

Sliding is the hard part. A glacier can be wet and still slow; what matters is the effective pressure at the bed, which is overburden minus water pressure, and that number is measured in boreholes at a handful of points and modelled everywhere else. My own work has been mostly in those boreholes, because a modelled water pressure that has never met a measurement is a number with a nice graph attached.

  • Till deformation can account for most of the motion at a soft bed, and it is invisible to a surface stake.
  • A single borehole is a point sample in a system that varies over metres.
  • Seasonal velocity changes of 20–30 % are normal and are not a signal of instability.

Limits

Four ways a core lies

i

Diffusion

Water isotopes smooth out over centuries in the firn and upper ice. A sharp event becomes a gentle bump, and a naive reading of the bump's width puts the event too late.

ii

Melt

One melt season percolates water downwards and carries the isotope signal with it. In the warmest parts of the ice sheet this is now the dominant uncertainty in the upper core.

iii

Drilling fluid

The ester used to keep the hole open contaminates the outer millimetres of every core. Cut the wrong way and your black-carbon record is a record of the drill.

iv

The age scale

Every proxy inherits the age model's error and none of them carry it in their error bars. That is the single largest source of disagreement in the published literature.
