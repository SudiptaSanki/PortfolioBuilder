Practical

What a core needs before anyone can read it

The line scan. A continuous optical image with a depth reference is the single most valuable thing you can send, and most cores have one already. It gives layer thickness, dust stratigraphy and a check on where the core was cut.

The storage record. Temperature, any power failures, and whether the core was ever moved without a lag. Melt at -20 °C is negligible; three days at -3 °C in a broken freezer is not, and it is invisible in the ice afterwards.

The site coordinates. Accumulation is a function of position on the ice sheet, so an age scale without coordinates is a guess with a small number attached.

The drilling log. Depth, breaks, and which fluid was used, because the fluid marks the outside of the core and it matters for anything measured by scanning.
