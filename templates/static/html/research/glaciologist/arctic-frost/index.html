The ice is a ledger. Every year writes a line, and some years write nothing at all

I read Greenland ice cores for the climate they recorded and measure how fast the same ice is leaving. Those two jobs are usually done by different people, and the gap between them is where the interesting questions live: not what the ice remembers, but what it does now that the memory is melting out of it.

Three readings

What a core can and cannot tell you

An ice core is a stack of proxies, each with its own clock and its own bias. The skill is not in reading one; it is in knowing which two agree.

01

Water isotopes

δ¹⁸O in the ice records the temperature at which the snow condensed, over the ocean it came from, minus what happened to it on the way. Over Greenland that gives a temperature history with a slope of roughly 1.5 °C per mil, and an error that grows in the wind-scoured interior.

02

Trapped air

Bubbles close off at 50–100 m below the surface, so the air inside is younger than the ice around it by centuries to millennia. The gas age–ice age difference is small in the Holocene and enormous near the bottom, which is why abrupt CO₂ questions are harder than they look.

03

Dust and chemistry

Calcium, sea salt and black carbon record the atmosphere's circulation rather than its temperature. A dust band is a date; a trend in calcium is a story about deserts, wind and the strength of the westerlies. Both survive the melting that destroys isotopes near a warm surface.

Recent campaigns

Four seasons on the sheet

  1. 0 m

    EastGRIP, surface programme

    Two hundred kilometres from the coast, on the fastest-flowing part of the northeast stream. Shallow cores and a strain grid over four summers, looking at how the top forty metres accommodate the flow.

    2026
  2. 2,540 m

    NEEM, replicate core

    A second core beside the 2008 main core, for the gas record in the Younger Dryas. The two age scales disagree by 90 years in the interval that matters, and neither is obviously wrong.

    2024
  3. 1,800 m

    Continuous flow analysis, Copenhagen

    Six weeks of meltwater analysis on 1.2 km of core, at 1 cm resolution. The dust record came out cleaner than the 2015 run and the isotope record came out worse, for reasons still being argued about.

    2023
  4. bed

    Till and basal water, Isunnguata

    Three seasons of boreholes and seismic lines at the margin, where the ice meets a bed of saturated till. The water pressure record is the least glamorous dataset I own and the most useful.

    2021–23
