Fieldwork 2013–2026

Nine projects, four of them at forty below

Field seasons are mostly logistics and occasionally science. The entries below are ordered by what came out of them rather than by where they rank in a grant report, and the failures are included because a fieldwork page without them is an advertisement.

Showing 9 of 9 projects

  1. EG-26

    EastGRIP surface programme

    Four summers of shallow cores and a 200-stake strain grid on the fastest-flowing part of the northeast stream. The top forty metres are not a passive record here: they are being stretched as they form, and the layer thickness at 20 m already carries the flow signal. Six papers, one of which is mostly about the strain grid.

    75.6° N2,700 m4 seasons
  2. NE-24

    NEEM replicate core

    A second core beside the 2008 main core, drilled for the gas record across the Younger Dryas. The two age scales disagree by 90 years exactly where the methane jump is, and neither is obviously wrong. The disagreement is now in the literature as a range, which is more honest than a mean.

    1,800 mgas age90 yr spread
  3. IS-22

    Till and basal water, Isunnguata

    Three seasons of boreholes, seismic lines and pressure sensors at the margin where the ice meets saturated till. The water pressure record runs to 4 million samples and is the least glamorous dataset I own. It is also the one every flow modeller asks for, usually after the model has failed.

    margin3 seasonspressure
  4. CFA-23

    Continuous flow analysis, Copenhagen

    Six weeks of meltwater analysis over 1.2 km of core at 1 cm resolution. The dust record came out cleaner than the 2015 run and the isotope record came out worse, for a reason that took a year to find: a leak in the sample line was adding a fraction of a millilitre of older meltwater at every joint.

    1.2 km1 cmleak
  5. GR-19

    The 1259 marker across three cores

    The Samalas dust band, found in three cores 400 km apart, used to tie the three age scales together. The band is unambiguous in sulphate and conductivity and nearly invisible in the isotope record, which is why every tie point should be checked in more than one proxy.

    3 cores1259 ADtie point
  6. GR-16

    Deepest ice, no age

    A 120 m section from the bottom of a marginal core that could not be dated at all: the layers were folded, the isotopes were diffused past recognition, and the dust was dominated by basal debris. It is in the freezer, labelled, and might be useful to someone with a better method in ten years. Most cores like this are thrown away.

    basal iceundatedkept
  7. IS-21

    The season the drill never left the crate

    Two weeks of wind at 28 m/s, a broken winch and a cargo flight that could not land. Nothing was measured. The logistics report from that season is the most useful document I have written, because it is the only one that records what the site actually costs.

    no data28 m/slesson
  8. GR-15

    Brittle-zone repair

    A method paper on re-assembling fractured core from the 700–1,400 m interval using the line scan and the electrical conductivity record. It is unglamorous and it recovered 60 m of record that would otherwise have been a gap in a dataset everyone uses.

    700–1,400 m60 m recovered
  9. EG-13

    First season on the stream

    The 2013 reconnaissance: 40 stakes, one GPS, a trench and a lot of digging. The velocity we measured that summer was 20 % higher than the model predicted, which is how the whole programme started.

    201340 stakes+20 %
