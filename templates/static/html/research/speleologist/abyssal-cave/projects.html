Five systems, and what the air does inside them

Each record is the one-page version of the survey file: the legs, the stations, the grade, and the measurements that changed how the cave is managed. The air numbers are from the last complete season.

C-01

Hang Sơn Đoòng, north branch

The north branch climbs away from the main river passage through a series of boulder chokes and opens into a chamber with a 388 m vertical range. We surveyed it in three trips in 2024 and finished the reduction in 2025. The passage is dry at the top and takes a waterfall at the bottom, which is why the air is clean: the falling water drags a steady current through the whole branch.

CO2 stays under 2,000 ppm year-round and radon is 940 Bq/m³ at the deepest station. The sump at the far end has been dived twice and refused both times; it is 40 m long and the visibility goes to zero at 12.

C-02

Hang Va

A river cave that starts as a 60 m pitch and then runs 1.6 km to a connection with the Sơn Đoòng system that we pushed in 2017. The connection was found by following a draught rather than a passage: the air moved at about 1 m/s through a constriction that no person could pass, and the only way on was to dig.

The cave is a good example of why the air model matters. The entrance pitch is small and the passage is deep, so exchange is weak; CO2 reaches 4,200 ppm in the far series in late summer, when the outside temperature is at its closest to the cave temperature and the chimney effect stops. In January the same station reads 1,100.

C-03

Hang Én, lower series

The show cave is the front of the system; the lower series is the part that runs under the doline and is not on the tourist route. We measured radon there in 2023 because the guides were reporting fatigue in the afternoon, and found 3,100 Bq/m³ in the lowest chamber, with the highest values after the monsoon when the water table is up and the passage is sealed by standing water.

The dose for a guide working 800 hours a year at that concentration is about 22 mSv, which is above the 20 mSv occupational limit. The management plan now rotates guides out of the lower series every two hours and the rota is built around the detector data, not the schedule.

C-04

Hang Tối, upstream

Short, wet, and the worst air in the massif. The cave floods from a single entrance and the passage beyond the second sump holds a pocket of stale air that does not clear between monsoons. Our measurement in April 2026 was 6,900 ppm CO2 and 2,050 Bq/m³ radon, with the CO2 rising to 11,000 ppm at the far wall.

The cave is now restricted to two visits a year, both with a monitor and both with a plan for what happens if the reading passes 10,000. Nothing about the restriction is about difficulty: the passage is a walk. It is entirely about the air.

C-05

Khe Ry resurgence

The spring that drains the western part of the massif, surveyed from the outside in for 1.1 km before the passage sumps. Dye tracing from a swallow hole 4 km to the south-east arrives at the spring in 31 hours, which gives a mean flow velocity of about 130 m an hour — fast enough that the water is not filtering much, which is why the spring is not used for drinking water.

This is the record that connects the cave survey to the hydrology. Every station in the resurgence is also a water sampling point, and the chemistry is what tells you which part of the massif the water came from.
