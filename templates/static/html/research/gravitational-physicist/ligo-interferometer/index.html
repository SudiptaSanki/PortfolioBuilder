Gravitational-wave instrument science · Cardiff

Listening for ripples. Ruling out trucks.

A gravitational-wave detector is the most sensitive measuring instrument ever built — sensitive enough to hear a passing lorry, a logging train and an earthquake on the other side of the planet, all louder than the signal. My work is detector characterisation: proving, channel by channel, that a candidate signal is not any of those — so that when your group claims a ripple in spacetime, the claim holds.

A strain of 10⁻²¹ across a 4 km arm is a length change a thousand times smaller than a proton. Everything in the building, the ground and the weather moves more than that. Characterisation is the discipline of knowing exactly how.

§ 01 · FIGURE 2

The noise budget, drawn

Switch sources off.
The curve answers.

Every detector publishes a sensitivity curve: the quietest strain it can hear at each frequency. The curve is not one noise but a budget of them, and each source owns a band. Seismic noise — the ground — rules the low frequencies and walls the band off below about 10 Hz. Thermal noise — the mirrors and their suspensions jiggling with heat — rules the middle, where most signals sweep through. Quantum noise — the counting statistics of the laser light itself — rules the high frequencies. Toggle the sources and watch which band each one owns.

10–40 Hz · Low band
Seismic
≈ 375 ×10⁻²⁴ /√Hz at 20 Hz

The ground itself. Microseisms, weather, traffic and trains move the test masses more than any signal could — which is why the band below ~10 Hz is a wall, not a slope.

40–300 Hz · The money band
Thermal
≈ 7.2 ×10⁻²⁴ /√Hz at 110 Hz

The money band, and heat owns it: thermal fluctuations in the mirror coatings blur the surface the laser reads. Most signals sweep through here.

300–5,000 Hz · High band
Quantum
≈ 26 ×10⁻²⁴ /√Hz at 1200 Hz

Shot noise: at high frequencies there are fewer photons per measurement interval, so the counting statistics of light itself set the floor.

Total noise at 110 Hz, the heart of the band: 8.3 ×10⁻²⁴ /√Hz — the sources add in quadrature, which is why killing the biggest one matters more than polishing the rest.

What moves the price

  • Channels × data span. An audit is priced on auxiliary channels audited times weeks of data examined — 250 channels over 6 weeks is the standard scope above.
  • On-site blocks. Work at the detector is sometimes the only way; travel and accommodation are charged at cost, agreed in advance.
  • Run deadlines. Work that must land inside six weeks of a run start carries a 25% urgency uplift — runs do not move, so the queue has to.
  • Cohort size. The school is priced to 12; a thirteenth analyst is another cohort, not a squeeze.
