Case 01 · Glitch-family investigation

The 2 a.m. harmonic

Client
University analysis group
Data span
6 weeks of a run
Duration
10 working days
Fee
£7,500 fixed

The brief

A search group kept losing candidates to a repeating glitch family: a narrow harmonic comb appearing roughly every 40 minutes, strongest in the small hours, sitting squarely in the band where binary inspirals sweep. It was eating livetime — every occurrence meant the surrounding data was flagged — and nobody could say what it was. The ask was blunt: find it, and either kill it or prove it can't be killed.

Decisions

Work the witnesses before the strain channel: the glitch's regular period pointed at machinery, not astrophysics, so the hunt started in the auxiliary channels. An accelerometer on a utility-room wall carried the same 40-minute rhythm, phase-locked to the comb. The source was a compressor cycling on a thermostat — its vibration reached the vacuum system and rang the suspensions. The veto was built on the accelerometer channel alone, never on the strain channel, and was then attacked with hardware injections: known fake signals pushed into the detector physically, which the veto had to let through untouched.

Outcome

The family was traced, named and vetoed with a measured deadtime under 0.1% — against the several percent the blanket flags had been costing. The injections all survived the veto, which is the only reason the group was willing to trust it in a search. The compressor itself was re-mounted on isolation pads in the next maintenance break; the veto stays in the pipeline as the receipt.

OutcomeLivetime recovered, veto validated against hardware injections, source fixed at the root in the following break. The testimonial on the home page is from this engagement.

Case 02 · Pre-run data-quality audit

Two hundred witnesses, cross-examined

Client
Institute detector team
Scope
214 auxiliary channels
Data span
6 weeks, prior run
Fee
£12,500 fixed

The brief

An observing run is a deadline that does not negotiate. Six weeks before lock, the institute's detector team wanted an outside audit of the auxiliary channel set — the seismometers, magnetometers, microphones, accelerometers and photodiodes whose entire job is to witness noise so the strain channel doesn't have to explain it. A witness that is dead, miscalibrated or pointed at the wrong thing is worse than none: it lends false confidence.

Decisions

Every channel was tested three ways: is it alive (does its spectrum move when the world moves), is it calibrated (does a known disturbance read at the right size), and is it coupled to the strain channel in the way its documentation claims. Channels failing any test were flagged with the evidence attached, not a verdict from authority. The deliverable was a data-quality flag set the search pipelines could consume directly, plus a one-page-per-shift checklist written for the person on duty at 3 a.m., not for the person who commissioned the audit.

Outcome

Of 214 channels audited, 11 were dead or effectively dead, 6 were miscalibrated — including one magnetometer reading a factor of two low, which would have understated every magnetic glitch in the run — and 4 were documented as watching equipment that had been moved in the previous upgrade. All were repaired, replaced or honestly de-listed before lock. The run started with a witness set the team could defend channel by channel.

Outcome21 of 214 channels failed audit; all resolved or de-listed before the run. Flag set and shift checklist delivered as documented, reusable code.

Case 03 · Analyst school

Teaching twelve analysts to distrust a trigger

Client
Research group, new intake
Cohort
12 analysts, 2 days
Data
Public — GWOSC
Fee
£4,800 / cohort

The brief

A group taking on twelve new analysts — doctoral students and postdocs — needed them literate in detector characterisation before their first shifts: able to look at a trigger, form a suspicion, and test it against the witness channels instead of either believing the strain channel or dismissing it. The group's seniors could teach it, but not twelve times, and not during a run.

Decisions

The school runs on public data from the Gravitational-Wave Open Science Center — real detector data, including real glitches, that anyone may download. Day one is the taxonomy: blips, whistles, scattered light, the comb families, each shown in strain and in its witnesses. Day two is method: coherence tests, veto construction, and the one rule that outranks all others — never veto on the strain channel alone. The school ends with a blind test: injections hidden in public data, some astrophysical in shape, some pure noise wearing a costume.

Outcome

Eleven of twelve passed the blind test outright; the twelfth caught the noise cases and over-vetoed one injection — the safe direction to fail, and the debrief covered exactly why it still counts as a fail. The group got analysts who could staff shifts in their first month, and a set of worked exercises, on public data, that the group now reuses with every intake.

Outcome12 analysts shift-ready; course materials on public data left with the group for future cohorts at no further fee.
