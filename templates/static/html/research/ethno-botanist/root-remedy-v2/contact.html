What happens next

A first conversation — an hour, unhurried — about whose knowledge it is and whether the community is already at the table. If it isn't, the first piece of work is helping you start that conversation, not designing a programme around its absence. No agreement is drafted, and nothing is billed, before that talk.

Enquiries this practice declines

Requests to buy remedies, plant material or a community's knowledge; requests for the locations of rare or protected species; and requests to document first and “sort out consent later”. All three are declined — politely, in writing, and without a counter-offer.

The practice

Root Remedy is the ethnobotanical documentation practice of Dr June Ashworth, based in Coimbra and working wherever consent is given. Programmes are documented in the open: briefs, pauses and outcomes all stay in the log.

The standing terms

Consent before recording. The community owns the record. Exact localities of sensitive species are never published. No remedies are sold, and no health claims are made — ever.
