Fieldwork & lab commissions

Eight projects, and the part of each one that did not go to plan

A collection project is a set of agreements, a set of sheets, and a set of numbers that may or may not mean what the first paper said they meant. The entries below are ordered newest first. Filter them by the part of the work you care about.

Showing 8 of 8 projects

  1. CAL 118473

    Periyar bark harvest — Cinchona ledgeriana

    Idukki, Kerala
    2026

    112 vouchers from four coppiced stands on the western slope, with a harvest calendar agreed with the estate before the first stem was cut. Bark quinine ran from 4.1 % in a young stand to 7.2 % in an old one, which is enough of a spread to change which stand gets cut first. What went wrong: the first 30 sheets were dried over a stove and came out brown and brittle, useless for DNA. They are still in the cabinet, marked as failed and kept as a lesson.

    barkcoppice planHPTLC
  2. CAL 117209

    Rauvolfia transects across elevation

    Nilgiri Biosphere
    2024–25

    Fourteen transects between 420 and 1,240 m, 1,180 plants counted, root samples from 96 of them under a permit that allowed a single root per plant. Reserpine content peaked at the shaded mid-elevation sites and fell away sharply above 1,100 m. What went wrong: the permit specified a 3-year-old root class and half the population turned out to be younger, so a third of the samples had to be dropped from the analysis rather than quietly pooled.

    population surveyreserpineelevation
  3. CAL 116844

    Khasi Hills voucher set

    Meghalaya
    2025

    68 sheets collected with the Seng Khasi healers' association over three weeks, with benefit-sharing terms filed before collection. The agreement named seven plants that could not be photographed, described in the paper only by family and use, which makes for a strange methods section and an honest one. What went wrong: two of the seven turned out to be the same species under different local names, and the association decided which name would appear.

    prior informed consent68 sheets
  4. CAL 120117

    Leaf alkaloids in Psychotria viridis

    Ucayali, Peru
    2024

    Forty individual plants sampled with a Shipibo maestro who selected them by stand and habit rather than by any measure I could write down. Leaf DMT ran 0.11 % to 0.47 % between individuals, a fourfold spread in the same forest. What went wrong: my first extraction used a solvent that pulled so much chlorophyll the HPLC column was unusable for a fortnight, which is the sort of mistake that teaches you to defat first.

    leafHPLC-DADindividual variation
  5. PDA 004411

    Sinharaja exchange and the determination backlog

    Sri Lanka
    2023

    A two-week exchange with the Peradeniya herbarium: 40 duplicates mounted, 6 determinations corrected, and a backlog of 300 unnamed Rubiaceae worked through far enough to say what they were not. What went wrong: three of the six corrections contradicted names that had been cited in a published forest inventory, and the correction had to be published as a note rather than quietly fixed.

    Rubiaceaeduplicatesnomenclature
  6. CAL 120884

    Ephedra stand audit, Ordos plateau

    Inner Mongolia
    2025

    A licensed stand audited for ephedrine content across a grazing gradient: 1.2 % in the ungrazed plots, 0.7 % where sheep had been through. The finding matters because the stand's value is assessed by weight, not by content, and the two move in opposite directions when grazing pressure rises. What went wrong: the field balance drifted 2 % over the season and every fresh weight had to be corrected from the drying ratio.

    stemgrazingcontent audit
  7. in-house

    Cold treatment for a beetle problem

    Kochi cabinet
    2024

    A cabinet is food. Two hundred sheets were frozen at −20 °C for a week, thawed, conditioned and re-examined after a fortnight, and the infestation did not return. The method is standard and the mistake was not doing it when the first frass appeared, which cost about forty sheets their flowers.

    collection carepest control
  8. workshop

    Pressing without a plant press

    Kochi & Thrissur
    2023–26

    A one-day workshop for school and college groups on making a usable herbarium sheet with newspaper, cardboard and a fan: 240 sheets made by students over four sessions, 60 of them good enough to keep. The point is not the sheets. It is that a person who has pressed a plant once reads a herbarium label differently for the rest of their life.

    teaching240 sheets
