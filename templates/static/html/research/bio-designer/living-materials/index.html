No. 04 — Material studies — grown, not made

A wall that grows in the dark and holds its shape in the rain

Mycelium is not a metaphor for building. It is a binder with a tensile strength of about 3 MPa when it is alive and a compressive strength of 0.6 MPa when it is dead, dried and pressed into a board. I grow those boards from agricultural waste in a workshop in Grefsen, Oslo, and test them until they fail.

Current study
Hemp hurd + Pleurotus ostreatus
21-day colonisation

Bench note 03

Grow a panel, then break it

Four variables decide almost everything. Substrate sets the pore structure, colonisation sets how much binder there is, pressing sets density, and the two of them together decide whether you have insulation or a floor.

A board at this density is a wall lining: it insulates well and carries nothing but itself.

Selected work

Three things that are already standing

01

Pavilion for a wet spring

An 18 m² shelter for a community garden in Grünerløkka. Sixty panels, grown on hemp hurd, left unpainted so the rain could test them. After two winters the roof panels lost 4 % of mass and gained 0.08 MPa.

Oslo

02

Acoustic baffles for a school hall

Two hundred and forty absorption panels for a gymnasium with a nine-second reverberation time. The mycelium surface is left open on purpose: the pores do the acoustic work that a painted face would destroy.

2024
Bergen
240 units

03

A packaging insert that composts in six weeks

Moulded pulp replacement for expanded polystyrene in a medical instrument crate. Grown in fourteen days, dried at 60 °C, and certified home-compostable. The client's drop test needed 300 mm; the insert survived 420 mm.

2023
Trondheim
14 days

Studio note

“The failure mode is rarely the fungus. It is the drying.”

A panel that dries too fast checks and cracks at the surface; one that dries too slowly fruits and turns green. The kiln schedule in the workshop is four pages long and has not changed in two years, which is the closest thing to a house style this practice has.
