Five projects, each one a different argument about what grown material is for

A pavilion that is allowed to weather, baffles that are not allowed to be painted, a crate insert that must survive a drop test, a desk that must survive an office, and plinths that must survive being moved eleven times.

01 / 2025

Pavilion for a wet spring

A 18 m² shelter for the Grønland community garden, built from sixty hemp-hurd panels on a larch frame. The panels are unfaced and unpainted, so the roof is a two-year experiment in what rain does to a grown material. Mass loss after two winters was 4 %; compressive strength on cores cut from a spare panel rose by 0.08 MPa, probably because the surface densified as it weathered.

The detail that took longest was the fixing: mycelium creeps under a permanent point load, so every panel sits in an aluminium channel rather than on a bolt.

02 / 2024

Acoustic baffles for a school hall

Two hundred and forty absorbers for a gymnasium with a nine-second reverberation time. The specification was 0.75 absorption coefficient at 500 Hz; the open mycelium face measured 0.82, and the client asked whether it could be painted. It could not. Paint closes the pores that do the work, and the argument is now written into the maintenance manual.

Reverberation came down to 1.4 seconds, which is still lively enough for a school and quiet enough for a assembly.

03 / 2023

A packaging insert that composts in six weeks

Moulded replacement for an expanded polystyrene insert in a medical instrument crate. Grown in fourteen days, dried at 60 °C, and certified home-compostable. The client's drop test required survival at 300 mm; the insert survived 420 mm, which is the only number in this portfolio that made anyone cheer.

Four thousand units were produced before the client moved the instrument to a smaller case and the tooling was retired.

04 / 2024

Reception desk, grown in four pieces

A 3.2 m desk for a small architecture office, grown in four moulds and joined with a clamped scarf. Density 205 kg/m³, compressive strength 0.78 MPa, sealed with a linseed soap that can be reapplied by the client. The first version cracked in the kiln; the second was dried with a 5 °C slower ramp and a weight on top.

Two years in, the only damage is a coffee ring that the soap will lift.

05 / 2022

Plinths for a travelling exhibition

Forty plinths in three heights for a design exhibition that toured five venues. The requirement was not strength but repairability: each plinth had to be re-skinned after the fifth move. The solution was a thin mycelium skin grown separately and glued on with a starch paste, so the object could be maintained like a floor rather than replaced like a crate.
