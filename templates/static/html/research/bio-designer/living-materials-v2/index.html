Grown, not made — and grown things vary.

Hypha is a living-materials studio. We grow mycelium composites for packaging, interiors and product teams, and we publish what the material tests at as ranges — because that is what it gives us.

The honest part, up front: no two batches are identical. Density, strength and colour move inside the ranges printed on this page. If your product cannot live with that, we would rather tell you before you pay us than after.

How long does a panel take to grow?

Biology sets the schedule; we just write it down. The planner below uses the studio's printed figures for one named system, at stated conditions. Change the thickness and the arithmetic is done in the open — including the slow version, because the slow version is the one that occasionally happens.

Printed planning figures — system H-7
SubstrateHemp-hurd, pasteurised
CultureGanoderma lucidum, studio strain H-7
Conditions21–24 °C · 80–90% RH · dark incubation

Stated variation±20% (2.0–3.0)

The mould fills the way the drawing fills — from the inoculation points outward, and it does not hurry.

Target panel thickness
25mm thick

Above 60 mm we laminate two grown layers and bond them with their own mycelium — add a week and ask us first.

Growth at 2.5 mm/day25 ÷ 2.5 = 10.0 → 10 days
Fastest, at 3.0 mm/day25 ÷ 3.0 = 8.3 → 9 days
Slowest, at 2.0 mm/day25 ÷ 2.0 = 12.5 → 13 days
Drying, kiln to stable weight4 days
Press & finish2 days
Book it as16 days
Honest range15–19 days

Growth is rounded up to whole days. A panel that is 90% colonised is not a panel; it is a smell and a lesson.

The sprint is two weeks, one material system, one test set included, and a written verdict — including when the verdict is do not grow this. Production work is quoted as a range with a ceiling after a sprint or a paid sample batch.

What moves the price

First: iteration count and testing depth — every extra design round is a full growth cycle, not an afternoon.

Second: mould complexity. Third: timeline pressure. Asking biology to hurry is the most expensive line item in this studio, and it usually buys you a worse panel.

“Femke told us in week one that the inserts would vary by a millimetre either way, and to design the box around the variation instead of fighting it. We did. Breakage returns fell to almost nothing — and the foam is gone from our packing bench.”
