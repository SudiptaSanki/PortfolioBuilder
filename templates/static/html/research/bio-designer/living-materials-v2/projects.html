Three programmes, told straight

Every programme in this studio ends as a case file in the growth log — the ones that worked, the one that failed, and the one where the right answer was somebody else's material. Clients are described by trade and city; the studio's figures are printed as the ranges they were delivered in.

Case file 01Client Stoneware studio, GhentSystem H-7 hemp-hurdYear 2025Status In production

An insert that replaced the foam

The brief

A stoneware studio shipping roughly 400 orders a month wanted the expanded-polystyrene insert out of its boxes. The replacement had to protect glazed ware in the post, survive a warehouse shelf for weeks, and go into a home compost bin at the end — with the conditions for that claim stated, not implied.

The decisions

We grew the insert at the dense end of the H-7 range (190–210 kg/m³) to buy drop margin, and told the client in week one to design the box around a ±1.5 mm thickness tolerance instead of specifying an exact fit. The insert is grown in a two-part mould as a single piece — no adhesive, no skin coat — because a coating would have decided the composting claim, and an uncoated panel is the only version we could make the claim for honestly.

The outcome

Studio drop log: 24 test parcels dropped from 1.2 m onto concrete, six orientations — no breakages in the grown insert's parcels. In service, the client's breakage returns fell from a foam-era average of 11 per quarter to 1 in the first two quarters. The insert costs more per unit than foam did; the client publishes that on their own site and charges it openly. We would rather you heard it from both of us.

“She told us in week one that the inserts would vary by a millimetre either way, and to design the box around the variation instead of fighting it. We did. Breakage returns fell to almost nothing — and the foam is gone from our packing bench.”

Operations lead, stoneware studio, Ghent

Case file 02Client Retail display orderBatch H-2409System H-7 hemp-hurdYear 2025Status Failed · remade at studio cost

The batch that failed

The brief

A run of 60 display blocks for a retail client, grown to a fixed opening date. Nothing exotic: the standard H-7 system, the standard moulds, a deadline we agreed to because the schedule said it fit.

What happened

On day four, green mould — Trichoderma, the commonest contaminant in this work — showed at the mould edges. We traced it to a new substrate delivery and a sterilisation cycle that had been shortened by twenty minutes to keep another batch moving. That decision was ours, and it was wrong. The whole run was scrapped and composted; there is no version of this trade where you trim the green off and ship the rest.

The outcome

Under the studio's printed terms, a contaminated batch is remade at the studio's cost. The client received the remade run nine days late, paid nothing extra, and kept the order — with the failure written into the case file they were sent, not discovered by them. The sterilisation cycle went back to full length the same week, and a day-three visual check was added to every batch card. Our contamination rate since: 1 batch in 38, and that one was caught on day three, not day nine.

Case file 03Client 60-seat restaurant, UtrechtSystem H-7 hemp · F-2 flaxYear 2024Status Delivered — dry zones only

The wall we refused to cover

The brief

A 60-seat restaurant with a hard, echoing dining room asked for grown acoustic panels on every wall, including the wall beside the open kitchen. The material story mattered to them; so did the sound.

The decisions

We supplied 46 panels for the dining-room walls — and declined the kitchen wall. Steam and grease vapour are a wet zone, and H-7 swells and softens in sustained humidity; a panel there would have failed slowly and then all at once. We specified mineral wool behind perforated timber for that wall instead, a material we do not sell, and wrote the reasoning into the proposal so the client could hold us to it.

The outcome

The client's own acoustician measured the dining room before and after: reverberation time fell from 1.9 s to 1.1 s at mid frequencies, on the grown panels and the treated kitchen wall together. Two years on, the dining-room panels are unchanged; the kitchen wall, in the material we recommended against our own interest, is also unchanged. That is rather the point.
