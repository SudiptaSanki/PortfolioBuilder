§1

The researcher

Dr Maren Ellingsen

Dr Maren Ellingsen has spent eighteen years on one layer of the atmosphere: the stratosphere, and the aerosol that drifts through it — volcanic, background, and hypothetical.

The route was ordinary for the field and is stated plainly. A doctorate in atmospheric physics, on stratospheric aerosol microphysics. Six years in a national meteorological service's research division, working on the assimilation of satellite aerosol observations. Five years in a university stratosphere group, split between balloon-borne measurement campaigns — as the scientist who writes the quality-assurance protocol, not the one who flies the balloon — and the modelling work that turns profiles into forcing estimates. In 2021 she left to found this practice, because the questions arriving from councils and academies were increasingly governance questions wearing technical clothes, and answering them well needed independence from both funders and campaigning.

The practice is small on purpose: Dr Ellingsen, two associate researchers, and a standing network of reviewers. It takes few commissions, publishes all of them, and declines — in writing, with reasons — the ones that ask for an answer before the evidence is in.

“I have never trusted a forcing estimate that arrived without its disagreement attached. Two models agreeing tells you something about the models. It tells you nothing yet about the sky.”

That sentence is the practice's whole method in one line. The stratosphere is measurable, and the measurements are good; the models are serious, and they disagree by factors that matter. A portfolio in this field is not a gallery of results. It is a record of how carefully the gap between measurement and model has been handled.

§2

How an assessment runs

Method, in five steps
  1. 1

    Scoping note

    The question is written down narrowly enough to be answerable, with the client, before any work is priced. Vague questions are returned for sharpening, free of charge — most assessment failures are scope failures that nobody caught at this step.

  2. 2

    Protocol published first

    Screening criteria, model configurations, diagnostics and comparison data are published before results exist. A protocol written after the results is a rationalisation, and readers can tell.

  3. 3

    The work

    Literature screened with the log kept; models run to the protocol; every exclusion and configuration choice recorded as it is made, not reconstructed afterwards.

  4. 4

    Independent review

    Reviewers are appointed by the client from a list this practice does not control, and their comments — including the unanswered ones — are published with the report.

  5. 5

    Publication

    The report, the screening log and the methods annex are published in full on an agreed date. A client may keep its name off a study. It may not keep the methods under seal, and it may not delay publication past the agreed date.

§3

Governance — who decides

The practice's position

This page is the reason the practice exists in its present shape. Stratospheric aerosol intervention, if it were ever deployed, would be a planetary act taken by a small number of hands. The governance questions are therefore not an annex to the science. They are the main text.

Clause 1

Decisions belong to publics

Whether to deploy is a decision for publics and their institutions — parliaments, treaty processes, and the people who would live under a managed sky — taken slowly and in the open. Not for researchers, however careful; not for funders, however wealthy; and not for this practice, which holds an advisory seat and intends to keep it.

Clause 2

Consent is not a detail

A deployed layer would affect rainfall, agriculture and ecosystems in places whose inhabitants never consented to be downstream of it. Any governance framework that treats that as a communications problem rather than a legitimacy problem has failed before it starts. The practice's assessments state, for each study, whose consent the work did and did not have.

Clause 3

The termination problem

A layer must be maintained, or the warming it masks returns within years — faster than adaptation. Starting is therefore not the decision; committing to continue, across governments and generations, is the decision. Any proposal that prices the start and not the continuation has not understood its own physics, and the practice's reviews say so in those terms.

Clause 4

Methods are published so the work can be argued with

Every protocol, screening log and configuration in this practice's reports is public, because a result that cannot be checked cannot be trusted — and in this field, untrusted results do damage in both directions: they can license recklessness, and they can discredit care. Openness is not a virtue signal here. It is the quality-control mechanism.

Clause 5

What this practice is not

It is not a deployment service, not a cloud-seeding operation, and not an advocacy shop for either side. Weather modification — tropospheric cloud seeding — is a separate, operational field with its own practitioners; nothing here is that. This practice studies the stratosphere, measures what is there, models what changes would do, and reports. Where the evidence supports proceeding with research, it says so. Where it does not, it says that — as Case Study 01 records.

Engagement terms. Research contracts only, with public bodies, academies, universities and consortia. Conflicts of interest are declared before scoping and published with each report. The practice holds no equity, patents or advisory roles in any deployment venture, and accepts no funding earmarked for reaching a particular conclusion.
