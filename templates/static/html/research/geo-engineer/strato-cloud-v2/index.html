Research the stratosphere. Decide nothing alone.

Ellingsen Assessment is an independent research practice working on stratospheric aerosols: what is up there, what the models say a changed layer would do, and what the evidence cannot yet support. The clients are research councils, national academies and university consortia. The practice publishes its methods, its disagreements — and, where the evidence points that way, its advice not to proceed.

Standing position — read first

Deployment is not a service this practice sells, at any price. Whether stratospheric aerosol intervention is ever deployed is a decision for publics and their institutions. This practice works in an advisory seat only: measuring, modelling, and saying plainly what is known and what is not.

Annex A — the forcing explorer

Teaching model

One relationship sits underneath most of this field: add aerosol optical depth to the stratosphere and the global-mean radiative forcing goes negative — the surface cools. The size of that relationship is where the honesty has to live. The model below is deliberately simple, and it is labelled for what it is.

Annex A · Worked exampleTeaching model — not a prediction

Step the optical depth. Watch the arithmetic, then watch it leave.

Table 1 gives the model's single coefficient. Published assessments put the global-mean forcing of a stratospheric aerosol layer at roughly −22 W m⁻² per unit of aerosol optical depth (AOD), as an order-of-magnitude teaching figure, with a spread of about ±30% between models and assumptions. Nothing on this page turns that into a forecast: regional effects, ozone, circulation and rainfall sit outside a one-line model, and the practice's reports say so every time.

Stratospheric aerosol optical depth (AOD)

0.05unitless · global mean
Steps of 0.01, from 0.00 (no layer) to 0.20. For scale: the quiescent background is ≈ 0.005; Pinatubo, 1991, peaked at ≈ 0.15.
ΔF = -22 × 0.05 = -1.10 W m⁻²

Model forcing: -1.10 W m⁻²

Plausible band at ±30%: -1.43 to -0.77 W m⁻². The band is part of the answer, not a footnote to it.

The termination consideration — in plain words

Run the chart backwards and the problem appears. If a deployed layer were masking a large amount of warming and the input then stopped — funding pulled, a government fell, a system failed — the masked warming would return within a few years, faster than ecosystems, farms or infrastructure could adjust. That is the termination problem. It is a governance fact before it is an engineering one, and it is the single strongest reason this practice insists that decisions of that scale belong to publics and their institutions, taken slowly, with research in an advisory seat.

What the practice does

Register of services
S—01

Evidence assessment

A defined question, answered from the published record: systematic screening, quality weighting, and a written statement of what the evidence supports, what it contradicts, and what it cannot decide. The screening log is published with the report, so any reader can re-run the inclusion decisions.

Clients
Research councils, academies
Basis
Fixed scope; person-days at printed rates
Status
Two commissions open, 2026
S—02

Model intercomparison & audit

Client models run against a shared protocol — same prescribed layer, same diagnostics — and the spread between them reported as the finding. Averaging disagreement away is the one thing this service refuses to do; the audit trail shows every configuration choice.

Clients
Modelling centres, consortia
Basis
Per-study; priced by number of models
Status
One slot from spring 2026
S—03

Measurement campaign support

Design and quality assurance for balloon-borne and ground-based stratospheric aerosol measurement, as science partner to the institution that hosts and flies the instruments. The practice measures the layer that exists; it does not add to it.

Clients
Universities, met services
Basis
By arrangement with the host institution
Status
Two campaigns supported to date
