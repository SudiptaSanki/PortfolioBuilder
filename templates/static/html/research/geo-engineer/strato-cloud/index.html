A sulphate layer is easy to draw and hard to live with

I measure what is already in the stratosphere with balloon-borne counters and lidar, and model what a deliberate addition would do to the light budget. The number that matters is not the cooling. It is how long the layer stays, how big the particles grow, and who has to decide.

Bench 2

Injection rate to radiative forcing

A steady-state box model: sulphur in, sulphate mass, optical depth, forcing. Sedimentation from Stokes' law with a slip correction sets one removal time, the Brewer–Dobson circulation sets the other, and the shorter one wins.

Sulphate density 1,770 kg/m³, stratospheric mean free path 0.7 µm, circulation exchange 1.2 yr. The model has no coagulation, no ozone chemistry and no heating feedback, so it overstates the forcing of small particles.

Four numbers

What decides whether a layer is a tool or a mess

Pinatubo put roughly 10 Tg of sulphur into the stratosphere in 1991 and cooled the surface by about half a degree for two years. Every proposal since is an attempt to reproduce that with control, and control is the part the volcano did not have to worry about.

1–2 yr

Residence

Particles leave the stratosphere through the circulation at the tropics and by settling everywhere else. A layer maintained for a century is a century of continuous injection, and stopping it is not a switch but a cliff.

0.2–0.5 µm

Size

Small particles scatter more light per kilogram but settle slowly and coagulate into larger ones. The size distribution is the design variable nobody can hold constant, which is why field tests of dispersers matter more than they sound.

−0.3 to −1.0

Forcing, W/m²

The plausible range for a programme of a few Tg S a year. It is a real number and a small one: the same order as the aerosol cooling already masking part of the greenhouse warming.

+1 K / −1 K

Termination

Stop a layer that is holding down a degree and the degree arrives within a decade, faster than ecosystems and infrastructure can follow. This is the argument that makes the governance question prior to the technical one.

Selected programmes

Measurement first, always

  1. P-24

    Kiruna balloon campaign

    Eleven balloon flights with optical particle counters and filter samplers between 16 and 24 km, launched inside the polar vortex. Two of the eleven found a volcanic layer nobody had reported.

    field · 2025
  2. P-19

    Lidar intercomparison, Andøya

    Three lidars, one site, one month, and a backscatter calibration that disagreed by 30 % at the start and 8 % at the end. The paper is mostly about the 22 % in between.

    field · 2024
  3. P-31

    Box-model intercomparison

    Six steady-state aerosol models run against the same injection scenarios. The spread in forcing at fixed injection rate was a factor of 2.4, which is the honest uncertainty on any single-model number.

    model · 2026
  4. P-08

    Non-use agreement analysis

    A study of the 2022 non-use initiative and the London Protocol amendment, written for a policy audience, with the legal gaps mapped against the technical ones.

    governance · 2023
