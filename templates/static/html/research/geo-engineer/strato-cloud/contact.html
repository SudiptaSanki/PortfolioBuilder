What I take on

Balloon instrument integration, lidar calibration, box-model runs, and the review of a retrieval that someone suspects is wrong. Small commissions, usually one or two months.

What I do not

Deployment advice, commercial SRM consulting, or anything that puts material into the stratosphere. The laboratory measures the atmosphere as it is; that constraint is the reason the numbers are worth reading.

Note

What a good request looks like

A one-page note with the instrument, the altitude range, the sampling period and the question you want answered. Attach the calibration record if there is one; a counter with no calibration is a counter with no error bar, and an error bar is the only thing that makes a stratospheric measurement publishable.

If the request is a model run, say which scenario and which output you need. A steady-state box model can tell you burden, optical depth and forcing. It cannot tell you the monsoon, and I will say so rather than run it anyway and put the number in a slide.

Lead times are honest rather than competitive: an instrument integration is six to ten weeks because the counter has to be recalibrated and flown on a test lift first, and a retrieval review is two to three weeks because it means re-running someone else's processing chain from the raw counts. A campaign collaboration is a year of planning before a balloon leaves the ground.

If you are a student, write anyway. About a fifth of the requests I answer are from people who need a paragraph of methodological advice rather than a contract, and those get the paragraph.
