Programmes 2023–2026

Eight programmes, and the dataset that did not survive review

Each entry gives the instrument, the altitude range and the result that mattered. Filters separate the field work from the modelling from the governance, because they are funded differently, reviewed differently and fail in different ways.

Showing 8 of 8 programmes

  1. P-24

    Kiruna balloon campaign, eleven flights

    Optical particle counters and filter samplers on eleven rubber balloons between 16 and 24 km, launched inside the polar vortex in January and February. The counter and the filter agreed within 12 % on sulphate mass. Two flights found a volcanic layer at 19 km that no ground-based report had flagged, which is the sort of result that pays for a campaign.

    Esrange, Kiruna11 flightscolumn: 0.031 AOD
  2. P-19

    Lidar intercomparison, Andøya

    Three lidars at one site for a month, all pointed at the same air, with a calibration standard brought in from the outside. The initial disagreement was 30 % in backscatter and 22 % in the retrieved extinction. Eight percent was achievable; below that the geometry of the site defeats you. The paper is mostly about the correction method for the horizontal separation between beams.

    69.3° N3 lidars30 % → 8 %
  3. P-27

    Ozone column series, Barcelona

    A Brewer spectrophotometer, four observations a day, five days a week, for eleven years. The instrument is boring and the record is not: it caught the 2022 Hunga Tonga water-vapour perturbation as a slow drift in the retrieved column that took two seasons to settle. Long series are unfashionable and irreplaceable.

    Brewer11 years1 station
  4. P-31

    Box-model intercomparison, six models

    Six steady-state aerosol models, the same injection scenarios, the same forcing efficiency. The spread in forcing at fixed injection rate was a factor of 2.4, driven almost entirely by the treatment of particle growth. That number is the honest uncertainty on any single-model estimate, including the bench on this site.

    6 modelsfactor 2.4growth
  5. P-14

    Particle growth in a cooling layer

    A coagulation-and-condensation model run at four injection rates to see how quickly a monodisperse injection becomes a broad distribution. At 10 Tg S a year the median radius doubles in about 40 days, which is fast enough that a disperser tuned on day one is mistuned by day sixty.

  6. P-33

    The dataset that did not survive review

    A retrieved size distribution from three campaigns, pooled and published as a climatology. A reviewer noticed that two of the three campaigns used different counter calibrations and the pooled median was an artefact of the mixing. The correction was published; the climatology was withdrawn. It is listed here because a portfolio that only shows the surviving results is a brochure.

    withdrawncalibrationcorrected
  7. P-08

    Non-use agreement analysis

    A study of the 2022 non-use initiative and the 2024 amendments to the London Protocol, written for a policy audience and reviewed by two lawyers who disagreed with each other in print. The technical gap it identifies is verification: no current instrument network could confirm whether a state had stopped injecting.

    policyverification gap2023–24
  8. P-35

    A monitoring design nobody funded

    A costed proposal for a verification network: six lidars, two balloon sites, one satellite retrieval cross-calibrated against both. The cost was modest by space-agency standards and no agency wanted the mandate. The document is public and the design is still sound; the reason it exists on a shelf is a useful thing to know about this field.

    proposalunfundedpublic
