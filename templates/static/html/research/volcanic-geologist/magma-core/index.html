§ 01 · The instrument

The Alert-Level Explorer

Every observatory bulletin compresses three kinds of unrest — earthquakes, gas, ground movement — plus one fact, whether an eruption has begun, into a single alert level. Set the readings as a duty scientist would find them, and the bulletin readout applies the scheme exactly as printed in § 02. There is no hidden weighting: the loudest instrument sets the level.

§ 02 · The scheme, in full

Four levels. One rule each.

The alert levels below follow the USGS volcano alert-level scheme, the system most observatories outside Europe use in some form. The explorer in § 01 applies this table and nothing else — if the two ever disagreed, the table would be the one to trust, and I would want to know.

Table 1 — Volcano alert levels and the rule that sets each

Alert levelWhat it meansSet whenBulletin rhythm
NORMAL The volcano is in its typical background, non-eruptive state. All three unrest parameters at background, and no eruption under way. Monthly
ADVISORY Signs of elevated unrest above the known background level; watched closely for any renewed increase. Any one parameter above background — seismicity, gas or deformation. Weekly
WATCH Heightened or escalating unrest with increased potential of eruption, timeframe uncertain — or an eruption under way that poses limited hazards. Any parameter high or escalating; or an eruption under way whose hazards stay inside the exclusion zone. Daily
WARNING A hazardous eruption is imminent, under way, or suspected. Any parameter extreme; or an eruption producing hazards — flows, lahars, heavy ash — beyond the exclusion zone. Continuous

§ 03 · The size scale

VEI: how big was that eruption?

The Volcanic Explosivity Index sorts eruptions by the bulk volume of what came out. Pick a number. Read what it buys in volume, which eruption textbooks reach for — and, at VEI 3, the reason this practice never quotes VEI without context.

Bulk volume erupted
Reference eruption
Frequency, worldwideRoughly one a year

The scale is logarithmic: each step up is roughly ten times the volume of the step below. A VEI 5 is not slightly larger than a VEI 4 — it is ten of them.
