Sheet 01

The window calculator

Every rendezvous plan lives or dies on one line of arithmetic: the synodic period — how often a favourable Earth–target geometry comes back. Set a target's orbital period and the sheet works the printed relation in front of you, line by line. Nothing is fitted, tuned or rounded in the practice's favour.

Set the target's period

1/S = | 1/PE − 1/PT |
PE = 1.00 yr (printed — Earth's period)

PT range 0.60–6.00 yr spans the near-Earth objects a mission can plausibly reach and the main belt it mostly cannot. Step it, slide it, or take a reference target.

      Sheet 04

      Evidence: Case File 02 — The Window

      A rendezvous mission team had baselined its arrival date for funding reasons. The target's period is 1.30 years, so its windows recur every 4.33 years — run it in the calculator above. Their date sat nowhere near a feasible geometry, and no propulsion budget they had could buy back 31 months of orbital mechanics.

      “The synodic arithmetic ended a six-month argument in one meeting. We stopped planning for the window we wanted and started engineering for the window that exists. The mission is smaller now. It is also real.”
      — Dr. A. Ferreira, Mission Scientist, rendezvous mission study (Case File 02)

      Sheet 05

      What this practice will not write

      Three refusals, printed in advance so nobody has to discover them on a call.

      Refusal 01

      A resource statement the spectra don't support

      If the class fit says ordinary rock, the dossier says ordinary rock — whoever is fundraising on it. Case File 01 is this refusal, already made once.

      Refusal 02

      A “trillion-dollar asteroid” valuation

      Pricing an unvisited body means inventing a grade, a recovery rate and a market that would crash on first delivery. The practice does not price unvisited rock.

      Refusal 03

      A deadline the synodic period forbids

      The practice will show the arithmetic, name the real window, and help rebuild the plan around it. It will not sign a plan that requires the solar system to hurry.
