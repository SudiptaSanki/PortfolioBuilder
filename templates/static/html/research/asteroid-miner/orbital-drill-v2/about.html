The practice

The product is the word “no”, said early and in writing.

Dr. Lena Okafor spent six years on the science team of a ground-based near-Earth object survey before founding Orbital Drill in 2019. The survey taught the trade's central fact: most targets are ordinary, and finding out early is worth more than any other line in a mission budget.

The practice exists because of a pattern its founder kept meeting from the other side of the table: a target would acquire a reputation — metal-rich, conveniently placed, worth a fortune — long before anyone had reduced the spectra. By the time a scientist was consulted, the reputation had a budget, a render and a press release. The science was being asked to decorate a decision, not to inform one.

Orbital Drill reverses the order. Characterisation comes first: the orbit audited, the spin measured, the spectra fitted, the windows computed. Only then does anyone get to say what a target is for. Clients are mission teams, agencies and research groups — and, through the claim-review service, the investors and insurers who are asked to fund other people's targets.

The credentials are the ordinary ones for this work: a doctorate in planetary science, survey-team years, peer review for small-bodies sessions, and a dossier record that includes public downgrades of fashionable targets. The practice's working rule is that a characterisation is a measurement document, not a marketing one — every claim in it is graded, and the grade is printed next to the claim.

It is a small practice on purpose: one lead scientist, a bench of analysts, and a hard limit of three full dossiers at a time. Windows are missed by overloaded consultants just as surely as by spacecraft.

Method

How a characterisation runs

Five stages, in order. A study can stop after any of them — and a screening exists precisely so that most targets do.

  1. 1

    Orbit & ephemeris audit

    The orbit is re-derived from the observation arc, its uncertainty stated, and the synodic windows computed — the arithmetic that decides whether a mission calendar is real. If the arc is short, the dossier says the orbit itself is still a rumour.

  2. 2

    Lightcurve — rotation & shape

    Brightness over time gives the spin period, a first shape, and the tumbling state. A fast rotator or a tumbler changes every later assumption about visiting, anchoring or sampling.

  3. 3

    Spectra — class & composition clues

    Visible and near-infrared spectra are fitted against the standard classes. This is the stage resource claims stand on — and the stage at which most of them fall, because a class is a clue to composition, never an assay.

  4. 4

    Radar & thermal, where geometry allows

    When an apparition brings a target within radar reach, delay-Doppler imaging sharpens the shape and near-surface; thermal inertia hints at whether the surface is dust, rubble or slab. Both are opportunistic — the practice plans around apparitions, and says when none is coming.

  5. 5

    The dossier — every claim graded

    The deliverable grades each claim Measured Inferred Unknown — measured means the data carry it directly; inferred means a model stands between the data and the claim; unknown is printed as unknown, with the observation that would resolve it.

Honesty

What a characterisation cannot do

It cannot assay a body it has never touched

Remote characterisation is inference from light and radar echoes. The only ground truth in this field is returned sample — and in all of history, exactly two asteroids have provided it: Ryugu (Hayabusa2, 2020) and Bennu (OSIRIS-REx, 2023). Every other composition claim, including this practice's, is graded inference and is labelled as such.

It cannot make mining exist

No commercial asteroid mine exists. No extraction, refining or return of asteroid material at commercial scale has ever been demonstrated. A practice that implies otherwise is selling the implication. Characterisation is real work with real buyers today; the mine is somebody else's century.

It cannot hurry the solar system

Windows recur on the synodic period, not on funding cycles. The practice will compute the real window and help rebuild a plan around it; it will not sign a plan whose dates require the geometry to be other than it is.

It cannot grade its own homework twice

Claim reviews are performed on targets the practice has not characterised for the claimant's counterparty, and the practice takes no equity, success fee or percentage of any raise connected to a target it assesses. The conflict rule is printed on the fees sheet, where clients can hold the practice to it.

Terms

Engagement terms

Fees & invoicing

Screening is a fixed fee ($11,200). Studies are billed at the printed day rates, invoiced monthly against person-days worked, with a written estimate ceiling agreed before work starts. Data access — telescope time, radar epochs — is bought by the client or passed through at cost, invoice attached.

Stopping

Either side may stop a study at a stage boundary. If the evidence at any stage kills the target's case, the practice will say so in an interim memo rather than completing a dossier nobody needs — and bill only to that stage.

Ownership & publication

The client owns its dossier. The practice may publish methods and anonymised lessons, never a client's target, data or verdict, without written permission. Target designations in these case files are withheld for exactly this reason.

Capacity

Three full dossiers at a time, one rendezvous support slot per season. Screening and claim reviews are taken between dossiers. Current booking: Q3 2026.
