The engineer

Tobias Renn

I grew up in Kiruna, where the ground is frozen for eight months and the mine moves a hundred thousand tonnes a day. My first job was automating a raise-borer for a contractor that could not keep operators on site. The lesson came early: the machine is only as good as its ability to notice that the rock has changed.

Between 2007 and 2018 I ran drilling campaigns in Western Australia and northern Chile — RC rigs, diamond core, and a remote-operated unit for a tailings dam that no one wanted to stand on. The last terrestrial project was an autonomous anchor test for a tidal turbine, which is where I first worked on the problem of holding something down when the medium refuses to cooperate.

I moved to space resources in 2019 through a Luxembourg programme and have stayed because the physics is honest. Nothing in microgravity is held by habit. If the anchor does not work, the rig leaves, and everyone can see it in the telemetry.

Method

Four rules a campaign runs by

Test the failure first

Every campaign starts by breaking the anchor on purpose. Knowing the failure mode and the force that produces it is worth more than a margin quoted from a model.

Simulant is not the asteroid

Our simulant has a cohesion of 0.4–0.9 kPa. A real C-type may be a rubble pile with no cohesion at all. Every number I give carries the simulant it came from.

No water number without an energy number

A tonne of 12 wt% regolith holds 120 kg of water and needs about 3.8 kWh per kilogram to release it. Quoting the first without the second is how proposals lie.

Leave the rig able to retreat

Every anchor design has a release. If the rig cannot let go, a jammed bit becomes a lost mission instead of a slow day.
