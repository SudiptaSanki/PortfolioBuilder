The problem

An asteroid does not hold still, and it does not hold you.

Surface gravity on a 1 km body is around 0.0003 m/s². A drill pushing down at 6 kN therefore pushes the spacecraft up with almost the same force. Every extraction architecture starts with a question that lunar and Martian mining never has to ask: what is the anchor attached to, and what happens to the reaction when the bit jams?

Our answer is not a bigger anchor but a different order of operations. We anchor first with a low-force auger, use the resulting hold to drive two more, and only then bring the drill to full feed. The rig is designed to lose the argument gracefully — the flight computer sheds feed force faster than the anchor can fail.

Process

Four operations, in this order, every time.

  1. A
    Prospect from 40 km

    Thermal inertia and a 2.2 µm hydration band pick the landing ellipse. We need a rock we can reach, not a rock with a good spectrum.

  2. B
    Touch and anchor

    A 40 mm auger into the first 300 mm at 0.8 kN, then a pull test to 1.5× the expected reaction before the drill is released from its launch lock.

  3. C
    Dig and hoist

    Regolith goes to the oven in 2 kg batches. The rig moves 20 t a day at 12 wt% water, which is one tonne of material every 71 minutes.

  4. D
    Cook and store

    The cavity receiver holds 420 °C. Vapour passes a cold trap at 180 K; the non-condensables are vented, and the product is kept as ice, not liquid.

Limits

What this design cannot do.

Deep ice

Below 4 m the auger cannot clear cuttings in vacuum; the hole packs and the bit seizes. Anything deeper needs a different cuttings path, which we have not flown.

Fast rotators

Above a 2.2 h rotation period the thermal cycle fatigues the anchor and the solar array cannot track continuously. We decline those targets rather than design for them.

Metal-rich bodies

M-types are worth more per tonne and are far harder to drill. Our auger is qualified for regolith, not for a nickel-iron surface, and I will not pretend otherwise in a proposal.
