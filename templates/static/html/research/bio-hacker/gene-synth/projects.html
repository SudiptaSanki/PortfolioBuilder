Five protocols, each with the step where it usually goes wrong

Every entry was written by the person who ran it, then re-run by someone else. If a step has a failure note, the failure note is the most useful part, so it is printed in full rather than moved to a forum thread.

P-001
Assembly

Golden Gate assembly of a four-part construct

Four inserts with BsaI-compatible overhangs, one backbone, one pot. The version here is the one that worked after eleven failures; the failures were all in the overhang design, not the enzyme.

  1. 01Design overhangs of 4 bp with no palindromes and no internal BsaI site. Check the junction, not the part.
  2. 02Mix 40 fmol each part, 1 µL T4 ligase buffer, 0.5 µL BsaI, 0.5 µL ligase, water to 10 µL.
  3. 03Run 37 °C 5 min / 16 °C 5 min for 30 cycles, then 60 °C 5 min to kill the enzyme.
  4. 04Transform 2 µL into chemically competent K-12. Plate on the backbone's marker.
  5. 05Colony PCR across the largest junction. Sequence anything that looks right.
Yield60–200 colonies
Time3 h + overnight
Cost$11 / reaction
Runs27

P-007
Hardware

A six-lane gel rig from a sealed box

Laser-cut acrylic ends, platinum wire electrodes, a 12 V 2 A supply and a food-storage box with a gasket. The design files are open and the bill of materials fits on one page.

  1. 01Cut the acrylic ends to 120 × 60 mm and solvent-weld them to the box with a 10 mm lip.
  2. 02Run platinum wire 40 mm apart, 0.5 mm diameter, sealed through the end walls with silicone.
  3. 03Cast 1.2 % agarose with a comb cut from the same acrylic, 6 teeth at 6 mm pitch.
  4. 04Wire the supply through a 500 mA fuse. Label the anode; everyone gets this wrong once.
  5. 05Leak-test with water for an hour before the first gel, not after.
Cost$38
Builds23
Voltage100 V
Time2 h

P-012
Yeast

Lithium acetate transformation of S. cerevisiae

The standard small-scale protocol, written for a bench with one heat block and no shaking incubator. Doubling times are longer at room temperature, so the timings are given in doubling times rather than hours.

  1. 01Grow to 5 × 10⁶ cells/mL. At 22 °C this is about 7 hours from a 1:100 inoculum, not 4.
  2. 02Wash once in water, once in 100 mM LiOAc, resuspend at 10⁸ cells per 50 µL.
  3. 03Add 1 µg plasmid, 50 µg single-stranded carrier DNA, 300 µL PEG/LiOAc, vortex.
  4. 04Heat shock 42 °C for 15 min. The failure note: 20 min kills more than it transforms.
  5. 05Recover in 1 mL YPD for 2 doubling times before plating on dropout medium.
Efficiency10⁴ / µg
Time2 days
Cost$4 / transform
Runs19

P-023
Instrument

An OD600 meter that costs less than a good lunch

A 600 nm LED, a photodiode, an Arduino and a 3D-printed cuvette holder. It is not a spectrophotometer and it does not pretend to be; it is a relative measure that is reproducible across units if they are built to the same drawing.

  1. 01Print the holder with black filament. White walls triple the stray light and destroy the linear range.
  2. 02Align the LED and diode on a 6 mm axis through a 5 mm cuvette slot.
  3. 03Read the diode through a 10 kΩ load, 10-bit ADC, 16 samples averaged.
  4. 04Calibrate against a blank and report relative absorbance only. Do not print an OD600 value.
Cost$26
Range0–1.2 rel.
Builds31
Time90 min

P-031
Teaching

Six-week CRISPR workshop, biosafety first

Twelve people who have never pipetted, two instructors, no mammalian cells. Weeks one and two are rules and ethics; the practical work is a guide RNA design exercise and a yeast-based demonstration that does not edit anything dangerous.

  1. 01Week 1 — containment, waste, what BSL-1 means and what it does not.
  2. 02Week 2 — what editing is, who has done it badly, and the case for a pause on some uses.
  3. 03Week 3 — pipetting, sterile technique, a gel that has nothing to do with CRISPR.
  4. 04Week 4 — design guides against a public yeast locus, score them, argue about the scores.
  5. 05Week 5 — run the demonstration, read the result, write the failure note.
  6. 06Week 6 — each participant teaches one step back to the room.
