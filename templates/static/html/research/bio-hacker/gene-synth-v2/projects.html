File OSR-2025-074

The 120-mer that arithmetic rescued

Closed — redesigned
ClientIndustrial biocatalysis programme
Work typeYield troubleshooting (S.03)
ScreeningPassed — before design discussion
OutcomeFull-length fraction 16.6% → 74.4% per fragment

The brief

A strain-engineering team had a long oligo — 120 bases, ordered at standard grade — that kept arriving weak and impure. Two re-orders, same result. They came to me suspecting their design, their provider, or both.

The review

The design was fine. The arithmetic was not: at standard grade, c = 0.985, the full-length fraction for 120 bases is 0.985119 ≈ 16.6%. Five of every six molecules in the tube were truncations, and no purification plan survives that ratio gracefully. The fix was on paper: split the design into two 60-base fragments at premium grade — 0.99559 ≈ 74.4% full-length each — and assemble.

The outcome

The redesigned order worked first time. The fragment route cost more per base and less per result: one successful synthesis instead of three failed ones. The team's design-review checklist now runs the length-vs-grade line before any order over 80 bases.

“We were ready to blame the design and then the supplier. Dr Osei blamed a number — 0.985, raised to the 119th power — and she was right. The redesign cost less than our second failed order had.”

File OSR-2024-031

The order that stopped at screening

Refused at screening
ClientWithheld — by design
Work typeSequence screening (S.01)
ScreeningComponent matched a regulated list
OutcomeRefused and reported; engagement closed

The brief

A multi-construct order arrived for design review through the normal intake: organisation identified, facility named, paperwork in order. Nothing about the enquiry was unusual — which is precisely why screening exists as a step and not a suspicion.

The review

At screening — before any design discussion — one component of the order matched a regulated-agent list. That is the whole of what this file records, and it is all it will ever record here. The match was not a matter of interpretation, and it was not discussed into a grey area.

The outcome

The order was refused in full and the match was reported through the proper channel, as the lists require. The screening fee was charged, per the printed terms — screening is the work, whatever it finds. The engagement closed at that line, and the client was told the refusal is final.

File OSR-2025-102

The redesign that cut the bill by a third

Closed — saving printed
ClientAcademic research group
Work typeConstruct design review (S.02)
ScreeningPassed — before design discussion
OutcomeSynthesis quote €14,200 → €8,900 (−37%)

The brief

A university group had a construct programme quoted at €14,200 for synthesis — long single-piece orders at premium grade throughout, because that is how the design had been drawn. The grant would cover it, barely, once.

The review

The design was redrawn for assembly: the long pieces became shorter fragments at the grade each length actually needed, with the yield arithmetic checked fragment by fragment against Table 1. Two fragments the group had specced at premium grade ran at high-efficiency with no loss — length, not habit, set the grade.

The outcome

The re-quoted synthesis came back at €8,900 — a saving of €5,300, or 37% — and the assembly succeeded on the first attempt, because the fragments arrived at fractions the purification could handle. The saving stayed in the grant and funded the group's follow-on constructs.
