Review docket · D.03 — method & refusals

Screening first. Before design, before discussion.

The order of operations is the whole method. A design conversation about an unscreened sequence is a conversation this practice does not have — not from caution as a mood, but because the screening is what makes the rest of the work legitimate.

The geneticist

I am Dr Naomi Osei. I trained as a geneticist and spent the first part of my career inside a university core facility — the room everyone visits when the synthesis has already failed. The patterns were never mysterious: orders placed at the wrong grade for their length, purification asked to perform miracles, and paperwork treated as an errand that runs after the science instead of the licence that runs before it.

I left to do the work in the other order. Since 2019 this practice has reviewed 214 constructs and screened 1,940 sequences for industrial biocatalysis programmes and academic groups. The work is deliberately unglamorous: arithmetic, lists, records. It is also the work that decides whether the glamorous part is allowed to happen.

The honesty page

Screening is not bureaucracy. It is the licence to do the work at all. The regulated-agent lists exist because the same synthesis that builds an industrial enzyme can be pointed elsewhere; a profession that wants the first freedom accepts the second duty. Any provider who treats screening as an optional courtesy is telling you something about their other standards.

On the word “biohacker”: this template arrived wearing neon and a helix, and the word still sits in its catalogue description. The aesthetic suggests garages, workarounds and rules treated as decoration. Professional practice is the opposite of all three — identified clients, regulated facilities, records an inspector can read. This site sides with practice, and dresses like it.

Why there are no sequences or protocols here: because that material belongs in regulated facilities and reviewed channels, not on a portfolio website. A consultancy that published its clients' construct details as content would fail its own screening standard. What can be published — the arithmetic, the fee shape, the refusals, the outcomes — is published.

“Screening is not the paperwork before the science. It is the science behaving as if it plans to be allowed to continue.”

Engagement terms

  • Screening is charged on every engagement, per construct, whether the answer is yes or no. The work is identical either way.
  • A refusal is final. It is not appealed by email, softened by deadline, or routed around by re-submitting components separately. Matches to the regulated lists are reported through the proper channels.
  • Clients must be identifiable — a named institution and facility — before intake opens. Anonymous and unidentifiable enquiries are not screened; they are declined.
  • Sequence material moves only inside the review process, through the secure channel arranged at intake. Never by website form, never by ordinary email.
  • Turnaround: screening results within five working days of a complete intake; design reviews scheduled in day blocks, typically within three weeks.
  • The report is part of every engagement — findings exist in writing, at the fixed report rate, or the engagement does not close.

How a review runs

01

Intake. Organisation, facility and work type identified. Nothing else is discussed yet.

02

Screening — first. Every sequence checked against the regulated-agent lists. A match ends the engagement here, with a report.

03

Yield analysis. Length against grade against purification load, on the printed arithmetic. Most failures are found at this desk, not at a bench.

04

Design review. Assembly strategy, grade per fragment, order structure — the changes that are free because they are still on paper.

05

QC standards. What will be checked on arrival, what counts as a failure, what the records must show.

06

Report. Findings, decisions and numbers in writing. The file closes when the report is filed.

ITEM 05

The refusal register, in full

The same register as the front page, unabridged. It is a short document because clarity is the point.

Refused

Pathogen work of any kind. No design review, screening advice or troubleshooting for work on pathogens.

Refused

Toxin work. No constructs involving toxins or toxin-production pathways, in whole or in part.

Refused

Enhancement of transmissibility or virulence. No work whose purpose is to make an organism spread better or harm more, under any research framing.

Refused

Anything matching the regulated-agent lists. Refused at screening and reported through the proper channels; the screening fee still applies.

Refused

Unscreened or anonymous work. No bypass, no “screen it later”, no unidentifiable clients, no exceptions for anyone.

Work that belongs outside the register is welcome.

Industrial biocatalysis, academic constructs, facility QC — screened first, then reviewed properly.

The standing rules

Screening before design, on every engagement. Refusals are printed, final, and reported where the lists require it. This site holds no sequences, no protocols and no wet-lab instructions — that material belongs in regulated facilities, not on a portfolio.
