Season files · the record, including the nulls

Fourteen seasons. Three files tell you how this practice works.

A seeding portfolio that only shows wet winters is advertising, not evidence. These three files were chosen deliberately: an evaluation that came back inconclusive and was reported with its bounds, a season stood down early and billed exactly as the contract printed, and a contract the practice refused. The other eleven files read much like the first — which is rather the point.

FILE 01 · 2023–24

The season that measured nothing, carefully

Reported — inconclusive · renewed

The brief

A water authority's third winter over its headwater catchment: continue the aircraft programme, and — the actual ask — produce an evaluation the board could defend to ratepayers. Target/control design with a randomised crossover: on seedable days, a sealed schedule decided whether the target ridge or the control ridge was seeded, so the season compared like with like instead of comparing weather with weather.

The decisions

The protocol was signed before the first sortie and could not be renegotiated after the snow fell. 38 sorties, 61 flight hours, every one logged against the five criteria; 11 held days ended in no-gos and were billed at standby, as printed. Two sorties were scrubbed mid-flight when the deck thinned below criterion C3 — recorded as aborts, not quietly reclassified.

The outcome

The double-ratio estimate came back at +6%, with a 90% interval from −4% to +17% — which is to say: consistent with a modest real effect, and consistent with none. The report printed exactly that, in those words, on page one. The authority renewed for two further seasons — on the strength, its manager said, of finally owning a number whose limits were stated as plainly as the number. That testimonial sits on the Operations page, attached to this file.

FILE 02 · 2024–25

Stood down by Christmas

Closed early — billed as printed

The brief

A reservoir operator's first season: 40 contracted flight hours over a low, warm catchment, sold to their board the previous spring — before anyone had run the climatology this practice now insists on doing first. The winter that arrived was the warmest in the catchment's short record.

The decisions

Week after week the soundings failed the same criterion: cloud tops warmer than −5 °C, outside the silver iodide band, with supercooled water scarce at seeding level. The crew flew the nine hours that genuinely met Table 1 and logged 112 decision records for the days it did not fly. On 19 December, both parties signed the early stand-down the contract provided for.

The outcome

The client paid for the nine flown hours, the standby days as printed, and demobilisation — and nothing for the 31 hours that were never flown, because the contract said so before the season started. The season report was mostly a no-go log, and the operator's board used it, verbatim, in its drought-plan review: documented evidence of why the catchment's seeding window is narrower than the sales literature of this industry suggests.

FILE 03 · 2025

The hail contract we declined

Declined — on the record

The brief

An agricultural cooperative asked for a hail-suppression programme over orchard country: aircraft on call through the convective season, at a fee roughly double a winter programme's. It was, on paper, the most lucrative enquiry of the year.

The decision

Declined, in writing, with the reasoning attached. The evidence for hail suppression by seeding does not support selling it: the dramatic claims trace to mid-century programmes whose results were never reproduced under randomised designs, and no controlled demonstration since has carried the claim. Selling a cooperative of growers a suppression guarantee on that record would be exactly the trade this practice exists not to be.

The outcome

The practice offered instead a feasibility assessment for a winter snowpack programme — the cooperative's actual long-term water problem — at the standard fixed fee. They declined, and bought suppression elsewhere. This file stays on the site because a practice is known by what it refuses, and because the next cooperative that asks deserves to find the answer already written down.
