Programme records — 2015 to 2026

Five programmes, and the one that failed is on the list too

Operational records for every programme I have run or evaluated. Target areas are stated, gain estimates are stated with their confidence intervals, and the hail trial is included because leaving it out would make the rest look better than it is.

P-01

North Fork basin, winter orographic

Fourteen ground generators on a line west of the North Fork watershed, operated on a randomised crossover design since 2019. Seeds winter storms with westerly flow and cloud base below −6 °C; roughly 34 storm periods per season, of which half are seeded.

Median gain 8.4 % in snow-water equivalent, 95 % bootstrap interval 2.1 to 14.7 %. The target basin supplies about 9 % of a mid-size city's water, which is the entire justification for the budget.

P-02

South Pass, ground-generator trial

A two-season trial using six generators and a denser snow gauge network, designed to test whether a smaller target area could show a detectable signal. It could not: the natural variability in a 400 km² basin swamped a 6 % effect with the available gauge density.

The trial was stopped rather than extended. Its real output is a power calculation that now informs every other programme: a 400 km² basin needs at least 18 gauges and six seasons to detect a 7 % gain.

P-03

Spring snowpack enhancement study

Seeding aimed at spring storms with shallower cloud and warmer temperatures, where the ice-nucleation window is narrow. Eleven seeded periods over two springs produced a 3 % median gain with an interval that included zero.

The programme continues as a measurement study rather than an operational one. Spring seeding is not obviously worth the fuel, and the honest answer is that we do not know yet.

Target620 km²

Seasons2

Median gain+3 %

P-04

Hail suppression trial, 2016–17

A two-season trial with four airborne flares and a radar-based seeding decision. The intended outcome was a 20 % reduction in hail days over the target; the observed difference was within noise and the trial did not reach its design sample.

It is listed here because hail suppression is the part of weather modification with the weakest evidence base, and because a programme that only publishes its wins is not a programme. The final report recommended no operational deployment.

P-05

Public snow measurement network

Twelve snow gauges, two silver sensors and a public dashboard that updates every six hours through the winter. Built so that anyone can check the programme's own numbers without asking me for them.

The dashboard is the most useful thing the programme has produced. It has also ended two arguments with the county about whether a given storm was seeded.
