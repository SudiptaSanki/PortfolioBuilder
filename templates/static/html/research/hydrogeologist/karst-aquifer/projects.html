S-01 · 2019—2021

Recharge area of the Ljubljanica springs

Client: Ljubljana water utility. Question: does the western valley drain to the same springs as the plateau?

The Ljubljanica rises from a group of springs south of Ljubljana, and its catchment had been drawn along surface divides that made little sense in limestone. Two valleys were treated as separate, which put a proposed industrial zone outside the source-protection area. We injected uranine at three swallow holes on the same rising stage and monitored nine outlets for 26 days.

S-02 · 2022—2024

Landfill leachate above the Pazin spring

Client: Istrian municipal utility. Question: is the closed landfill the source of rising ammonium at the spring?

The landfill sits on a dolomite plateau above a spring that supplies four villages. Ammonium at the spring had doubled in three years, and the operator argued the cause was agricultural. A drilling campaign found no liner and no geological barrier: a fissure zone ran from the waste body directly into the conduit feeding the spring.

The tracer test also identified a second fissure that the cap does not cover; it is under a monitoring licence until 2028.

S-03 · 2021—2025

Submarine springs of the Istrian coast

Client: coastal monitoring programme. Question: how much fresh water leaves through submarine springs, and what does it carry?

Submarine springs — vrulje — are visible as patches of colder, fresher water, but they are hard to gauge: the discharge is diffuse, tidal and often below a boat's draft. We placed a 24-electrode conductivity array across a known outlet for eight months, with a pressure sensor on the seabed and a reference station 400 m offshore.

S-04 · 2020—2022

Leakage beneath a Dinaric reservoir

Client: hydropower operator. Question: where does the 1.4 m³/s lost from the reservoir go?

The reservoir sits in a syncline of heavily karstified limestone. Filling the reservoir raised the water table by 34 m and reactivated an old spring 3.2 km downstream that had been dry since the 1970s. The operator needed to know whether grouting could recover the loss or whether the water was leaving the catchment entirely.

Outcome. A 240 m grout curtain across the mapped fissure zone cut the loss to 0.4 m³/s. The residual is monitored with a downstream weir and two pressure transducers, and the operating rule now limits the filling rate to 0.6 m/day.

S-05 · 2023—2025

Forty years of spring discharge

Client: regional water agency. Question: is the falling summer baseflow a drought or a trend?

Four springs in the Kras have daily discharge records going back to 1983. The agency was worried that summer baseflow had fallen since the 2000s, and needed to separate a run of dry years from a change in the aquifer itself. We rebuilt the record, filled the gaps with a rainfall–runoff model, and tested for trend in the annual minima and the recession constant.

Outcome. The trend is real but not monotonic. The agency now uses a drought-recovery index rather than a fixed minimum in its abstraction permits, and funds a winter recharge measurement campaign. The dataset is public and updated monthly.
