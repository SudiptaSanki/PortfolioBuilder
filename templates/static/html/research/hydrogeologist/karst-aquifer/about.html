I started in cave surveying and stayed for the water

Sixteen years of measuring where water enters, how fast it moves and what it carries has taught me one thing: the map is always more connected than the last one said.

I grew up in Sežana, on the edge of the Kras plateau, where the schoolyard drain emptied into a hole in the ground and nobody could say where the water came out. That question became a degree in geology, then a doctorate on tracer transport in the Dinaric karst, then a job that has not stopped asking it.

My work combines the old method with the new. Dye, salt and a stopwatch still settle most arguments in a catchment, because a tracer test measures the connection directly. Continuous loggers and numerical models then extend a week of measurement into a decade of prediction, which is what a utility actually needs when it decides where to drill or what to protect.

I am careful about what a tracer test can claim. A recovery of 85% says most of the water took the path you monitored; it does not say the other 15% is safe, or that a second, unmonitored outlet does not exist. Most of my reports carry a section on what the data cannot rule out, and I have argued against my own clients' preferred conclusions more than once.

Outside the contract work I survey caves with the Sežana caving club, teach a summer field course on karst methods, and maintain a public spring-watch dataset that anyone can download.

Teaching and field courses

Karst field methods — a nine-day summer course with the University of Ljubljana. Students design a tracer injection, install fluorometers, do the mass balance and defend the result in front of a utility engineer. Running since 2018, 14–18 students a year.

Groundwater modelling for regulators — a two-day short course on what a MODFLOW-CFP model can and cannot tell a permitting decision. Given for the Slovene water agency and twice for a Croatian basin authority.

Reviewing and service

Associate editor, Acta Carsologica (2023–). Reviewer for Journal of Hydrology, Hydrogeology Journal and Water Resources Research. Member of the Slovene karst commission on source protection.

I also answer questions from landowners along the Ljubljanica about sinking streams, which is not in any contract and is the most useful hour of some weeks.
