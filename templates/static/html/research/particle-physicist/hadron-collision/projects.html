Case files — engagements, written up

The work, with the numbers left in

Three engagements, told the way they are reported to clients: the brief as it arrived, the decisions that mattered, and the outcome — including the parts that were my profession's fault, not the client's. Client names are withheld under the engagement terms; client types, durations and figures are the engagement's own.

CASE 01

The trigger that cried wolf

ClientUniversity-led experiment group
Duration10 weeks, 2024
DeliverableRate report + revised trigger menu
Fee basisFixed-fee trigger study

The brief

A rare-decay search group was spending ≈ 900 Hz of trigger output bandwidth against a 300 Hz allocation — three times its budget — and the overrun was quietly taxing every other group's physics. The menu owners suspected their own signal path; the DAQ team suspected the luminosity levelling. Both were partly right, which is why nobody had fixed it.

The decisions

Rates were re-derived from zero-bias data — the prescaled sample of ordinary crossings — rather than from simulation, so the argument would be about measurements, not models. One unprescaled path turned out to be fed by pileup jets faking the signal signature: at high pileup, two soft jets from different vertices reconstructed as one hard object. The fix was a pileup-jet identification requirement and a tightened isolation cone — applied to that path only, not sprayed across the menu. Signal efficiency was re-measured on control samples before and after, so the group could see the cost of the fix in their own channel, not in a promise.

The outcome

Output rate 310 Hz — inside allocation — with signal efficiency held at 94% of its previous value, the 6% cost stated in the report's first paragraph rather than buried. The menu passed its next review without conditions, and the zero-bias rate derivation became the group's standing pre-review check.

Client's words on this engagement are on the home page, attached to this case as they should be.

CASE 02

The bump that wasn't

ClientResearch group, precision measurement
Duration14 weeks, 2024–25
DeliverableWritten discrepancy report + corrected simulation
Fee basisSimulation campaign

The brief

A precision-measurement group had a persistent excess in a control region — data sitting above simulation where no signal should be. It was small, it was stable, and it was blocking the analysis: an unexplained control-region discrepancy becomes a systematic uncertainty on everything downstream, and reviewers know it.

The decisions

The discrepancy was chased as a modelling question, not a statistics question. Generator-level comparisons isolated it to the parton shower's treatment of initial-state radiation: the tune in use radiated too little at wide angles, so simulated events were too clean and the control region too empty. The decisive evidence was produced in writing — a short report with the generator variations, the data comparisons, and the reasoning laid out so the group's own conveners could defend it in review without me in the room. A corrected tune was validated against two independent control regions, not just the one that had complained.

The outcome

The excess closed. The analysis unblocked with a smaller, honest systematic in place of the large unexplained one, and the report — not a meeting, the document — is what the group cites when the question comes up. Simulation campaigns I run now start with the shower tune on the checklist, in writing, because of this case.

CASE 03

An analysis that outlives its authors

ClientInstitute, funder-mandated preservation
Duration12 weeks, 2025
DeliverableContainerised, re-runnable analysis + runbook
Fee basisFixed-fee preservation engagement

The brief

A published analysis — years of work, a result other groups build on — could only be re-run by two people, on one ageing machine, from memory. The institute's funder required preservation; the institute required that preservation mean something stronger than a tarball.

The decisions

The acceptance test was agreed first and in writing: a physicist who had never seen the analysis must reproduce its headline result from the preserved materials alone. The workflow was containerised with pinned software versions; inputs were moved to versioned, documented formats; every manual step — and there were more than anyone remembered — was either automated or written into the runbook as a decision with a reason. Continuous integration re-ran a reduced version of the full chain on every change, so decay would announce itself instead of arriving silently.

The outcome

Eighteen months later, an incoming postdoc with no connection to the original team reproduced the headline result in two days, and the runbook's decision log answered the three questions she would otherwise have emailed to people who had left. The institute adopted the acceptance test as its template for the next four analyses.
