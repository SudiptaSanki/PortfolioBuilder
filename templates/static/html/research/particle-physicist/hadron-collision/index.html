Four leptons: a Higgs candidate

H → ZZ* → 4ℓ · transverse view, schematic

What you are looking at

Four charged tracks leave the interaction point and nothing else does. Two stop in the electromagnetic calorimeter — electrons, which shower and die there. Two punch through every layer to the muon chambers — muons, the only charged particles that can. No jets, no missing energy worth the name. This is the cleanest signature a hadron collider produces.

Pairing them: the e⁺e⁻ pair reconstructs near the Z mass (≈ 91 GeV); the μ⁺μ⁻ pair, from the off-shell Z*, is lighter (≈ 33 GeV). All four together: ≈ 125 GeV.

Why the trigger kept it

Kept by a dilepton trigger: two leptons above threshold at Level-1, confirmed in the high-level trigger. Four-lepton events are so rare and so clean that the trigger keeps essentially every one it sees — the opposite problem from the pileup event.

The mass, honestly

Add the four measured energy–momentum vectors and the combination has an invariant mass. For this schematic event the arithmetic lands at m(4ℓ) ≈ 125 GeV — on the Higgs peak in the spectrum below. One pair sits near the Z mass (91 GeV); the other, from the off-shell Z*, is lighter. That pairing is the fingerprint.

The four-lepton mass spectrum — schematic

The shape follows the published four-lepton mass distribution — a narrow excess at 125 GeV over a smooth ZZ background. Drawn as a schematic: no real events are plotted, and none are implied.

Log 02 — Exposure, in one equation

How many events? N = σ × ℒ

A cross-section σ says how likely a process is; integrated luminosity ℒ says how much collision exposure was delivered. Multiply them and you get the only number a group actually plans around: how many events of that kind exist in the dataset. Try it — the printed example underneath is the check.

Cross-section σ — pick a process

Integrated luminosity ℒ — pick an exposure

Printed check: a process with σ = 1 fb, exposed to ℒ = 100 fb⁻¹, gives 1 × 100 = 100 events. Set the panel to those two chips and it must say 100 — if it ever doesn't, the panel is wrong, not the physics.

N = σ × ℒ = 55,000 fb × 140 fb⁻¹
7,700,000 events

That is the Higgs figure: of order 7.7 million Higgs bosons produced in Run 2 — before branching fractions take their cut (the four-lepton channel keeps roughly one in ten thousand) and before the detector's acceptance takes its. Abundance and scarcity are the same number, read at different stages. This arithmetic — exposure, branching, acceptance — is the first page of every study I write.
