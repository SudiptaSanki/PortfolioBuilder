Build 12 · 2026 · Arcade Obrera

Tren Ligero

A four-player linked cabinet built around a light-rail map of the city. Each player controls one line and has to keep the others moving; the joke is that everyone is the problem. Custom JAMMA harness, four control panels with trackball-free sticks, and a link cable run through the cabinet base because the venue refused to let me drill the floor.

The hard part: four CRTs in one row means four different tube wear profiles. I wrote a per-monitor colour profile and burned it into each cabinet’s ROM so no player gets a brighter screen than the others.

Build 09 · 2025 · Mercado Coyoacán

Peso Punch

A one-player boxing game where the opponent is a market vendor with a broom. Two buttons: jab and duck. It runs on a modified JAMMA board with a ROM swap I burned myself, and the whole game is 512 KB. Sitting in a market means it gets played by people who have never used a stick, so the first fight teaches the two buttons without a single line of text.

The hard part: the venue is loud and the attract mode had to be audible over a market at noon. I re-cut every sound to sit in the 800–2000 Hz band, which is where a crowd leaves a hole.

Build 06 · 2024 · Licorería Roma

Meteor Kids

Two-player co-op shooter with a shared lives pool, because the fastest way to make two strangers talk is to give them one health bar. Cabinet is a converted generic upright with a new control panel, new power supply and a repaired flyback. The game is mine; the cabinet was somebody’s dead Street Fighter II.

The hard part: co-op on one screen at 60 Hz means both players share the same frame budget. Enemy bullets are drawn as 2 × 4 pixel dashes because a 4 × 4 sprite at that speed reads as a smear on a worn tube.

Restoration · 2024 · private collection

Centipede upright, 1981

A cabinet that had been in a garage in Naucalpan since 1996. New side art from a scan, new t-molding, rebuilt trackball with new bearings, and a monitor recap that took two evenings and one burnt finger. The original board was fine; the edge connector was not.

The hard part: the original power supply was running 4.6 V instead of 5 V, which made the game boot only when the room was warm. I replaced it with a modern switching supply and a small load resistor so the sense line behaves.

Restoration · 2023 · Arcade Obrera

Galaga cocktail table

A cocktail table with a dead screen and a swollen top. The glass was replaced, the tube was swapped from a donor television, and the control panels were re-chromed. It now sits in the corner of the bar where people put their drinks, which is what happens to every cocktail table eventually.

The hard part: the donor tube had a different yoke, so the image was rotated and pinched. Adjusting it took an afternoon with a mirror and a lot of patience.

Build 13 · 2027 · in progress

Untitled rhythm cabinet

A two-button rhythm game where the music is generated from the player’s inputs rather than the other way around. It is either the best idea I have had or a machine that plays itself; the prototype is on the bench and I will know by March.

The hard part: latency. A rhythm game at 60 Hz gives you 16.7 ms per frame, and a CRT adds nothing while an LCD adds everything. The cabinet will only ship with a tube, which makes the parts list a scavenger hunt.
