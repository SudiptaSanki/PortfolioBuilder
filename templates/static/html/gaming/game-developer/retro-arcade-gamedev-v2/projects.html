§2 · Title Log

Every title. Three lists.

Most portfolios show you the shipped games and quietly delete the rest. This log keeps three lists — shipped, jam, cancelled — and never merges them, because each one proves something different. Shipped proves the work survives contact with players. Jam proves speed, and nothing else. Cancelled proves the budget sheet is allowed to say no. Every figure carries its platform and its date; every title carries its credits.

Bullet Garden

Shipped · On sale
Class
Score-attack arena shooter
Platforms
PC (Steam) · Switch
Released
Mar 2024 · Nov 2024
Figures
41,300 copies, both platforms, 31 Oct 2026

The ask: a one-screen arena shooter where the flowers are the bullets — ninety-second runs, a score table that resets weekly, and a frame that must never, ever dip, because a dip in a score-attack game is a stolen run.

What I did: fixed timestep, input polled first, and a budget sheet agreed before the art started — the same sheet printed as Test 01 on the Test Mode page. The PC build shipped with everything on at 14.38 ms mean. The Switch build's profiler said otherwise: the CRT post pass pushed the frame to 16.74 ms against a 16.67 line. The pass was cut, the scanlines were faked inside the sprite shader for a tenth of the cost, and the game shipped at a held 60.

The outcome: 41,300 copies across Steam and Switch as of 31 October 2026, counted from the two platform dashboards — not rounded, not projected. The weekly table peaks every Sunday; the support contract is two weeks a quarter, spent almost entirely on balance tuning the waves the data says are unfair.

CREDITS — Design & code: Hollis Crane · Music & SFX: June Park · Key art: Theo Marchetti · Switch certification testing: the publisher's QA team, named in the game's own credits screen

One Credit Clear

Shipped · On location
Class
Original cabinet game
Platform
2 cabinets · Replay Rooms, Brighton
Installed
Sep 2025
Service record
98.2% uptime · 14 months · operator's log

The ask: Replay Rooms wanted a game nobody could play at home — one credit, one life, a cabinet leaderboard that belongs to the room. If it broke down more than their forty-year-old machines, it was coming out.

What I did: built the game and both cabinets as one system — a route-clearance score game on a deliberately modest board (the whole frame costs 9.1 ms; the rest of the budget is headroom the hardware will never need), industrial microswitches, and a service menu the arcade's own staff can run: input test, bookkeeping, and a frame report that prints to the operator's laptop. Every update since has shipped with its frame report attached.

The outcome: 98.2% uptime across the first 14 months by the operator's service log — three service visits, one failed microswitch, zero board faults. It is the second-highest-earning cabinet in the room, behind a light-gun game with a thirty-year head start.

CREDITS — Game, cabinet & service system: Hollis Crane · Cabinet artwork: Theo Marchetti · Music: June Park · Commissioned by Replay Rooms (Brighton); operations contact Dan Whitaker

Gravity Well 84

Shipped · On sale
Class
Pocket score game
Platform
PC · itch.io
Released
Jun 2023 · £3
Figures
9,800 downloads as of Oct 2026

The ask: my own — could a complete, honest score game be built inside a 16-colour palette and a 4 ms frame on purpose, as training for the bigger titles?

What I did: one mechanic (slingshot a probe around a well, don't touch the walls), forty levels generated from twelve hand-made seeds, and a frame so far under budget that the leftover went into a replay system — every run records its inputs, so a disputed score can be re-simulated exactly.

The outcome: 9,800 downloads at £3 as of October 2026, counted from the itch.io dashboard. Small numbers, printed at their real size. The replay system it funded became the support tool I now use on every title.

CREDITS — Everything: Hollis Crane · Playtesting: the Sheffield Game Dev Meetup, Tuesday group

Tea Break Defence

Event
Weekend jam · theme: “one button”
Built
Nov 2022 · 48 h
Status

Lesson banked
One-button input discipline

The ask: the jam theme was “one button”. Defend a tea tray from wasps with a single input — hold to aim, release to fire.

What it taught: a one-button game lives or dies on the exact frame the input is read. The input-test toy on the Test Mode page exists because of this weekend. It placed 14th of 212 entries in the jam's own ratings — a jam placing, printed as a jam placing, worth exactly what that's worth.

CREDITS — Code & design: Hollis Crane · Art: Theo Marchetti (one afternoon) · Sound: none — it was a jam, the wasps are silent

The ask: count moths around a lamp before the lamp burns out — a counting game where the particle system is the score.

What it taught: my particle cap (400, oldest die first) survives a jam's worst idea — spawning everything at once — without a single dropped frame. That cap, unchanged, is line PRT on the Bullet Garden budget sheet. Four more jam builds exist and are not written up; a jam game is a sketch, and sketches don't get case files.

Cancelled

2 titles · printed with reasons · salvage noted

Dreadnought Dawn

Cancelled · Month 5
Class
Naval score-attack, commissioned
Ran
Aug 2023 – Jan 2024
Cause
Publisher funding withdrawn
Salvaged
The sprite batcher — now Bullet Garden's renderer

The ask: a small publisher commissioned a capital-ship score game — broadsides, flooding compartments, a damage model you could read at a glance.

What happened: five months in, the publisher's parent company cut its entire indie slate. The game died with its funding, fully paid to the last milestone — that's the whole story, and it's a common one. What I won't do is pretend it shipped, or list it as “unreleased” in the shipped column with a trailer.

What was salvaged: the sprite batcher written for its broadside effects — one atlas, one draw call per layer — became the renderer in Bullet Garden, where it is line SPR on the budget sheet, still the most expensive system I own and still worth it.

CREDITS — Design & code: Hollis Crane · Music (unreleased): June Park · The publisher is not named: their slate decision was theirs to announce, not mine

Signal Harvest

Cancelled · By the budget sheet
Class
Self-released puzzle-arcade
Ran
Feb – Apr 2025 · prototype stage
Cause
Design could not hold 60 on target hardware
Salvaged
The wave-director maths, reused twice since

The ask: my own design — route signals through a living switchboard of 300 simultaneous nodes, every node simulated every frame.

What happened: the prototype's budget sheet said 21.4 ms against the 16.67 line, and every honest optimisation I tried bought back less than the design spent. I could have shipped it at 30 fps and called the stutter “retro”. Instead it was cancelled at prototype, by the sheet, in April 2025 — the same sheet you can flip the switches on, on the Test Mode page. That is what the sheet is for.

What was salvaged: the wave-director mathematics (spawn pressure as a budget, spent over time) — reused in a client port and in Moth Count. The prototype is archived, playable at game-dev meetups, and labelled with its real frame time on the title screen.

CREDITS — Everything: Hollis Crane · The kill decision: also Hollis Crane, argued with at length, lost
