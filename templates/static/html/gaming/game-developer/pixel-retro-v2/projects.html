File 01

Lanternfall — a game of one rock and one lamp

Shipped
The ask
Make a complete game — alone, in the margins of commission work — that could only exist at low resolution: a lighthouse keeper’s week, where the whole world is one rock, one lamp, and the weather.
Constraints
Canvas 240 × 135 · palette 14 colours, locked before production · keeper sprite 12 × 14 and lighthouse 16 × 16, each drawn in 6 colours · walk cycle 8 frames · no text in the world itself — the logbook carries the words
Decisions
The 14-colour palette was locked for nine months and never reopened; two planned islands were cut when their tiles broke the palette, and the game is better for the smaller sea. The lamp’s beam is three rows of pixels and a timing table, not an effect. The soundtrack was commissioned from a human: Iker Agirre, chiptune composer (Bilbao), credited in-game and here — the sketch on the home page is my synthesis, not his music.
Outcome
Released 2024 on itch.io and Steam; 4,100 copies across both stores as of October 2026. Still patched: the November 2026 update fixed the tide table in chapter four, a bug that had survived two years because nobody fishes in chapter four.

Lanternfall · own title · itch.io & Steam, 2024 · art, code & design: Marisol Vega · music: Iker Agirre

File 02

The Bramble Knight — a commission for Thornwick

Delivered
The ask
A two-person studio in Gdańsk needed the full art set for Thornwick, a hedgerow adventure: a knight protagonist, four creatures, and the tileset — in nine weeks, at 32 × 32, readable on a handheld screen in daylight.
The work
214 frames delivered: the knight’s set (four directions, walk 16 frames, attack 8, hurt 4, idle 6), four creature sets, and a 48-tile tileset. Palette locked at 11 colours with the client before frame two — the knight’s first frame was approved in a day, which is the system working as designed.
Revisions
The contract included three revision rounds; two were used — one to shrink the knight’s plume (it read as a speech bubble at 32 × 32, which is exactly the kind of thing first-frame approval exists to catch, one round late) and one to re-time the attack. The third round was returned unused and uncharged.
Outcome
Thornwick shipped in 2025 with the set as delivered; sources handed over as .aseprite files at final payment, full commercial rights in the game included, per the terms on the practice page.
“Marisol sent the first frame before she sent the contract. The knight read clearly at 32 × 32 from across the room — that is the whole job, and she had done it in frame one.”
— Tomasz Zieliński, lead developer, Thornwick (Gdańsk)

File 03

The Glass Meridian — shelved, and shown

Shelved
The ask
My own design: a navigation puzzle game about a cartographer’s compass that lies — solve each chart by working out how it lies. Seven months of evenings, 2024–25.
What killed it
Readability. At the game’s 240 × 135 canvas, the difference between a true bearing and a false one was two pixels and a shade of brass — indistinguishable in play, however long I stared at it in the editor. Every fix (labels, callouts, a bigger compass) broke the game’s one rule: no text in the world. A puzzle the player cannot read is not difficult; it is broken.
What was salvaged
The compass rose itself, released free for other developers to use, with the palette file. And the lesson, now printed in the practice terms: prototype the readability at final resolution before the art starts — a rule Low Water was designed under from its first week.
Status
Shelved in 2025, not abandoned in secret and not quietly deleted from this page in 2026. If the bearing problem is ever solved honestly, the file reopens. Until then it stays here, labelled.

The Glass Meridian · own title · 2024–25 · shelved 2025 · art released free; design retired
