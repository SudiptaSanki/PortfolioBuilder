How I started

My uncle gave me a second-hand NES in 2001 with two cartridges and no instructions. One of them was a bootleg that started at level four and could not save. I spent a year learning the game around that missing beginning, and I think that is where the interest in constraints comes from — I never saw the intended version, so I made one in my head.

I studied computer science in Pune because it was practical, then took a job writing Android interfaces for two years. The interfaces were 48-pixel icons in a grid, which is not far from a tilemap. In 2016 I started drawing sprites at night and putting them on a blog. A studio in Bengaluru saw them and offered me a contract; I moved and have been doing this full time since 2019.

A sprite is a drawing where every pixel costs something. That is the whole discipline.

What I am good at

Reading at small sizes. I can look at a character at 16 × 16 and tell you which two pixels are wrong. I am also good at tile budgets and palettes — designing a 16-colour set that can carry a whole game, and then sticking to it when a new idea wants a seventeenth colour.

I program my own engines in C for 2D games, which means I can promise a build size and keep the promise. Rooftop Rani is 1.8 MB including music, font and every sprite. That is not a stunt; small builds load fast on cheap hardware, and cheap hardware is where most players are.

What I am not

I am not a 3D artist, I do not animate cutscenes, and I cannot write a shader without copying from a tutorial and then breaking it. I am also not fast: four to six sprites a day is my honest rate, and I would rather tell you that now than deliver late.

If your project needs a hundred characters in a month, I am the wrong person and I will say so in the first email.

Working with me

I take two projects a quarter. Art-only work is fine; art plus code is better, because the two decisions argue with each other in useful ways. I ask for a written scope, a palette decision and a hard deadline before I start, and I send a sprite test in the first week so you can see whether my reading of your game is right before either of us spends a month on it.

I keep all source files, including the Aseprite documents, in a repository you get access to. If we stop working together, the files stay yours.
