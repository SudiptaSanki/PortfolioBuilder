Nine games, two of them good.

Commercial releases, Playdate experiments, contract work and one cartridge that runs on a 1983 console. Each entry lists what I actually made, because “pixel art by” can mean anything from a full tileset to three icons.

Night Bus 64

A real NES cartridge: 32 KB PRG, 8 KB CHR, custom mapper, battery save. You drive a night bus through a city that only exists between 1 and 4 a.m., picking up passengers who are not entirely there. Written in 6502 assembly, which is a sentence I enjoy saying and did not enjoy living.

NES · homebrewsoloin progress
2025

Rooftop Rani

A runner with a cancel window on every jump, because a runner where you cannot take a jump back is a rhythm game with worse music. Four levels, 32 KB soundtrack, custom tile engine in C. Shipped on Switch and PC; the Switch build was 1.8 MB including the font.

Switch · PCdesign, code, art, musicreleased
2024

Moss Census

One screen, 21 species of moss, a crank to focus. The entire game is looking closely at small things and writing a number in a notebook. Built in 11 weeks for Playdate; sold 2,300 copies. The best thing I have made and the least commercially sensible.

Playdateeverythingreleased
2023

Talwar Tales — art direction

Six months of contract work for a Chennai studio: 340 sprites, 12 tilesets and a colour script for a 16-bit-styled action game. I set the palette, drew the characters and then handed the style guide to their in-house team. Credit reads “pixel art direction”; the actual pixel work after month four is theirs.

PC · contractart direction + spritesreleased
2022

Chai Cart

A time-management puzzle about a tea stall with one burner and eight impatient customers. Made for a 48-hour jam, then expanded over five months. The expanded version is worse than the jam version; I keep both on the shelf as a lesson about scope.

PC · itcheverythingreleased
2021

Static

A Game Boy-style horror game about a television repair shop. Four-colour palette, 160 × 144 resolution, and a monster that only moves when the screen flickers. Built in GB Studio in three weeks; the flicker effect is a bug I decided to keep.

GB Studio · webeverythingreleased
2020

Two hundred icons

Not a game, but the most useful thing on this shelf: an icon set for a fleet-management dashboard, drawn at 16 × 16 and delivered as SVG and sprite sheet. The constraint that made it good was the client’s request that every icon read at 100% zoom on a warehouse tablet.

UI · contract200 iconsdelivered
2019

Pocket Garden

A tiny garden that grows while the phone is off. Contract work for a studio that wanted a “calm” game and then asked for notifications. I drew 180 plant sprites and built the growth simulation; the notifications were not my idea and I have made peace with them.

Mobile · contractart + systemsreleased
2018

First Cart (lost)

My first attempt at NES assembly: a scrolling shooter with no sound and an enemy that could not be killed. The ROM exists on one hard drive and boots to a grey screen. I keep it listed because everyone’s shelf should admit where it started.
