The casebook

Work, written down.

A resale shop's portfolio is not a wall of product shots — it is the collections it has graded honestly, the programmes it runs on printed terms, and the pairs it has refused. Three entries from the stock book.

CASE 01 · CONSIGNMENT

The Hartley Collection

Intake
62 pairs, one collector
Window
60 days, Sep–Nov 2026
Split
80 / 20 consignment
Status
Closed — 58 of 62 sold

The ask

A collector downsizing twenty years of buying asked us to sell 62 pairs as one consignment. His own list graded 41 of them deadstock. He wanted the collection sold as a story; we said we would sell it as 62 individual pairs, each with its own card.

What we did

Three weeks of intake: every pair through the two-checker inspection, graded against the printed standard, priced inside its rate-card band. Two pairs he listed as deadstock came back Grade B and Grade C — one with a tried-on crease at the toe box, one with sole yellowing that put it outside the A definition. Both downgrades were written on the check sheets with photographs, and he signed them before anything was priced. No pair was listed at a grade its sheet could not defend.

The outcome

58 of 62 pairs sold inside the 60-day window for $11,940 in total; at the printed 80/20 split the seller received $9,552. The four unsold pairs were returned with their check sheets, free. The two downgraded pairs sold at their true grades for more than a neighbouring shop later offered him for them as “deadstock” — because the buyers could read exactly what they were getting.

CASE 02 · PROGRAMME

Trade-In Saturdays

Launched
January 2026
Terms
65% credit / 55% cash
First quarter
143 pairs traded
Status
Running — terms unchanged

The ask

Customers kept asking what their worn pairs were “worth to us” and getting a shrug in most shops. We wanted a trade-in programme whose answer could be printed on the wall before anyone brought a pair in — no haggling, no number invented at the counter.

What we did

We published the splits first — 65% of agreed value as credit, 55% as cash — and built the valuation on the rate card: inspect, grade, take the band, agree the value, apply the percentage. The calculator on the home page is the same arithmetic the counter uses. Staff were instructed to show the band on the card before naming a number, every time.

The outcome

143 pairs traded in the first quarter at an average agreed value of $118 — an average credit of $76.70 per pair. 31 pairs were rejected at intake, most for condition below Grade E and two on authenticity doubts; every rejection came with the written check sheet. The terms have not changed since launch, which is the point of printing them.

CASE 03 · REJECTION

The Pair We Sent Back

Presented as
Grade A deadstock
Checks failed
2 of 12
Fee charged
$0 — intake check is free
Status
Returned to seller

The ask

A seller brought in a deadstock-claimed pair of a sought-after release, with a box in excellent condition and a price expectation to match. It was, on the surface, the easiest intake of the month.

What we did

The pair failed two checks on the standard sheet. The stitching density along one panel was inconsistent with the release's production pattern, and the box label's typography did not match the label stock the release shipped with. Either failure alone stops an intake; together they ended it. The second checker repeated the inspection blind and reached the same result before we spoke to the seller.

The outcome

The pair was returned the same day with the completed check sheet showing both failures, and no fee — intake checks are free precisely so that a rejection never looks like a revenue decision. The seller told us he had bought the pair in good faith from an online marketplace; our sheet became the evidence he used in his refund claim there. That sheet is now the shop's standard intake form.
