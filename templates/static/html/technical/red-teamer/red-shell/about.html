Operator · ROE-first offensive security

The operator is the control

Tools do not decide whether a test is safe. A person does, with a written scope, a stop word and the authority to end the operation. This page is about who that person is and what she will not do.

From the SOC to the other side

I spent my first six years defending. Two at a Portuguese bank’s security operations centre, where I learned what an alert looks like at 04:00 and why most of them are noise, then four at a consultancy building detection content for clients who had bought every sensor and tuned none of them.

Moving to offence was not a career change so much as a change of evidence. As a defender I could only guess which of my detections would survive a real adversary. Running the intrusion myself answers the question directly: either the rule fires and the analyst finds it, or it does not, and both results are useful.

I have led 38 operations since 2019 — assumed breach, phishing-led, cloud and identity, and a small amount of OT work with a safety officer in the room. The operations I am proudest of are not the ones that ended in domain admin. They are the ones where the blue team caught us on day four because a rule we had written together two months earlier worked exactly as designed.

Rules of engagement, in plain terms

Nothing runs without a signed scope. The document names the target systems, the source addresses, the accounts, the testing window, the exclusions and the escalation path. If a system is not listed, it is out of scope even if it is one DNS lookup away.

There is a stop word and it is not decorative. The client’s safety officer can stop the operation with one word, at any time, for any reason, without explanation. If they use it, we stop, we confirm the state of every system we touched, and we do not restart without a new written instruction.

No persistence without permission. I do not leave implants, accounts, scheduled tasks or certificates behind unless the brief explicitly asks for a persistence test and the client has agreed the removal procedure. Everything is removed and verified at the end, and the removal is logged.

Data is evidence, not loot. If customer data is reachable, I prove reachability with a count and a hash, not by copying the records. Anything captured is stored encrypted, listed in the report, and destroyed on a date agreed at scoping.

No third parties. If the path leaves the client’s estate — a supplier, a cloud provider’s shared service, a payment network — the operation stops there and we discuss it. The client’s authorisation does not extend to someone else’s systems.

What I will not do

I do not test a system without written authorisation from its owner, and I do not accept “we own it, trust us” as that authorisation. I do not run “surprise” tests against a client’s staff without their leadership knowing. I do not take work whose purpose is to justify a purchase already made or to discredit a vendor. I do not sell exploits, and I do not keep a copy of anything I find.

The team

Most operations are me plus one associate: a second operator for the long nights, or a detection engineer who watches the blue team’s queue in real time and tells us what they are and are not seeing. Both associates are named in the scope document and are bound by the same rules. I do not subcontract to a bench I have not worked with.

Why the operator matters more than the toolchain

An offensive tool will run the same exploit against a test box and a production controller. What stops it is a person who knows which is which, and who has decided in advance to stop when the situation changes.

That is why every engagement begins with a conversation about consequences rather than a scan. What happens if this system goes down for an hour? Who is on call? What must never be touched, even to prove a point? The answers go into the rules of engagement, and they are the reason I am comfortable running the work at all.

Stop word
Holds from any client safety officer, at any time, no explanation required.
Scope
Named systems, accounts and windows. Anything else is out of bounds.
Persistence
Only with explicit permission, and removed and verified at close.
Data
Prove reachability, never copy records. Captured material is destroyed on an agreed date.
