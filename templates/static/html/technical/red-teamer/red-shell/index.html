02 Case files

Three operations, three different arguments

Client names withheld. What remains is the technique chain, the detection that fired — or did not — and the change that came out of it.

01

Assumed breach at a regional insurer

Sector · financial services
Window · 6 weeks
Objective · domain admin, policy data

Assumed breachPurple teamOn-site

We started with a single low-privilege account and a laptop on the corporate VLAN, as agreed. Domain admin in 11 days, policy data in 14. The path was unremarkable: an outdated service account, a scheduled task with a writable binary, and a domain controller that accepted an NTLM relay from a print server.

The argument that mattered was the detection timeline. Seven of the eleven steps had a rule that should have fired, and four of those rules were disabled after a noisy deployment in 2023. Restoring them exposed 2,300 events a day that nobody had triaged.

02

An ICS site that could not be scanned

Sector · water utility
Window · 4 weeks
Objective · reach the pump controller

OTPassive firstSafety officer

No active scanning inside the process network, no exploitation of anything that could move a valve, and a hard stop if the safety officer called it. We worked from a compromised engineering workstation image and the site’s own maintenance laptop, both supplied by the client.

We reached the engineering VLAN in nine days and the pump controller in sixteen, using only credentials found in the workstation’s notes and a vendor remote-support tool with a default password. No exploit was used. The safety case was rewritten, and the vendor tool was removed from forty-one sites.

03

Cloud tenant, no malware at all

Sector · SaaS
Window · 5 weeks
Objective · customer data via CI

CloudIdentityNo implants

The client expected an endpoint story. The estate was entirely managed, so we took the identity path instead: a stale personal access token in a public repository, a CI runner that trusted any token from the organisation, and a deployment key with read access to a production bucket.

No implant, no command and control, no file dropped. Every action was an API call from an allowed region. The only detection that fired was a data-transfer alert three days later, triggered by volume rather than by behaviour. The fix was token scope, not more EDR.

03 Method

Four phases, one stop word

Offensive work goes wrong when the operator forgets that the client’s business is running while you test it. The process exists to keep that from happening.

Phase 01

Scope and rules

Targets, exclusions, hours, accounts, escalation contacts, the safety officer’s authority and the stop word. Signed before a single packet moves.

Phase 02

Recon and access

Open-source work, then the foothold the brief allows — a supplied account, a phishing simulation, or an assumed breach. Nothing outside the agreed surface.

Phase 03

Escalate with evidence

Every step logged in a timeline with timestamps, commands and the detection expected at that point. If the blue team calls, we stop and verify together.

Phase 04

Debrief and retest

A joint readout within 48 hours, a prioritised detection backlog, and a retest of the three highest-risk paths once the fixes are live.

“The client wanted a score. What they needed was the four disabled rules and the name of the person who disabled them.” Both went in the report.
