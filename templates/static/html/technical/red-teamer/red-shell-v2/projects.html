The brief

Two years and a substantial budget into a detection programme, the bank’s board asked the only question that matters: if somebody came at us properly, what would we actually see? The CISO wanted an answer with numbers attached — not a maturity score, and not a red team’s highlight reel.

The decisions

Full scope, including the branch-network estate, because a partial scope would have let everyone assume the untested part was fine. The detection team knew the quarter, not the week; the white cell was staffed jointly with the bank’s head of detection as authoriser. Objectives were synthetic tokens planted in agreed locations — no customer record was ever in play.

The outcome

The defenders won the opening: first alert at 38 minutes, inside the working window, and the initial-access class was detected six times out of six. The exercise continued against an alerted team for the full window — which is the honest test — and still found its two gaps. Persistence actions were seen once in six attempts (16.7%): the logging that would have caught them was collected but never forwarded. Exfiltration-class actions were seen twice in five (40.0%): the control watched the front door and not the service accounts. Both were configuration and plumbing problems, not effort problems. Both were fixed within the quarter, and a re-test day confirmed both classes scoring as measured.

Client verdict, quoted with permission: “They told us the best result they could give us was catching them early. Then our team did — and they still found the two gaps we would have sworn were covered. That is the report we paid for.” — Group CISO.

The brief

After a damaging incident at a peer organisation, the group’s board funded a detection team and asked for two things: a baseline they could believe, and a way to prove — not assert — that the money was working. Patient data made the rules of engagement stricter than usual: clinical systems were out of scope entirely, and every objective was a synthetic token on the corporate estate.

The decisions

A purple-team format, with the detection team inside the measurement from day one: after each class of action, the white cell paused the clock and both sides compared notes. The class profile was held as constant as an honest exercise allows across both years, so the two rates compare like with like. Remediation spending between exercises was aimed at the two worst classes first, not spread thin.

The outcome

Exercise I scored 29 of 71 — 40.8%, unobserved: a fair picture of a team that had tooling before it had habits. A year of aimed work later, Exercise II scored 52 of 76 — 68.4%, partial in the upper band, with first alert down from over four hours to 22 minutes. The remaining gap is named plainly in the report: credential-access actions were seen three times in nine (33.3%), an identity-logging problem the group has now funded as its own project. Exercise III is scoped for April 2026, against the same profile.

The brief

The operator wanted detection tested on the corporate estate that sits next to its operational systems — with the operational side excluded absolutely, in writing, at named-system level. The value of the exercise depended entirely on the boundary holding.

The decisions

The exclusion boundary was drawn at named systems, not at a network diagram nobody fully trusted. One stop condition mattered most: if the only forward path from any position required touching an excluded system, the exercise stopped there — no improvisation, no “just checking”. Stop authority sat jointly with the white cell and the client’s duty manager, and either could call it alone.

The outcome

At hour six of day one, the operators reached a position where every forward path crossed the boundary. The white cell called STOP, the exercise closed cleanly, and both sides went to the debrief with a finding nobody had paid for but everybody needed: the corporate and operational estates were adjacent in a way no current document described. The partial measurement still stood — three of eight classes completed, 11 of 14 actions detected (78.6%) — and the scope was redrawn around the true boundary, with a re-run booked against it. A stopped exercise is the rules working. The alternative headline writes itself, and it is not one either party wanted.
