§ 01

The Pack Budget Tool

Arithmetic from Tables P1 + Q1, below

Brochures quote flight time with the whole pack spent. Racing doesn’t work that way: my packs are landed with 20% still in them, because a pack landed empty is a pack retired early. Pick a pack and a quad — the tool does the arithmetic from the printed tables, line by line, and tells you usable time, not brochure time.

Run the numbers

Usable time = usable capacity ÷ average draw. Sessions per pack = usable time ÷ printed session length, rounded down — a heat you can’t finish isn’t a heat. The rotation line is how a race day is actually flown: one pack per heat, the rest on the charger.

Draws are season averages, not peaks — a 5″ race quad peaks far above 34 A and idles far below it; the average is what empties the pack. Cold packs, old packs and headwind all shorten the number. The sheet plans on the average and lands on the reserve.

File 02 — Coaching · Milan Drone Club junior programme

First gates to qualifying pace in ten weeks

A 15-year-old club pilot arrived with a best lap of 58.4 seconds on the club course and a league qualifying standard of 40.0 to reach. Eight coaching days later — line choice before throttle, DVR reviewed after every pack, no new parts bought — her best lap was 38.9 seconds and she qualified 12th at her first league round. The month-by-month sheet is in the full file.

“Dario coached the lap, not the shopping list. In ten weeks our junior went from 58 seconds to qualifying pace, and the only thing he let us buy was more gates.”
