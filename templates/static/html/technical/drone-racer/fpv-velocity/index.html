01 — Build

The numbers that decide a heat

A five-inch race quad is a compromise written in grams. Four numbers set the compromise, and I change only one of them at a time.

8.8:1

Thrust to weight

Below 6:1 the aircraft cannot recover from a mistake at gate three. Above 12:1 the throttle becomes a switch and the line gets sloppy. Eight to nine is where a five-inch race quad actually lives, and every gram you take off moves it up a point.

34%

Hover throttle

The single most useful tuning number. A hover near a quarter stick leaves three quarters of the range for the corner, and it means a heavy gust does not put the aircraft on the ground while you are looking at the next gate.

233 m/s

Prop tip speed

Past about 200 m/s the prop starts to lose efficiency to compressibility and gets loud, which on a race circuit means protest forms. It is also the number that tells me a motor is too hot for the prop.

1.8 min

Pack endurance

A heat is 90 seconds of hard flying. The pack is sized for a heat plus a lap of margin, not for cruising, because a 1,300 mAh pack that lands at 3.5 V per cell is a pack that was the right choice.

03 — Method

How a build gets faster

  1. 01

    Weigh everything, then believe the scale

    Every component goes on a 0.1 g scale before it goes on the frame. A build sheet that says 600 g and a scale that says 618 g is a build sheet that is lying, and the hover throttle will tell you which one to trust.

  2. 02

    One variable per session

    Prop, KV, pack or mass. Never two. Three laps of a known circuit are enough to see whether a change helped; a whole session of mixed changes is a session with no data in it.

  3. 03

    Log the crash, not the lap

    Every DVR file gets a note: which gate, which corner, what the stick was doing. The fastest pilots I know can describe their crashes better than their clean laps.

Builds, tunes and race coaching

I take on a handful of builds a season and coach two or three pilots. If you are moving from a whoop to a five-inch, the first session is about the bench numbers, not the sticks.
