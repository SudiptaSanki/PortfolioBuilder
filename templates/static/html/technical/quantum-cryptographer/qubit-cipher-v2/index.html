Instrument — 01

The Mosca check

If X + Y > Z, you are already late

Michele Mosca’s relation is the whole triage in one line. If the years your data must stay secret (X), plus the years your migration will take (Y), exceed the years until a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (Z), then data recorded today outlives your plan — under that scenario. This is the “harvest now, decrypt later” risk, and it belongs to long-lived data specifically: an adversary who records a 50-year archive’s traffic today can wait; a session key from this morning is not worth their storage. Run your own numbers.

Variable XX

Required secrecy lifetime. How long the data must remain confidential, counted from today — set by retention law, contract, or the sensitivity of the record itself.

Variable YY

Migration duration. How long your organisation realistically needs to inventory, replace and re-issue its cryptography. Measured in years, not in vendor quarters.

Variable ZZ

Time to a cryptographically relevant quantum computer. Unknown. This page refuses to guess it — Table 1 gives three stated scenarios, and the check runs against the one you choose.

The working, line by line

The check is a triage instrument, not the audit. It cannot see your estate — which algorithms sit where, which data classes carry which lifetimes. That is what the inventory is for, and it is why every engagement below starts there.

Reference — 02

What migration costs, in bytes

Sizes as standardised · FIPS 203 / 204 / 205

Migration is not a flag day; it is a size change that ripples through certificates, handshakes, tokens and archives. The standards were finalised in August 2024, so the sizes are no longer moving targets — they are printed here, next to the classical figures they replace.

Read the last three rows against the first seven: a 32-byte key exchange becomes 1,184 bytes at the category most estates will choose. Nothing here is an argument against migrating — it is an argument for planning it, because protocols, MTUs and 1990s certificate stores will meet these numbers whether you plan or not. A case in brief

Full files on the Case Files page

Case File 01 — the firm that had time. A 40-partner law firm arrived expecting to be told to panic. Its inventory said otherwise: most client data carried a 7-to-10-year secrecy lifetime, and only a thin long tail — wills, deeds, trust records — reached past 25. The audit’s conclusion was “you have time — spend it on inventory, not panic”, and the fee was the standard day rate, because that is the rule printed above.
