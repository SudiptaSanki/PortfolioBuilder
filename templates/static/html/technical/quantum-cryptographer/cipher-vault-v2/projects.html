CF-01

The route that lost

QKD LINK STUDY — REGISTER S.02
Client
Retail bank (anonymised)
Sector
Finance
Span
38 km fibre route
Year
2025
Outcome
Not built — advised against

The ask

A bank's infrastructure team had a vendor proposal for QKD between its two data centres, quoting key rates “up to” a figure measured in the vendor's laboratory. Before signing, they commissioned an independent link study: would this route carry this link?

What was done

The route was surveyed and its loss budget measured and itemised. Nothing exotic — fibre, splices, connectors, and the margin any honest budget carries for the repairs the next decade will bring:

Budget lineBasisLoss (dB)
Fibre attenuation38.0 km × 0.20 dB/km7.6
Fusion splices14 × 0.08 dB1.1
Connector pairs4 × 0.30 dB1.2
Patch panels & bend allowancemeasured0.9
Ageing & repair marginpractice standard3.0
Total route loss≈ 4.2% of photons arrive13.8 dB

The outcome

At 4.2% transmittance, the proposed detectors' own dark counts — noise that has nothing to do with any eavesdropper — would have held the QBER at or past the 11.0% abort threshold at the key rate the bank required. The link would have spent its life at the edge of aborting, delivering a fraction of the brochure rate on its good days.

Verdict, as delivered: do not build this link. The same budget funds the post-quantum migration the bank's inventory actually called for — hybrid key exchange on the inter-site VPN within two quarters. The bank funded the migration. The study fee was identical to what an approval would have cost; that is the point of printing it in advance.
CF-02

The forgotten corner

CRYPTOGRAPHIC INVENTORY — REGISTER S.01
Client
Regional electricity utility
Sector
Energy
Scope
214 systems inventoried
Year
2024
Outcome
Exposure remediated

The ask

The board's question was the one every board now asks: “How exposed are we to a quantum adversary?” They expected an answer about their newest systems. The honest answer required writing down all of them.

What was done

214 systems were inventoried — key exchanges, certificates, VPN endpoints, embedded libraries — and classified by data lifetime and exposure. The flagship systems were, as the client hoped, largely hybrid-ready. The material exposure sat somewhere nobody had looked in years: a legacy remote-access path into the operational-technology estate, still negotiating obsolete key exchange, reachable by exactly the harvest-now-decrypt-later route the board was worried about. It is described here at class level, as it was in the report; this practice publishes no exploit detail, ever.

The outcome

The path was retired, its jump host rebuilt with hybrid key exchange, and the inventory became a living register the client's own team now reviews quarterly. No quantum hardware was bought. None was the point.

The lesson the file is kept for: the systems you remember are rarely the problem. An inventory exists for the ones you don't.
CF-03

The link that was built

LINK DESIGN + ACCEPTANCE — REGISTER S.02 / S.05
Client
Payments processor (anonymised)
Sector
Finance
Span
22 km metro pair
Year

Built — accepted

The ask

Key exchange between two data centres, 22 km apart, that an external auditor would sign — with the acceptance criteria written before installation, not negotiated after it.

What was done

The link was designed to its budget (route loss 5.9 dB, inside the envelope where the chosen detectors hold QBER near 2%), installed by the client's integrator, and then measured by this practice over a 30-day acceptance window. The figures below are the acceptance report's, printed next to the numbers they replaced:

The outcome

The link was accepted. Its keys rekey the AES-256 encryptors on the inter-site path — QKD supplies the keys; AES does the carrying — and the acceptance report, brochure figure included, went to the auditors as written.

“Cipher Vault's acceptance report is the first vendor document our auditors accepted without a follow-up question. The measured rate was in it, the brochure rate was in it, and the difference was explained in plain dB.”
