C-19 · 2026

Berlin metro ring, four nodes and one honest kilometre count.

Brief. Connect four sites across Berlin with a quantum key distribution ring, 148 km of installed fibre, and hand the keys to an existing IPsec gateway.

What I did. Measured every span before quoting: the "35 km" link was 41 km once the patch panels were counted, which cost 40% of the key rate. Specified detector characterisation after every maintenance window, and wrote the trust-boundary statement the integrator had left out of the architecture.

Finding. QBER 3.1% at the longest span, with 18 kb/s of sifted key after amplification — enough for the gateway, with margin. The two trusted nodes are documented as single points of compromise, and the customer accepted that in writing rather than pretending otherwise.

C-17 · 2025

Satellite key delivery, for the ninety seconds that matter.

Brief. Use a low-Earth-orbit pass to refresh a ground-segment key that cannot be reached by fibre, and prove the key is fresh at the moment of use.

What I did. Built the QBER budget for a daylight pass, which is where the background counts come from, and set the elevation mask at 30°. Added a key-freshness assertion to the handover API, because the original design could serve a key that was two passes old.

Finding. 4.2 kb/s at peak, 61 s of usable window, QBER 2.4% with the mask at 30°. The freshness bug would have let a compromised ground station replay a previous key; it is now a test in the integration suite.

C-15 · 2025

A bank’s post-quantum migration, counted honestly.

Brief. Audit a bank’s readiness for post-quantum cryptography, with an inventory of every place RSA or elliptic-curve key exchange is still in use.

What I did. Walked the certificate estates, the HSMs and the vendor contracts, which is where the surprises live. Produced a migration order by data lifetime rather than by system criticality: a 25-year mortgage record needs a longer key than a session cookie.

Finding. 38% of systems migrated in six quarters, and a hard blocker in two HSMs that cannot hold ML-KEM keys without a firmware update the vendor has not scheduled. The report named the vendor and the date the answer is needed.

C-14 · 2024

A QBER budget that disagreed with the dashboard.

Brief. A data-centre operator reported 2.1% QBER and wanted a second opinion before signing a service contract. The vendor’s figure was the lower bound of a range with no dark-count term.

What I did. Measured with an independent source and detector set for 90 minutes, then built the budget from the components: dark counts, source imperfections, Raman scattering in the fibre, timing jitter, polarisation drift.

Finding. 4.9% measured against a 4.9% budget. Still under the 11% threshold, so the link works — but the margin the operator thought it had was half the real one, and the dark-count rate was drifting upward by 4% a month.
