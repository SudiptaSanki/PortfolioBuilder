Work · 2021–2026

Six systems, measured on the machine that runs them.

Every number below was measured on the target hardware at the stated resolution. Accuracy without a latency is half a result, and a depth map without a confidence interval is a picture.

6 projects

01

Depth from defocus in the instrument field

A surgical robot needs 2 mm accuracy at 40 cm, and a stereo pair cannot fit on the instrument shaft. We used a single lens with a known aperture and learned the relationship between blur and distance, trained on a rail with 5 cm steps and validated on tissue phantoms.

The interesting failure was specular highlights: a wet surface has no usable blur cue. The final model predicts depth and a confidence value, and the controller ignores the low-confidence band rather than averaging it.

02

Instrument segmentation under cautery smoke

Smoke removes texture exactly where the instrument is, which is exactly where the network needs it. We added a temporal prior from the previous frame and a physics-inspired smoke generator, then trained on 18 hours of theatre video with instrument masks labelled by two clinicians.

IoU on smoke-heavy frames went from 0.77 to 0.88. The gain came from the temporal prior, not from the generator, which was the opposite of what we expected.

03

6-DoF pose from a fingernail-sized marker

A calibration target small enough to sit on a tool, tracked through motion blur and partial occlusion. The network predicts the four corner offsets; the pose comes from a geometric solve, so a wrong pose can be explained by a wrong corner rather than by an opaque regression.

At 2.6° and 1.8 mm RMS it is not the most accurate tracker in the lab, but it is the one that survived a 90-minute procedure without drifting out of frame.

04

Stereo depth for a mobile manipulator

A warehouse robot needed depth at 3 m for grasping on a moving base. Stereo with a learned refinement beat a structured-light sensor in daylight and lost badly on white shelving, which is the kind of trade-off that only shows up when you test both.

We shipped a hybrid: stereo for daylight, active light for the shelving aisle, with a confidence-based switch that the planner can query.

05

Surface defect detection on a coating line

Six cameras, 40 m/min, defects between 0.3 and 3 mm. The classical filter bank was fast but could not separate a drip from a reflection; the network could, but only at 12 frames a second. We ran the filter bank as a proposal stage and the network only on candidates.

Recall went to 0.96 at a false-alarm rate the line operators accepted, which was the real constraint: a system that cries wolf gets switched off in a week.

06

A calibration service nobody has to think about

Every deployed camera drifts. Rather than shipping a rig to each site, we built a calibration routine that uses the robot's own motion: a short scripted sweep, a target the operator already has, and a fit that reports whether the result is good enough to use.

Drift after six hours is 0.07 px with the re-fit, and the routine refuses to certify a calibration it cannot verify, which is more useful than a green tick.
