§3 · ABOUT & METHOD

The person who reads the fine print of numbers.

I came to evaluation from the other side of the bench. My doctorate was in computer vision — detection models, the usual benchmarks, the usual celebrations — and my first job was leading the vision team at an industrial inspection company. We were good at models. What we were not good at, I slowly realised, was knowing how good they were. Our test sets were inherited, our splits were folklore, and our accuracy figures survived contact with exactly one thing: the slide deck.

The turning point was a deployment I have retold, with permission and anonymised, as File EV-01's older sibling: a system that lost fifteen points between our laboratory and the customer's floor, on data nobody had thought to sample. Nothing was fraudulent. Everything was unexamined. I moved sideways into measurement full-time, spent three years building evaluation protocols inside that company, and founded this practice in 2019 to do the same work for other people's systems — with the one advantage an outsider has: no model of mine is ever on the table.

Since then: 37 test sets audited, 214,000 labels re-adjudicated, and a shelf of reports whose conclusions their clients did not always enjoy. That is the job. A measurement practice that only delivers good news is a marketing department with better arithmetic.

§3.1

How a study runs

FIVE STAGES · IN THIS ORDER, FOR A REASON

The order is the method. Agreement is measured before accuracy because a model scored against labels that humans themselves only agree on at κ 0.34 has not been evaluated — it has been compared to a coin with a memory. Provenance comes first because everything downstream inherits the split.

STAGE 1

Provenance first

Every image in the test set is traced: which session, which site, which sensor, which day. Near-duplicates across the split are hunted mechanically and by eye. If the split leaks, the study stops here and says so — cheaply, before anyone has paid for the expensive stages.

STAGE 2

Agreement before accuracy

Two annotators label the evaluation sample independently; κ is computed per class, not just overall, and the disagreements are mapped to the guideline lines that produced them. Broken guidelines are rewritten and re-measured before a single model output is scored.

STAGE 3

The field sample

Benchmarks are re-measured against a sample drawn from the deployment itself — the client's cameras, sites, shifts and seasons. Benchmark figure and field figure are printed side by side, same model, same week. The gap between them is the study's headline, whatever it turns out to be.

STAGE 4

Failures, named

Errors are classified into a taxonomy with counts, built from reading the failures — not from the model's confidence scores. Each class gets an owner: data, sampling, protocol, or model. Most taxonomies I file have the model as the smallest owner.

STAGE 5

The report is the deliverable

A written report in which every figure carries its basis — whose data, which split, what agreement — plus a readout session where I defend the numbers in person. If a figure in my report cannot be recomputed from what is printed in it, the report is not finished.

ALWAYS

Your data stays yours

Studies run on your premises or under NDA on isolated machines. Client images never appear on this site, in talks, or in other clients' reports. The photographs on these pages are stock, captioned as stock, because that is what honesty about images looks like.

§3.2

The honesty page

PRINTED, NOT FOOTNOTED
  • H1

    Leaderboards measure the leaderboard. A public benchmark ranks systems against one fixed, ageing, much-trained-on test set. It tells you who is good at that set. Your deployment is a different set, and nobody has ranked anyone on it yet — that ranking is what a field sample is for.

  • H2

    A demo is a performance, not an evaluation. A demo runs on chosen inputs, in chosen light, with a human nearby to retry quietly. None of that is dishonest — unless the numbers from the demo are later quoted as if they came from a sample. Ask any vendor which of the two you are looking at. Watch the pause.

  • H3

    “94% accurate” is a sentence missing its second half. On what data, drawn from where, split how, labelled by whom, at what agreement? A figure that cannot answer those questions is not a measurement; it is a mood. This site prints the second half for every number it carries, including the ones about itself.

  • H4

    Benchmark and field are different numbers, and both can be true. The scandal is never the gap — every honest system has one. The scandal is publishing the first and letting your customers discover the second. EV-01's vendor did nothing unusual; that is precisely the problem the practice exists for.

  • H5

    I do not sell the model, so I have no reason to flatter it. This practice builds nothing, resells nothing and takes no vendor commissions. An audit that concludes your numbers were right costs the same as one that concludes they weren't — the fee cannot lean toward either answer.
