FILE EV-01

The benchmark that leaked

PROVENANCE AUDIT · 2024
SERVICE
EV-01 · Test-set provenance audit
CLIENT
Logistics automation company
SYSTEM
Palletising vision, 2 sites
STATUS
Closed · launch delayed 11 weeks

THE BRIEF

A deployment-readiness check, six weeks before a two-site launch. The vendor's dossier claimed 97.2% accuracy for pallet and wrap detection, and the client's board had already been shown the number. My job was to confirm it — or to be the person who said otherwise in time.

THE DECISIONS

First decision: refuse the vendor's test set as evidence and trace it instead. The split had been made frame by frame from continuous video, so near-identical frames sat on both sides of it — the model had effectively memorised its exam. Second decision: build the field sample properly — 2,400 frames from the client's own cameras across both sites and all shifts, labelled by two annotators with a third adjudicating (their pre-adjudication agreement, κ 0.41, printed in Exhibit A on the workbook page).

THE OUTCOME

On the field sample the same model scored 81.4%. The gap had two named causes, not one vague one: leakage through the frame-level split, and an unrepresentative sample — one site, one lighting rig, no night shift. The launch moved 11 weeks. The split was rebuilt at session and site level, the vendor retrained against it, and the re-measured field figure — 89.6%, same sampling frame — is the number the board now quotes. Lower, and true.

VENDOR FIGURE97.2% — their split, frame-level, same sessions as training
FIELD FIGURE81.4% — 2,400 frames, client's cameras, 2 sites, all shifts, adjudicated
CAUSES NAMEDSplit leakage (near-duplicates both sides) · unrepresentative sampling (1 site, 1 rig, no nights)
AFTER REBUILD89.6% — same sampling frame, split rebuilt by session and site
FILE EV-02

The guideline was the bug

AGREEMENT STUDY · 2025
SERVICE
EV-02 · Annotation-agreement study
CLIENT
Pressed-metal manufacturer
SYSTEM
Surface-defect labelling programme
STATUS
Closed · κ 0.34 → 0.79

THE BRIEF

A defect-detection programme whose model quality had plateaued, and a management conclusion already reached: the annotators were careless. Three experienced inspectors, labelling surface photographs as defect / no defect, were about to be retrained — one was about to be replaced. I was asked to measure first.

THE DECISIONS

Two annotators, 200 photographs, independent passes: κ 0.34 — the round worked live in §01 of the workbook. But the number was the least useful part. The disagreement map showed the disputes were not scattered; they clustered on one boundary, where the written guideline defined two classes with overlapping words. Decision: change nothing about the people. Rewrite four pages of the guideline with photographed boundary examples, then re-measure the same annotators on fresh material.

THE OUTCOME

Round 2: κ 0.79, same annotators, same task. The retraining was cancelled; nobody was replaced. The programme's labels finally meant one thing, and the model trained on them improved on the strength of a document. The client's Head of Quality said it better than I can — the testimonial on the workbook page is attached to this file.

DISAGREEMENT MAPDisputes clustered on one class boundary — a wording overlap, not carelessness
INTERVENTION4 guideline pages rewritten with boundary examples · 0 annotators retrained or replaced

FILE EV-03

1,900 errors, given names

FAILURE TAXONOMY · 2025
SERVICE
EV-04 · Failure taxonomy & error analysis
CLIENT
Retail technology operator
SYSTEM
Shelf-scanning, 140 stores
STATUS
Closed · taxonomy on quarterly refresh

THE BRIEF

A shelf-scanning system whose field accuracy had drifted for two quarters, and a backlog of 1,900 logged errors nobody had classified. The client's plan was a bigger model. My brief was narrower and ruder: say what the errors actually were before anyone bought anything.

THE DECISIONS

Every error read by a human — me, then checked against a second reader on a 20% overlap (agreement on class assignment: κ 0.81, printed because taxonomies are labels too). Nine classes emerged. The largest was not a vision failure at all: planogram changes that had never reached the training data — the shops had rearranged themselves and the dataset hadn't. Data staleness, filed under the model's name.

THE OUTCOME

The bigger model was not bought. The client's roadmap now runs on the taxonomy: a planogram-change feed into the dataset pipeline (class 1), a lighting protocol for two store formats (class 3), and a quarterly taxonomy refresh that re-counts the classes so drift shows up as a moving count, not a surprise. The classes are printed below — a pile of errors became a document a roadmap can run on.

CLASS 1 · 31%Planogram drift — shelves rearranged, training data not told. A data problem wearing the model's name.
CLASS 2 · 22%Occlusion — shoppers, carts and promo stacks between camera and shelf. A sampling problem.
CLASS 3 · 17%Lighting — two store formats whose fittings sit outside the training distribution. A protocol problem.
CLASSES 4–9 · 30%Packaging redesigns, seasonal stock, genuine model confusions between look-alike products — the only class that was actually the model, and the smallest of the four groups.
