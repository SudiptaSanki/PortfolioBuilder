# Credits & honest replacements — cyber-redteam-hacker

## Persona & content
Owen Hartley is a **fictional** offensive-security consultant, disclosed
as fictional in the footer of every page. The engagements in §1.3 and
§2 are illustrative composites written at **class level** (an
over-privileged service account, a forgotten staging host, a
business-logic flaw) — no real client, system, finding or person is
described, and no exploitation detail, exploit code, payload or
evasion technique appears anywhere in the template. Fees are a
plausible published rate card for the trade (£1,450/tester-day), with
fee bands that multiply out exactly from the durations printed beside
them.

## Honest replacements (stub promises removed, not rebuilt)
The stub was a fake interactive terminal ("CRT Terminal Matrix")
containing invented CVEs, a claimed "automated exploit payload
generator", a "stealth scanner daemon" and ROOT_ACCESS_GRANTED
costume. All of it was slop and all of it was deleted:

- The fake terminal → the **Scope Builder** and **Severity
  Explainer**: working tools that teach the trade's real product —
  scope, authorisation and honest ratings.
- The invented CVE list and exploit tools → case files written at
  class level, the way professional firms describe engagements
  publicly.
- The matrix/terminal theme → a document design system (the
  engagement report), because the anti-Hollywood stance is the
  concept: the report is the product.

## Media — drawing-led, no photographs (deliberate)
No photographs are used. Rationale: the brief bans hooded-hacker
stock clichés, and generic office/server photography would be pure
decoration on a site whose design system is the document itself.
The one figure (Fig 1, about page) is a hand-authored SVG schematic
of the five engagement phases, captioned as a schematic. This is the
program's sanctioned drawing-led route (matte-lumen / ligo-interferometer
precedent), recorded here as required.

## Sound ruling
**Fails the need-test — no audio anywhere.** A terminal beep or
synth would be costume. Verified in code: no `<audio>`, no Web Audio,
no audio files. The ruling is stated in every page footer.
