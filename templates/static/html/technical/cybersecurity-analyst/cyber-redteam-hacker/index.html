Not the films

There are no hoodies on this site, no green rain of code, and nobody types furiously while a progress bar races a firewall. Real offensive security is mostly paperwork, patience and prose: scoping calls, evidence handling, and long hours writing findings up clearly enough that a busy engineer can reproduce and fix them. The glamorous version of this trade is a costume. This site looks like a report because the report is the product — the break-in is just how the evidence is gathered.

        §1.2

        Severity, explained honestly

        Impact × likelihood

        The commonest dishonesty in security reports is inflation: everything rated critical so the report looks dramatic, until the client stops believing any of it. A rating here is impact if exploited, weighed against how likely exploitation actually is. Pick a finding class and read the reasoning — including what would have to change for the rating to move.

        Table 1 — the rating scale used in every report · scrolls sideways on small screens
        Severity is a promise about urgency. If everything is critical, nothing is.
        RatingWhat it meansExpected response
        CriticalExploitable now, by an unskilled attacker, with serious consequences — data, money or control of the system.Fix before anything else. Drop other work.
        HighExploitable with modest effort or a short wait, with major consequences if it happens.Fix in the current cycle — days, not quarters.
        MediumReal, but conditional: it needs another weakness, an insider position, or a victim's mistake to bite.Schedule it. Track it until it closes.
        LowA hardening gap. On its own it gives an attacker nothing, but it removes a layer of defence.Fix opportunistically, with the next change nearby.

        Fees, in print

        Rate card 2026

        Security testing is usually sold behind a “contact us for pricing” wall, which helps nobody plan a budget. The rate is £1,450 per tester-day; engagements are quoted as a fixed fee from the bands below once the scope is written, and the fixed fee does not move because a test took longer than estimated.

        What moves the price: the number of applications or estates in scope, out-of-hours testing windows (+25%), a board-level reporting track alongside the technical report, and travel beyond the North West. What never moves it: how many findings there are. A test that finds nothing costs the same as one that finds twenty things — you are buying the looking, not the loot.
