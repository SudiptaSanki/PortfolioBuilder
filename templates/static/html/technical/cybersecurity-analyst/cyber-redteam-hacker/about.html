§3 — Method & rules

The paperwork is the professionalism.

Anyone can run the tools of this trade; they are mostly free. What you are hiring is judgement inside a frame of written rules — and the discipline to stop when the rules say stop. This page is that frame, published in full, because a practice's rules should be readable before you ever sign them.

§3.1

The consultant

Background

Owen Hartley spent eight years in infrastructure and systems administration before moving into security — a route he still recommends, because you cannot judge how a network fails until you have been paged at 3 a.m. by one that did. Six years of testing followed inside a consultancy, then independent practice in Manchester from 2021: 60+ engagements across logistics, fintech, healthcare, retail and SaaS, from five-day web application tests to eight-week red team exercises.

The work follows published methodology — the OWASP Testing Guide for web applications, recognised network-testing practice for infrastructure — and is covered by professional indemnity insurance, as any engagement contract should require you to check. Testing is done by the person you scoped with. Engagements are never subcontracted without written agreement, and reports are never written by someone who wasn't there.

§3.2

Rules of engagement

Agreed before day one

Every engagement runs under a written rules-of-engagement document, signed by someone with the authority to sign it. It is not boilerplate; it is the difference between a test and an incident. It always covers:

  • Written authorisation. A scope letter naming the systems, addresses and accounts in scope, signed before any testing begins. Verbal permission is not permission. Testers carry the authorisation with them — the traditional “get out of jail” letter — for the whole engagement.
  • Named contacts, both sides. A client contact who knows testing is live and can be reached during it, and a tester contact the client can call at any hour of the test window. If either phone goes unanswered, testing pauses.
  • Stop conditions. The circumstances that end testing immediately: signs of production instability, any touch on a system that turns out to be out of scope, any encounter with real personal data beyond the minimum needed as evidence. Case 03 is these conditions working as designed.
  • Data handling. Evidence is kept to the minimum that proves a finding — a screenshot of access, not a copy of the database. Everything is stored encrypted, transmitted through agreed channels only, retained for twelve months in case of dispute, and then destroyed, with destruction confirmed in writing.
  • Hours and windows. When testing may run, when it may not, and what happens out of hours if a critical finding surfaces (it is reported immediately, not filed for Monday).

The reporting standard

Every finding in every report is held to the same four tests. Reproducible: it carries the evidence a competent engineer on your team needs to see the same thing — because a finding your team cannot reproduce is an opinion. Rated: on the scale in §1.2, with the reasoning shown, including what would change the rating. Paired with a fix: a concrete remediation, sized honestly — “an afternoon of configuration” and “a quarter of re-architecture” are both acceptable answers; “improve security posture” is not. Owned: written for a named reader — the engineer who will fix it, the manager who will schedule it, or the board member who will fund it — with an executive summary that respects all three.

What this practice will not do

  • No unauthorised testing. Not of your competitors, not of your employer, not “just to check”, not as a free sample. There are no exceptions, and being asked twice ends the conversation.
  • No testing of third parties. Your suppliers' systems are theirs. Their access paths into you can be assessed; their networks cannot be touched without their own written consent.
  • No retained data. Evidence is minimised on capture, encrypted at rest, and destroyed on schedule. Findings are never sold, shared, or used to market — your report is yours.
  • No theatre. No findings inflated for drama, no counts padded with trivia, no “critical” that isn't. A short report you can trust beats a long one you can't.

Engagement terms

Fees are fixed in writing after the scoping call and do not rise because testing took longer than estimated. One retest round of fixed findings is included in every fee. Reports are delivered within five working days of the end of testing, encrypted, to named recipients only. The practice carries professional indemnity insurance and works under NDA as standard — your NDA or a mutual one, whichever your legal team prefers. Availability is stated honestly at enquiry: if the diary cannot fit your window, you will be told at the first call, not after the scope is signed.
