Instruments · 2015 to 2026

Six cells that left the lab.

Each entry is a sensor that was built, characterised and handed to somebody who had to make it work in a room I never saw. The measured sensitivity is from the acceptance run, not from the design note.

MEG helmet, 48 channels

A clinical magnetoencephalography helmet replacing a liquid-helium SQUID array. Each sensor is a 12 mm cell with its own heater, laser and three-layer shield can, potted in a 3D-printed frame that fits four head sizes.

The hard part was not sensitivity, it was thermal isolation: 48 heaters at 168 °C inside a shell that must stay under 38 °C at the scalp. The answer was 9 mm of aerogel and a per-sensor thermal cut-out that trips at 42 °C.

2024 · 48 units
4.2 fT/√Hz measured
1.2 kHz bandwidth
Clinical partner, Jena

Gradiometer for ordnance survey

A two-sensor gradiometer on a 0.6 m baseline, towed behind a vehicle at walking pace across land that a coil survey cannot reach. Common-mode rejection of the geomagnetic background is 34 dB, which puts a 250 kg bomb at 5 m well above the noise at 0.1 nT.

Twelve units were built and eleven are still in service. The twelfth was run over by its own tow vehicle, which is a failure mode no datasheet covers.

2021 · 12 units
8 fT/√Hz per head
34 dB CMRR
Survey contractor, NRW

Borehole magnetometer

A 45 mm probe for geomagnetic monitoring in a 120 m borehole, built to survive 40 bar of water pressure and a temperature ramp of 2 °C per minute. The cell sits inside a sapphire tube with a titanium end cap and a ceramic feedthrough.

Two units have run continuously for 410 days each, with a calibration check against a proton precession magnetometer every quarter. The drift over that period is under 40 pT, which is the number the client actually cares about.

2022 · 2 units
0.4 pT/√Hz at 1 Hz
410 days continuous
Geophysical institute

Three-axis satellite study

A design study for a CubeSat payload: three orthogonal cells in a 28 mm cube, with a single laser split three ways and a shared heater. The flight model was never built, but the bench unit produced the best cell of the programme and a thermal design that two other projects have since borrowed.

2023 · study only
0.9 fT/√Hz single axis
28 mm cube
ESA-funded study

Transfer standard, 2 units

A rack instrument for calibrating other magnetometers against a known field, with a Helmholtz coil set and a cell that never leaves its shield. Two were built for a national metrology institute; the requirement was not sensitivity but repeatability, and the units agree to 12 fT over a year.

2020 · 2 units
12 fT unit-to-unit
Helmholtz coil set
Metrology institute

Cell 178, lab record

The best cell the lab has made: an 8 mm quartz cube with an OTS coating, sealed at 45 torr of neon, measuring 0.9 fT/√Hz on the acceptance bench and still holding after 500 thermal cycles. It lives in the transfer standard and is not available for loan.

2024 · lab record
0.9 fT/√Hz
500 cycles, no drift
Retained, Jena

Sensitivities are acceptance measurements at 10 Hz, single axis, in the three-layer shield. Field performance is always worse, usually by a factor of two to five.

What the next instrument needs

A field range, a bandwidth, an environment and a size. If the environment includes a moving vehicle or a human head, that constraint will decide the design long before the sensitivity does.
