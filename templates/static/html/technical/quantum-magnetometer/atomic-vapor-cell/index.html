01 — Physics

Four things the cell has to do at once

A SERF magnetometer works by making the atoms forget their own spin-exchange collisions. That happens at high density and low field, which are two conditions that fight everything else in the design.

Hold density without broadening

More atoms means a bigger signal, but spin-exchange collisions broaden the resonance. The SERF regime buys the density back by making those collisions spin-conserving, and it only works below about 10 µT and above about 10¹² atoms per cubic centimetre.

10¹² cm⁻³

Keep the wall out of the wall

Every atom that touches the glass loses its spin. A paraffin or antirelaxation coating keeps a fraction of them alive for a few hundred collisions, which is the difference between a coherence time of 1 ms and 10 ms.

10 ms

Pump hard, detect gently

The pump laser polarises the ensemble; the probe reads its rotation. Pump power that saturates the transition heats and broadens the sample, so the optimum is a compromise measured on the bench, not calculated on paper.

60 mW

Shield the field, then measure it

The sensor measures a field in the femtotesla range inside a room holding a 50 µT field. A three-layer mu-metal shield with a ferrite inner liner gets 60 dB; the rest of the job is done by not moving anything made of iron.

60 dB

02 — Cell

What is inside the glass

A 10 mm cube of borosilicate, filled at 60 torr of neon, with a droplet of rubidium that has been distilled three times and a coating that is applied in a rotating oven over nine hours.

03 — Field

Where these sensors go

Three applications pay for the work: brain fields, buried ordnance and geological anomaly mapping. Each one asks a different question of the same glass cell.

10 fT

Magnetoencephalography

Neural currents produce fields of 10 to 1,000 fT at the scalp. A helmet of 48 sensors can resolve them without liquid helium, which means a patient can move their head during a scan for the first time.

0.1 nT

Unexploded ordnance

A 250 kg bomb at 5 m looks like a 0.1 nT anomaly against the geomagnetic background. Gradiometer pairs reject the background and leave the target, at walking pace, over ground that a coil survey cannot reach.

1 pT

Geophysical mapping

Airborne surveys resolve crustal magnetisation at the picotesla level. A sensor with 1 fT/√Hz sensitivity and a 1 kHz bandwidth lets the aircraft fly faster over the same ground, which is the whole economics of a survey.

Bring a field, a bandwidth and a budget

I build three or four sensors a year and take one development programme at a time. If you need 100 identical units, the useful conversation is about who manufactures the cell, not about who designs it.
