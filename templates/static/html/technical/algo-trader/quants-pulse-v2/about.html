Practice · The developer

A backtest is a hypothesis with a chart.

I am Dr Piotr Nowak. I trained in computational physics, spent six years building connectivity and execution tooling inside trading firms, and have run this practice from London since 2016. The doctorate is in simulation; the trade is scepticism, applied to other people's charts — and then, fairly, to my own.

Firms hire me at three moments: before capital follows a backtest, when an execution stack needs to be measured rather than admired, and before a strategy goes live without a control layer. The common thread is that somebody senior wants the numbers checked by a person with no stake in the answer. My fee does not change with the conclusion. That independence is, in the end, the product.

Show me your fill assumptions and I will tell you your Sharpe before I see a single return. It is not a parlour trick — it is the whole discipline. Returns are the last thing I look at, because they are the easiest thing to fabricate by accident.

How an engagement runs

Four stages, in this order

Stage 1

Freeze the data

Everything the work touches is snapshotted, versioned and made point-in-time: every record carries the moment it became knowable. No silent restatements, ever — if the data changes mid-engagement, the sheet says so.

Stage 2

Model the costs

Fees, spread and slippage are written down as a model before any code is reviewed or written — the same sheet as Worksheet C-1, with your venue's figures. Every later claim is reconciled against it.

Stage 3

Walk forward

Validation moves through time in one direction: fit on the past, test on the next slice, roll forward. Parameters are never tuned on the data that judges them. A result that only exists in-sample is reported as exactly that.

Stage 4

Reconcile daily

Positions, fills and cash are checked three ways, from independent sources, every day the system runs. A break stops the next session until a human signs the sheet. Boring is the design goal.

The honesty page

The three ways a backtest lies

None of these requires a dishonest researcher. All three happen by accident, in good firms, to careful people — which is why the counter-measures are procedural, not personal.

1 · Overfitting

Test enough parameter combinations against the same history and some will shine by pure chance. The backtest is then a portrait of the noise, tuned until it flatters. The more beautiful the curve, the more questions it should attract.

The counter-measureEvery tried variant is logged, not just the winner; the final test set is touched once; results are reported with the count of trials beside them.

2 · Look-ahead

The system quietly uses information before the market could have had it: results joined by period instead of publication, revised figures standing in for first prints, today's index members projected into yesterday's universe. File 01 on the Engagements sheet is this lie, caught.

The counter-measurePoint-in-time data as an engineering requirement — every record timestamped by when it became knowable, and the join audited like code.

3 · Survivorship

The test universe contains only the instruments that still exist. The delisted, the suspended and the bankrupt — often the ones the strategy would have held — have vanished from the data, and with them, their losses.

The counter-measureUniverses rebuilt as they stood on each historical date, dead instruments included, with their final prices carried honestly to the end.

The market charges for every assumption. A cost you refuse to model is not a cost you avoid — it is a cost you meet later, in production, at market prices. That sentence is the closest thing this practice has to a motto, and it is printed on the first page of every audit report I sign.

Engagement terms — the short version

Terms, in plain lines

  • ScopeEngineering services to firms: audits, builds, models, control layers. Engagements are scoped in writing before work starts.
  • Not offeredNo investment advice, no portfolio or fund management, no signals, no strategy sales, no retail work of any kind — at any price.
  • FeesDay rates and fixed fees as printed on Sheet 01. The fee never depends on the conclusion of an audit or the performance of a system.
  • DataClient data stays on client infrastructure wherever possible; snapshots taken for an audit are returned or destroyed at sign-off, with the destruction noted on the sheet.
  • FindingsAudit findings belong to the client. Method-level write-ups (like the files on Sheet 03) are published only with identifying details removed.
  • AvailabilityOne engagement at a time for audits; builds may run alongside a validation programme. Current booking status is in the services register on Sheet 01.
