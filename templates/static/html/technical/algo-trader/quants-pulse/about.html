Method

The edge is small, so the plumbing has to be exact.

A systematic book with a 2.1 gross Sharpe and a 14 basis point edge per round trip does not survive a sloppy execution layer. Here is how the plumbing is built.

Research, and what counts as a result

Every signal starts as a hypothesis about who is on the other side and why they would keep being wrong. That sentence matters more than the backtest: a pattern with no counterparty is usually a data artefact, and a pattern with an obvious counterparty is usually already crowded. I keep a log of both, because the crowded ones are useful for knowing when to leave.

A candidate has to survive four tests before it trades: out-of-sample on three years it has never seen, a cost model at the 75th percentile of measured slippage, a capacity estimate at one-third of gross alpha, and a drawdown that the risk budget can absorb without cutting size at the bottom. Most ideas fail at the second test, which is the cheapest place to fail.

Cost is a first-class input, not a haircut

The backtest and the fill log are the same document here. Every simulated trade carries the slippage it would have paid at the time, using the model fitted to that quarter's fills. If a strategy is profitable only in the first hour of the session, it is tested with the wider spread and the thinner book that hour actually has.

The cost model itself is deliberately plain: half the quoted spread, impact growing with volatility and the square root of size relative to volume, and a timing term from the decay of the signal. Three parameters, refitted quarterly, with the out-of-sample error published next to the in-sample one.

Capacity and the size that is actually safe

Capacity is where impact consumes a third of gross alpha. Trading at that size is technically possible and practically unwise, so the book runs at roughly half of it, and the portfolio is capped at a third of the sum of the individual capacities. The gap between those two numbers is the price of three strategies becoming one trade in a crisis.

Position limits are per instrument and per strategy, and they are checked before the order is sized, not after. A limit that can be overridden in a hurry is not a limit.

Risk, in one paragraph

Volatility-targeted at 8 % annualised per strategy, with a hard stop at a 12 % drawdown that halves size and a 20 % drawdown that stops the book for a week. Correlations are measured on a rolling 60-day window and the portfolio is rebalanced when the first principal component explains more than 55 % of the variance. In practice that happens about twice a year, and it is always uncomfortable.

What I do not do

  • Manage client money. The book is proprietary, and the disclosures on this site are not a solicitation.
  • Publish live signals. The research notes are method, not instructions.
  • Trade on news. The models are price-based, and the one time I tested an event overlay the slippage in the first minute ate the entire edge.
  • Promise a Sharpe. Three years is a sample, not a property.

Why a newspaper

Because a research note is a piece of writing, and the format forces an argument: a headline, a claim, a number and its caveat. The desk does not need another dashboard; it needs a page where the reasoning is visible and the number can be checked.

Stack

Python for research, C++ for the order gateway, a time-series store for ticks and a flat-file log for fills. Nothing clever: the clever part is that the fill log and the backtest share a schema.

Reading

Almgren & Chriss on optimal execution, Kyle on market microstructure, and the CME's daily volume files, which are duller than either and more useful.
