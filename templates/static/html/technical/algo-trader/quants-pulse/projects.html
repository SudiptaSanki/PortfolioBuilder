Research

Seven notes on what fills actually cost.

Written from the fill log of a small book, which is the only advantage a small book has: every trade is measured, and none of it is a rounded number from a vendor deck.

7 notes

N-01

Square-root impact, measured on 41,000 fills

Impact against size relative to volume, fitted per quarter since 2024. The exponent sits between 0.44 and 0.58 depending on the regime, which is close enough to the textbook square root to use it and far enough to matter when sizing a large order.

The residual is not noise: it correlates with the five-minute return after the order starts, which means the model is missing the part where my own signal predicts the move.

41,000 fills · 11 quarters
exponent 0.44–0.58
out-of-sample error 0.8 bps
N-02

Participation is a risk choice, not a speed setting

Going from 15 % to 5 % participation cuts impact by about a third and doubles the exposure time. On a signal with a two-hour half-life, that trade is roughly neutral; on a signal with a twenty-minute half-life, it loses money. The schedule should be derived from the alpha decay, not set by habit.

impact −34 % at 5 %
timing +2.1 bps
break-even half-life 1.4 h
N-03

Capacity collapses when the correlations rise

Three strategies with a 0.2 average pairwise correlation have a combined capacity close to the sum of their parts. At 0.6 it is about half. In March 2025 the correlation went to 0.8 for a week and the safe size was a quarter of the model's estimate, which is why the portfolio cap is one third and not one half.

ρ 0.2 → 0.9 × sum
ρ 0.6 → 0.5 × sum
ρ 0.8 → 0.25 × sum
N-04

The closing auction is not free liquidity

For a book that must be flat overnight, the closing auction looks like an answer: deep, single price, no queue. On ES the auction is thin relative to the continuous session, and the imbalance published in the last ten minutes is a signal other people trade against. My measured cost there is 1.8 bps, against 2.4 bps in the last half hour — better, but not the discount people claim.

auction 1.8 bps
last 30 min 2.4 bps
sample: 213 closes
N-05

Drawdown control that does not sell the bottom

Halving size at a 12 % drawdown and flattening at 20 % is crude, and it works: the recovery time after a stop is shorter than the time spent in the drawdown it avoids. The rule is tested on the 2024 and 2025 episodes and on synthetic paths with the same volatility, and it is the only risk rule I have never been tempted to override at 2 a.m.

max DD −5.1 % portfolio
stop triggered 3 times
avg recovery 19 days
N-06

Cleaning the tape without deleting the signal

Exchange tick data has bad prints: crossed quotes, zero-volume updates, timestamps out of order. The naive filter removes 0.4 % of rows and, unfortunately, most of the fast moves. I keep the bad prints, flag them, and let the impact model learn from them, because the market did trade there.

0.4 % flagged, not dropped
3 years of ticks
storage 1.1 TB
N-07

Backtest and fill log, one schema

The single most useful engineering decision on this desk: simulated fills and real fills are rows in the same table, with a flag for which is which. Slippage analysis is a query, the cost model trains on both, and a research result that cannot be joined to reality is visible immediately.

one table, 2.1m rows
query time 400 ms
schema shared since 2024
