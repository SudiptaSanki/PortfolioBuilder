Dr. Nadia Rahman — the short version

PhD in natural language processing (2016), then six years inside product companies — search quality at a marketplace, then ML platform lead at a logistics firm — before going independent in 2021. The through-line of the employed years: I kept being the person asked, three weeks before launch, "how good is it, actually?" and discovering nobody could answer. This practice exists so the answer exists.

I am an engineer, not a prompt stylist. The deliverables are systems: a retrieval layer with a version-of-record register, an eval harness your team can run without me, a cost model reconciled to invoices, and a gate report that has, on four occasions, said stop — including once to a client who had already printed the launch announcement. Three of the four shipped later, better. The fourth is the engagement I am proudest of.

My one professional opinion, stated plainly: most LLM projects do not fail because the model is weak. They fail because nobody wrote down what "correct" means before the demo made everyone feel finished. A prompt is a component. An eval set is an asset. Companies keep polishing the component and neglecting the asset.

§ 01

How an engagement runs

Four stages, in this order, always

  1. Define "correct" before touching a model

    We sample your real traffic — tickets, documents, queries — and build the frozen eval set with your own domain experts labelling it. Thresholds are agreed in writing at this stage, when passing is still hypothetical and honesty is cheap.

  2. Measure the demo you already have

    Your existing prototype runs the frozen set. Most clients meet their failure taxonomy here: retrieval misses, stale sources, format drift, silent truncation — counted, not vibes. This report is yours even if we stop here; several clients have.

  3. Build against the taxonomy

    Engineering effort goes where the failures are — which is why corpus work so often outranks model work in my budgets. Model choice happens here, by eval score per euro, and is revisited whenever a new model earns a re-run.

  4. The gate, then the cost model, then launch

    The frozen set is re-run untouched. Pass, and we finalise the cost model — per-request arithmetic from the printed price table, reconciled after the first production month. Fail, and the report says so, with the fix list ordered by effect. The gate is not a ceremony; it has stopped launches, and it will stop yours if the numbers say so.

§ 02

The honesty page

Printed here so you can hold me to it

  • Prompts are not IP and not a system. A clever prompt is a paragraph. It will leak, drift and be obsolete by the next model release. Your eval set, your labelled data and your failure taxonomy are the assets — I build those, and they belong to you.
  • A vendor benchmark is not your workload. A model that tops a public leaderboard on exam questions may still fail your customers' spelling, your documents' tables, your language mix. The only benchmark that counts is the frozen one built from your traffic.
  • "The demo worked" is the failure mode. Demos are run on questions chosen by hope. Every claim on this site carries its eval basis for the same reason: unmeasured confidence is how launches die.
  • Guardrails are engineering, not magic words. Telling a model "never reveal X" is a request, not a control. Real guardrails are output validation, retrieval scoping, permission boundaries and logging — built, tested, and red-teamed at the level your risk actually requires.
  • Fine-tuning is the last resort, not the first line. It is expensive to do, expensive to redo, and usually proposed to fix problems that are actually retrieval or data problems. Zero fine-tunes sold before an eval existed — that scoreboard line is a policy, not a boast.
  • I will not write your accuracy claim. If marketing needs a number, we measure one — on your data, with the method printed next to it. If the honest number is 87%, the honest number ships.
  • This site's console has no model behind it, and says so in its own boot text. A practice that sells "don't fake AI" should not open with fake AI.
§ 03

Failure taxonomy — the field guide I work from

Counted in every gate report

Table 2 — The six failure classes, in the order they usually dominate
ClassWhat it looks likeThe usual fix
Retrieval missThe right document exists; the answer never saw itChunking, metadata, hybrid search — corpus work, not model work
Stale or conflicting sourceThe answer faithfully quotes a superseded policyVersion-of-record register, deduplication, source dating
Ungrounded generationFluent answer, no retrieved supportCitation-required output, abstention threshold, escalation rule
TruncationThe relevant passage fell outside the context budgetContext budgeting, reranking, section-aware chunking
Format driftDownstream systems reject the output shapeSchema validation with repair loop and a counted failure budget
Escalation failureThe system answers what a human should have handledExplicit escalation classes in the eval set, reviewed by your leads

In Case 01, the first two classes were 68% of all failures. In most engagements I have measured, the model itself — the thing everyone wants to discuss — is under a fifth of the problem.

§ 04

Engagement terms

  • Fixed written quote after a scoping call; the quote names the eval depth and integration surface it assumes, so any growth is visible and agreed, never quiet.
  • Your data stays yours. Eval sets, labelled data, harnesses and reports are your property. Client traffic used for eval construction is processed under your DPA, never retained after the engagement, never used for another client.
  • The gate report is a deliverable, including when it says stop. You may show it to your board, your auditors, or your next engineer.
  • Capacity is real: three concurrent engagements maximum. The register shows the queue honestly; a waitlist place is a place, not a sales technique.
  • What I don't do: growth-hack chatbots, engagement-bait generation, undisclosed automated outreach, or evaluations designed to be passed. If the engagement needs a number to come out a particular way, I am the wrong engineer.
  • Cancellation: either side, in writing, with work-to-date invoiced and all artefacts handed over. The eval set leaves with you.
