What moves the price

Eval depth first: how many failure classes you want counted — and labelling real traffic costs real hours. Integration surface second: how many of your systems the feature must touch, authenticate against, and fail safely around.

Model choice barely moves it. Swapping frontier for mid is an afternoon; discovering at launch that nobody defined "correct" is a quarter.

CASE 01

The copilot that failed its own launch gate

Gate held · Feb 2026 Shipped · Apr 2026

The ask

A B2B SaaS company had a support copilot that demoed beautifully and a launch date six weeks out. They hired me to "do the final integration." There was no eval set. There is always an eval set by the time I leave; the only question is whether we build it before or after the launch.

What I did

Built a frozen eval set from 412 real resolved tickets, labelled by two of their support leads. Set the gate in writing: groundedness ≥ 92%, correct escalation ≥ 95%. First full run: 84.6% and 91.2%. The gate held. The failure taxonomy said why — 41% of failures were retrieval misses, not model errors.

The outcome

Launch moved six weeks. We re-chunked the policy corpus by section, required citations, fixed the escalation rule. Same frozen set, re-measured 27 March 2026: 93.8% grounded, 96.4% escalation. Shipped 2 April. The delay cost less than one bad launch week would have.

"We were two weeks from launching a copilot our own agents didn't trust. Nadia's gate stopped it, showed us the 41% retrieval figure, and six weeks later the same eval set passed it. I have the stop report framed. Nobody frames a launch announcement."

— Jonas Weber, Head of Customer Support, Halden Mobility GmbH · on Case 01, the support copilot
