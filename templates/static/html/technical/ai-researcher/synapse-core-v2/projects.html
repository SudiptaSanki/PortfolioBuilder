CH·02 · CASE FILES

Three files, told with their bases on.

A win, a refusal and a ruler. Each file is told the way it appears in the client report: the ask, the decisions, the outcome — with the workload and the measurement basis in the tombstone, where they belong. Client names are withheld under NDA; roles, workloads and numbers are not.

FILE 01

The aisle that mostly sleeps

SHIPPED · 2024
WORKLOAD
Event-camera aisle counting; 20,000 neurons at 6 Hz mean rate, night-weighted
HARDWARE
NM-A2 mixed-signal board (Table H1)
BASIS
Harness H-3, system-effective, sensor + host included
OUTCOME
80.7× less energy than the duty-cycled conventional module

THE ASK

A logistics-automation OEM needed battery-powered nodes that count people and forklifts in warehouse aisles — no mains, no cabling, and a twelve-month service interval or the product did not close. Their first prototype, a camera plus a conventional accelerator, was measuring its battery life in weeks.

THE DECISIONS

We recorded the aisles for two weeks before choosing anything. The data was the decision: long empty nights, a burst at the 06:00 shift change, almost nothing in between — a mean network rate of 6 Hz, which is sparsity you can build on. We specified an event camera and the NM-A2 board, and priced the conventional alternative honestly, duty-cycled at 6.2 mW average, so the client saw the real comparison rather than a strawman.

THE OUTCOME

On the stated workload the spiking node drew 76.8 µW average against the baseline's 372 mJ per minute — 80.7× less energy, the default configuration of the spike budget on the bench-log page. The nodes shipped and hold their service interval. When the client's second site proved busier — rate up, sparsity gone — the same arithmetic said the win shrank to under 3×, and they shipped the conventional module there, on our numbers.

“Lucia measured first and argued later. The spiking node ran a picking aisle for a year inside the power budget she printed in week two — and when our second site turned out to be too busy for the same trick, she told us to buy the conventional module, and showed us her own numbers for why.”

FILE 02

The benchmark that said no

CONVENTIONAL SHIPPED · 2025
WORKLOAD
Always-on keyword spotting; 16 kHz audio, speech present ~30% of the day
HARDWARE
NM-D1 digital board vs conventional audio NPU
BASIS
Harness H-3, both systems measured, same front-end
OUTCOME
Ratio 0.94× — parity, inside measurement noise. Client shipped the NPU

THE ASK

A consumer-audio company wanted a spiking keyword spotter, largely because “neuromorphic” reads well in a launch deck. They asked for a design. I sold them a benchmark instead — one week, fixed fee, both architectures on the same harness.

THE DECISIONS

The workload definition did the damage, as it usually does. Always-on audio is not sparse: the microphone delivers a dense stream whether or not anyone is speaking, and with speech present a third of the day the network's effective firing rates sat exactly where the event-driven advantage goes to die. We measured both systems anyway — a refusal should be a measurement, not an opinion.

THE OUTCOME

The spiking configuration measured 0.94× the conventional NPU's energy — parity, slightly the wrong side of it, well inside what a different Tuesday would reverse. The report's recommendation was one sentence: ship the conventional part. They did. The engagement was billed in full, and the report is quoted in their design review — which is what a benchmark is for.

FILE 03

A ruler other people can use

PUBLISHED OPENLY · 2023–2025
WORKLOAD
Six reference workloads, W-1 to W-6, definitions published in full
HARDWARE
All three Table H1 classes, plus conventional baselines
BASIS
Harness H-3 itself is the deliverable; protocol versioned
OUTCOME
Adopted by the consortium's three labs; two vendor claims re-measured, one survived

THE ASK

A university research consortium had three labs quoting three incomparable energy claims for similar systems — every group measuring on its own harness, with its own unstated basis. They did not need another result. They needed the results to be about the same thing.

THE DECISIONS

We built the deliverable as an instrument, not a paper: harness H-3 with a written protocol — what counts as a spike, what is inside the measurement boundary, how the baseline is duty-cycled — and six workload definitions, W-1 to W-6, published with their data. Every choice that could flatter a number is documented in the protocol, because a ruler with a thumb on it is worse than no ruler.

THE OUTCOME

All three labs adopted the harness; the workload definitions are the part other groups cite. Two vendor claims were re-measured under it: one survived intact, one did not, and both outcomes are in the consortium's public report. The spike budget on the bench-log page is this harness's arithmetic, with its training wheels left on where you can see them.
