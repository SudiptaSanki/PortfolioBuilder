Thermal photons set the floor

Quasiparticle and photon excitation scale with n̄ = 1/(ehf/kT − 1). At 5 GHz and 15 mK that is 1.1 × 10⁻⁷; at 100 mK it is 0.098, and at the still it is 1.6. A single photon in the resonator is enough to shift the qubit frequency through the dispersive term, which is why the cold attenuators are not optional.

Loss is a participation problem

T1 from dielectric loss falls as 1/(2πf tanδ p), where p is the fraction of the electric energy sitting in the lossy material. Sapphire at 10 mK carries tanδ near 1 × 10⁻⁶; a contaminated silicon surface can be thirty times worse. Most of my fabrication weeks go into that number, not into the junction.

Echo coherence is not twice T1

A Hahn echo cancels slow frequency drift, not relaxation. T2 echo approaches 2T1 only when dephasing is negligible; with residual flux noise or a thermally populated resonator it sits well below. Reporting 2T1 as an expectation is how a chip passes review and fails in the fridge.
