Plates and the measurements that decided them.

Six pieces of hardware from the last four years. Each entry gives the problem as it arrived, the change I made, and the number that came back from the fridge — including the plates that only half worked.

Showing 6 of 6 · measurements taken in Oslo unless noted

P-09

Egeria-9, a 24-qubit tile

Fabrication · 2026

The problem. A 16-qubit chip measured well but lost 40% of its median T1 once the readout plane was added. The suspicion was the resonator metal, not the junctions, because the loss appeared only after the second metallisation.

What changed. I moved the resonators onto a separate niobium layer with a 300 nm silicon interlayer, kept the junction area fixed, and rerouted the flux return so no line crossed under a resonator. The tile grew by 1.1 mm, which cost one row of dies per wafer.

What came back. Median T1 rose from 186 to 318 µs across the 24 qubits, and the spread narrowed from 4.1× to 1.9×. The best pair reached 99.41% CZ; the worst functional pair sat at 97.8%, which is the number the compiler will actually see.

P-08

Tantalum re-entrant resonators

Process · 2025

The problem. Niobium resonators on sapphire showed a loss tangent near 3 × 10⁻⁶ independent of temperature below 200 mK. That is substrate-surface loss, and it capped T1 at roughly 180 µs no matter how clean the junctions were.

What changed. We replaced the resonator metal with α-tantalum deposited on a 60 nm niobium seed, then etched a re-entrant post to pull the electric energy away from the sapphire interface. Three process splits, one cooldown each, same junction wafer.

What came back. The fitted loss tangent fell to 6 × 10⁻⁷ and median T1 went to 340 µs. The gain was not uniform: qubits near the tile edge improved by 2.4×, while the four centre qubits barely moved, which we still cannot fully explain.

P-07

Flux-line crosstalk map

Control · 2025

The problem. Z-pulse calibration needed a 6 × 6 correction matrix, and the nearest-neighbour terms were as large as 6.2%. Every calibration run leaked into its neighbours, so a sweep of 24 qubits took eleven hours instead of ninety minutes.

What changed. The bias lines shared a single return trace that meandered between pockets. I stitched that return to the ground plane every second pocket, widened it from 8 to 22 µm, and moved the bias tee’s capacitor to the 100 mK plate where the dielectric loss matters less.

What came back. Nearest-neighbour crosstalk fell to 0.8% and the calibration routine dropped to a diagonal correction plus one neighbour term. Total sweep time went to 2 h 10 min. The change also removed a 40 kHz oscillation that had been blamed on the fridge.

P-06

Amplifier chain with a travelling-wave parametric amp

Readout · 2024

The problem. Single-shot readout fidelity stalled at 96.1% because the HEMT at 4 K set a 8.4 K noise temperature on a signal that had already lost 3 dB in the isolator. Integration time could not go below 600 ns without the discrimination overlapping.

What changed. I installed a travelling-wave parametric amplifier at the 20 mK plate, before the isolator, with a 6 GHz pump and a 24 dB directional coupler. The pump line needed its own IR filter and a 1.2 m coax stub to keep the pump from reaching the qubits.

What came back. System noise dropped to 3.1 K, readout fidelity reached 99.2% in 280 ns, and the measured qubit T1 did not move — but the pump did add 0.7% to the CZ error until the stub length was tuned. That trade is in the report; it is not free.

P-05

Indium bump yield at 120 µm pitch

Packaging · 2023

The problem. After flip-chip assembly, 21% of readout lines were open. The pattern was not random: every failed line sat in a corner, and the failures moved when the die was rotated in the pocket.

What changed. The interposer was bowing. I added two dummy bump rows at the tile edge to act as mechanical stops, increased the bond force from 18 to 26 N per die with a 3 s dwell, and reflowed at 156 °C instead of 170 °C to keep the indium from dissolving into the gold pads.

What came back. Yield went to 98.4%. The two remaining opens were traced to a scratched landing pad and are now caught by an automated optical check before assembly. Bond height varies by 1.1 µm across the die, which is acceptable but leaves little margin for a larger tile.

P-04

Bias filtering at the cold plate

Cryogenics · 2023

The problem. Four qubits on the same tile showed a shared 120 kHz oscillation in their Ramsey fringes. It survived a change of fridge and disappeared when the room-temperature electronics were battery-powered, which put the noise on the bias wiring.

What changed. I moved the bias tee from room temperature to the 100 mK plate and put a 1 nF capacitor in shunt, with a 10 kΩ resistor in series to keep the DC path. The filter housing is a machined copper box, bolted at four points to the plate.

What came back. The oscillation disappeared and the echo coherence of the four qubits rose from 41 to 96 µs. The filter adds 0.4 nW of heat at the 100 mK plate, which the fridge absorbs without changing base temperature. This is now on every bias line.
