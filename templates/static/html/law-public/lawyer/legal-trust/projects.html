Section II · Recent matters

Matters, described the way
we are allowed to describe them.

A solicitor's files are confidential, so these are not case studies in the marketing sense. Each is a real shape of work, anonymised and composited, told at the level of process: what we were asked to do, the decisions taken about how to run it, and whether it finished inside the timescale and fee basis printed on the home page. No legal detail, no outcomes promised — the stages are the story.

An estate, administered in the order it was promised

MATTER 01 · PROBATE

The brief

An executor — the deceased's wife — instructed the practice after a death in the family. The estate held a house, several bank accounts and a share portfolio; some paperwork was missing, and one account only came to light part-way through. She asked for the whole administration to be taken off her desk.

The decisions

At the first meeting the two probate services were laid side by side: grant-only at £850 fixed, or full administration at the hourly rate with a written estimate. She chose full administration. The estimate was given in writing before any hourly work began, the stages were printed on the engagement letter, and every bill that followed was itemised in six-minute units against them.

The outcome

The grant issued, the house was sold by the estate, and the final account was approved and the estate distributed in month seven — inside the printed 6–9 month band for full administration. The final bill matched the written estimate; the testimonial on the home page is this client's, given with permission.

A four-link chain, held together by early paperwork

MATTER 02 · CONVEYANCING

The brief

A couple selling their house and buying another, in a chain of four linked transactions. Chains fail at their slowest link, and the usual cause is paperwork that starts late: identity checks undone, title documents unrequested, forms half-completed.

The decisions

The unglamorous decision was to front-load everything. Identity and source-of-funds checks were completed at instruction, not at exchange; the title and the seller's protocol forms were chased in the first week; searches on the purchase were ordered as soon as the contract pack arrived rather than once the mortgage offer landed. Each Friday the clients received a one-page position note: where their two files stood, and whose desk the chain was waiting on.

The outcome

Both files were exchange-ready ahead of the rest of the chain, and contracts exchanged on the date the chain had agreed; completion followed the same week. Total elapsed time: ten weeks, inside the printed 8–12 week band, at the printed fixed fees for the sale and the purchase band.

Twelve contracts a year, read before they were signed

MATTER 03 · SMALL BUSINESS

The brief

A small retail business with a dozen supplier and service contracts a year, previously signed unread because each one felt too small to justify a solicitor's bill — until a renewal clause nobody had noticed cost more than a year of reviews would have.

The decisions

A standing arrangement instead of a series of panics: every contract over a set value comes to the practice before signature, is read against a short checklist agreed with the owners — term, renewal, price changes, exit — and is returned within five working days with the clauses that matter flagged in plain English and the ones that don't left alone. The fee for each review is quoted before the reading starts, so the owners can decide whether a small contract earns one.

The outcome

The arrangement has run for two years. Every contract in that period was reviewed inside the five-day undertaking; terms the owners would not have accepted were identified before signature rather than after, and two contracts were renegotiated or declined on the strength of the review. What the clauses said is the client's business; that they were read in time is ours.

What these three have in common

Nothing dramatic — which is the point. In each matter the fee basis was fixed or estimated in writing before the work, the stages were the printed ones, and the finish landed inside the printed band.
