Section III · The solicitor

Clare Ashworth

Clare Ashworth qualified as a solicitor in 2009, training at a regional firm in the North West before spending seven years in the private-client department of a larger practice — wills, estates and conveyancing, in volume. In 2016 she opened this practice on Northgate Street with a short list of things she intended to do differently, and the list has not needed much editing since.

The first item was the fees. Work she had done for years had predictable stages, yet clients were quoted an hourly rate and a shrug. Here, the predictable work carries fixed fees, printed on the home page of this site where anyone can check them before making an appointment — and the genuinely unpredictable work, estates above all, is estimated in writing with the reasoning shown.

The second was the writing. Every report, estimate and engagement letter from this office is written to be read by the person it is for: clauses explained, stages numbered, and no paragraph that exists only because it always has. The third was size. The practice stays small on purpose — one solicitor, a legal executive and an assistant — so that the person you meet at the first meeting is the person who runs your matter to its end.

Clare is the practice's principal and its only solicitor; the fiction note in the footer of this page explains the standing of this template, and the professional framework described across the site is the real one her counterparts work under.

§1 · THE WORKING PAGEHow instruction works

Five steps stand between an enquiry and work beginning. They are the same for a £240 will and a full estate administration, and none of them can be skipped — two exist because regulation requires them, and the rest because surprises in legal work are always expensive ones.

  1. Enquiry & conflicts check

    Before anything else, we check that we do not already act for another party to your matter — an opponent, a seller, an employer. A solicitor who acts for both sides serves neither; if a conflict exists, we will tell you at once and suggest other practices. Until this check is done, please do not send us confidential detail.

  2. The first meeting — £60 fixed

    Forty-five minutes with Clare, at the office or by video. You set out the position; she sets out whether the practice can act, the stages, the timescale and the fee basis. A written summary follows within two working days. The £60 is credited against the matter fee if you instruct within thirty days.

  3. Identity checks

    Solicitors are required by anti-money-laundering regulation to verify every client's identity — photo ID and proof of address, and for purchases, where deposit funds come from. It applies to everyone, including clients we have known for years; it is a regulatory reality, not a judgement about you, and it happens before work starts, not halfway through.

  4. The engagement letter

    Nothing begins until you have signed an engagement letter that states the scope of the work, the fee basis, the timescale we are working to, and how to complain if we fall short. If it is not in the letter, it is not part of the instruction — which protects you from scope creep in both directions.

  5. The work, billed as agreed

    Fixed means fixed: the fee in the letter is the fee on the bill. Hourly work is recorded in six-minute units and itemised. And if a matter stops being the standard matter the fee assumed — a will that uncovers a dispute, a title with a defect — work stops, the change is explained, and a new basis is agreed in writing before another hour is spent. No silent meter, ever.

§2 · TERMS IN SHORTEngagement terms, without the small print voice

Fees & VATFees are as printed on the home page and confirmed in your engagement letter; VAT is added at the prevailing rate. Disbursements are charged at cost and itemised.
PaymentFixed fees are payable on completion of the matter unless the letter says otherwise; estates are billed at the stages stated in the letter. Interest and pause provisions, where they apply, are in the letter in plain terms.
Your papersOriginal wills and deeds are stored in the deed store without charge while you are a client, and are released to you — or, after a death, to your executors — on request.
Ending the instructionYou may end an instruction at any time in writing; we may do so only for good reason and on reasonable notice, as the professional rules require. Fees are payable for work properly done to that point.
ConfidentialityYour matter is confidential. Files are not discussed with third parties — including family members — without your written authority.

§3 · IF WE FALL SHORTThe complaints route, stated as it should be

Tell us first. Most problems in legal work are delays or misunderstandings, and most are put right fastest by the practice itself: raise the matter with Clare directly, and you will receive a written response under the practice's complaints procedure, a copy of which is enclosed with every engagement letter and available on request.

If we cannot resolve it, you may ask the Legal Ombudsman to look at the complaint — normally after eight weeks from raising it with us, or once we have given our final response. Solicitors in England & Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, which sets the standards the practice works to. None of this is fine print; it is part of what you are entitled to expect, and it is printed here for the same reason the fees are.

Information, not advice

Everything on this site describes the practice and its services. It is general information, not legal advice about any person's situation, and reading it does not make you a client or create a solicitor–client relationship. Advice is given only after instruction, on the facts of a specific matter, and usually in writing.
