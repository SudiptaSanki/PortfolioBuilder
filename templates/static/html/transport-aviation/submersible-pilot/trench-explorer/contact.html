Enquiries

Tell me the depth and the question.

The useful first message is short: where the site is, how deep, what you want to bring up or film, and the month you can sail. I answer within two working days, and I will tell you if the vehicle, the weather window or the science plan does not add up.

This form runs in the browser only. It does not send anything until the site is connected to a mail service.

Mobilisation

Dive series are booked in blocks of five days: one day mobilisation, one day wet drills, three days on site with a weather day held in reserve. A five-day block to 4,000 m is typically 11 to 14 days of vessel time once transit is counted.

Before you write

If the site has never been dived, send the multibeam. A depth from a chart is a suggestion; a depth from a swath is a plan. If the target is inside a marine protected area or a licence area, say so in the first message.

Answer times

What happens after you send it

Day 1–2

Read and answer

I read every enquiry myself. If the depth is inside the vehicle's rating and the season works, you get a one-page feasibility note with the dive count I think it needs and the failure cases I would plan for.

Week 1

Call with the chief scientist

Half an hour, usually with the client scientist and whoever runs the boat. We settle the sample handling, the deck space and who signs the dive card. This is where most plans get cheaper.

Week 2–4

Dive plan and permit check

A written plan per dive, a ballast sheet, a comms schedule and a permit checklist. Nothing is booked until the plan is signed by both sides, because a dive plan that changes on the dock is a dive that has not been planned.
