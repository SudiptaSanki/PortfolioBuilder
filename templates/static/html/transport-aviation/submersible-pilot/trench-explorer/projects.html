Dive log · 2019 to 2026

What came back up, and what did not.

Six expeditions listed in full, with the depth band, the number of dives flown and the outcome. The remaining eight are in the printed log and available on request; four of them produced nothing worth publishing and are marked as such.

Beebe vent field, Cayman Trough

Six dives over eleven days with the vent-chemistry group at Southampton. Paired fluid samples from four orifices at 358 °C, a photogrammetry model of the northern spire from 12 cameras, and a temperature log across the diffuse flow apron that the group used to revise their heat-flux estimate upward by 18%.

The last two dives were lost to a current that swung 90° overnight and buried the sample basket in resuspended sediment on the first touch. We recovered the basket, not the samples.

2024 · 6 dives
Max depth 4,980 m
Bottom time 22 h 40 m
Client: NOC Southampton
Output: 41 samples, 1 model

Kermadec Trench, northern wall

The deepest dive in the log. A seven-hour descent to a wall that drops 800 m in 400 m of horizontal distance, for two push cores and two amphipod traps at 6,380 m. The current ran 1.1 m/s across the wall and pushed the trim 40 m off the planned heading, which meant the first core came up from a different sediment facies than the one the geologist had picked from the bathymetry.

It turned out to be the more interesting core. The traps recovered 96 amphipods of two species, one of them new to the trench's species list.

2023 · 4 dives
Max depth 6,412 m
Bottom time 19 h 05 m
Client: NIWA Wellington
Output: 4 cores, 96 specimens

Loki's Castle, Knipovich Ridge

Eleven dives for a three-part natural-history series, with two camera positions and a 24 V lighting rig cobbled onto the vehicle's spare bus. The carbonate chimneys are fragile and the brief was simple: do not touch them. We flew the same three approaches at 40 cm and 80 cm standoff for eleven days and got 26 hours of usable footage, including a shrimp swarm that took four dives to anticipate.

2022 · 11 dives
Max depth 2,368 m
Bottom time 41 h 10 m
Client: documentary unit, BBC Studios
Output: 26 h rushes, 0 contact

TAG hydrothermal field, Mid-Atlantic Ridge

A structure survey for a client mapping inactive sulphide mounds ahead of a licence application. We flew a 1.4 km lawnmower pattern at 8 m altitude and 0.35 m/s with the multibeam logging continuously, which is slow work and produces a very dull dive card. The client got a 12 cm resolution surface and a report; we got a nine-hour bottom time and a thruster that needed a new seal.

2021 · 3 dives
Max depth 3,665 m
Bottom time 24 h 30 m
Client: survey contractor
Output: 1.4 km² swath, 12 cm

Von Damm vent field, Mid-Cayman Spreading Centre

Two dives to test a revised sample-basket design on a field with a brine pool at the bottom of the basin. The pool is 4 °C above ambient and dense enough to bend the vehicle's trim; we hovered above it and lowered the basket on a 3 m tether instead of flying into it. The design worked. The video of a shrimp walking on the brine interface is still the most-requested clip in the client's library.

2020 · 2 dives
Max depth 2,310 m
Bottom time 11 h 20 m
Client: WHOI-funded programme
Output: 6 samples, 1 design test

Clarion-Clipperton nodule zone, eastern Pacific

A baseline imagery transect for an environmental consultancy working on a nodule-mining impact assessment. Two dives, 4 hours each, flying a straight line at 2 m altitude with a downward camera and a laser scale. The imagery is unglamorous and the scientific value is real: it is one of a handful of pre-disturbance baselines in the zone, and it will still be useful in twenty years.

2019 · 2 dives
Max depth 4,290 m
Bottom time 8 h 00 m
Client: environmental consultancy
Output: 2.6 km baseline imagery

East Scotia Ridge, segment E2

Three dives on a vent field dominated by a yeti crab population, for a population-genetics group. The work was mostly holding station while a suction sampler did the sampling, with one dive given over to recovering a temperature logger that had been bolted to a chimney 14 months earlier. It came up with 400 days of data at 1-minute resolution.

2025 · 3 dives
Max depth 2,402 m
Bottom time 13 h 45 m
Client: Oxford population genetics
Output: 84 specimens, 400 days of logger data
