01 — Depth

Five bands of water, five ways to work in them

A dive plan is not a depth target. It is a pressure envelope, a light budget and a way back up. These are the bands I plan in, with the numbers I plan against.

02 — Method

Three decisions come before the science plan

A

Ballast before ambition

Every gram of the descent load is weighed and signed off on deck. The target depth sets the pressure; the trim sets whether you arrive, hover or plough into silt. I write the drop weight, the reserve and the release order into the dive card, then brief all three occupants on it before the hatch closes.

B

Two ways up, always

Tern-3 carries 400 kg of drop weight in three independent releases, a redundant lift bag, and a 9-hour life-support reserve against a 6-hour dive plan. The second way up is not a page in a manual; it is practised at the surface each morning, in the water, before anyone goes below 100 m.

C

The log is part of the vehicle

Depth, trim, battery state, O₂ partial pressure and every manipulator action are logged at 1 Hz and handed over with the samples. A dive that produces a beautiful specimen and a thin log is a dive the client cannot publish. Sampling without a chain of custody is just collecting.

03 — Selected dives

Four descents, and what came back up

  1. 4,960 m

    Beebe vent field, Cayman Trough

    Six dives over eleven days for the vent-chemistry group at Southampton. Collected paired fluid samples from four orifices at 358 °C and mapped the field's northern spire with a 12-camera photogrammetry rig.

    2024
  2. 6,412 m

    Kermadec Trench, northern wall

    Deepest crewed dive in the log: a seven-hour descent to the trench wall for sediment cores and two amphipod traps, in a 1.1 m/s current that pushed the trim 40 m off the planned heading.

    2023
  3. 2,352 m

    Loki's Castle, Knipovich Ridge

    Filming unit for a three-part natural-history series. Eleven dives on the carbonate chimneys, with two camera positions and a lighting rig that had to run from the vehicle's 24 V bus.

    2022
  4. 3,630 m

    TAG hydrothermal field, Mid-Atlantic Ridge

    Wreck and structure survey for a client mapping inactive sulphide mounds. Flew a 1.4 km lawnmower pattern at 8 m altitude, 0.35 m/s, with the sonar logging continuously.

    2021
