Programme · training, medical, flight day

Ninety seconds before the engine lights

The flight is nine minutes. The preparation is three days and two weeks, and it is the part that decides whether the nine minutes are memorable or merely survived.

RoleCaptain, suborbital flight operations. Pilot in command for research campaigns and private flights; also the training captain for two operators.

Hours62 flights above 80 km, 412 simulator hours, 3 100 hours total time including 1 800 in high-performance jets.

CurrencyClass 2 medical renewed annually, altitude chamber every 12 months, water egress every 6.

RuleNo passenger flies with me who has not done the depressurisation drill in a suit, in the chamber, at altitude.

A suborbital flight is a very short, very fast trip to the edge of the atmosphere. What makes it different from a jet is not the altitude: it is that the vehicle leaves the atmosphere, and everything that means for control, for the body and for the plan when something goes wrong.

The three days

  1. 01

    Cabin and suit

    The first day is the vehicle: seats, restraints, hatches, the three ways out and the one way you will actually use. Then the pressure suit, donned and doffed until it takes under four minutes without help.

    Day 1
  2. 02

    Load and rehearsal

    A centrifuge run to 3.5 g with a 5 g entry profile, then the full flight in the simulator with the actual callouts and the actual checklist. The second day is where nervous passengers become competent ones, or where we find out that they will not fly.

    Day 2
  3. 03

    Contingencies

    Depressurisation, a pad abort, a parachute landing and a water egress in the pool. Nobody enjoys this day. Everybody who has done it is calmer on the pad, and that is measurable in the heart-rate trace.

    Day 3

The most useful thing a pilot can give a passenger is a checklist they have already run four times, in the suit, with the noise on.

Medical, honestly

The screening is a class 3 equivalent: cardiology, a lung function test, an orthopaedic review for backs and necks, and a conversation about anxiety that some applicants find harder than the treadmill. Around one in nine is deferred, and almost none of those deferrals are about fitness in the ordinary sense. A previous spinal injury is a bigger problem than a resting heart rate.

I am not the examiner. The examiner is independent, and I have flown with people the examiner cleared and declined people the examiner cleared, because the pilot in command has the last word and should use it.

What I will not do

I will not fly a passenger who has been sold a flight as a thrill ride without the training, and I will not sign a mission where the schedule is the reason the drill was shortened. Both of those have happened at other operators. Neither of them is a mistake you get to make twice.

Reading the profile

The model on the front page is the same arithmetic I do before a briefing: how much velocity the burn has to deliver for a given apogee, how long the weightless window is, and what the entry load will be. It is one-dimensional and it ignores wind, lift and guidance, so treat it as an order-of-magnitude tool with a pilot's margin on top.
