Campaign enquiries

Send the envelope, not the brochure.

The useful first message has four lines: the airframe, the Mach and altitude range you need, where you can put microphones, and what the regulator has already agreed to. I answer in three working days, and I will say no quickly if the campaign cannot produce a certificate.

Campaign shape

An eight-week certification campaign is typical: one week on site for ground stations and calibration, five weeks of flying with weather spares, two weeks of data review with the client engineers.

Before the first sortie

Three things happen in the six weeks before an aircraft arrives, and none of them involve flying.

  1. 01

    Ground-station plan

    A map with named sites, access routes, power, and one person responsible per site. Stations are sited for the carpet geometry, not for convenience; a station in a barn is worth less than a station in a field with a clear line of sight to the track.

    Week 1–2
  2. 02

    Atmosphere and cut-off study

    Balloon data from the previous three years at the site, run through the boom model to find the altitudes where an inversion would focus the ray. This is where the test matrix gets its altitude steps.

    Week 2–4
  3. 03

    Card review

    Every pass written out with its tolerance, its abort and its expected signature. The client's test engineer signs it, the regulator sees it, and it does not change after the first flight without a written reason.

    Week 4–6
