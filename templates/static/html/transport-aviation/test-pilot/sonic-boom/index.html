01

What a certificate actually measures

Three numbers decide whether a supersonic type is allowed over land. None of them can be read off a drawing; all three come from a ground station, a microphone and a sortie flown to a written card.

Carpet width

18.4 km

How far to either side of the ground track the shock is audible. It narrows fast with altitude and widens with Mach, and it is the number that decides which farms, schools and hospitals get a letter before the first flight.

Overpressure at the lateral edge

1.4 psf

Peak pressure step at the edge of the carpet, where the ray has grazed the boundary layer and lost most of its energy. Regulators write the limit here, not under the track, because this is where a complaint comes from.

Focus factor

2.7×

The multiplier from a manoeuvre, an acceleration or a temperature inversion that bends rays together in one place. A steady cruise boom is easy; an accelerating turn at the wrong gradient is how a 2 psf aircraft produces a 6 psf event.

02

How a boom sortie is flown

Four passes, each with a stated purpose, a toleranced parameter, and an abort criterion written before take-off. If a pass does not meet its tolerance, we do not average it into the data; we fly it again or we go home.

  1. 01

    Calibration pass, Mach 1.15

    Straight and level on the survey line at 11,000 m, with two ground stations live and the aircraft in clean configuration. Establishes the microphone chain and the atmosphere of the day. No data taken from this pass; it is there to prove the instruments before the expensive one.

    18 min
  2. 02

    Carpet pass, Mach 1.4 to 1.8

    Four accelerations through the transonic band, 0.02 Mach at a time, holding each for 40 s. The critical pass is the one through Mach 1.05; it produces the strongest focus and the worst rise time, and it is the pass the client will ask about in the review.

    26 min
  3. 03

    Turn and focus pass

    A 30° banked turn at constant Mach, flown twice in opposite directions, to measure the focus factor from the acceleration normal to the flight path. Ground stations at 4 km and 9 km lateral watch for the ray convergence.

    14 min
  4. 04

    Descent and cut-off

    Accelerate to the highest Mach of the card, then descend at 2,000 ft/min until the boom no longer reaches the primary station. That altitude is the lateral cut-off the certificate quotes, and it is the last number anyone wants to get wrong.

    22 min
03

Why the rise time matters more than the peak

A 2 psf boom with a 4 ms rise is a gunshot. The same 2 psf with a 180 ms rise is a door closing downstairs. Human response tracks the rise time almost as strongly as the peak, which is why the quiet-supersonic programmes spend their money on airframe length and shaping rather than on shaving the last 0.2 psf.

My card always carries both numbers, and I refuse to sign a report that quotes the peak alone. The ground station that matters is usually the one at the lateral edge, where the waveform is stretched and the peak is low but the duration is long enough to shake a window frame for a quarter of a second.
