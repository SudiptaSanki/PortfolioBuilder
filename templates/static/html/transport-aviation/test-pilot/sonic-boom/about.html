Flight test · 2004 to 2026

I fly the card, then I write the card.

Test flying is a documentation job with an aircraft attached. The sortie is the easy part; the two days before it decide what the flight is worth, and the four hours after it decide whether anyone can use the result.

From line pilot to envelope expansion

I flew commercial regional turboprops for six years before moving to flight test, which is where I learned that a stabilised approach and a stabilised test point are the same discipline. In 2009 I joined a research outfit at Mojave as a project pilot on a transonic demonstrator, and spent the next five years learning how much of a boom is geometry and how much is weather.

Since 2015 the work has been certification support: campaign planning, ground-station layout, and flying the passes that produce the numbers a regulator will accept. Seven type certificates have used data from sorties I planned or flew. None of them were decided by a single spectacular flight; all of them were decided by 20 boring ones flown to tolerance.

I hold an ATPL with a test pilot rating, a Class 1 medical, and a Mach 2.2 endorsement. High-altitude pressure suit and ejection seat currencies are current. I do not fly prototypes without an engineer in the loop, and I do not accept a campaign where the abort criteria are written after the first pass.

The brief, in the order I give it

Every sortie starts with the same twenty minutes on the ground. If any of these six items is missing, the flight does not happen, no matter how good the weather is.

  1. 01

    What number are we buying

    One sentence, written on the card: the overpressure at the lateral edge, the cut-off altitude, the rise time at a stated station. A sortie that tries to buy three numbers buys none of them.

    2 min
  2. 02

    Which station has to hear it

    Ground stations are named, with their coordinates, their microphone height and who is standing next to them. An unattended station is a station that produces a disputed data point.

    4 min
  3. 03

    The atmosphere of the day

    Balloon data to 20 km, temperature at each test altitude, and the wind gradient between 8 and 16 km. A 6 K inversion over the carpet will move the focus and it will not be in the forecast.

    5 min
  4. 04

    The tolerance and the abort

    Each pass has a parameter tolerance and a stated abort. If Mach holds within 0.01 and altitude within 150 m, the pass counts. If not, it is flown again once, then abandoned.

    3 min
  5. 05

    Who is on the ground with a phone

    Two names, one for the client and one for the airfield. Boom complaints arrive within four minutes of the first pass, and they arrive at the tower, not at the test office.

    3 min
  6. 06

    The way home

    Fuel to the alternate, a diversion field inside the boom carpet, and the minimum fuel state at which the last pass is cancelled rather than flown. Written before take-off.

    3 min

Two programmes a year

One certification campaign and one research aircraft. Campaigns run 8 to 14 weeks on site, and I would rather see the ground-station plan in week one than the aircraft in week four.
