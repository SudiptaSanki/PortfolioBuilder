Card 02 · The instrument — works from Table T1, printed below

The Mach & cone sheet

Every test card states its Mach number, and every Mach number hides a temperature. The speed of sound falls as the air gets colder, so the same true airspeed is a different Mach at 50,000 ft than at sea level — and the cone behind the aircraft, the thing a community hears as a boom, opens or closes with it. Pick an altitude band from Table T1, step the true airspeed, and watch the sheet work. Below Mach 1 there is no cone, and the sheet says so rather than inventing an angle.

Fees — printed, not negotiated in the dark

The fee shape, and what moves it

Rates are per flying day unless stated. What moves the price, in order: programme duration first, chase and telemetry needs second, travel to the range third. The scrub rule is printed here because a case on the Flight Cards page turns on it: a day scrubbed on procedure before engine start bills at half the day rate, chase at half the chase rate. Nobody should have to ask what a scrub costs while deciding whether to call one.

From the flight cards — file 01, with its testimonial attached

The Quiet Carpet, in one paragraph

Eighteen sorties and seventy-two carded passes at Mach 1.40 and 50,000 ft over a ground microphone array, flown so the programme's dataset — not anyone's flying — was the story: 68 of 72 passes inside their tolerances (±0.02 Mach, ±500 ft), the other four re-flown without argument. The full file, and the scrub that cost a programme less than a full day and saved it a season, are on the Flight Cards page.

“Her passes were the cleanest data we took all season — same point, same tolerances, sortie after sortie. When she called a scrub on a telemetry fault nobody else had flagged, the programme manager backed her in the room, because her cards had earned it.”
