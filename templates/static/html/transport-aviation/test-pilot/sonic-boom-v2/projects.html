Card 02 · The flight cards — engagements, filed as they closed

Three cards, filed honestly

Each file is told the way a debrief runs: the brief, the decisions, the outcome — with the numbers left in, including the ones that cost somebody money. Programmes are described at class level throughout; the details belong to the programmes.

FILE 01

The Quiet Carpet

Complete — dataset accepted

The brief

A low-boom research programme needed a season of repeatable supersonic passes over a ground microphone array — the same point, at the same Mach and altitude, flown the same way, so that the signatures recorded on the ground could be compared like-for-like. The ask was not speed. The ask was sameness.

The decisions

The card set fixed every pass at Mach 1.40 and 50,000 ft, with tolerances of ±0.02 Mach and ±500 ft, and a hard rule: a pass outside tolerance is re-flown, not argued about. Two passes a day, never three — the third pass of a long day is where tolerances quietly go to die. Chase flew every pass, because the array's timing marks are only as good as the position calls behind them.

The outcome

Eighteen sorties, seventy-two carded passes. Sixty-eight landed inside tolerance; four were re-flown the following day without a meeting about it. The dataset was accepted without qualification, and the programme's own report credited the tolerance discipline — not the aircraft — for its cleanest comparison set of the season.

“Her passes were the cleanest data we took all season — same point, same tolerances, sortie after sortie. When she called a scrub on a telemetry fault nobody else had flagged, the programme manager backed her in the room, because her cards had earned it.”

FILE 02

Build-Up

Complete — 0 points skipped

The brief

A training organisation had six experienced pilots — all current, all competent — who had never expanded an envelope. Their flying was not the problem; their habits were airline habits, where the envelope is a fence you stay well inside. Flight test needs the opposite discipline: approach the fence one measured step at a time, and write down what the fence felt like.

The decisions

Two cohorts of three, five flying days each. Every pilot wrote their own cards before flying them — a pilot who cannot write the point cannot fly it. The syllabus ran 41 test points per pilot, ordered strictly by build-up: no pilot touched a point whose neighbours had not been flown and debriefed. Two points across the two cohorts were re-flown after debrief. They are counted in this file, not edited out of it.

The outcome

All six pilots completed the syllabus to card standard: 41 of 41 points each, 2 re-flown, 0 skipped. The organisation now runs its own build-up courses on the same card format, which was the actual point of the engagement — the method transferred, the instructor left.

FILE 03

The Scrub

Complete — re-flown in full

The brief

A single-day extension to a data campaign: six passes on one card, a full team on the range, a full day rate committed. Everything about the day said go — which is precisely the weather in which scrubs get talked out of existence.

The decisions

At T−40 minutes, ground checks showed the telemetry stream dropping frames — the same stream the whole dataset depended on. Separately, the range crosswind was recorded at 31 kt against a card limit of 25. Either item alone met the knock-it-off criteria that had been briefed, out loud, that morning. The call was made in one sentence, and the day was spent on the ground fixing a telemetry harness instead of flying on a broken one.

The outcome

The day billed at the printed scrub rate: half the test-support day ($1,400) plus half the chase day ($625) — $2,025 against the $4,050 a flown day would have cost, because the scrub rule is in the fee schedule and not negotiated in the moment. The card was re-flown two days later: six passes, six in tolerance. A programme that cannot afford a scrub cannot afford the truth, and this one could.
