SHEET A

The aerodynamicist

Nagoya → Munich · independent since 2011

I trained as an aeronautical engineer in Nagoya and spent nine years in a rolling-stock manufacturer's aerodynamics group in Japan — tunnel and track work on two production fleets — before moving to Munich in 2011 to work independently.

Since then the practice has audited 31 fleets and vehicle programmes, logged 214 wind-tunnel runs, and re-profiled nine tunnel portals, for operators, manufacturers, infrastructure owners and one ministry. It works across wheel-rail and maglev programmes alike — which is why, further down this page, it argues with both.

The formative experience of this trade is standing in a tunnel at three in the morning watching a pressure trace ruin a beautiful model. The tunnel and the track settle arguments. My job is to make sure the argument happens before the concrete, not after the opening ceremony.

Everybody in this industry wants to sell you the last 50 km/h. I am the person who prices it.

M.1
Baseline before opinion

Coast-downs and a data audit first. If the client's brochure number cannot be reproduced, the study starts there — that discrepancy is often the finding.

M.2
Model, then measure

CFD builds the hypothesis and the test matrix — which configurations are worth tunnel time. The tunnel (moving ground for vehicles, moving-model rig for tunnels) tests it.

M.3
Correlate or confess

Model and measurement are published side by side in the report. Where they disagree, the disagreement is printed, and the measurement is senior.

M.4
Recommendations costed

Every fix is priced per square metre of Cd·A, per pascal of pulse, per watt-hour per passenger-kilometre — so the client can buy in order of value, not in order of fashion.

SHEET C

The honesty page

Four standing positions · printed so they can be quoted back

H.1A CFD picture is a hypothesis with good lighting

Computation decides what is worth measuring; it is never the measurement. Every CFD figure in a report from this practice carries its tunnel or track correlation beside it — or the words not yet correlated, in the same size type.

H.2Speed is a cost line, not a trophy

The stub this site replaced advertised a 600 km/h record and a drag coefficient with no basis. This practice claims no records. A top speed is a decision about power, concrete and noise, and it is priced like one — see Run 01.

H.3No load factor, no energy figure

Energy per passenger-kilometre halves on paper if you quietly assume a full train. Every figure here names its load factor (the worked example on the Run log uses 65%; Study File 03 uses the route's real 58%). An empty fast train is not an efficient train, and a full slow one often beats it.

H.4The neighbours pay part of every speed

Tunnel pressure pulses and lineside noise are costs borne by people who never ride the train. They enter my studies as design constraints on day one — not as a public-relations problem discovered at the opening.

SHEET D

Engagement terms

  • Who commissions: operators, manufacturers, infrastructure owners and ministries. Not vehicle marketing departments — a study whose conclusion is pre-written is not a study.
  • Scope before facilities: the test matrix is fixed and signed before tunnel time is booked; tunnel days are booked in the client's name and billed at cost.
  • Ownership: reports belong to the client. The practice keeps its run logs and may publish methods and anonymised arithmetic — never client data, never a client's identity without permission.
  • Inconvenient conclusions are delivered in the same size type as convenient ones, and the estimator's price does not depend on which kind yours turns out to be.
  • Capacity: two programme slots per quarter, taken in order of signed scope. The aeroacoustics register (S.05) is waitlisted until March.
