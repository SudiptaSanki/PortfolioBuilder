Method · from the control network up

The ground is the machine

A maglev line is a 40 km precision instrument that has to survive weather, settlement and a train passing 300 times a day. Here is the order we build it in, and the part of each stage that usually goes wrong.

PracticeSlipstream KK, Nagoya. Twelve engineers: survey, structures, magnetics and aerodynamics, plus a site team that has cast guideway in three countries.

StandardsJapanese guideway alignment specifications for the test line, EN 1992 for the concrete, IEC 61373 for the on-board equipment vibration, and the operator's own ride-comfort criterion, which is stricter than all of them.

RuleNo slab is accepted on the survey of the day it was cast. It is accepted after one full thermal cycle and a re-survey at night.

ToolsTotal stations on forced-centring pillars, a 3 m chord gauge, and a laser tracker for the coil packs.

The magnet is on the train and the coil is in the ground, so every millimetre the ground moves is a millimetre of the control loop's authority spent. The design tolerance is ±1.5 mm on a 3 m chord. That is not a number you can hit with concrete alone.

  1. 01

    Control network

    Monumented pillars every 200 m, tied to the national datum, with a redundancy that lets us lose one and still close the traverse. The network is measured at night, because a 20 °C gradient across a 40 km alignment is worth more error than the instrument.

    6 weeks
  2. 02

    Substructure and settlement

    Piers are cast and left to settle for a season before the beam is placed. Where the ground will not settle in time we use a adjustable bearing rather than a thicker pier, because a bearing can be corrected and a pier cannot.

    3–9 months
  3. 03

    Beam alignment

    The guideway beam is set on its bearings to a pre-camber curve that anticipates the thermal movement of the finished line. We measure at 4 a.m. and again at 2 p.m. and design to the average, not to the night value.

    1 week / km
  4. 04

    Coil packs

    Figure-eight coils are set on a jig, levelled to 0.3 mm and torqued in a sequence that does not distort the pack. The pitch tolerance is ±0.5 mm because the pitch, not the individual coil, sets the ripple the control loop has to reject.

    2 days / km
  5. 05

    Gap and guidance test

    A low-speed carriage is run over the section at 30 km/h with the magnets energised and the gap logged at every coil. Anything outside 100 ±15 mm is traced back to a slab, a coil or a magnet before the section is accepted.

    3 days / km
  6. 06

    High-speed proving

    Speed is stepped up in 50 km/h increments with the pressure in the tube reduced at each step. The last increment is always the one that finds the aerodynamics, not the levitation.

    8–14 weeks

A maglev does not float because the magnets are strong. It floats because the gap is the same everywhere, and the gap is the same everywhere because somebody measured a concrete beam at four in the morning.

Thermal movement is the design driver

A 40 km steel-and-concrete line expands about 18 m between a winter night and a summer afternoon if it is restrained at one end. We do not fight that; we put expansion joints at every pier group and design the alignment so that the movement is along the line rather than across it. The lateral component is what the control loop cannot absorb.

The vehicle sees the same problem from the other side: the on-board magnets are at 4 K inside a cryostat, and the coil pack is at ambient. The thermal gradient across the 100 mm gap is 290 °C, which is good for the superconductor and hard on every seal around it.

Aerodynamics, and why the tube is not optional

At 500 km/h in sea-level air the aerodynamic drag on a 40 tonne car is about 90 kN, more than twice the magnetic drag at the same speed. A partially evacuated tube cuts that by a factor of two and a half, and creates the pressure wave that the tunnel cross-section has to be sized for. The wave limit on the test line is 0.12 bar at the portal, which sets the block length and the vent area.
