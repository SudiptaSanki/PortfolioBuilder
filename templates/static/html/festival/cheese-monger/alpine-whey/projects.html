The wheels · eight in the cave

Everything here is cut to order and wrapped in paper.

Eight wheels, four kinds of milk, one cave. Prices are per kilo and include the rind, which you may eat or not. Anything under 200 g is cut with a wire; anything larger is cut with a knife and weighed twice.

8 wheels

01

Alpkäse

Cow · summer pasture

30 months

The oldest wheel in the cave and the one I would take to a desert island. Dense, saline, allium and toasted hay; the rind is 18 mm and smells of the cellar floor. Cut it thin and let it warm for twenty minutes before eating.

CHF 58/kg

02

Glarner Bergkäse

Cow · 980 m

18 months

The everyday wheel: firm paste, small eyes, a clean walnut finish and enough salt to sit with bread and beer. It melts without oiling off, which is why the restaurant on the corner takes two a week.

CHF 46/kg

03

Emmentaler

Cow · hay-fed

12 months

Bigger eyes, sweeter paste, a hazelnut middle. Made in a warm room for three weeks before it comes to me, which is what makes the eyes. Good for fondue and better on a plate with pear.

CHF 42/kg

04

Mutschli

Cow · rindless

12 weeks

A small wheel sold young and eaten fast. Mild, lactic and springy, with a thin washed rind that some customers peel and some do not. Put it on a plate with radishes and a cold beer.

CHF 34/kg

05

Tomme

Cow · mixed pasture

10 weeks

A thin, grey-rinded tomme with a supple paste and a faint barnyard note that fades after ten minutes on the board. The wheel to buy when the table has more bread than money.

CHF 32/kg

06

Ziegenrolle

Goat · ash-rubbed

6 weeks

Rolled in ash and aged six weeks, so the rind is thin and the paste is bright. Goat, lemon peel and a little salt; best in the month it is cut, and best with a Sauvignon from the valley.

CHF 52/kg

07

Schafkäse

Sheep · 1,100 m

5 months

Sheep milk from a farm above Elm, aged five months in the same cave. Lanolin, cured olive and brown butter; a small wheel, so it dries faster and the rind is thinner than the cow cheeses at the same age.

CHF 64/kg

08

Schabziger

Cow · skimmed, blue fenugreek

4 weeks

The Glarus cheese: skimmed milk, blue fenugreek and a short cave, pressed into small cones and grated or sliced thin. It is sharp, herbal and strangely green, and it belongs on buttered bread with a slice of onion.

CHF 38/kg

Cutting & keeping

How to buy a wheel without wasting it.

Buy by the week, not the month

Cut cheese keeps for about a week in paper in the vegetable drawer, less if it is a soft tomme. Buy what you will eat, come back next Thursday. A wheel cut and rewrapped three times loses its rind and its temper.

Ask for the middle

On a big wheel the middle is sweeter and the edge is saltier. Both are good; if you are buying a piece to put on a board, ask for a wedge that runs from the centre to the rind, so you get the whole story in one slice.

The rind

Eat it or do not. It is not wax and it is not plastic, so it will not hurt you. On the Alpkäse the rind is too hard to enjoy; on the Mutschli it is the best part. If you are cooking, drop the rind into a soup and take it out before serving.

If it smells of ammonia

Ammonia at the rind of an old wheel is normal; let the piece breathe for half an hour and it will go. Ammonia through the paste means the cheese was wrapped too tightly, and I will replace it. Tell me the date on the label and I will look up the wheel.
