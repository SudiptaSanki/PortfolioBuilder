Questions the desk gets

Will you record a story that is sacred or restricted?

Sometimes, but the community decides the terms. Some material should not be published at all, and the right answer is to record nothing. Tell me what the restrictions are in your first message and we will start there.

Do you pay tellers?

Yes. Tellers receive a session fee, half of the episode's licensing income and a copy of the final audio and transcript. Translators are paid a day rate and credited by name in the audio.

Can I use an episode in a classroom?

Yes, without asking, for non-commercial teaching. The transcripts are free to print. For a public screening or a paid course, write to the desk and we will sort out a simple licence.

Do you take unsolicited audio?

Send a note first, not a file. If the recording is a good fit I will ask for a short excerpt and a consent note from the person speaking. Do not send anyone's recording without their agreement.

What makes a good pitch

One paragraph is enough. Name the tale, the teller, the language and the place. Say whether the teller knows you are writing. If you are the teller, say what you want listeners to understand and what should stay out.

Do not send a fully written script. The best episodes begin with a conversation, not a document.

Bring the show to a room

Live recordings run 75 minutes with a break, for libraries, schools, festivals and small theatres. I travel with one microphone and a projector for the transcript, so the audience reads as well as listens. Fees are on a sliding scale; a school in Assam pays less than a corporate festival.
