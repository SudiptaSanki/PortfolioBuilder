EP 041

The gourd vine that spoke

Tejimola, the orphan girl who returns as a vine, a gourd and then a bird. Two tellings from Nalbari, recorded forty years apart, are set beside each other rather than merged.

Assamese24 minBurhi Aair Sadhu2 tellers
Read an excerpt

Teller: They cut the gourd as if it were a gourd. Nobody in that house expected a voice. But when the knife went in, the vine spoke from inside the fruit, and it called the stepmother by the name only the dead girl used.

Host: In the version you heard as a child, was the bird already there?

Teller: There was no bird in my mother's telling. The gourd spoke and that was the end. The bird came later, from a book, I think. But I like the bird. Let us say the bird came later.

Teller

Anima Deka, Nalbari

Translation

Rituraj Bora

EP 040

Nine nights at Kamakhya

What is said on the hill during Ambubachi, and what is deliberately left unsaid. Built almost entirely from night tape and one long walk down from the temple gate at dawn.

Assamese32 minField recordingAmbubachi 2025
Read an excerpt

Host: We are standing below the western gate. It is 2:40 in the morning and the hill is not quiet.

Voice: Do not say the name near the steps. Say it when we are past the gate, where the ground is ordinary.

Host: I ask what happens if someone says it in the wrong place. The answer is a long pause and then a laugh that is not quite a laugh.

Recorded

Kamakhya, Guwahati

Consent

Two voices withheld

EP 039

Behula's boat

The Manasa legend told as a journey: a merchant's daughter, a snake, a river at flood and a goddess who will not be ignored. Recorded with a singer who performs the cycle over three nights.

Bengali41 minManasa cycleSong + interview
Read an excerpt

Singer: People call her cruel because she takes a husband from a new bride. But listen to what she is offered before that. Nothing. A goddess with no shrine is not a goddess, she is a rumour.

Host: So the story is a negotiation.

Singer: The story is a contract, and Behula is the lawyer. She argues with everyone, including the dead.

Teller

Sujata Pal, Silchar

Translation

Mitali Saikia

EP 038

The river that came from a horse

Brahmaputra, Lauhitya, Tsangpo, Yarlung: one waterway with four tellings. A solo episode about origin stories, and about what a flood myth has to do when the flood is real.

English26 minSolo narration3 sources
Read an excerpt

Begin with the sound of the river at Pandu in February, when the sandbars are wide enough to walk on. Then say the names in the order the river meets them. The story of the horse comes late, because the river is older than the story, and the people who live beside it know that better than the poets do.

Written by

Mitali Saikia

Sources

3 collections

EP 037

Why the jackal limps

A short animal tale from the Burhi Aair Sadhu, told to a room of eight-year-olds in a Guwahati library. The children were promised the real ending. They were not promised a kind one.

Assamese18 minLive audienceAge 8+
Read an excerpt

Host: Why does the jackal limp?

Child in audience: Because he lied.

Host: That is one answer. The tale gives another, and the tale is older than your answer, so we will hear it and then you can decide.

Recorded

Guwahati, 2025

Audience

24 children

EP 036

Churning, and what came after

Samudra Manthan, told by a Sanskrit teacher who insists the poison is the point and the nectar is the advertisement. Includes the parts every children's book leaves out.

English38 minMahabharataAdi Parva
Read an excerpt

Teacher: Everyone remembers the nectar because the nectar is the happy ending. But the churning produces thirteen things and only one of them is worth drinking. The rest are questions.

Host: Such as?

Teacher: Such as why a mountain had to be borrowed, and why nobody asked the mountain.

Teller

Dr. K. Sharma, Guwahati

Sources

Adi Parva, 2 commentaries
