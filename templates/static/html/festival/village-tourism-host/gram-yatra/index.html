The house The village Walks Stay

A village is not a backdrop.
It is somebody’s Tuesday.

I am Gangavva Hegde. My family has farmed areca and cardamom on this slope for four generations, and since 2016 we have kept four rooms for people who want to see how that actually works — the 6 a.m. cutting, the drying yard, the temple bell at seven, and a great deal of sitting still.

No pool, no buffet, no “village experience” staged for a camera. You will eat what we eat, walk where we walk, and be woken by the same rooster.

The four rooms

All four open onto the same verandah, which means all four share the same view and the same 6 a.m. noise. Two are in the old house, two in the cow-shed wing my father built in 1988 and we rebuilt in 2017.

Day one — arrive and look around

    Times are for a day in November. In December the light goes twenty minutes earlier and the falls road is slower. Nothing on this sheet is fixed until you are here and the weather has an opinion.

    One ordinary day

    1. 05.50

      The pump starts. If you are awake, the verandah is the best place in the district.

    2. 06.30

      Areca cutting. Two men, one tree at a time, a rope and a knife. Watch from the path, not from underneath.

    3. 08.15

      Ghatti breakfast: thick rice dumplings broken by hand, coconut chutney, a bowl of sambar, filter coffee.

    4. 10.00

      The drying yard. Turning the areca so it dries even, which is dull work and the reason the crop is worth anything.

    5. 13.00

      Lunch, then the house sleeps. This is not a figure of speech; the shutters come down and the dogs lie in the shade.

    6. 16.30

      Tea on the verandah, with jaggery and whatever fruit is going over.

    7. 19.00

      The temple bell, then dinner at half past eight, and a lantern on the path to the rooms.

    Four things to know before you book

    • There is no television, and the phone signal is one bar

      The verandah has a chair, a bookcase and a plug point for the one device you need charged. If you need to take calls, the roof is the only place that works, and it works best at noon.

    • We eat at seven and eight, not when you are hungry

      Dinner is at 20.30, one sitting, and it is vegetarian by default. Tell me about allergies and I will cook around them; tell me at the table and I will do my best, badly.

    • Do not walk into the plots without one of us

      The areca is thorny, the wells are open, and the bull is on a long rope in the afternoons. Any of us will take you; it takes twenty minutes and costs nothing.

    • Bring shoes you can ruin

      The laterite stains, the path to the tank is muddy until December, and there is no laundry service — only a line and a sun that dries anything by noon.
