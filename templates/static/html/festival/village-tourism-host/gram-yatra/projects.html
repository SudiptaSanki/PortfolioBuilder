The house The village Walks Stay

What we will actually take you to

Eight things within an hour of the house. Four are on the farm and cost nothing; four need a car and a packed lunch. We do not run them all in one week and we do not pretend they are secret.

01 · October to March

The areca cutting

Two men, one tree, a rope and a knife, starting at half past six. You watch from the path and are told why a tree is cut when it is cut, which has to do with the beetle, the buyer and the price on the previous Tuesday. Then the yard, where the nuts are spread and turned twice a day for six weeks.

Time 6.30 a.m., two hours Distance 1.2 km on foot Cost included in the room

02 · any morning

A ghatti breakfast, cooked by you

Rice soaked overnight, ground, kneaded and broken by hand into a coconut chutney and a sambar that has been on the fire since six. You will be given a job — usually the breaking — and your fingers will not do it right for the first ten minutes. It tastes better because of that.

Time 9.30 a.m., two hours Distance in the kitchen Cost ₹600 a person

03 · dawn, all season

The tank behind the plots

A twenty-minute walk to a temple tank that holds water all year. Herons, kingfishers, egrets, and in December a pair of lesser whistling ducks that arrive and leave on their own schedule. The path is muddy until the end of November and there are no railings.

Time 6.00 a.m., two hours Distance 2.5 km on foot Cost included; bring binoculars

04 · any day

Weaving an areca-leaf plate

Two houses down, the Shetty sisters weave plates and cups from the sheath of the areca palm for the temple and for weddings. An hour and a half, most of it spent being corrected. You keep what you make, which will be usable and slightly wrong.

Time 11.00 a.m., 1 hour 30 Distance 1.2 km on foot Cost ₹400, paid to the sisters

05 · Saturday only

The haat in Sirsi

The weekly market is the most interesting hour in the taluk: areca by the sack, bananas by the bunch, bangles, knife-sharpening on a bicycle, and jaggery that is better than anything in a shop. We go early, in the jeep, and are back before the heat.

Time 8.00 a.m., about two hours Distance 14 km by car, 0.8 on foot Cost ₹300 for the jeep

06 · a clear morning

Unchalli falls

A car to the top, then steps down through evergreen forest to a fall that drops a hundred metres into a gorge. The climb back up is the part nobody mentions. Go early, take water, and do not go past the rope at the bottom.

Time half a day from 7.00 a.m. Distance 32 km by car, 2 km on foot Cost ₹2,200 with the jeep and a packed lunch

07 · a half day

Banavasi and Sahasralinga

The Madhukeshwara temple at Banavasi, the old Kadamba capital, and then the stone shrines at Sahasralinga in the Shalmala river, where the carvings sit in the water and are best seen in January when the river is low. Two stops, one lunch, back by four.

Time 8.00 a.m., half a day Distance 44 km by car Cost ₹2,400 with the jeep and lunch

08 · November to February

A Yakshagana night

At the school ground, a painted face, a drum you can hear from the plots, and a story that runs until two in the morning if the audience stays. It is free, it is in Kannada, and it is not shortened for visitors. We will explain the plot on the way there and you will still be lost by midnight.

Time 8.30 p.m., three hours or more Distance 8 km by car Cost free; ₹300 for the jeep

What a day out costs

The jeep, the driver and the fuel are one price; the entrance fees, if any, are yours. We do not add a commission to anything, and the money for the leaf-plate weaving goes to the sisters directly.
