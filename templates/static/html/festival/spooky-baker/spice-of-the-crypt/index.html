II — WHAT COMES OUT

Four things worth the walk

The counter changes with the market, but these four are always there. Everything is baked the morning it is sold; nothing is frozen and nothing is kept for tomorrow.

FIRST THING IN THE CASE

Grave-Dirt Brownies

Black cocoa, brown butter, a spoon of espresso, smoked salt on top. Dense enough to cut clean and dark enough that children are a little suspicious. The first batch is always a little underdone; that is the batch we eat.

SATURDAYS ONLY

Pumpkin Rye Boule

Half rye, half bread flour, roasted pumpkin and a levain that has been alive since the bakery opened. The crumb is tight and sour; the crust takes a proper knife. It is the only loaf I have ever been given a nickname for.

SUNDAYS · UNTIL NOON

Banshee Buns

Cardamom in the dough and cardamom in the filling, which is not a mistake. The pearl sugar is Swedish and the shaping is not: I tie them in a knot that looks like a scream, which is where the name came from and which I now regret slightly.

YEAR ROUND

Ossuary Shortbread

Rice flour for snap, rosemary for the savoury edge, black sesame for the look of old stone. Cut into bones with a cutter my grandmother used for something else entirely. They ship badly and travel well, which is the opposite of most things.

IV — ORDERS

Cakes, trays, and the occasional funeral

I take custom orders for birthdays, wakes, and office trays. Two weeks’ notice for a cake, four days for a tray. If it is for a funeral, say so in the first line and I will move things.
