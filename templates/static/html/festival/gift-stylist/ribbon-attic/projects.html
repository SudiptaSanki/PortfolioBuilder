Job 118 · November 20251,200 parcels in 9 days

1,200 hampers for a publishing house

A Vienna publisher sends a book, a candle and a bag of coffee to every author, translator and bookseller it worked with that year. Twelve hundred parcels, one design, posted across Austria and Germany in the second week of December.

The design had to survive a courier and still look like a gift: kraft paper, a band of grosgrain in the house green, a printed card tucked under the ribbon rather than taped to the paper. The first forty were wrapped slowly and photographed. The remaining 1,160 were wrapped by four people at two tables, with the same paper cut to the same 42 × 30 cm sheet by a guillotine I borrowed from the printer downstairs.

Nine days, eleven rolls of paper, three kilometres of ribbon, and a stack of spare cards in case the courier crushed a corner. It is the largest job the attic has taken, and I said no to a second one of the same size in the same month.

Quantity
1,200 parcels, 1,180 delivered intact
Materials
Kraft 90 gsm, grosgrain 25 mm, printed card
Team
Four, including me, over nine days
Paper
11 × 150 m rolls; offcuts to a school
Price
€6.20 per parcel, materials included

What went wrong

“Forty parcels were packed on a wet Thursday and the paper cockled overnight in the van. We re-wrapped them on the Friday morning and changed the loading order for the rest.”
Job 126 · December 2025A bookshop on Währinger Straße

A window about reading in winter

The bookshop where I once wrapped badly, now a client, wanted a window that was not reindeer. We built a slanted shelf of parcels — forty of them, each wrapped in a different paper and none labelled — with one open book on a stand and a single line of text cut from black paper on the glass.

The parcels were empty boxes, weighted with sand so the stack could not shift when the door opened. The paper ran from brown kraft to a pale blue tissue that looked almost like snow in the window light. The bookseller sold more gift-wrapped books in that fortnight than in the previous December, which he attributes to the window and I attribute to the staff.

Installed
28 November 2025, one afternoon
Contents
40 boxes, 7 papers, one open book
Lighting
Two warm lamps, no coloured gels
Removed
6 January 2026; paper reused in the shop
Price
€640, installation included

From the brief

“No snowflakes, no reindeer, no gold. I want people to feel like reading.”
Job 097 · June 2025Burgenland · 240 favours

Two hundred and forty boxes, and one colour problem

A couple married in a vineyard near Rust and wanted favours that matched the invitations: a dusty rose that looked one way on a screen and another in the barn. I wrapped three samples and drove them out on a Saturday, and we chose the second one under the actual light of the actual barn.

Each favour was a small kraft box with a band of velvet ribbon, a sprig of dried lavender from the bride's aunt's garden, and a name card letterpressed by a friend. The boxes were assembled flat, filled on the Friday, and carried to the tables in four trays that I covered in the same paper as the boxes.

Two hundred and forty, plus twenty spares. The spares went home with the bride's mother, who has since asked me to wrap a christening present and told me the lavender still smells.

Quantity
240 favours + 20 spares
Materials
Kraft box, velvet 38 mm, dried lavender, letterpress card
Colour
Matched in the barn, not on a screen
Assembly
Boxes flat, filled on site the day before
Price
€5.40 each, including the boxes

From the couple

“She drove out with three samples at eight in the morning. Nobody does that. We picked the one that looked like the invitations, not the one that looked best on the phone.”
Job 081 · October 2024A museum shop, first district

A wrapping counter that paid for itself

A museum shop sells reproductions, books and small ceramics, and was losing money on gift wrap: staff wrapped slowly, paper was stored badly, and nobody wanted to do it on a busy Saturday. I spent three days behind the counter, then rebuilt the whole station.

The result was a wall-mounted roll holder at counter height, a cutter on a wire, ribbon pre-cut to the two lengths that cover 90% of purchases, and a laminated card of four folds. Staff were trained in a two-hour session. Wrapping time fell from about five minutes to under two, and the shop started charging for it, which it had been afraid to do.

Work
Station design, stock, two training sessions
Change
Wrap time 5 min → under 2 min
Stock
Three papers, four ribbons, paper tape
Result
Gift wrap became a paid service in 2025
Price
€1,900 including the fixtures

Shop manager

“We had the paper, we had the ribbon, we did not have a system. Now the Saturday staff fight over who gets the wrapping desk.”
Job 141 · March 2026One object, five days

A box for a cello bow

A cellist with the Vienna Philharmonic was giving a bow to a student and wanted it presented rather than posted. A bow is 72 centimetres long, slightly curved, and about as easy to wrap as a wet rope. Paper alone would have looked like a rolled carpet.

I made a slim box from 1.5 mm greyboard, lined it with wool felt from the passementerie in Ottakring, and built a shallow cradle inside so the bow could not move. The outside was covered in a natural linen paper with a single band of velvet and no bow at all — a knot, because a bow on a bow is a joke that stops being funny at the second telling.

Five days, mostly drying time between the glue and the paper. The student opened it in a rehearsal room and, according to the cellist, refused to take the bow out for a full minute.

Object
Cello bow, 72 cm, curved
Box
1.5 mm greyboard, felt cradle, linen paper
Finish
Velvet band, plain knot, no tag
Time
Five days, including drying
Price
€145

The cellist

“I asked for a box. I got something that made a grown student stand still. Worth every cent, and I am not generous about these things.”
