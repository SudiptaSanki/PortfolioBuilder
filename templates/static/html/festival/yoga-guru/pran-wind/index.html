Chapter 01

The practice is the breath, not the shape

6:00 – 9:00, six days

You can put a body into most positions with enough instruction. What you cannot do by instruction is make the mind stay in the room while the body is there. That is why we begin with the breath and not with the sequence.

My classes are small on purpose: six mats in the morning, eight in the evening. I need to be able to see your ribs and your jaw, because those are where people hold what they are not saying. A class of thirty is a performance. A class of six is a practice.

What a class looks like

Twenty minutes of settling: lying down, knees bent, hands on the lower ribs. Then standing work, slow, usually four or five postures repeated rather than twenty visited once. Then a seated forward fold held long enough to become boring, which is the point. Then ten minutes of quiet, and I stop talking.

No adjustments without asking. I will offer a hand at the sacrum or the shoulder blades and you may say no. There is no correction here that is worth being startled for.

“I do not teach people to touch their toes. I teach them to notice the moment they decide to stop breathing.” Leela, in an interview, 2023

The shala is on the third cross in Gokulam, up one flight, with a red floor and a plaster ceiling that has been patched four times. Mats and bolsters are here. Bring a towel in April.

Chapter 02 · The builder

Make a practice you can keep

Add postures in the order you want them. The clock adds up. Press begin and the mat-side player counts each posture down, with the breath cue running underneath. Keep it under twenty minutes if you are new; the body does not need a long practice, it needs a repeated one.
