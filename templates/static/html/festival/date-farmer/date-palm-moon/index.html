The planner

Work back from the fruit

A date is not ripe when it is sweet; it is ripe when it has finished turning. The four stages below run from the day the female spathe is opened and pollinated by hand. Choose a variety, a block size and a pollination date, and the planner will lay out the season for you.

Pollination is done from the ground and from a rope ladder, one spathe at a time. A block of 240 palms takes our crew of four about six days.

The grove

1,240 palms in the ground

My grandfather planted the first sixty palms in 1978, in a strip of land that the salt had already given up on. Date palms will take water that would kill most crops, and they will take salt with it, which is why the grove starts where the wheat stops. Nine blocks now, four generations, and the same three varieties, because those are the three that have proved they can finish in this heat.

01–03

Khalas

620 palms. The backbone of the harvest: firm, amber, and the one the traders ask for by name. Ripens early, which is a risk when the monsoon is late.

04–06

Aseel

410 palms. Darker, softer, heavier in the hand. Slower to dry, so it goes to the mat house rather than the shelf.

07–09

Barhi

210 palms. Picked at khalal while it is still crisp, packed in trays and sent to Ahmedabad and Mumbai within three days.

A year in the grove

What happens, and when

The work follows the palm, not the office diary. These are the months we keep, with the moon notes that decide the exact week.

  1. Jan

    Pruning the old fronds, cleaning the crowns, repairing the rope ladders before the climb.

  2. Feb

    Pollination. Spathes open over three weeks; the crew goes up twice a day while the pollen is fresh.

  3. Mar

    First irrigation of the season. The well is tested for salinity, which rises every year.

  4. Apr

    Bunch thinning. Removing a third of the strands is what makes the rest worth picking.

  5. May

    Bagging the early bunches against birds; the khalal colour starts in the last week.

  6. Jun

    Heat, and the long wait. Nets go over blocks 1–3. Nothing is picked.

  7. Jul

    Barhi comes in green and crisp. The mat house is swept and the boxes are folded.

  8. Aug

    Khalas and Aseel. Sixteen-hour days, two pickings a week, drying on the roof.

  9. Sep

    Last picking, sorting, and the slow work of packing for the year.

  10. Oct

    Frond cutting, manure, and the first repair of the walls after the rain.

  11. Nov

    Offshoots are separated from the mother palms and potted for the next block.

  12. Dec

    Rest. The well is cleaned, the accounts are settled, and the seed stock is ordered.

Three varieties

Taste is a weather record

The same palm, watered differently, gives a different date. These are the three we grow and the three we can defend.

Khalas

Blocks 1–3 · 620 palms · harvest Aug

Firm at the shoulder, honey at the tip. The sugar is clean rather than heavy, which is why it goes with coffee and not with dessert.

Aseel

Blocks 4–6 · 410 palms · harvest Aug–Sep

Dark, soft, and a little smoky at the skin. It does not travel as well as Khalas, so most of it stays in Kutch and Gujarat.

Barhi

Blocks 7–9 · 210 palms · harvest Jul

Eaten crisp and yellow, before it ever becomes a date. It snaps like an apple and tastes of sugarcane. Three days from palm to tray.

Medjool

Trial block · 40 palms

We are four years into a trial with Medjool on the eastern edge. It is thirsty and it splits in a wet August, but the fruit is the largest we have grown. Ask me again in 2029.
