Three generations · since 1978

A house that was
built for the morning light.

My grandfather built Stillhaus because he liked where the sun landed at eight o’clock. Everything since — the nine rooms, the long table, the boot room, the stove — has been an argument about how little to change.

01The family

Three Brunners, one set of keys

Josef Brunner was a carpenter in Bregenz who spent every winter weekend on the Arlberg and every summer wondering how to stay there. In 1976 he bought the plot above Zug from a farmer who wanted the money for a new barn, and in 1978 he built nine rooms with his own crew and a borrowed crane. The house opened on 20 December 1978 with four guests, all of them friends of his mother.

His son Martin took over in 1994, put in the sauna, doubled the kitchen, and made the decision that still defines the place: no televisions in the rooms, one sitting for dinner. My father believed that a house full of people who eat together stops being a hotel and stays a house. He ran it until 2016, when his knees told him to stop carrying luggage up the stairs.

I grew up in the room above the kitchen, studied hospitality in Innsbruck, worked four seasons in St. Moritz and one in a fishing lodge in Norway, and came back in 2014. I have run the house since 2017 with my partner Jonas, who cooks, and two people from the village who have worked here longer than I have.

What has changed: the heating, the insulation, the Wi-Fi, and the coffee. What has not: the stove, the stairs, the number of rooms, and the fact that I know every guest’s name by the second evening.

The first winter

Four guests, one wood stove, and a hand-written tariff of 180 schillings a night.

9

Rooms, always

Two plans to add four more were drawn and abandoned: in 1989 and in 2006. The long table cannot seat more than twenty-two.

02How the house runs

The staff, the food, the rules

There are four of us in winter: me, Jonas in the kitchen, Anna on the floors, and Sepp, who has driven the village bus for thirty years and comes in at six to light the fires and clear the steps. Nobody wears a uniform. If you need something, you ask one of us, and one of us will get it or tell you honestly that we cannot.

Dinner is at 19:30 because that is when the last guest comes down from the mountain. There is one sitting because two sittings turn a dinner into a service. Jonas cooks what the valley gives him — lake fish from Bodensee, cheese from a dairy in Klösterle, lamb from a farm above Zürs in autumn — and he will cook around an allergy if you tell him at breakfast, not at the table.

  1. Quiet
    The house is quiet after 22:30

    The stairs carry sound and the walls are 1978. There is a small sitting room downstairs for anyone still awake.

  2. Boots
    Boots stay in the boot room

    Not for tidiness: the wooden floors are original and the salt eats them. There is a heated rack and a bench that fits six.

  3. Dogs
    Dogs in the two ground-floor rooms

    By arrangement, and not in the dining room during dinner. There is a bowl by the door and a field behind the house.

  4. Smoking
    Outside only

    On the terrace, under the eaves, with the ashtray that my grandfather made from a piece of the original roof.

03Off the mountain

What we tell guests who do not ski

Walk

Zug to Lech, the river path

Fifty minutes along the Lech, flat, cleared every morning by the commune. Go early and you will have it to yourself; go at four and you will meet every dog in the village.

Look

The Huber house, Lech

A 400-year-old wooden house with a collection of carved masks and a very good small museum upstairs. Closed on Mondays, which surprises everyone.

Soak

The Aqualino, Lech

Indoor pool and sauna, ten minutes by bus. Guests with children use it on the third day, when everyone needs a break from each other.

Eat

Kaffeehaus, Lech

Strudel at four, at a table by the window. Jonas will pretend he is not jealous and then ask what you had.

Come and meet the stairs.

They are steep, original and not negotiable. If that is a problem, say so and I will tell you honestly whether the house suits you — there are two good hotels in Lech with a lift and I will name them.
