Where I have danced, and what I danced

Eight evenings from the last three years. A stage is a stage whether it is a proscenium in Hyderabad or a temple courtyard with one microphone and a generator, and the programme changes accordingly.

No. 128 · February 2026

Kuchipudi Mahotsavam, Kuchipudi village

The one I say yes to every year. A temporary stage in front of the temple, a generator that hums through the quiet parts, and an audience that knows the story better than I do. I danced Bhamakalapam in misra gati, and the old men in the third row mouthed the words along with the recording.

Programme Bhamakalapam, tarangam Tala Adi, misra gati Runs 34 minutes With live mridangam, vocal, cymbals

No. 124 · November 2025

Ravindra Bharathi, Hyderabad

A full solo evening, ninety minutes with an interval, which is a long time to hold a hall that size. The second half was lighter than the first on purpose: the padam in misra chapu, then a javali, then the thillana. Two people left at the interval and I have decided not to take it personally.

Programme Six items Tala Adi, rupaka, misra chapu Runs 92 minutes With live orchestra, five musicians

No. 119 · August 2025

A school in Secunderabad, three mornings

Sixty children, ages nine to fourteen, none of whom had seen classical dance except on a phone. We did syllables, then walking in tala, then a two-line jati they could all do together. On the third morning they performed it for the rest of the school, badly and with enormous concentration.

Programme Rhythm and stance Tala Adi, chaturashra Runs 3 mornings, 90 min each With two assistants

No. 113 · March 2025

With a jazz trio, Goethe-Zentrum

An experiment: Kuchipudi jatis against a double bass and a drum kit, with no mridangam and no singer. It worked for about eleven minutes and then the two rhythmic languages started arguing. The tarangam at the end, unaccompanied, was the best thing in the evening — proof that the plate does not need help.

Programme Jatiswaram, tarangam, improvisation Tala Adi, rupaka Runs 55 minutes With piano, bass, drums

No. 108 · January 2025

A temple courtyard, Warangal district

A festival committee, a stone stage that had been swept that morning, and one microphone for the whole evening. The audience sat on the ground and stayed for two hours. I cut the javali — it does not work for a temple audience at eleven at night — and put the sabdam in its place.

Programme Shabdam, padam, thillana Tala Khanda chapu, misra chapu Runs 48 minutes With recorded music, reluctantly

No. 101 · October 2024

Nrityagram, Bengaluru

An outdoor stage at dusk, which changes everything: the light goes during the second item and the last twenty minutes are performed under lamps that were not designed for dance. The audience could not see my face for the padam, so I danced it for the people in the first three rows and hoped.

Programme Jatiswaram, padam, tarangam Tala Rupaka, misra chapu, adi Runs 52 minutes With live mridangam and violin

No. 094 · June 2024

A film shoot for a museum, Chennai

Six items filmed over two days for a permanent gallery on classical forms. The camera wants everything slower and smaller than a stage does, and the director kept asking for the same jati again from a different angle. Exhausting, and the only record of the tarangam I am happy with.

Programme Six items for camera Tala Adi, rupaka Runs 2 days, 14 min final cut With film crew of nine

No. 087 · February 2024

A workshop for Bharatanatyam teachers, Hyderabad

Fourteen teachers who wanted to know what is different about Kuchipudi besides the plate. We spent the morning on the upper body — Kuchipudi lets the torso move in a way Bharatanatyam does not — and the afternoon on the tarangam, which half of them tried and all of them respected.

Programme Technique and tarangam Tala Adi, tisra gati Runs one day With 14 teachers
