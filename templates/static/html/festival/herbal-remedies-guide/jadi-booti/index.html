The shelf · botanical plates

The eight I would not run out of

Each entry gives the part used, what it is good for, and the caution that matters. If a plant has no caution worth printing, it is not on this shelf.

Ocimum tenuiflorum

Tulsi holy basil

Part
Leaf, fresh or dried
For
Cold and cough, a heavy head, the first day of a fever
Caution
May lower blood sugar. Careful with diabetes medication and blood thinners.

Zingiber officinale

Adrak ginger

Part
Fresh rhizome, grated
For
Nausea, a cold stomach, the ache before a fever breaks
Caution
Large doses thin the blood. Stop a week before surgery; go easy with warfarin.

Foeniculum vulgare

Sonf fennel

Part
Seed, lightly crushed
For
Bloating, griping after a heavy meal, a baby's colic (as weak tea, over one year)
Caution
Fine as food. Avoid medicinal doses in pregnancy and with epilepsy.

Cinnamomum verum

Dalchini true cinnamon

Part
Inner bark, one small quill
For
A sluggish digestion, a warm evening drink, a little sweetness without sugar
Caution
Use Ceylon, not cassia. Cassia is high in coumarin, which is hard on the liver in daily doses.

Glycyrrhiza glabra

Mulethi licorice

Part
Root, cut in short sticks
For
A raw throat, a dry cough, hoarseness after a long day of talking
Caution
Raises blood pressure and holds water. No daily use with hypertension, kidney disease or pregnancy.

Bacopa monnieri

Brahmi water hyssop

Part
Whole leaf, dried
For
Concentration, a frayed evening, the day before an exam
Caution
May upset the stomach and does not mix well with sedatives. Not in pregnancy.

Plates drawn from fresh material in the workshop, except mulethi, which came dry from a supplier in Neemuch. No illustration here is copied from another book.

How I work

Four rules, learned the hard way

  1. 01

    One plant at a time

    Before you mix three herbs, you should know what one does to you. Start with a single leaf tea, a small cup, and a note in the margin about how you slept.

  2. 02

    Weight, not handfuls

    A "handful" is not a dose. I weigh dry herbs in grams and measure fresh ones in teaspoons. The recipe cards give both where it matters.

  3. 03

    Name the risk

    Every plant on this shelf can be too much. The caution line is not legal decoration; it is the most useful sentence on the card.

  4. 04

    Two weeks, then stop

    Unless a vaidya has prescribed otherwise, a home kadha is a two-week course. If nothing has changed, the plant was not the answer.
