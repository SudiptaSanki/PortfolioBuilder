Ragas & courses · July–December 2026

Six courses. One raga at a time.

Every course runs for a term and ends with a baithak, not a certificate. The fees are monthly; the raga is yours for as long as you keep singing it.

F-01 · Foundation6 seats

Raga Yaman

Kalyan thaat · early evening · the first raga

Yaman is where most voices learn to lift. The tivra Ma teaches the ear to reach without straining, and the evening prahar gives the raga time to settle.

You will learn the aroha and avaroha, three palte in Yaman, one vilambit bandish in teentaal and a short drut composition. We listen to two archival recordings and take them apart.

Duration12 weeks

F-02 · Foundation4 seats

Raga Bhairav

Bhairav thaat · sunrise · the slow morning

Bhairav asks for a different patience. The komal Re and komal Dha are not ornaments; they are the raga's whole argument. We sing it at six in the morning, because that is when it makes sense.

Includes the aroha–avaroha, a morning bandish, and the first lesson in andolan — the slow, deliberate shake that is not a vibrato.

Duration10 weeks

I-01 · IntermediateFull

Kafi & the thumri line

Kafi thaat · late evening · text first

Kafi opens the door to thumri, hori and dadra. The komal Ga and komal Ni give the raga its sideways smile, and the text decides where the phrase leans.

We work on one thumri and one hori, with attention to bol-banaav — the way a word is stretched, repeated and left unfinished.

Raga Bhimpalasi

Kafi thaat · late afternoon · the ache

Bhimpalasi is a raga of the hour before evening, when the light is going and nobody has turned the lamp on. Its komal Ga and komal Ni make the ascent feel like a question.

We build a vilambit bandish with a long, unhurried vistar, then a drut composition that lets the raga run without losing its weight.

A-01 · Advanced2 seats

Darbari Kanada

Asavari thaat · late night · the slow one

Darbari cannot be hurried. The oscillated komal Ga and komal Dha need a voice that has already learned to wait. We sing it late, when the house is quiet and the tanpura is the loudest thing in the room.

Includes a vilambit bandish in jhumra, a drut composition in teentaal, and the beginnings of a taan vocabulary that stays inside the raga.

T-01 · Technique6 seats

Laya & taan

All ragas · rhythm first · no hiding

A course for singers who can carry a raga but lose their place when the tempo moves. We work with teentaal, jhaptaal and rupak, first clapping, then singing, then improvising.

You will learn to build a taan from the raga's own phrases rather than from a pattern book, and to land on sam because you meant to, not because the tabla pushed you.

Duration8 weeks
ClassSat 10:30–11:45
Starts18 July 2026
NeedsThree years of study

How to choose. Foundation courses assume nothing except a voice and a willingness to sit still. Intermediate courses assume you can hold Sa against a tanpura and have learned at least four bandishes.
