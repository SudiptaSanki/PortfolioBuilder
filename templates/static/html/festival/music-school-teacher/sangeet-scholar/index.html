The method

Seventy-five minutes, in four movements.

Every lesson has the same shape, whether it is a first month or a fifteenth year. The raga changes; the order does not.

01

The drone 10 minutes · tanpura

We tune the tanpura to your Sa, not mine. Then you sit with it and do nothing else. The first job is to hear whether you are sharp. Most new students are. Most fix it within a month.

02

The palte 20 minutes · alankars

Patterns: Sa Re Ga Ma, Ma Ga Re Sa, then in threes and fours. They look mechanical and they are — that is the point. The palte build the ear before the ego arrives. We do not move on until the pattern is even at both ends.

03

The bandish 25 minutes · composition

A bandish in the raga of the term, on a text worth singing. We learn the sthayi first, then the antara, then where to breathe. Breath is part of the composition, not an interruption to it.

04

The vistar 20 minutes · improvisation

Now you take the raga apart slowly: aroha, avaroha, vistar, and only then a taan. Improvisation is not decoration. It is the test of whether the raga has entered the voice or is still sitting on the page.

A house rule. One raga at a time. Students often ask to learn four in a term; I would rather they leave able to sing one at three in the morning without a book. Depth is slower, and it lasts.
