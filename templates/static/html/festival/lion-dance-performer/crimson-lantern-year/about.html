The troupe · working page

Nineteen seasons, run the same way

Daniel Teo first carried a pair of cymbals at eleven, in a community troupe in Springvale, because he was the smallest person who could be trusted to keep time. Nineteen seasons later he leads the Crimson Lantern Troupe — head performer under the lion, and the person who keeps its ledger. This page is the working half of the site: who does what, what we bring, the space we need, and the terms we book under.

Roles & minimums

A lion is always two people

These minimums are why the troupe sizes on the booking sheet are what they are. They are not padded: below them, the performance is not the performance.

Lion — head & tailTwo performers per lion, always. The head works the eyes, ears and mouth; the tail is the spine and the lift. Pairs train together for a full season before performing as a pair.
DrumOne drummer, who leads the performance — the lion dances to the drum, not the other way round. A spare drummer travels with every booking of two lions or more.
Gong & cymbalsOne gong and at least one pair of cymbals. The percussion minimum for any booking is drum, gong and one cymbal pair — below that the lion has nothing to answer.
Cai qing hostThe robed figure who guides the lion, presents the lettuce and works the crowd — the face of the troupe while everyone else is under a costume or behind an instrument.
CoordinatorRuns the route, the clock and the client. On the day, this is who you talk to — the performers are not reachable mid-performance, by design.
Troupe minimumSeven, for the smallest school programme. The troupe does not perform below its minimums, and does not send a “lite” version under its own name.

Kit register

What travels with us

Two performance lionsSouthern-style heads in the Fut San pattern — bamboo and papier-mâché frames, fur-trimmed bodies. Repaired and repainted every winter; a head is retired, never patched past its season.
Pole set (jong)Twelve poles from 0.8 m to 2.8 m on weighted steel bases, used only where our own survey has measured 6.5 m of clear ceiling. The set travels with its own floor mats.
Drum, gong, cymbalsOne barrel drum on a stand, one gong, two pairs of cymbals — plus a damped drum skin reserved for school and indoor programmes.
Cai qing kitHost costume, lettuces for every stop, a calligraphy scroll for the final stop, and red packets for demonstration. Clients provide their own packet for the lion if they wish to give one.
Banner & flagsTroupe banner and two carriers' flags for parade work, where the format includes them.

Space & safety, in print

The rules that decide bookings

FloorsDry, level and clear. Polished stone, wet tiles and loose gravel are refused — a tail performer works blind, trusting the floor completely. If we would not fall on it, we will not dance on it.
Ceilings2.7 m for a standard visit, 4 m for a ground set, 6.5 m clear for the pole set — measured by us, at our survey, not taken from a venue brochure. Case 02 is what happens when the numbers disagree.
Wet weatherPapier-mâché heads do not perform in rain. Outdoor bookings carry a written rain plan: move under cover, move indoors, or take a reschedule credit — decided with the client by 7 am on the day. No troupe member is ever asked to “push through” weather in a costume they cannot see out of properly.
VolumeA lion dance is loud at close range — that is its job. Indoors we damp the drum on request; for schools and sensitive audiences the full adapted programme on the booking sheet applies. Firecrackers are never part of our act.
Crowd lineA two-metre working edge around the performance area, kept by the coordinator and the host. Children are welcome at the front — behind the line, where the lion can come to them.

Booking terms

How a date is held

  1. Lead times. Peak season dates (Day 1 to the first weekend) usually book four to six months ahead; the 2027 peak opens in September 2026. Off-season dates are often available inside a month.
  2. Deposit. Thirty per cent holds the date and takes it out of the ledger. The balance is due on the day of performance.
  3. Surveys. Atrium and pole-set bookings include our own space survey before the deposit is taken. If the space fails, there is nothing to cancel — the booking is reshaped or declined first.
  4. Cancellation. More than 60 days out: deposit returned in full. 30–60 days: deposit held as credit for a future date. Under 30 days in the peak fortnight: deposit is retained, because the date cannot be resold.
  5. Weather. A rain-plan reschedule under the wet-weather rule is never treated as a cancellation, by either side.
