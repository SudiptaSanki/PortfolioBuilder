The order of an evening

What four hours actually looks like

People imagine henna as a queue. It is closer to surgery with gossip: the paste has to sit, the hand has to stay still, and the bride has to eat before her fingers are joined.

  1. 16:00

    Paste out of the freezer

    Cones come out two hours before the first hand. Cold paste will not flow; warm paste will run. It gets squeezed onto a cloth first to check the line.

  2. 17:30

    Feet first, then hands

    Feet take the longest and are the least interrupted. The bride sits with her feet on a bolster, and the pattern goes up to the ankle in one continuous line.

  3. 19:30

    Palm, wrist, fingers

    The centre is drawn last so the bride can still hold a cup. When the palms are done, the hands are turned over and the backs are filled with the same motif, mirrored.

  4. 21:00

    The wrap and the wait

    Lemon-sugar dabbed on, then cling film and a sock on each foot. She sleeps in it. The colour comes up over the next thirty-six hours and settles for ten days.
