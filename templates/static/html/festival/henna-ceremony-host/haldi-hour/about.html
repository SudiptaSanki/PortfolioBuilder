Practice

Paste, patience, and a bowl of water.

Mehndi is a stain, not a paint. What makes it last is the powder, the mixing, the body heat under the wrap and the two days nobody touches water. Everything else is decoration.

Where the powder comes from

I buy henna powder from two families in Sojat, Rajasthan, where the leaf is dried and milled. The powder is sifted three times through a muslin cloth here in Jaipur until it is the texture of talcum. Anything left in the sieve goes to the compost, not into a cone.

The paste is mixed the night before a ceremony: powder, strong tea, lemon juice, a spoon of sugar, a few drops of clove oil. It rests twelve hours in a covered bowl so the dye releases. In the morning it is tested on the inside of my wrist — if the line comes up orange in an hour, it is ready.

I pour my own cones from the same mix. Shop cones are often cut with dye to look black and stain fast; a real henna line is green-brown when it goes on and comes up the colour of a dried rose over two days.

The mix, in weights

  • Henna powder500 g
  • Strong tea, cooled600 ml
  • Lemon juice60 ml
  • Sugar3 tsp

  • 01

    One hand at a time

    I take two brides a day at most. A bridal set of hands and feet is four hours of drawing and the quality drops sharply after the third hour. The second artist on my team does guest hands only.

  • 02

    Skin first, pattern second

    Every guest is asked about allergies, recent waxing and any cuts on the hand. Henna does not go over broken skin, and it does not go on a child under five. I keep a small pot of coconut oil for anyone who wants the paste lifted early.

  • 03

    Fresh cones, used or thrown

    Cones are poured the morning of the ceremony and any left at midnight are frozen once, then used for a test hand. Nothing is kept from one wedding to the next.

  • 04

    The haldi is ground at six

    Turmeric, gram flour and milk, ground fresh on the morning, never the day before. A paste that sits overnight goes sour and stains the skin yellow for a week instead of a day.

  • 05

    Flowers counted, not guessed

    Marigold is ordered by the kilo from the Phool Mandi at four in the morning: 42 kg for an average wedding, half of it for the string curtains, the rest for the haldi thali and the floor.

  • 06

    No photograph of a hand I have not drawn

    Every pattern in the book on this site was drawn by me or by the two artists I train with. I do not copy another artist's bridal set and call it a design.
