6 plates

  • Silver plate · 28 cm · nine items · 2026

    Sharma–Bansal wedding

    A plate for the kanyadaan table, engraved with both family marks set opposite each other. The well had to be shallow enough for a silver katori with a foot.

  • Brass · 24 cm · five items · 2026

    Vaishali Nagar house-warming

    A plate for a family moving into a flat with a small puja niche. We made it 2 cm narrower than standard and added a hanging ring so it can live on the wall when it is not in use.

  • Copper · 26 cm · seven items · 2025

    Navratri set, C-Scheme

    Three copper plates, one for each of the three nights the family hosts. Each carries a different ninth-night motif: a lamp, a trident, a lion.

  • Ashtadhatu · 26 cm · nine items · 2025

    An heirloom for a Barmer family

    Eight metals, cast rather than raised, with the family's 1911 wedding mark chased into the rim. It weighs just over a kilogram and is the only plate we have made that needed two people at the anvil.

  • Brass · 22 cm · five items · 2025

    A first plate for a new shop

    Ordered by a stationer on MI Road for the morning of his opening. Small, thick-rimmed, engraved with a ledger motif that matches the shop's signboard.

  • Brass · 22 cm · six items · 2025

    Diwali plate, Mansarovar

    Six items, against the rule, because the family wanted two diyas on the plate for the Lakshmi puja. We set the second lamp opposite the first so the even count reads as a pair rather than a gap.

What we need from you

Three things: the occasion, the date, and whether the plate is to be used or given. A used plate is sized to the shelf or the niche; a given plate is sized to the wrapping cloth and the journey.

If there is a family mark, send a photograph of it on any old object. We will redraw it at the right scale and keep it in the order book, so a second plate years later matches the first.
