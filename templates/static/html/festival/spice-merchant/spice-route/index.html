Folio 01 The shop on Bazaar Road

I sell spices by the weather they were dried in.

A chilli dried slowly in a Guntur shed in February tastes of fruit and smoke. The same chilli dried in a mechanical dryer in May tastes of heat and nothing else. I buy the February lots, which is why the shelf changes and the price list has an asterisk.

    04

    The year, harvest by harvest

    Jan–Feb

    Guntur chillies

    The cold-dried crop from Andhra comes down by lorry. I taste every fifth sack for smoke.

    Mar–Apr

    Pepper

    Wayanad berries are picked green, sun-dried until black, and graded by size at the estate.

    Jun–Jul

    Cumin & coriander

    Unjha and Kota arrive together. The monsoon decides how long they take to dry.

    Sep–Oct

    Cardamom

    Idukki pods are picked at 8 mm, cured slowly, and never bleached. The good lots go fast.

    Oct–Nov

    Saffron

    Pampore picks at dawn for two weeks only. A gram is a small season in a vial.

    Four things the shop will not do

    1. i

      No colour, no chalk

      Turmeric is turmeric. If a powder is neon, someone has been at it with a dye drum.

    2. ii

      No pre-ground stock

      Powders are ground to order on Thursdays and sell out. Nothing sits in the window going flat.

    3. iii

      No anonymous lots

      Every sack on the floor has a talc tag: village, month, moisture, and the price I paid.

    4. iv

      No hurry

      Smell, taste, then buy. The shop has been on Bazaar Road since 1974 and is not going anywhere.
