Lots · 2022 to this week

What leaves the shop in pouches.

Restaurants, caterers and one hospital canteen buy by the kilo; families buy by the hundred grams. The lots below are the ones still running, with the weight, the rhythm and what the kitchen says about them.

Current lot book

Lot 26-11-G
Running since 2023

Potli’s house garam masala

A 22-seat kitchen in Mehrauli that cooks with a wood oven and a very short menu. Their chef wanted a blend that could go into a dal at the end and onto a grilled peach at the start. We settled on less clove than a Delhi household blend and a higher cardamom share, so it reads floral rather than sweet. Four kilos a month, ground the morning it is collected.

Weight 4 kg / month
Rhythm First Tuesday, 8 a.m.
Ratio Coriander 32 · cumin 22 · cardamom 20 · cinnamon 15 · clove 11
Tasting note “Less Christmas, more garden.” — the chef, in the ledger, 2024

Lot 26-10-C
One order, 240 hampers

A wedding, 240 cotton pouches

A Gurugram family asked for something guests would actually use: 90 g of chai masala in a cotton pouch with a printed card and a small brass spoon. We weighed, ground and filled across three days in October. The card carries the lot number and the village of the cardamom, which the bride's father thought was a nice touch and the printer thought was unnecessary.

Weight 240 × 90 g = 21.6 kg
Rhythm Delivered in 12 crates, 2 days before
Ratio Cardamom 32 · cinnamon 22 · ginger 18 · clove 14 · pepper 14
Tasting note One pouch returned for more; eleven guests asked for the shop's address.

Lot 26-09-T
Running since 2019

Chai masala for a Lodhi Road tea shop

Twelve kilos a month for a shop that boils its tea in a copper vessel and has done so since 1962. Their only instruction, every month: keep it the same. I keep a sealed 200 g sample of the first delivery in the drawer and compare the new grind against it on the nose before the sack leaves.

Weight 12 kg / month
Rhythm Every second Friday
Ratio Cardamom 32 · cinnamon 22 · ginger 18 · clove 14 · pepper 14
Tasting note Ground a shade coarser than a household blend so the strainer catches it.

Lot 26-08-S
Running since 2021

Saffron and cardamom for a Chandni Chowk halwai

Forty grams of mongra saffron and two kilos of 8 mm cardamom a month for kheer, rasmalai and a saffron syrup that goes on warm jalebi. The halwai's son tests the saffron by dropping three strands into a glass of milk at the counter. If the colour takes a minute to bloom, the lot goes back.

Weight 40 g saffron · 2 kg cardamom
Rhythm Monthly, on the 5th
Tasting note Mongra only, no yellow style; the syrup is strained through muslin.

Lot 26-07-P
Running since 2024

Sambar podi for a South Indian caterer in Mayur Vihar

Eight kilos a week from a caterer who cooks for temple festivals and office lunches. He roasts his own dals and only buys the spice base from me, so the podi is heavier on coriander and pepper than a finished sambar powder. The chilli is Guntur Teja, sun-dried, ground with the seeds in.

Weight 8 kg / week
Rhythm Thursday afternoon
Ratio Coriander 34 · chilli 20 · cumin 16 · pepper 14 · turmeric 10 · mustard 6
Tasting note “Roast your dal, not my podi.” — written on the invoice

A baking and cooking school in Saket wanted a teaching shelf: 24 spices in matching glass jars, each with a card naming the village, the harvest month and three things it goes with. We built it over six weeks, replacing anything that lost its nose before the term started.

Weight 24 jars × 250 g, plus refills

Lot 26-05-R
Running since 2022

Low-chilli rasam podi for a hospital canteen

The canteen at a hospital in Saket serves rasam to patients on bland diets. The blend keeps the pepper and drops the chilli to almost nothing, with a little extra cumin for warmth. It is the only order I grind twice, because a fine powder is easier on a sore throat.

Weight 6 kg / week
Rhythm Monday, before the kitchen opens
Ratio Coriander 38 · pepper 26 · cumin 22 · turmeric 10 · chilli 4
Tasting note Sharp, warm, no burn; the dietitian signs off each batch.

How a commission runs

Step 01

Taste

Come to the shop, or send a sample of what you cook with now. We taste against two or three lots on the counter.

Step 02

Fix the ratio

We weigh a 200 g trial, grind it, and you cook with it once before anything is agreed. The trial is free.

Step 03

Reserve the lot

If it works, the sack is tagged with your name and the monthly weight is held. No deposit below 10 kg a month.

Step 04

Keep it the same

A sealed sample of the approved grind stays in the drawer. Every delivery is checked against it, by nose, before it leaves.

PackingWhole spices travel in cotton bags inside a kraft carton. Powders go into nitrogen-flushed 500 g or 1 kg pouches; larger orders in food-grade tins. There is a ₹40 packing charge on orders under 2 kg.

DeliveryWithin Delhi, by the shop's own boy on Tuesdays and Fridays, ₹150 or free over ₹2,500. Outside Delhi, by insured courier at cost; saffron always travels in a sealed vial.
