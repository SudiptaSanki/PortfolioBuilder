The bellows bench

A button box does not give you every note in both directions. On a diatonic row the bellows choose half the scale, and the bass hand follows: tonic when you close, dominant when you open. Set a row, pick a button and hear what comes out. The sound stays off until you press for it.

The bench shows one octave of a two-row box. On a real keyboard the same logic repeats ten or eleven buttons along each row — and the bass hand keeps pairing the tonic with the push and the dominant with the pull.

m. 04

What a night sounds like

Two 45-minute sets, then a break long enough to refill a glass. The first hour leans on waltzes and old parish favourites while the room fills; after that the tempo climbs and stays there until the last polka, which is always the same one.

Requests are welcome and usually granted. If the hall has a favourite — a wedding polka, a grandmother's waltz, a tune the lodge has sung since 1952 — tell me a month ahead and it will be learned properly rather than faked at volume.
