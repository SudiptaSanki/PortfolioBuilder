Six yukata in the studio book

These are the designs the studio returns to, not a catalogue of everything it has made. Each one has been cut at least nine times; the measurements below are for a 162 cm client and are redrafted for anyone else.

夏の布

Seigaiha, indigo

No. 04 · chūsen on Chita cotton

Blue waves over blue ground, printed before cutting so the arcs meet across the seams. The oldest pattern in the book and still the one most clients choose for a first yukata.

  • Ground Ai indigo #1E2E4A
  • Sleeve Standard 49 cm
  • Cloth used 8.9 m of 12
  • Care Cold wash, line dry in shade

¥68 000

Matsusaka stripe, narrow

No. 02 · woven in Mie, dyed in Kyoto

Two warp stripes and a darker ground, the cloth of merchants and travellers. Woven rather than printed, so it keeps its line after years of washing and ironing.

  • Ground Kuriiro #33302C
  • Sleeve Standard 49 cm
  • Cloth used 8.9 m of 12
  • Care Warm wash, iron damp

¥74 000

Arimatsu shibori, fine dot

No. 07 · tied by hand, 4 000 knots

Tied in Aichi by her mother's workshop, dyed in Kyoto. The dots are never quite regular, which is the point: a machine can make a perfect grid, but not this one.

  • Ground Ai indigo #1E2E4A
  • Sleeve Long 68 cm
  • Cloth used 9.6 m of 12
  • Care Hand wash, no wringing

¥148 000

Asanoha, pale blue

No. 11 · chūsen on fine cotton

The hemp leaf, a pattern for growth, drawn at a small scale so it reads as texture from a distance and as geometry up close. The lightest cloth in the studio, at 120 g/m².

  • Ground Asagi #3F6F7E
  • Sleeve Standard 49 cm
  • Cloth used 8.6 m of 12
  • Care Cold wash, dries fast

¥72 000

Kikkō, sumi

No. 15 · chūsen, discharge ground

Tortoiseshell, the pattern of a long life, in a grey that reads almost black at night and almost blue at noon. Cut with a slightly wider body, which suits a taller frame.

  • Ground Sumi #2A2A2E
  • Sleeve Standard 49 cm
  • Cloth used 9.1 m of 12
  • Care Cold wash, no bleach

¥76 000

Beni ground, undyed line

No. 18 · safflower over cotton

The one bright colour in the book, and the one the studio makes fewest of. Safflower fades in sunlight, deliberately: by the end of the third summer the red has softened to the colour of a ripe plum.

  • Ground Beni #A6384B
  • Sleeve Long 68 cm

  • Care Cold wash, dry in shade

How to read the prices

A price includes the cloth, the dyeing, the pattern and the sewing, plus the first fitting and one repair within the first year. It does not include the obi, which is chosen separately and usually costs between ¥18 000 and ¥45 000 depending on whether it is a half-width cotton or a full nagoya obi.

Shibori and safflower cost more because they take longer: a fine-dot bolt is tied over three weeks in Aichi before it ever reaches the dyehouse. If the budget is fixed, say so at the first visit — the studio would rather adjust the sleeve length or the pattern scale than have a client choose a cloth they cannot quite afford.
