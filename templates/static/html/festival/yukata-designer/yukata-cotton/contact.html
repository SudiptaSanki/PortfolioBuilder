The studio

Tōri Cotton
Nakagyō-ku, Kyoto
Open Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00–17:00

The building is narrow and the sign is small. Look for the blue noren with the seal 通 on it, opposite a rice shop. Appointments are forty minutes; walk-ins are welcome when the kettle is on.

What to bring

  • The sandals you will wear, or their heel height.
  • An obi, if you already own one you love.
  • A photograph of a colour you keep returning to.
  • Nothing else. The studio has cloth and tea.
Can I have it before a specific festival?

Usually. Six weeks is the honest lead time; four is possible for plain cotton in a quiet month. Gion Matsuri week is never possible — the studio closes and the dyers go to the parade like everyone else.

Do you make men's yukata?

Yes, in Matsusaka stripe or plain indigo, cut with a narrower sleeve and a darker obi. The measurements are different enough that the studio keeps a separate pattern block.

Is the cloth machine washable?

Yes, in cold water, except shibori, which should be hand-washed and never wrung. Every yukata goes home with a care card that says the same thing in two languages.

Do you ship overseas?

By EMS, at cost, usually ¥4 000–¥7 000. The studio will not declare a lower value on the customs form, so please account for your own import duty.

Can I see the cloth before commissioning?

Yes — the sample box holds every current bolt in a 30 cm piece. For overseas clients the studio posts a set of swatches for ¥2 000, refunded against the commission.
