How it works

One drawing, then a printed proof. The proof is the important part: colours on a screen are not the colours of the ink, and a riso proof is the only honest test. Two rounds of changes are included; after that the studio charges by the hour.

Half is payable on the brief, half on delivery. For banners the printer is paid directly by the client, because the invoice should be transparent about what the street costs.

What to send

  • The date it must be up, and the date it comes down.
  • Where it will be seen: a wall, a road, a table, a phone.
  • Any existing logo, in vector, not a screenshot.
  • The year, if the animal matters. Check the lunar date if it is near the new year.
Can you draw a specific person's zodiac?

Yes, and the interpreter on the front page will give you the animal and element. For a gift, the studio can print the recipient's name in the poster's footer in place of the cycle number.

Do you license the twelve animals?

The set is available for licensing for packaging and editorial use, in the two-ink version or as single-colour line art. The fee depends on the print run, not on the size of the company.

Can you print in other colours?

Each riso drum is one colour and a drum costs about S$180. If the job needs a colour the studio does not own, that cost is quoted separately, or the job goes to a friend's press in Kallang.

Will you draw outside the zodiac?

Sometimes. The studio takes two non-zodiac commissions a year if the subject can be drawn as a single-weight line. Food, buildings and cats have all passed that test; a photorealistic portrait has not.

Do you ship the banner?

No. Scrim banners are printed and collected in Singapore, and the studio will not courier one. Overseas clients are welcome to use the artwork with a local printer.
