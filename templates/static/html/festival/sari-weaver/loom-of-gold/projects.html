The Forty-One Day Bridal Delivered · Oct 2025

The ask: a mother in Chennai wanted her daughter married in a temple-border sari no shop could sell to two brides — her own wedding sari, forty years old, had come from a Kanchipuram loom, and she wanted the same town on her daughter.

What I did: my brother punched a new peacock jacquard — twelve days of card work before a single pick. The maroon was dyed in three lots until it held the same in daylight and lamplight. Then eighteen days of weaving, the temple border beaten in with the body in one pass, as it must be.

The outcome: forty-one days door to door. The design days were charged to the loom, not the bride — the bill is the loom card's bill, ₹59,172, and the card is folded inside the sari still.

A First Diwali in Toronto Delivered · Oct 2025

The ask: a young woman in Toronto, married in the spring, would spend her first Diwali away from Madurai. She wanted Kanchipuram silk her grandmother would approve of — and a weaver who would show the counting, not just the cloth.

What I did: wove the standard six yards to the card — Mulberry 3A, tested zari, medium border — and photographed the filled loom card before the sari was folded, so she could check every figure herself.

The outcome: couriered in nine days and worn for Diwali 2025. The grandmother approved of the border first and the colour second, which is the correct order.

The Replacement Green Delivered · Jun 2025

The ask: a family brought a green sari from 1962, torn at the pleats past any repair, and a surviving fragment no bigger than a hand. Could the sari be woven again, using the cloth itself as the pattern?

What I did: counted the fragment first: 30 picks to the centimetre, the same count I weave today. The green had faded unevenly for sixty years, so I dyed three lots and matched the middle of the fragment, where the sun had not reached. Half-fine zari, narrow border, as the fragment's border had been.

The outcome: the new sari is worn on the family's festival days. The 1962 fragment is framed in their house in Salem — a loom card of its own kind.

Nine Yards, No Argument Delivered · Feb 2026

The ask: a seventy-four-year-old in Kanchipuram itself has worn the nine-yard madisar every day for fifty years. Her family, kindly, suggested a six-yard sari would be easier. She came to the loom shed herself to settle the matter.

What I did: dressed the wider reed — 5,280 ends — and wove the full 9.6 metres, tested zari, medium border, twenty-one days at the pit.

The outcome: she inspected the border, nodded once, and wore it home over the sari she had arrived in. The family has stopped suggesting.
