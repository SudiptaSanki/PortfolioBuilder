The Forty-One Day Bridal Delivered

The ask: a mother in Chennai wanted her daughter married in a temple-border sari no shop could sell to two brides. What I did: punched a new peacock jacquard with my brother, dyed the maroon in three lots until it held in both daylight and lamplight, and wove 18 days without a break in the border. The outcome: delivered in October 2025, forty-one days door to door — the design days were mine, not hers; the bill followed the loom card below, to the rupee.

A First Diwali in Toronto Delivered

The ask: a young woman in Toronto, married in the spring, wanted Kanchipuram silk for her first Diwali that her grandmother in Madurai would approve of down the phone line. What I did: wove the standard six yards to the loom card — tested zari, medium border — and sent the card's photograph before the sari, so she could check my counting. The outcome: couriered in nine days, worn for Diwali 2025; the grandmother approved of the border first and the colour second, which is the correct order.

How a sari happens

Five strips of work

1

The warp is stretched

4,800 ends are measured along the warping frame in the lane behind the house and counted twice — once by me, once by my daughter. A miscounted warp cannot be corrected later; it can only be confessed. Three days.

2

The silk is dyed

Vat-dyed in the courtyard and dried in shade, never full sun — sun takes the maroon first. Two days, and a third if the rains argue.

3

The beam is dressed

Every end is drawn through the reed and the heddles by hand. This is the day visitors are sent away; a distracted dressing day shows in the cloth for six yards.

4

The shuttle flies

Thirty picks to the centimetre, thrown by hand, beaten by the reed. The border is woven in the same pass as the body — a Kanchipuram border is never stitched on afterwards, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you two cloths as one.

5

The fold

Border pressed, tassels knotted, the loom card filled in and folded inside the sari. It leaves in cotton cloth, never plastic.
