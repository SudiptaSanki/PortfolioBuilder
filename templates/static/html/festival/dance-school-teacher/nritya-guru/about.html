The guru

Vasanthi Rao has taught in the same hall since 2004.

Third teacher of the school, second generation of the family, first to admit she was a bad student. She still demonstrates the adavus herself, at fifty-six, and still stops the class when someone's shoulders are up.

Born
Mysuru, 1969
Training
Achala Devi, 1976–1992
Arangetram
1987, Mysuru
Teaching
Since 2004
Also
Mridangam, four years; nattuvangam

My grandmother started this school in the front room of the house because the temple hall would not give her a regular evening. She taught thirty-two children there for eleven years, with a harmonium and a borrowed mridangam, and she never once advertised.

I grew up in that room. I did not want to be a dancer. I wanted to be a veterinarian, and I was, for two years, until I understood that I was spending my mornings thinking about adavus and my afternoons cleaning out kennels. My grandmother was not pleased. She said a dancer who does not want to dance is worse than no dancer, and then she made me start again from the first tattu.

I teach the way she taught, which is not gentle and not cruel. The class stands in lines. The youngest at the front. Everyone says the tala aloud. If a student is late, they wait at the door until the next avartana before joining, because the door is where the count is.

“A dancer who cannot stand still for eight counts will not hold an audience for eight seconds. We begin with the part nobody photographs.”

Why there are no mirrors

The hall has one long wall, a wooden floor, a picture of my grandmother, and a brass lamp. There is no mirror, and there has not been one since 1978. Students ask for one every year.

A mirror teaches you to watch yourself from outside, which is useful for a photograph and fatal for a performance. When you dance, the audience is in front. If your attention is on your own reflection, it is in the wrong place. Instead, students watch each other, and are watched. It is slower and more embarrassing, and it produces dancers who can be seen.

The first year

Beginners do not learn a piece. They learn: how to stand, how to sit, how to say the tala, six adavus, and the names of the nine rasas. That is the whole first year, three evenings a week. At the end of it they perform one minute of nritta in the annual day programme, and most of them are visibly terrified, which is correct.

By the third year a student begins abhinaya, and the work changes. Nritta is arithmetic: correct, tiring, satisfying. Abhinaya is the part where a fifteen-year-old has to show grief she has not yet experienced, and has to learn that technique can carry her until experience arrives.

What I ask of a family

Three evenings a week, for at least four years, before any arangetram is discussed. No missed classes for birthday parties. The costume is not bought until the second year, and the arangetram is not promised at admission — it happens when the student is ready and not before. I have refused four arangetrams in twenty years, and each refusal was correct.

Fees are ₹1,800 a month. Two full scholarships are open at any time for children from families who cannot pay, arranged quietly through the office, and the other students are not told.

I am aware that this is an old-fashioned way to run a school. I have watched the online academies and I understand what they offer: convenience, recorded lessons, a certificate. What they cannot offer is a room where a girl of nine watches a woman of fifty-six hold a posture for the length of an avartana and decides, without being told, that she will learn to do that.

The school, in four dates

  1. 1978

    Achala Devi opens the front room

    Thirty-two children, a harmonium, one borrowed mridangam. The school is called Nritya Gurukul after a phrase in her own guru's letter of blessing.

  2. 1994

    The hall in Gokulam

    The school moves upstairs from a printing press. The wooden floor is laid from a dismantled theatre stage; it still has the nail holes.

  3. 2004

    Vasanthi Rao becomes guru

    Achala Devi stops teaching and sits in the corner of the hall every evening for another six years, saying nothing, which was its own kind of examination.

  4. 2019

    The mridangam residency

    A permanent accompanist joins the school, and tala classes become part of the weekly timetable rather than a guest visit.
