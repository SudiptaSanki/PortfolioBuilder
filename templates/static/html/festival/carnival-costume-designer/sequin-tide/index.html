Goa Carnival · Red & Black · float troupes

Costume is a building you wear for four hours.

I make carnival costumes in a first-floor room in Fontainhas, with two tailors, one beader and a very patient ceiling fan. A gown that looks weightless on the float still has to survive a Panjim afternoon, a hundred metres of cobbles and a dancer who has not eaten since noon.

The sequin bench

Count the sequins before you promise the costume

Every extra pass of coverage adds a few thousand sequins and a few hundred grams. This bench turns a drawing into a shopping list: how many sequins, how much fabric, how many sewing hours, what it weighs on the dancer, and what it costs in rupees.

What the parade asks for

Red & Black ball

A black duchess satin column with a coral collar built on wire and fishnet. The rule of the ball is only two colours; the trick is making black read at all under a red gel.

Silk tulle · 5 mm French · 8,400 sequins · 3.1 kg

King Momo’s court

Five metres of gold organza cut as a single cape so the court can raise it with one arm. The headdress is a wire fan, not a crown: it has to fold flat into a car.

Organza · 8 mm mirror · 3,600 pieces · 2.4 kg

Street troupe

Forty dancers, one pattern, three colours. Mirror work instead of crystal because the sun hits it at nine in the morning when the float is still stuck on the square.

Cotton blend · 8 mm mirror · 1,200 pieces · 0.9 kg

How a costume is made

From a pencil line to a dress rehearsal

Six weeks, if the sequins arrive. I will not take a costume I cannot fit twice, because a carnival gown that has never been danced in is only a drawing with sleeves.

  1. 01

    Sketch & weigh

    The drawing is only the start. We put the beads, fabric and frame on a scale before a single sequin is sewn.

  2. 02

    Pattern & toile

    A calico mock-up, fitted in the atelier, then cut again in the real cloth. Tulle moves differently from calico; we allow for it.

  3. 03

    Sew & set

    Hand-set sequins on the bodice, machine rows on the skirt where the eye will not go. The beader works from the hem up.

  4. 04

    Walk & alter

    A full rehearsal on the cobbles outside, with the real shoes. Anything that catches, tears or drags comes back to the table.

“If the costume cannot survive the walk from the square to the float, it is not a costume. It is a photograph.”

Aloma Fernandes, in conversation with the atelier’s apprentice, 2025
