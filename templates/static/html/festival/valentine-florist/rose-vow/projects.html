Order 114014 February 2025

The boat on the Hooghly

A customer wanted to propose at sunset on a boat, with the roses already aboard and hidden. The difficulty was not the flowers; it was that the boat was booked for six, the sunset was at five thirty-five, and the person being proposed to was punctual.

We delivered sixty red Naomi in three buckets to the ghat at two, with a folding table and a length of cloth to hide them under. The boatman kept them in the cabin. At five twenty, he put them on the bow while the couple was at the stern. The proposal happened at five thirty-six, which is one minute later than planned and, I am told, entirely the boat's fault.

Order 110214 February 2025

Three hundred hotel rooms before six

A hotel on Park Street puts a single rose and a card in every room on the fourteenth. Three hundred stems, three hundred cards, three hundred small bud vases, all placed by housekeeping before the first check-in at six in the morning.

The roses arrived at four, already open — too open for a room that would be entered at eleven at night. I sent the van back for two hundred tighter stems and we mixed them, using the open ones in the lobby and the restaurant. The cards were printed in Bengali and English; the Bengali proof had a typo in the third line, which I caught at midnight and had reprinted at a shop in Bhowanipore that opens at five.

Flowers
300 Red Naomi, 300 bud vases
Team
Four, from 04:00 to 07:30
Cards
300, bilingual, 100 reprinted at 05:00
Result
All rooms done by 06:10
Price
₹19,500

The housekeeping manager

“Ten minutes late and nobody noticed. The Bengali cards were the part that mattered — most of our guests read that one first.”

Order 0871December 2024

A mandap of roses and marigold

A wedding in a garden near Barrackpore, with a mandap that had to be built in the afternoon and photographed at seven. The family wanted roses but the priest wanted marigold, and both were right: marigold for the ritual, roses for the photographs.

We used four hundred marigold strings for the pillars, two hundred pink Mondial for the canopy, and loose red petals for the path. The garden had no shade, so the roses went up at six in the evening, an hour before the ceremony, and were misted twice. The mandap lasted the night; the roses in the canopy were tired by morning, which nobody saw.

“The priest got his marigold, my daughter got her roses, and the photographs look like a film. I do not know how she did it in that heat.”

Order 1055September 2025

Eighty desks, one Monday

An advertising agency wanted a single rose on every desk on a Monday morning, as a thank-you after a difficult pitch. The office was open-plan, the desks were hot-desked, and nobody knew who sat where.

We put one rose and a blank card on each chair instead, at six in the morning, before anyone came in. It took forty minutes with two people and a trolley. The cards were blank on purpose; people wrote their own names on them and left them on their monitors, which the agency later used in a recruitment post without asking me, which I minded less than I expected.

80 Avalanche, 80 blank cards
Delivery

Detail

Price
₹11,200

The account director

“Eighty people walked in to a flower on their chair. Half of them took a photograph before they sat down. Best money we spent that quarter.”

Order 1218February 2026

An anniversary, remembered at nine at night

A man telephoned at twenty past nine, having remembered. The shop had closed at nine. I was still there, doing the accounts, with about forty stems left in the bucket and no ribbon cut.

He was fifteen minutes away in Gariahat. I made a bunch of twelve Explorer with ruscus and a handwritten card, and he collected it at ten to ten. It was not the bunch I would have made with a day's notice, but it was a bunch, and it was in his hand before ten.

This happens about thirty times a year, usually in the week before the fourteenth, and always at night. I have stopped pretending the shop closes at nine in February.
