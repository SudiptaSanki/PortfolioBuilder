Shows · 2013 – 2026

Every number is a floor plan before it is a dance.

The diagrams below are drawn from the actual cue sheets: where the dancers stood, where the light came from, and what the audience could see. The problems were different each time.

Rang De

Two hook steps for two lenses: a wide that had to read in the rain, and a mirror shot for the close-ups. Eleven dancers, six nights, one very patient rain machine.

Film · 20264 min 12 s11 dancers

Nautch Nights

A kathak-contemporary evening for 900 seats, built around two minutes of footwork and breath with no music at all. The hardest part was teaching the audience to stop clapping.

Stage · 202470 min14 dancers

The Long Count

Dance theatre co-directed with a light designer: nine dancers, one working lamp, and a count that gets slower every eight until it stops. NCPA, three nights.

Stage · 202552 min9 dancers

Madras Mix

Associate choreographer on a stadium spectacular. Thirty-six dancers on a revolve, which meant every step had to work from four sides at once — and the back row had to be the safest.

Stage · 20238 min36 dancers

Dhoom Dham

My first solo film numbers. Two lines, one camera, and a lesson I have kept: the dancer nearest the lens cannot be the only one performing.

Film · 20212 numbers18 dancers

Sangeet floors

Nine to forty family members, three rehearsals on a hotel carpet, and one rule: the grandmother joins by count three. Over two hundred nights, and no two floors the same.

Private · 2019–3 sessions9–40 dancers
