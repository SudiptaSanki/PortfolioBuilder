Kumkum Glow·Repertoire

Six items, and the order
they belong in.

A full Odissi evening is not a playlist. It moves from invocation to pure dance to expression to release, and the order is part of the form. These are the items I currently perform, with the raga, the tala and the honest length.

I

Mangalacharan

Invocation · Bhairavi · Ekatali, 4 beats

A prayer to the earth, the guru and the deity, followed by a short pure-dance passage in chowk. The item establishes the dancer's relationship to the stage: the first movement is always downward, a salutation to the ground.

I perform the version taught by my guru, with the stances kept low and the pushpanjali offered at the end. It is the only item where I do not vary the choreography.

9 minSolo
Full evening or festival

II

Kalyan Pallavi

Pure dance · Kalyani · Ekatali, 4 beats

A pallavi in Kalyani that starts with a single phrase in the feet and unfolds it through twelve variations. The tempo rises once, in the middle, and then settles. The piece is about the pleasure of repetition, not the display of speed.

The version I dance is my own choreography on the traditional structure, developed with my mardala player over two seasons. It is the item I open with for audiences new to Odissi.

14 minSolo
Full evening or festival

III

Shankarabharanam Pallavi

Pure dance · Shankarabharanam · Aditala, 8 beats

A longer, more architectural pallavi in eight beats, with the jati worked out in the feet and repeated in the hands. It needs a wooden floor and a mardala player who knows the dancer; on a bad floor it becomes an exercise in balance.

This is the item I take to festivals, because it shows the technique without requiring any knowledge of the language.

16 minSolo
Festival or full evening

IV

Sakhi Gopa Jane

Abhinaya · Odia padam · Bhairavi

A sakhi is sent to tell Krishna that Radha is waiting. The poem is one line long; the item is twelve minutes of a woman inventing reasons to delay. The dance is in the eyes and the hands, and the veil does the rest.

There is no fixed ending. Depending on the room, Radha either goes or does not. I decide on the night, and the singer follows.

12 minSolo
Full evening only

V

Lalita Lavanga Lata

Abhinaya · Jayadeva’s Gita Govinda · Mohana

An ashtapadi from the Gita Govinda, sung in Sanskrit, in which the sakhi describes Krishna's beauty to Radha. The item is a lesson in restraint: the poetry is already lush, so the dancer must not decorate it further.

I perform it in the version learned from my guru, with one change in the final stanza to suit a lower register.

11 minSolo
Full evening or temple

VI

Moksha in Teentala

Release · Aditala, 8 beats

The close. Fast nritta that gets quieter instead of louder, ending in a still pose held until the room settles. The last drumbeat is followed by silence, and the silence is part of the item.

I will not shorten the final stillness for applause. If a presenter needs a shorter evening, we cut an earlier item instead.

7 minSolo
Closes every evening

Programme notes

What a presenter receives.

Two weeks before the performance: a running order with lengths and ragas, a programme note of 150 words, the musicians' names and a technical rider. One week before: a rehearsal schedule and a stage plan. After: an invoice and an honest note about what worked.

4–5musicians on stage
20 ftminimum stage width
90minutes with interval

The rider asks for a wooden floor, two washes and a special, a mirror in the green room, and water at room temperature. The green room door must close. Everything else can be solved with a phone call.

Booking

Tell me the date and the stage.

A full evening runs ninety minutes with an interval. Festival slots of thirty or forty-five minutes get a different order: Mangalacharan, one pallavi, one abhinaya, a short Moksha.
