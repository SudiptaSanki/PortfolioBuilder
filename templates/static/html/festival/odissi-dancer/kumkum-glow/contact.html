Before you write

What does the rider actually require?

A wooden floor (or a proper dance floor laid over concrete), a stage at least twenty feet wide and sixteen deep, two washes and one special, a mirror and a closing door in the green room, and room-temperature water. The musicians need four chairs and two music stands.

How many musicians come?

Four for a full evening: vocal, mardala, violin and flute. For a festival slot, three. The artists are named in the programme and paid before the dancer; that is the order of the invoice.

What does a performance cost?

One fee covers the dancer, the musicians, rehearsal and local travel. Outstation travel and stay are added at cost. Schools and colleges pay a reduced fee; one government school performance a year is free. I will put the number in writing before you commit.

How much rehearsal time do you need?

One technical rehearsal of ninety minutes on the day, with the full orchestra, and a thirty-minute spacing call before the doors open. If the mardala player is local and new to me, add a two-hour rehearsal the day before.

Can the performance be filmed?

For a short archive clip, yes, with the audience told and the camera kept out of the front row. For a full recording or broadcast, we agree separately and the fee changes; the musicians are paid for the recording too.

The short rider

Wooden floor. Twenty feet of stage. Two washes and a special. A mirror. A door that closes. Water at room temperature.

Four chairs and two music stands for the orchestra. One power point. That is the list.

Running order

Full evening: Mangalacharan, Pallavi, interval, Abhinaya, Moksha. Ninety minutes including the interval.

Festival slot: Mangalacharan, one Pallavi, one Abhinaya, short Moksha. Thirty-five to forty-five minutes.

For presenters

The programme note arrives two weeks before, in English and Odia, with the musicians' names and a short introduction to each item. I do not send a biography longer than 120 words; audiences read the programme, not the file.
