The body bows.

Odissi is a conversation between stillness and a single step. I dance the old repertoire — Mangalacharan, Pallavi, abhinaya — in Bhubaneswar and wherever a stage has a wooden floor and a green room with a mirror.

The shape of an evening

Four items, in the old order.

  1. 01

    Mangalacharan

    The invocation. A prayer to the earth, the guru and the deity, then a short pure-dance section. Nothing is rushed; the audience is being told to sit back.

    8–10 min · Bhairavi · Ekatali

  2. 02

    Pallavi

    The flower that unfolds. A pure nritta piece built on a raga and a tala, with the body repeating a phrase and varying it until the phrase becomes a mood.

    12–18 min · raga of the evening

  3. 03

    Abhinaya

    The expressive item: a poem sung, and a dancer acting every word. This is where the audience stops watching the feet and starts watching the face.

    10–14 min · Odia or Sanskrit

  4. 04

    Moksha

    Release. Fast nritta, then a slow close, ending in a still pose that is the whole point of the evening. The last drumbeat is followed by complete silence.

    6–8 min · Teentala

Mudra desk

Build a pallavi, and see the hand that starts it.

Choose a mudra, a raga, a tala and a length. The desk works out the cycles, the beats and a running order, and draws the mudra as the hand is held. It is a rehearsal aid, not a notation system; the guru still decides.

A cycle is one avartana. The count assumes the pallavi is the only pure-dance item and the tempo holds.

Hand study

Pataka

The flag. All four fingers together, thumb bent to the palm.

Used for: salutation, a flag, the beginning of a item, the palm of the earth.

    Pallavi · Kalyani

    Kalyan Pallavi

    Fourteen minutes of unfolding, built on a phrase that starts in the feet and ends in the eyebrows. The one I open with when the audience is new to Odissi.

    14 min · Ekatali · solo

    Abhinaya · Odia

    Sakhi Gopa Jane

    A sakhi tells Krishna that Radha is waiting. Twelve minutes of acting with one line of poetry and no prop except a veil. The ending depends on the room.

    12 min · Bhairavi · solo

    Moksha · Teentala

    Moksha in Teentala

    The close. Fast nritta that gets quieter instead of louder, and a final pose held until the audience remembers to breathe.

    7 min · Aditala · solo
