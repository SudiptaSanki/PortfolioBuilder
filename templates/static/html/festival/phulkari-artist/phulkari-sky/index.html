A garden stitched from the wrong side.

Phulkari means flower work. A bagh is a garden: the whole cloth covered so not one thread of the ground shows. I stitch in Patiala the way my grandmother did, from the back of the cloth, with floss silk on hand-spun khaddar, and I count the months, not the hours.

Phulkari

Flower work. Sparse motifs on khaddar, a shawl for every day, a border for a dupatta. The ground is allowed to breathe.

Bagh

A garden. The embroidery covers the cloth so completely that the ground disappears. Made for weddings, and never sold in the family that made it.

Chope

The stitch a girl learned first: a double line along the selvedge, given to the maternal grandmother to embroider for the wedding.

Stitch a square of bagh

Pick a thread and a motif, then drag across the grid. The stitches land from the back, as they do on the cloth; the pattern appears on the front. A full bagh is about 240,000 stitches, so this square is one palm's width of a very long winter.

Four stitches, four meanings

The motifs are not decoration. Each one carries a wish the grandmother stitches into the cloth without saying it aloud.

Chand-tara

Moon and star. A wish for a marriage that outlasts the dark. Stitched in the centre of the bagh, where the couple sleeps under it.

Mor

The peacock. Rain, fertility, and the monsoon arriving. A mor on a bagh is the grandmother asking for a good year.

Kikar

The acacia that grows in the village. Shade, and the memory of the fields the family left. A quiet motif, usually in the corners.

Suraj-mukhi

The sunflower, a newer motif from the last fifty years. Its seeds are counted like children; the stitch is always mustard.

A bagh for a wedding

Most commissions are for a daughter or a son, and they begin a year before the wedding. Tell me the couple's names and the date; I will tell you honestly whether the cloth can be finished. A bagh is not rushed, and a rushed bagh looks like it.
