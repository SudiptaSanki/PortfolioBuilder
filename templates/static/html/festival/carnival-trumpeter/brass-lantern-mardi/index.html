The route

Three things a parade asks of a horn

  1. I

    Endurance over eleven hours

    Fat Tuesday starts at a 6 a.m. call and ends after dark. A cornet on the lead line plays roughly four thousand bars in that window, most of them in the same key. I build the season around it: shorter phrases, more breath, no wasted motion in the shoulders.

  2. II

    A pitch reference the drums can trust

    Snare lines tune to the horn that starts each tune, not to a tuner. If the cornet drifts, the whole line drifts with it. Every call begins on a concert B♭ I check the horn at the meet-up and again after the second mile, when the brass gets cold.

  3. III

    Room to be answered

    A parade call is a question thrown at the crowd, and the crowd answers with whatever it has. The best moments on the route are the ones where the band leaves two beats of nothing and the street fills them in.

You do not lead a parade from the front. You lead it from the first bar, and then you keep the same bar going for six miles.

Théo Baptiste, rehearsing at Kermit's on North Robertson
