01

Krewe of Ash, float escort

Carnival 2026 · St. Charles route · 6.2 miles

A nineteen-float parade needed a brass line that could hold a pocket between float stops without covering the announcer. We wrote three calls that resolve on the same two-bar figure, so the band can restart cleanly at any corner without a count-in the crowd can hear.

The arrangement puts the cornet on the melody and the tenor sax on a counter-line a third below, which reads as fuller than doubling the tune in octaves and saves the lead player's lip over six miles.

What went wrong

The second call was written a tone too high for the cold. By the fourth mile the cornet had moved it down by ear, which meant the sax counter-line no longer matched. We now carry a cold-weather transposition for two of the calls.

  • Section cornet, trombone, tenor sax, snare, bass drum
  • Calls three, in concert B♭
  • Result held all 19 floats

02

Second Line Sundays

Workshop series · Seventh Ward · 14 sessions

A free Sunday workshop for twelve players between fifteen and twenty-two, held in the back room of a hall that lets us use the space for nothing as long as we sweep up. The whole curriculum is three things: long tones, listening to the snare, and playing the same eight bars until nobody has to look at anybody.

Four of the twelve now play paid parade dates. Two of them play in my section, which is the part of this work I am most proud of and the part I did least to earn.

What went wrong

The first four sessions were too technical. Attendance dropped to six. We rebuilt the hour around playing tunes and taught the technique inside them, and the room filled again by the second month.

  • Players 12 enrolled, 9 finished
  • Hours 28 across 14 Sundays
  • Outcome four players on paid dates

03

Hymn for the Seventh Ward

Recording · one room, two mics · 2025

A six-minute piece recorded live in a church hall with two microphones and no overdubs. The cornet carries the melody in the first half, then hands it to the trombone and moves to a harmony line that sits under the melody rather than above it.

We recorded four takes. The third is the one on the record, because on the third take the room's heating came on halfway through and nobody stopped, and the hum sits under the last two minutes like a held breath.

What went wrong

The first take was technically the cleanest and completely lifeless. It took three more passes to accept that the small imperfections were the reason to record in a room instead of a booth.

  • Players five
  • Takes four, third released
  • Length 6 min 12 s

04

Lantern Line, night parade score

Arrangement · river route · Lundi Gras 2025

A night parade asked for music that could be heard between the floats and the crowd, in the dark, with the band lit only by the lanterns on the float. We wrote a short motif of five notes that the horns repeat while the drums move underneath it, so the line stays legible from a block away.

The motif is deliberately narrow, a fifth at most. Wide intervals vanish in street noise; a fifth survives a crowd, a generator and a marching band three blocks over.

What went wrong

We rehearsed in daylight and the arrangement sounded thin at first. Under lantern light, with the crowd close, the same notes carried further than we expected and the drums had to come down two steps in the mix.

  • Motif five notes, a fifth wide
  • Route 3.4 miles, river side
  • Set nine minutes, repeated
