Field notes · 2012 – 2026

The tree, the light, the wait

I am not a botanist and I am not a documentarian. I am a person with a heavy camera who walks the same path every morning for eleven days and occasionally gets a frame.

Why film, honestly

Partly because a 6×7 negative holds a gradation in the petals that a small sensor turns into a stair-step. Partly because thirty-eight rolls a season forces me to wait instead of taking eleven versions and choosing later. And partly because the camera is slow enough that people do not perform for it.

The lab in Fushimi develops my colour and pushes my black and white. I have used the same two people for nine years; they know that my Portra is usually rated at 320 and that I would rather have a thin negative than a blocked one.

The tree does not need me. That is the useful part.

I print at home, in the kitchen, with the window blacked out and a fan in the doorway. Ten prints a week is a good week. The fibre paper takes four hours to dry and another day to flatten, which is why a print takes two weeks and not two days.

What is in the bag

One body, two lenses, a meter I mostly ignore, and a plastic bag for rain. The bag itself is a canvas satchel from a shop in Teramachi that has been repaired four times.

  • BodyPentax 67, bought used in 2012. The meter is a stop cold; I know it and compensate.
  • Lens105 mm f/2.4 — the standard lens, and the only one I use before eight in the morning.
  • Lens55 mm f/4 — for a whole tree, or a path with somebody in it.
  • FilmPortra 400, rated at 320. Provia 100F when the sky is doing something. HP5 Plus for the grey days.
  • MeterSekonic L-308, for incident light when the branch is backlit and the camera meter lies.
  • TripodOnly before sunrise. After that it is a walking stick with a quick-release plate.
  • ClothA microfibre square for the lens, and a second one for my glasses, which fog at 5:30.

Photographing a person

A blossom photograph is a landscape; a person in it is a different contract. I ask before I raise the camera, every time, even when the person is forty metres away and small in the frame. If they say no, I put the camera down and thank them. If they ask what it is for, I tell the truth: prints, maybe a book, never advertising.

Frame 101 exists because a woman with an umbrella stopped to watch me photograph an empty path and asked if I was waiting for someone. I said I was waiting for the light. She laughed and walked on, and I asked if I could make one frame as she went. She said yes, and asked me to send it, and I did.

Fourteen springs

  • 2012Moved to Kyoto for a job in a bookshop, bought the Pentax with the first month's pay.
  • 2014First spring photographing seriously. Twenty-two rolls, four keepers, one of them still on my wall.
  • 2017Started printing at home after the lab in Fushimi stopped doing fibre paper.
  • 2019First small show, in a coffee shop in Demachiyanagi. Sold nine prints, which paid for the season's film.
  • 2022Began the bloom calendar, because friends kept asking the same week and I kept answering late.
  • 2024Led the first hanami walk: two people, three hours, no tripods, thermos of tea.
  • 2026Fourteen seasons, roughly four hundred rolls, and one book in progress that may never be finished.
