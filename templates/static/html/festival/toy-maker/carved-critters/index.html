Chapter one · the animals

Animals cut
from a single
block of lime

I make jointed wooden animals the way my father did: a body turned on the lathe, ears and legs cut from the offcuts, brass pins at the joints, and a finish of beeswax and linseed oil that a child can chew on without harm.

Everything on the shelf is made to be held by someone under five years old. That decides the wood, the paint, the size of the smallest part, and how much of it can break before it stops being a toy.

Chapter three · the shelf

Three animals, three ages

01

The hare, 1994

My father's design, still the best seller. Long ears, a body wide enough for a fist, four wheels that turn on brass pins. Nothing on it has changed except the finish, which is now food-safe.

Lime · 21 cm · 6 pieces

02

The bear, 2007

Made for a shop in Dresden that wanted something for smaller hands. Shorter body, no tail, ears sanded almost flat so they cannot break off in a mouth.

Beech · 16 cm · 5 pieces

03

The fox, 2018

A commission from a kindergarten: a fox with a tail that curls on a spring, so it can be pulled without snapping. The spring is the only metal part I have ever added to a toy.

Walnut · 24 cm · 8 pieces

A toy that survives one child is a product. A toy that survives three is a craft.
