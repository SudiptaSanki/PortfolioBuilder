The workshop is above the lathe, which is above the cellar where the wood dries. In winter the window is the only heat in the room, so the wood and I work at the same temperature.

Every piece passes the same test before it leaves: dropped from a metre onto a stone floor, then inspected at the joints. If an ear comes off, the ear was wrong.

My father turned wood in this room for thirty-one years and never called himself a toy maker. He made small figures for the Christmas market in Seiffen, the kind that stand in a window in December, and the animals were the things he made when nobody had ordered anything.

The lathe

It is a cast-iron lathe with a belt drive and a foot pedal that has been repaired four times. It turns at one speed, which is slower than I would choose, and that slowness has shaped the whole workshop: every design has to be one that a slow lathe can make in numbers.

In 2009 I looked at a new lathe with a variable motor and a digital readout. It cost as much as a small car, and I would have had to learn my own hands again. I bought wood instead.

Wood and weather

Lime comes from a sawmill two valleys over, cut in winter when the sap is down and left to dry for two years under the house. Green wood moves; a toy turned from green wood will be a different shape by Easter, and the joints will open.

The drying is the part customers never see, and the reason a commission takes six weeks rather than six days. In a wet autumn I have been known to bring the wood into the kitchen. My husband has opinions about this.

Why nothing has a face

Most of my animals have one eye and a suggestion of a mouth, and nothing else. A painted face fixes an expression; an unpainted one lets a child decide whether the hare is frightened or pleased to be pulled across the floor. That is also why the toys photograph badly and sell well.

What I will not do

No plastic eyes, no batteries, no sound modules. No paint on a surface a child will put in their mouth: the coloured parts are stained with plant dye and sealed with beeswax, and the rest is bare wood with oil. If a customer wants a toy that lights up, I send them to a shop in Dresden and mean it kindly.
