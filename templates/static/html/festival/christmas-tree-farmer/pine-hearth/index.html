Seven years to grow a tree. Twenty minutes to choose one.

We plant about nine hundred firs a year on eleven acres of old dairy pasture. In December you walk the rows with a saw and a length of string, find the one that fits your room, and we shake it, bale it and tie it to the roof of your car.

Three firs, honestly compared

The farm year

A Christmas tree is a crop with a nine-year lead time. Everything we do in June is for a tree somebody will choose in a decade.

Read the almanac
  1. March

    Prune the leaders. Walk every row with a pocket knife and take out double tops before they harden.

  2. May

    Plant bare-root seedlings, 900 of them, by hand, in the rows we mowed the previous autumn.

  3. June

    Shear. Six weeks of it, with a long knife, shaping each tree by eye. This is what makes a tree a tree.

  4. August

    Mow between rows, count the deer damage, replant the gaps in the lowest field where the frost sits.

  5. November

    Open the gate the Saturday before Thanksgiving. Bale, shake, tie, and sell wreaths at the barn.

  6. January

    Sharpen every blade, mend the baler, order next year's seedlings from a nursery in Michigan.

Come up the hill

We are twenty minutes from Montpelier, off Route 2, then a dirt road that the town ploughs late. Wear boots. The barn has hot cider and a stove, and the dog is friendly but old.
