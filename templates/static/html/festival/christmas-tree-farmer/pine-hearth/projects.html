What is growing, and where

Eleven acres in four fields, planted in sequence so there is a crop to cut every year. Heights are measured in October, from the ground to the top whorl.

Rows are counted from the stone wall at the road. The numbers on the gate posts match this ledger; if you get lost, follow the numbers down.

Plot 1

Fraser fir · planted 2019 · 1.9 acres · 214 trees

The oldest field, on the east slope where the snow goes first. Dense trees, 6 to 8 feet, and the rows closest to the barn. Most people stop here and never reach the others.

Plot 2

Balsam fir · planted 2020 · 2.4 acres · 268 trees

North of the wall, where the ground stays wet until May. The balsam like it. Narrower trees than the Fraser, with the strongest scent on the farm and a few gaps where the deer got in.

Plot 3

Canaan fir · planted 2021 · 2.2 acres · 236 trees

The flat field by the pond, the easiest walking on the farm. Canaan grows a fuller tree than balsam and holds its needles through a dry January. Good for families with small children and sleds.

Plot 4

Fraser fir · planted 2022 · 2.1 acres · 191 trees

The upper slope behind the spruce windbreak, the coldest ground we farm. Smaller trees, 4 to 6 feet, sold as table trees. We leave the best of them for next year, which is how a rotation works.

Also at the barn

We make wreaths from the trimmings, which is the only way to use the brush we cut in June. Nothing on the farm is bought in, including the ribbon.

Pick a row, bring a saw

We have saws and gloves at the barn if you forget yours. The field is uneven, the mud is real, and the walk from the gate to Plot 4 takes about twelve minutes at a four-year-old's pace.
