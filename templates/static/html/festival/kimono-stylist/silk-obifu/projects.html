Studio / Wardrobe

Six wardrobes, and the decisions inside them.

A dressing is only the last hour of a much longer job: fitting, sourcing, mending, and telling someone that the kimono they love is not the kimono for this room.

一

A wedding for twelve

WeddingBend, OregonOctober 202512 people, 2 dressers

Both families wanted kimono and neither had worn one in thirty years. We started with a fitting day in August: four months of lead time, one borrowed tomesode that needed the sleeves let down, and a grandmother whose furisode fitted her granddaughter almost exactly. On the day we dressed twelve people in four hours, in a rented cabin with two mirrors and a very patient uncle holding the steamer.

Four of the twelve

二

Tea in the snow

Tea ceremonyPortland Japanese GardenJanuary 20246 guests, 1 dresser

An outdoor tea gathering in a pavilion with no heat. The brief was simple and impossible: everyone had to sit on the floor for ninety minutes, in the cold, and stand up looking as though nothing had happened. We chose unlined silk for two guests and lined for the rest, and I retied every obi twice on the trial run until the knot sat flat against the back.

Moss iromuji

三

Graduation, twice over

GraduationLewis & Clark CollegeMay 20249 graduates, 2 dressers

Nine graduates in hakama, all dressed in a lecture hall before eight in the morning. The hakama cords were the day’s real problem: the standard length is made for someone taller than most of the class, so we shortened four sets the week before and kept the spare cord in the car. The photographs from behind are the ones that mattered, and from behind the cords are the whole picture.

Indigo and hakama

四

A temple bazaar’s yukata wardrobe

Summer festivalObon, PortlandAugust 2023120 rentals, 3 dressers

A hundred and twenty yukata rented in one evening, most of them to people who had never worn one. We set up three dressing stations and a mirror, and made a rule: nobody leaves without being able to retie their own obi. The queue was long, the cotton was hot, and eleven people came back the following year to tell me they had done it themselves.

Cotton, three patterns

五

The Taishō closet

Museum consultationPortland, private collection2022–202340 garments dated

Forty garments inherited from a family in Hood River, most of them from the 1910s and 1920s, several with the original wrappers. I dated them by the weave, the sleeve shape, and the way the lining had been attached, and wrote a short note on each for the family. Six pieces went to a conservator; the rest were stable enough to be worn again, which is what the owner wanted and what the museum had not thought to suggest.

Taishō, c. 1921

六

Two actors, one winter

Film wardrobeAstoria, OregonFebruary 20256 weeks on set

A period film set in a Japanese-American fishing family in 1908. Two principal actors, four changes each, and a costume designer who wanted the kimono to look lived-in rather than correct. We built the daily dressing into the shooting schedule, kept a steam kit on set, and taught the continuity assistant how to spot a collar that had shifted. The rain in Astoria did the rest.

Indigo, worn

Where the clothes come from
