Wedding, winter

Black tomesode · maru obi

FORMALITY
Kimono
Black silk tomesode with a single crest at the back and a pattern that stays below the waist. Lined.
Obi & knot
Maru obi. Double-width, tied in a formal taiko knot. It is heavy, it takes two people, and it is the correct answer for a wedding.
Accessories
Gold obijime with a pearl obidome, white tabi, zōri with a gold cord. A folding fan in the left hand, never the right.
Season note
Winter calls for lined silk and a motif that can carry the dark: pine, plum, or a woven ground with no figure at all.

Do not wear a furisode to another woman’s wedding. The long sleeve is a young unmarried woman’s signal, and at a wedding it reads as a claim on the occasion.

席 Occasions

Six days that ask for six different dresses

People come to me with a photograph on their phone and a date. What they need is not a costume but a set of answers about the room they are walking into.

婚礼

Wedding

Black tomesode for the mothers, furisode for the unmarried sisters, and something quietly expensive for everyone else. Two dressers, ninety minutes, and a steamer in the car.

茶会

Tea ceremony

The most exacting room in the business. The pattern must not shout, the colours must belong to the season, and the obi must let you sit on your heels for an hour without a single fold out of place.

卒業

Graduation

Hakama over a kimono, tied high, with the cords arranged so the photograph from behind looks as considered as the one from the front.

夏祭

Summer festival

Yukata, a hanhaba obi, and the honest advice that cotton creases. I tie them so they can be worn for six hours and untied by the wearer alone at the end of the night.

正月

New Year

Auspicious motifs, a little more gold than usual, and sleeves short enough to eat a meal in. The first shrine visit is a photograph; the family lunch afterwards is the real test.

法事

Memorial

Muted, unpatterned, no jewellery that catches the light. The dressing is simple; the conversation before it usually is not, and I leave time for both.
