How it is made

Four days in the workshop

  1. Day one — the armature

    A cone of newspaper tied to a reed, then rounded out with crumpled paper until the body has a belly. Nothing is glued yet; the shape is argued with until it stands on its own.

  2. Day two — four layers

    Wheat paste and torn newspaper, four passes with a day of drying between each. Torn edges, never cut: a cut edge makes a hard line that cracks when the piñata is hit.

  3. Day three — the fringes

    Crepe paper is folded, cut into strips a centimetre wide, then layered in rows from the bottom up so each row hides the seam of the one below. This is the long part, and the part people remember.

  4. Day four — the door

    A flap is cut at the top, the candy goes in, and the flap is glued shut with a strip of the same paper. Then the piñata hangs on the rope and waits until the party decides it has waited long enough.

A piñata that lasts more than one party was built wrong.
