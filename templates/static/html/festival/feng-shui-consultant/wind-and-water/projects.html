Selected readings · 2019–2026

Six buildings,
six bearings

A reading is a document, not a performance. These are six of them, with the facing direction, the life guas of the people who sleep there, and the changes that were actually made.

A five-room flat, Tiong Bahru

Facing south-west · period 7 · 2025

Two adults, one child, and a back bedroom that had become a store. The husband's gua put his Tian Yi in the north-east, which in this flat was the store room; the wife's Yan Nian fell on the kitchen wall. Neither had noticed, because nobody had drawn the two together on one plan.

We cleared the back room and moved the bed to the north-east wall, which was only possible after moving a wardrobe by forty centimetres. The dining table turned to face the wife's Yan Nian direction. Nothing was bought. The follow-up at three months was the first time a client has told me they had stopped arguing about the store room.

A shophouse office, Chinatown

Facing east · period 8 · 2025

A design studio on two floors of a 1930s shophouse, with the entrance on the five-foot way and a stair that cuts the plan in half. The owner had put his desk at the back, facing a wall, because that was where the light was best. His Sheng Qi was to the east — the entrance, where the meeting table sat.

We turned the studio around: desks along the east wall facing into the room, meeting table at the back under the skylight, and the printer — noisy, hot, constantly on — out of the wealth sector. The drawing boards stayed where the light was. That was the compromise, and it was the right one.

A Katong landed house

Facing north-east · period 6 · 2024

A three-generation family in a house that had been rebuilt in 1994 and never read. The main door faced north-east; the kitchen sat in the north-west; the grandmother's room was over the old well. There were five life guas to reconcile, which is the real work — a house cannot face four directions at once for four people.

I gave the grandmother the room with her Fu Wei, moved the eldest grandson out of the north-west room, and left the living room exactly as it was. When a house has five people in it, most of the reading is deciding who gets which corner.

A condo, Bukit Timah

Facing west · period 8 · 2023

A new flat bought off-plan, read before the owners moved in, which is the best time and the rarest. The developer's plan had the kitchen open to the living room on the west wall, where the afternoon sun lands hardest. Both owners had their Jue Ming in the west.

The recommendations were structural but small: a half-wall at the kitchen, a solid door instead of a sliding one, and the study in the north room rather than the west balcony. All of it went to the contractor as a marked-up plan with dimensions, and all of it was built.

A restaurant, Joo Chiat

Facing south · period 7 · 2022

A forty-seat restaurant in a corner shophouse where the previous two tenants had failed. The owner wanted to know whether the unit was cursed. It was not: the kitchen extractor discharged over the entrance, the cash counter sat in the six-killings sector, and the door opened onto a service lane.

We moved the counter to the south-east, put the extractor outlet along the back, and added a light over the entrance that stays on until closing. The unit has had one tenant since. I have no way of proving the reading did that, and I say so in the report.

A childcare centre, Toa Payoh

Facing north · period 8 · 2019

A centre for ninety children in a void deck, with the nap room in the north-west and the door opening straight onto the play area. The principal's question was practical: the children would not sleep.

The nap room was the coldest, darkest room in the plan and also the noisiest, because the air-conditioning compressor sat against its wall. Feng shui did not solve it; moving the compressor did. What the compass added was the seating of the cots: heads to the east, away from the door, which the teachers said made the room calmer within a week.

The compass found the cot direction. The compressor was the real problem.

She told us the unit was not cursed and that we had a ventilation problem. Then she moved the counter and charged us for a report, not a ritual. That is why we sent her to the next three tenants.
