The practice

My father read houses.
I read them the same way, with better notes.

I am Mei-Ling Tan. I have been reading buildings since 1998, first in Penang with my father, then in Singapore on my own. The method is classical compass school: Eight Mansions for the people, flying stars for the building and the year.

My father kept a shop of Chinese medicines on Chulia Street and read houses in the evenings, which in Penang in the 1980s was not an unusual combination. He had a luopan he had bought in Hong Kong in 1962 and a rule that he never took a reading standing inside a doorway, because the door frame bends the needle. I still do not.

I studied architecture at the National University of Singapore, worked four years in an office that designed condominiums, and left when I understood that the part of the job I cared about was the part nobody paid for: how a plan makes a person feel before it is furnished. In 1998 I started taking readings on weekends. By 2004 it was the whole of my work.

What I do is narrow. I do not do blessings, I do not do space clearing, and I do not sell objects. I take a bearing, read a plan, and write down what should move and by when. Most of my clients are people who have just bought a flat, or a business that has moved into a shophouse and cannot work out why the back of it feels wrong.

Two schools, one report

Eight Mansions (八宅, Ba Zhai) gives each person a life gua and four directions that support them. It is about where a body should sleep, sit and eat. Flying stars (飛星, Fei Xing) gives the building a chart from its facing direction and the period it was built in, and changes every year. It is about what the rooms are doing. A useful reading needs both, plus the form of the site — what is actually outside the window.

I write everything down. The report is dated, marked on your own floor plan, and written in plain English with the Chinese terms in brackets, because I have met too many people who were told to put a bowl of water somewhere and never told why.

What I will not do

I will not tell you that a mirror will fix your marriage. I will not blame a previous occupant or a neighbouring building for an illness. I will not give you a reading over the phone from a photograph, because a photograph cannot tell me where north is.

And I will not promise an outcome. Feng shui is a way of arranging a place so that it supports the life you are already living. The life is yours.

Method, plainly

What the compass is actually measuring

The bearing Taken at the main door with a luopan held level, clear of metal, lifts and risers. In a Singapore flat the corridor can bend a needle by ten degrees, so I take three readings and use the median.

The chart The building's facing and sitting directions give a flying star chart for its period. Period 9 began in 2024 and runs to 2043; most flats I read are period 7 or 8 buildings facing the new period's numbers.

The people Each occupant's life gua is set against the rooms they actually use. A good direction in a room nobody enters is a fact without a use.

She spent twenty minutes at our front door with a compass and a notebook before she said a word about the flat. By then she already knew more about it than we did.
