The Work

Readings, told straight

Every reading below is told the way the report is written: what the client asked, what the plan showed, what we moved, and what the client reported afterwards. Outcomes are the clients' words, not my promises — a queue standing somewhere better is a layout result, and that is as far as any honest reading goes.

Tiong Bahru · 68 m² shophouse bakery · Read November 2025

The bakery that queued out of its own door

The ask

The owners were preparing for the Lunar New Year rush and losing the morning to it: the queue for the till crossed the doorway, customers outside could not see in, and the two window tables were always the last to fill.

What the plan showed

Squaring the plan put the till island in the centre of the grid — the one area I keep clear in every reading — and the queue it made ran straight through the career path by the door. A mirror faced the front door, hung by a previous tenant to "bounce the wealth back in". It bounced back the corridor.

What we did

  • The till moved to the wealth corner (top-left), forty-odd centimetres of counter and one rewired point.
  • The queue was rerouted along the fame wall, under the menu boards, so waiting customers read the menu instead of blocking the door.
  • The door-facing mirror came down. It went to the back wall, beside the wash basin, where a mirror does ordinary work.
  • The centre of the floor was left empty. In a shop, the centre is where the crowd stands; nothing may be stored there.

The outcome, in the client's words

The queue no longer crosses the doorway, and two more tables fit where the till stood. The owners reported their best New Year fortnight in the shop's six years — their words, and a bakery's season depends on more than a floor plan. What the plan can honestly claim is the doorway, the queue and the tables.

“She moved our till less than a metre and gave us back our doorway. The queue stands along the wall now, under the menu boards, and two more tables fit where the till used to stand. The plastic frog stayed in the market, where it belongs.”

Grace Wong, co-owner — on this reading, November 2025

Fee: shop reading S$680 + site visit S$180, from the printed table. Objects bought for the reading: none.

Toa Payoh · 92 m² four-room flat · Read March 2025

Homework in the career path

The ask

A family of five. The children's homework happened at the dining table in the middle of everything; the parents' bedroom felt, in their words, "like sleeping in a corridor"; and they wanted the flat read before the seventh month, while there was time to move things slowly.

What the plan showed

The door faces west. Laid from the door, the dining table sat squarely in the career path — the route from door to windows, which in a flat is also where everyone walks, argues and carries laundry. The study desk, such as it was, stood in the centre with no wall behind the chair. The main bedroom fell in the fame area: the brightest, busiest corner of the plan, lit by the corridor light.

What we did

  • The desk moved to the knowledge corner, chair back to the wall, lamp on the left. Homework moved with it, off the dining table.
  • The parents swapped bedrooms with the spare room, which fell in the relationships corner — quieter, darker, and away from the corridor light.
  • The stove was turned ninety degrees so the cook no longer stands with her back to the kitchen door.
  • The centre was cleared of the storage boxes that had gathered there. They went to the bomb shelter, like everyone else's.

The outcome

The family reported homework staying at the desk and the dining table being a dining table again. Total spent on objects: S$0. Every correction was furniture they already owned, moved once, in an afternoon.

Tanjong Pagar · 140 m² accounting office, nine staff · Read June 2025

Backs to the door

The ask

Two partners, seven staff, and a question they asked carefully: the office "felt unsettled", people kept leaving, and could the plan be part of it? My answer is in the outcome below, and it is the answer I give every office that asks.

What the plan showed

Both partners sat with their backs to the office door — in form-school terms, working all day without seeing who arrives. The meeting room, on the fame far wall where clients should be received, had become the store room: boxes, a dead printer, last year's calendar stock. The staff desks faced a blank wall in a row, canteen-style.

What we did

  • The partners' desks turned, so both sit with a wall behind the chair and the door in view.
  • The meeting room was cleared back to the fame wall — the shopfront of an office — with the firm's name and its light where a client's eye lands first.
  • Staff desks paired face-to-face in the family and knowledge areas, off the career path, so the route from door to windows runs clear.

The outcome, plainly

I told them the turnover was a pay review, not a floor plan. They did both. Six months on, the partners report the office calmer and two hires still in post — and they are the first to say the pay review did the heavy lifting. The plan's share was smaller and real: nobody works with their back to the door any more.

Joo Chiat · Peranakan shophouse café · Festival refresh, January 2026

The door onto the five-foot way

A short one, and a common shophouse problem: the front door opens straight onto the descending steps of the five-foot way, so the plan's career path runs downhill and out. The reading put a screen and a heavy threshold mat just inside the door — furniture, not a trinket — moved the menu board to the fame wall where the street can see it, and the till to the wealth corner. The café's own tiled floor, earth underfoot, needed nothing at all. Read in an afternoon, in time for the New Year.
