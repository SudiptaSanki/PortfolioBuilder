Advent · the second Saturday · 7.30 pm

Four voices, nine lessons, and a church lit only by candles.

I direct a choir of twenty-four singers at St Brigid’s. Once a year we sing the service of nine lessons and carols in the dark, and everything — the tuning, the balance, the breath — has to be settled before the first candle is lit.

    The four parts

    Who stands where, and why

    Soprano

    C4 – A5 · 6 singers

    The tune, mostly, and the descant when there is one. I put the strongest voices at the ends of the row, not the middle, because the ends are what the congregation hears.

    Alto

    G3 – D5 · 6 singers

    The hardest line in most carols: close to the soprano, never louder than it, and expected to find a note that is not in the tune. Altos are the reason a chord sounds in tune.

    Tenor

    C3 – G4 · 6 singers

    Often the third of the chord, which means the tenor decides whether the harmony is warm or hollow. They also carry the melody in two of the nine carols.

    Bass

    F2 – D4 · 6 singers

    The floor. In a candlelit service the bass is felt more than heard, and if it is even slightly sharp the whole choir floats upward by the fourth carol.
