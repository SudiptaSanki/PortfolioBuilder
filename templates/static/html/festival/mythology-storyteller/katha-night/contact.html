Before you write

What does a night cost?

Public courtyard nights are a split of the gate after the host's costs, with a small guarantee. Private events are a flat fee, quoted after I know the date and travel. Schools in Lucknow pay half. I do not ask for a fee from a public baithak that cannot cover its own tea.

How much space do you need?

A telling space of about 2.4 by 2 metres, a wall or screen behind it, and enough depth for the audience to sit in rows. The courtyard planner on the home page will tell you whether your room fits before you write.

Can children come?

Yes, and they usually make the night better. Tell me the age of the youngest listener so I can choose the last tale. The parrot story is safe for five and up; Savitri and Hidimba are better after twelve.

Do you travel outside Lucknow?

Yes, for festivals and for a cluster of nights. Travel and one night's stay are added to the fee. I do not fly for a single hour-long set; it is bad for the telling and worse for the carbon.

Is the telling recorded?

Only if you ask and the audience is told at the gate. I prefer not to record: a tale told for a room behaves differently when it knows there will be a file. If you need a recording for an archive, we can agree on one camera and one microphone, and the audience can move if they would rather not be in it.

What I bring

Two battery lamps and a third oil lamp, a low seat, a shawl that doubles as a prop, and a printed programme with the sources on the back. No screen, no projector, no soundtrack.

The audience brings the rest: coughs, laughter, a child who asks the question the tale was waiting for.

The host's part

Tea at the interval. A person at the gate with the guest list. Chairs for anyone who needs one, and a quiet word with the neighbours about the drum we do not have but they may expect.
