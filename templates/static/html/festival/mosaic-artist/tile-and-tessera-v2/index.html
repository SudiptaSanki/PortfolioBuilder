Public · Festas de Lisboa 2026

The Shoal (Um Cardume)

The ask: the Graça parish festa committee wanted something permanent for a blank gable wall after nine years of printed banners that faded by August.

What I did: designed a sardine shoal that turns the corner of the wall, cut 29,725 tesserae of Venetian glass and ceramic, and set them over 51 days with the street watching.

The outcome: unveiled 12 June 2026, the night of Santo António. The wall is now a stop on the festa walking route.

Private · Guesthouse, Sintra

The Pool at Sintra

The ask: a guesthouse pool wall that would not fade, peel or lift the way the painted mural before it had.

What I did: 18 m² of frost-rated ceramic in eleven blues, set on a waterproofed render with epoxy grout at the waterline.

The outcome: two full summers of guests and chlorine; not one tessera lifted. The blues read differently at noon and at dusk, which the owner now charges extra for, jokingly.

Private · Farmhouse, Évora

A Floor That Points North

The ask: a courtyard floor for an Évora farmhouse, made from the marble and travertine offcuts the family had kept from a quarry job in Estremoz.

What I did: sorted three tonnes of offcuts by tone, cut them to 25 mm, and set a compass pattern whose long axis sits on true north, checked with the owner’s grandfather’s compass.

The outcome: a floor you can read like a dial at noon. The offcuts that were “too ugly to keep” are the dark ring everyone asks about.

Private · Bookshop, Príncipe Real

The Beetle

The ask: a natural-history bookshop wanted a permanent sign — not a board, not paint — for the wall above its entomology shelf.

What I did: one stag beetle at twenty times life size in smalti at 10 mm, the smallest tessera I work in, so the wing cases could carry a real gradient of tone.

The outcome: children find it before their parents do. The shop has since ordered a second panel — a moth — for 2027.

How a commission runs

Four stages, no surprises

1

The wall visit

I measure, photograph and tap the wall. A hollow render or a damp patch changes the plan, and I would rather find it in week one than month two.

2

The cartoon

A full-size drawing of the piece, pinned to the actual wall. You approve the andamento — the flow lines the tesserae will follow — before a single piece is cut.

3

Cutting & setting

Sections are set on mesh in the Alfama studio, then carried to the wall — or set directly on site for floors. Deposit 40% at this stage; the rest on completion.

4

Grout day

The grout colour is chosen with you, on the wall, in daylight — never from a chart. Then sealing, and a care sheet written for your wall, not a generic one.
