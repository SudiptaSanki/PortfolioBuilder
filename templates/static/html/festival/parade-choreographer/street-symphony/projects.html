01 Parades · 2019–2026

Six routes, and what the street changed.

Every parade has one constraint you cannot argue with: the street. These are the charts that taught me the most, with band sizes, tempos and the move that had to be redrawn.

The chart book

26-01
Jan 2026

The New Year's morning block

A 400-piece band on Colorado Boulevard, eight ranks wide and fifty deep, with a 12-count company front at the broadcast camera. The problem was the turn from Colorado onto Green: the street narrows by six feet exactly where the front rank needs to widen. We took the turn in two parts, with the outside file holding a half-step, and the camera never sees the adjustment.

Band 400 · 8 ranks
Tempo 120 bpm, one cadence
Formations 6 in 2.5 miles
Hard part The Green Street turn
Note Broadcast camera on the south curb

25-04
Apr 2025

Carnaval, on a hot afternoon

A 90-piece community band in a Carnaval parade, with dancers between the sections. The chart had to leave gaps for the dance troupes to perform in, which meant the band was effectively three blocks of twenty, each with its own drum major. The heat was the other constraint: at 2 p.m. the tempo came down from 124 to 116 and the cadence changed to something with longer rests.

Band 90, in three blocks
Tempo 116 bpm (from 124)
Formations 4, with gaps for dancers
Hard part Three drum majors, one chart
Note Water stations written into the route

24-11
Nov 2024

A theme-park unit, twice a day

A 48-piece unit for a theme park parade that runs twice daily, 340 days a year. The chart has to be learnable by a rotating cast of musicians and repeatable in the dark. We kept three formations only, each entered on the same eight-count, and wrote the transitions so a new player could follow the person in front rather than count.

Band 48 · 4 ranks
Tempo 128 bpm
Formations 3, repeated nightly
Hard part Repeating cast, night lighting
Note Transitions taught by following, not counting

23-09
Sept 2023

A halftime show in the round

A 160-piece band on a football field with a stage in the centre. The drill had to form and reform around the stage without ever crossing it, which meant every move was a rotation rather than a translation. Eight minutes, 96 counts, four formations, and a final set that spelled the school's initials in a 40-yard arc.

Band 160 · field show
Tempo 132 bpm
Formations 4, 96 counts
Hard part Never crossing the stage
Note Final set in a 40-yard arc

22-06
Jun 2022

A town parade with one drum major and no time

A 38-piece town band with four rehearsals and a route that turned twice in six blocks. No room for complex drill, so the chart was almost entirely about tempo and spacing: a single block, one counter-march at the second turn, and a cadence simple enough to survive a band that had never marched together. It was the best-received parade I have charted, and the least complicated.

Band 38 · 3 ranks
Tempo 112 bpm
Formations 2, one counter-march
Hard part Four rehearsals total
Note Simplicity was the design

19-08
Oct 2019

A friendship parade in Osaka

A 120-piece band travelling to Japan, charting a route I had never walked, in a language I do not read. I flew out ten days early with a translator and a tape, and discovered that the route's famous bridge was narrower than the map suggested and that the crowd stood closer than an American parade would allow. The whole chart was redrawn in a hotel room in two nights.

Band 120 · 6 ranks
Tempo 118 bpm
Formations 4, redrawn on site
Hard part Bridge width, crowd distance
Note Ten days on site before the parade
