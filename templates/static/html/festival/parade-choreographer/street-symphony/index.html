02 The cadence bench

Eight counts, four lines. Hear it.

A cadence is a short repeating pattern that keeps feet together. Tap the grid to place hits, set the tempo, and press start. The page is silent until you do: no sound is loaded, and nothing is created until you ask for it.

03 How a chart is made

Paper first. Feet second. Cameras third.

  1. 01

    Walk the route

    At the hour of the parade, with a tape. Cameras, curbs, trees, the crown of the road and the places where the asphalt changes. A chart that ignores a curb is a chart that fails on the day.

  2. 02

    Fix the tempo

    A parade runs at 120, a competition march at 132 to 144, a funeral at 88. The tempo decides the step size and the size of every formation; it is the first number on the page and the last one changed.

  3. 03

    Draw the counts

    Every move is written in eight-count phrases, with a coordinate for each rank and file. If a move cannot be walked while playing, it is redrawn, not rehearsed harder.

  4. 04

    Rehearse to the camera

    The band runs it once for the feet, once for the music, once for the camera. The third run is the one the broadcast sees, and the one that decides whether the chart was any good.

04 The season

I take five parades a year, and one field show.

Charting begins eight months out: route walk in spring, counts in summer, rehearsals from September. The fee is per project, not per rehearsal, and includes the drill book, the cadence scores and two site visits.
