Tables I have poured at

Not all of them are in the room. Some were in bookshops, one was on a terrace during a power cut, and one was for forty people at a fair where the water ran out at six in the evening.

No. 118 · October 2026

A bookshop evening, Hindustan Park

Twelve people and a table of poetry, on the shop’s mezzanine, between the shelves. Two teas — a Wuyi Shui Xian and a 2009 puerh — chosen because the shop wanted something that would survive a reading going long. It did; the reading went forty minutes over and the third rinse of the puerh was the best cup of the night.

Guests 12 Teas Wuyi Shui Xian, aged puerh Vessel two 110 ml gaiwans Length 2 h 40 m

No. 112 · January 2026

A tea stall at the Boi Mela, Kolkata

Four hours a day for eleven days on the fairground, forty cups an hour at the peak, one plug point and no running water. The menu was deliberately short: a Darjeeling first flush brewed western-style in a big pot for people who wanted a cup of tea, and a gongfu oolong for the ones who sat down. The oolong table was full by the second evening and stayed full.

Guests about 340 a day Teas Darjeeling first flush, Ilam oolong Vessel 120 ml gaiwan, plus two 3-litre pots Length 11 days, 4 h each

No. 106 · September 2025

Staff day, a restaurant in Ballygunge

Nine people who pour tea for a living and had never been taught why their second pot tastes worse than their first. We spent the morning on water temperature and leaf ratio, and the afternoon rebuilding their service pot on a scale. They now brew at 4 g per 150 ml instead of a heaped spoon and a guess, and they throw the first pot away.

Guests 9 staff Teas Assam, Darjeeling, a green for contrast Vessel their own service pots Length one day

No. 097 · March 2025

A wedding breakfast, Lake Gardens

Twenty-two guests at seven in the morning, after a night of someone else’s music. A white tea and a light oolong, nothing roasted, nothing that would sit heavily. The family wanted the tea poured at the table rather than from a dispenser, so we ran three gaiwans and two pourers and it took twenty minutes to serve everyone the first cup.

Guests 22 Teas Silver needle, Ilam oolong Vessel three 100 ml gaiwans Length 3 h

No. 089 · November 2024

Gallery opening, a room with no kitchen

An opening for a painter who did not want alcohol. We set up on a trestle behind the entrance, two burners and a tray, and poured a single tea all evening — a Mi Lan Xiang dan cong — because a crowd of ninety cannot be given a choice. It was the first time I saw people standing in a queue for a second cup of tea at a gallery.

Guests about 90 Teas Phoenix dan cong, Mi Lan Xiang Vessel 150 ml gaiwan, continuous Length 4 h

No. 074 · May 2024

A hotel’s tea list, rebuilt

Six weeks of work with the food and beverage team: tasting forty leaves, cutting the list from thirty-one to nine, and writing the brewing notes that go behind the bar. The list is shorter, the staff can actually brew what is on it, and the hotel stopped buying a tea nobody ordered.

Guests 14 staff across two sessions Teas 40 tasted, 9 kept Vessel bar pots and one gaiwan Length 6 weeks, part time

No. 061 · December 2023

A birthday, six people, one old cake

A 1998 puerh cake brought by the guest of honour and opened at the table. We brewed it in the Yixing pot with two rinses and a full boil, and it gave fourteen infusions before it gave up. Nobody talked for the first three cups, which is the highest compliment a table can pay.

Guests 6 Teas 1998 puerh, then a light oolong Vessel 90 ml Yixing Length 2 h 30 m

No. 043 · April 2023

A Poila Boishakh table, a lane in Tangra

The first of the street tables, set up outside the door for the Bengali new year. Two benches, a shade cloth, and a menu written on cardboard. We served a green tea and a black, both iced after noon because the lane was touching forty degrees. The ice was made from the same filtered water as the tea, which is the only way iced tea is worth drinking.

Guests about 120 through the day Teas green, Assam, both iced after noon Vessel 150 ml gaiwan, two jugs Length 9 h

If you are thinking of booking

席

A table, not a show

I pour, you drink, and the conversation is whatever it is. There is no music, no incense and no costume. Come as you are; the tea does not care what you are wearing.

量

Tell me the numbers

How many people, which evening, and whether anyone cannot take caffeine after six. Those three answers decide the whole menu, and it is easier to settle them before the kettle goes on.

敬

One thing I ask

Do not wear strong perfume to the table. It is not etiquette, it is chemistry — a jasmine oolong and a rose attar are fighting for the same part of your nose, and the tea will lose.
