Pottery with no clay in it at all.

Blue pottery is quartz, glass and borax, shaped by hand and painted with cobalt oxide before a single low firing. We make it in Jaipur the way the Kottah potters brought it here in 1868 — and we still fire at 820°C, not 1,300.

The kiln is loaded at six in the evening and opened the next morning. A piece that cools too fast crazes; a piece that is painted too thickly runs. About one in five comes out of the kiln fit only for the shard pile.

A body that is mostly quartz

Blue pottery is not clay. The body is quartz powder, powdered glass, borax, sodium carbonate and fuller's earth, mixed with water and left to stand. It has almost no plasticity, which is why it is pressed into moulds and joined with slip rather than thrown on a wheel.

Because there is no clay, the body does not vitrify the way stoneware does. It is fired once, at 820°C, and the glaze does the work of making it watertight. Tap a plate with a fingernail and it rings like a bell; drop it and it breaks into long shards.
