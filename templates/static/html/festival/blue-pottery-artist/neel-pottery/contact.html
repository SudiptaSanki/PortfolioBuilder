Visiting

Ten minutes from Jal Mahal on the Amer road; any auto will know the gate. The front room is the shop and the workshop is behind it — ask, and someone will take you through. The kiln is not opened for visitors outside Tuesday mornings, because the temperature drop matters more than the tour.

Half-day course

Paint one plate or one tile under Shabnam's eye, then leave it to be fired with the next load. Three hours, ₹3,500, including the piece and shipping within India. Thursdays only, two people at a time, booked a fortnight ahead.

What a piece costs, and why

A bowl is ₹3,200 because a fifth of everything fired is thrown away, because the cobalt is painted by hand and not printed, and because the kiln runs for eight hours whether it holds six pieces or sixty.

Small surahis begin at ₹2,800. A 46 cm surahi is ₹8,400. Tiles are ₹1,900 each, or ₹1,600 in a run of fifty or more. A workshop course is ₹3,500 and includes the firing, which is the part people forget costs money.

Things to know before you order

Blue pottery is not ovenware, not flameware and not for the freezer. A cold plate under hot food can craze the glaze in a single meal, and a piece that crazes cannot be repaired. Hand wash, dry face down, and keep it out of the sun.

Colour varies between firings and sometimes within one load. If you are ordering a set for a table, order it in one go — a reorder in six months will not match, and no honest studio will tell you otherwise.
