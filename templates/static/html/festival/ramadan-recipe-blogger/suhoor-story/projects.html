Twelve recipes, and the mistakes I made learning them.

Every recipe here has been cooked before dawn at least three times. Times are honest — they include the washing, and they assume one person and one stove.

The recipes

Malai paratha

35 minutes, including the rest · makes 6 · vegetarian

You need

  • 300 g whole wheat atta, plus a little for dusting
  • 180 ml warm water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 60 g ghee
  • 2 tbsp thick cream (malai)

Method

Mix the atta and salt, add the water in three goes and knead for six minutes until the dough is smooth and slightly tacky. Cover it and leave it while you make the eggs — twenty minutes is enough, an hour is better.

Divide into six. Roll each one to a disc about 15 cm across, brush with ghee, dust with flour, fold into a fan and coil it into a spiral. Roll the spiral out again gently; do not press hard or the layers glue together.

Cook on a hot tawa for a minute, turn, brush the top with ghee, turn again, and press the edges with a cloth. The paratha should puff in patches, not all over. Finish with a smear of cream and a pinch of salt.

The mistake: cooking it fast. A paratha on a high flame browns outside and stays leather inside, and by the time you eat it, it is a shoe sole.

Anda bhurji

12 minutes · serves 2 · high protein

You need

  • 4 eggs, beaten with a pinch of salt
  • 1 small onion, chopped fine
  • 2 green chillies, slit
  • 1 tomato, deseeded and chopped
  • 1 tbsp oil or ghee
  • A handful of coriander

Method

Heat the fat, add the onion and chillies and cook until the onion is translucent but not brown — about three minutes. Add the tomato and a pinch of salt and cook until it collapses.

Pour in the eggs and stir slowly with a spatula, pushing the set parts to the middle. When about two-thirds is set, take the pan off the heat, add the coriander, and cover it. It finishes cooking in its own heat and is still soft when it reaches the table.

The mistake: cooking it dry. Dry bhurji is scrambled egg; soft bhurji is breakfast.

Sheer khurma

50 minutes · serves 4 · make the night before

You need

  • 1.2 litres full-fat milk
  • 80 g vermicelli, broken into short pieces
  • 120 g dates, stoned and sliced
  • 30 g ghee
  • 40 g sugar, or to taste
  • 20 g almonds and pistachios, slivered
  • A pinch of saffron in two spoons of warm milk

Method

Fry the vermicelli in half the ghee until it is the colour of pale straw — this takes two minutes and can go from pale to burnt in ten seconds. Set aside. Fry the dates in the same pan for a minute, then add the milk.

Simmer the milk with the dates for twenty minutes, stirring so it does not catch, until it has reduced by a third. Add the vermicelli, sugar and saffron and cook another eight minutes. Finish with the nuts and the rest of the ghee.

Cool it, refrigerate, and reheat in the morning with a splash of milk. It is better the next day, which is the whole reason it belongs in a suhoor plan.

The mistake: adding the vermicelli and walking away. It thickens fast and then it is porridge.

Keema matar

40 minutes · serves 3 · freezes well

You need

  • 400 g mutton mince
  • 150 g green peas
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tsp red chilli, 1 tsp turmeric, 2 tsp coriander powder
  • 1 tomato, chopped
  • 3 tbsp oil, salt, coriander to finish

Method

Brown the onions properly — twelve minutes, not four. Add the ginger-garlic and the dry spices, cook until the oil separates, then the tomato. Add the mince and fry hard for five minutes, breaking it up, until it has lost its pink colour and is starting to catch.

Add a cup of water, cover, and cook on a low flame for twenty minutes. Add the peas in the last five. Finish with coriander and a squeeze of lemon.

The mistake: mince that is too lean. If there is no fat, the keema will be dry and no amount of water fixes it.

Sattu sharbat

5 minutes · serves 2 · no cooking

You need

  • 4 tbsp sattu flour (roasted gram flour)
  • 500 ml cold water
  • 1 lemon
  • Black salt, roasted cumin, a little green chilli

Method

Whisk the sattu into the water a spoon at a time so it does not lump. Add the lemon, black salt, cumin and the chilli, finely chopped. Taste it: it should be savoury, sour and a little smoky. Drink it while you are cooking, not with the meal.

The mistake: making it the night before. It separates and turns into a paste at the bottom of the glass.

Dahi phulki

15 minutes in the morning · serves 2 · assemble only

You need

  • 300 g thick yoghurt, whisked
  • 60 g phulki (small gram-flour dumplings), fried the night before
  • 2 tbsp tamarind pulp
  • 1 tsp roasted cumin, black salt, chilli powder
  • Mint and coriander

Method

Soak the phulki in warm water for two minutes, squeeze it gently and fold it into the whisked yoghurt. Add the tamarind, the spices, and taste. It should be cold, sour and a little too salty — that is what makes it work at four in the morning.

The mistake: soaking it too long. Squashy phulki is the reason people say they do not like dahi phulki.

Six more are in the notebook and not yet typed up: the egg-and-bread porridge my father asks for, a chickpea sundal, a potato-and-onion paratha, a millet khichdi, a date-and-walnut shake, and the only thing my brother will eat at that hour, which is toast.

How to use this in Ramadan

Pick three, not seven

One heavy thing, one protein, one drink. A table with seven dishes at four in the morning is a table nobody eats from, because everyone is too tired to lift the lid.

Cook the long things at night

After taraweeh, before you sleep, make the keema, the khurma, the dough. The morning should be reheating and eggs, nothing more.

Salt less than you think

You will drink less water than you should. A very salty suhoor makes the whole next day heavier, and no one needs that at three in the afternoon.

Keep water by the bed

Not in the kitchen. You will not walk to the kitchen after the adhan, and the two glasses by the bed are the ones that actually get drunk.
