The meal nobody writes about.

Every Ramadan recipe book is about iftar. This one is about the other meal: the one at four in the morning, eaten half asleep, which decides how the next sixteen hours will go.

Three recipes from the last seven mornings, in the order I would cook them. Everything here is measured for two people and can be on the table in under forty minutes, because at four in the morning nobody is going to simmer anything for an hour.

    Notes from a kitchen at four

    The first Ramadan after my mother died, I cooked suhoor for my father and my brother for thirty days and wrote down every meal, because I could not sleep anyway and the notebook was on the table. That is how this started. It was not a blog for two years; it was a list of what worked and what my father refused to eat twice.

    What I learned in those thirty days is that suhoor is not a smaller iftar. Iftar is a feast with company and noise. Suhoor is a quiet, functional meal eaten by people who would rather be asleep, and the food has to behave accordingly: it must be quick, it must not need a second pan, and it must not be so heavy that it makes the day worse.

    So the recipes here have rules. No more than eight ingredients. Nothing that needs an hour of attention. Nothing that has to be fried at the last minute unless it takes under five. And nothing that tastes bad cold, because half of you will take it back to bed.

    At four in the morning, a recipe with twenty ingredients is not a recipe. It is a threat.

    What I keep in the pantry

    • Ghee, in a steel tin, never in the fridge
    • Whole wheat atta, ground every three weeks
    • Dates from Medina, and a second cheaper box for cooking
    • Sattu flour, for the days when nobody wants to chew
    • Green chillies, curry leaves, coriander — bought the evening before
    • A packet of vermicelli that is only for sheer khurma

    One letter a week, during Ramadan

    Every Friday before the last ten nights I send one email: what I cooked, what failed, and the shopping list for the week ahead. No advertising, no sponsored pans. It stops the day after Eid.
