About

Liesl Brandt keeps the long table.

Twenty-eight seasons at Kastanienhof am Isar. Before that, eleven years of waiting tables, which is where the bench arrangement actually comes from.

I grew up two streets from the garden and spent the summers of my teens clearing steins for the Pichler family, who ran it then. In 1998 they handed me the keys and a ledger with three years of weather written in the margins. I have added to the ledger since, one line per evening: wind, rain, the first evening the chestnuts opened, the night the brass band played until the trams stopped.

The work is not glamorous. It is 180 chairs counted twice, four hundred pretzels ordered on a Thursday, and a phone call to a bride because the forecast changed. What I have learned is that people do not remember the menu as well as they remember where they sat. So the seating comes first: who faces the trees, who sits near the serving hatch, which table gets the lantern and which gets the last of the sun.

Bavarian beer garden custom is unusually generous: you may bring your own food, you sit where there is room, and the beer comes from the brewery that has supplied the garden since 1954. I keep to that. My contribution is the room the evening happens in, and a table long enough that a group of six can become a group of fourteen without anyone standing up.

What a hosted evening includes

A laid table with linen or paper, depending on the evening; benches arranged and counted; the brotzeit boards built on site; a server for every twenty guests; steins pulled and refilled without a queue; and the tent decision made by three in the afternoon, not at the door.

I host April to October. Anything indoors between November and March I pass to the Wirtshaus around the corner, and I will tell you why I am passing it on.

Seasons worth writing in the ledger

A garden keeps its own record. These are the entries I still read back.

  • 1998

    The first evening

    Fourteen tables, sixty guests, one cask of Hacker-Pschorr and a chestnut that dropped into a bowl of Obatzda at eight o’clock. Nobody minded.

  • 2006

    The summer of the long bench

    We joined six tables end to end for the World Cup final. Forty-two metres of tablecloth, and the first time I realised the garden could hold a room that size.

  • 2014

    The tent arrives

    A week of rain in September taught me to rent the canvas before the season, not after. It has paid for itself every September since.

  • 2021

    Brotzeit from the region only

    I moved the cheese, the fish and the pretzels to three producers within forty kilometres. The menu got shorter; the evenings did not.

  • 2025

    The ledgers, scanned

    Twenty-eight years of weather notes went to the city archive. The originals stay behind the bar, in the drawer with the bottle opener.
