PILOT RPC 2041-KA · Bengaluru

Nine years of flying,
and not one incident.

I started with a Phantom over a friend’s farm in 2016, crashed it into a fence within a week, and spent the next two years learning why. The licence came in 2019. The habit of walking a site twice before I open the case came earlier, and it is the reason the record is still clean.

How I got here

I studied mechanical engineering in Manipal, worked three years in a CNC shop in Peenya, and spent my weekends flying a second-hand Phantom over the city’s lakes. A friend who ran a coffee estate in Chikmagalur asked for a video of his land in 2018; I shot it on a Saturday, cut it on a Sunday, and by Tuesday two more estate owners had written to me. In 2019 I left the shop, took the DGCA remote pilot course at a training school in Bengaluru, and registered the practice.

The engineering background is not incidental. A drone is a machine with a battery, four motors and a wind envelope, and the people who scare me most are the ones who treat it as a camera. I fly conservatively: 80% of my shots are at 40–110 m, the aircraft never leaves visual line of sight, and I will cancel a shoot for wind before I will cancel it for clouds.

Most of my work is in the Western Ghats — coffee, cardamom, tea, and the occasional bridge — but the shot log runs from Rajasthan to the backwaters. I work alone on small shoots and with a spotter on anything near people, roads or power lines.

Certification and permissions

  • DGCA Remote Pilot Certificate 2041-KA, issued under the Drone Rules 2021, renewed 2025.
  • Digital Sky permissions filed per site: green zones are auto-approved, yellow zones need a lead time, red zones are a hard no.
  • Third-party liability insurance to ₹10 lakh through a Bengaluru broker, certificate available on request.
  • Flight logging: every flight is logged with time, coordinates, altitude and battery state; the file goes to the client with the footage.
  • No-fly discipline: 5 km airport buffer, 120 m AGL ceiling, no night flights, no flights over crowds, and written permission before any protected monument.

What I will not fly

  • A site I have not walked, unless the client accepts that the first battery is a recce and may produce nothing usable.
  • Over a wedding crowd, a procession or a festival without a cordoned area and a second person watching the aircraft’s horizon.
  • Anywhere the wind is gusting over 25 km/h, in rain, or in the twenty minutes before a storm that a local can feel coming.
  • A protected monument, a defence installation or a red zone, however good the shot and however large the budget. The answer is no before it is a price.

Kit honesty

Everything on the gear list is mine and paid for. No rented bodies on a shoot, because I know how my own aircraft behaves in a gust and I do not want to learn another one on your schedule.

Three kinds of job, three ways of working

estate / landscape

One day

Recce at dawn, two flight windows, a 90-second cut in 6K, ten stills, all raw files. The client usually asks for a second day once they see the first.

architecture

Two days

One day of light study and one of flying, because a building faces one way and the sun does not care. Includes interior FPV with prop guards if the space allows.

documentary

Three to five days

Built around a subject that moves. I fly when the action allows and stand down when it does not; a documentary needs the drone to be invisible in the edit.
