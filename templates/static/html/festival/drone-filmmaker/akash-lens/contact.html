Permits I handle

Digital Sky filing, green- and yellow-zone permissions, ASI letters for protected sites, and the local police intimation that a surprising number of shoots forget. I do not promise a red zone; nobody can.

What I need before the day

The exact boundary of the property, the nearest airport, any power lines or high-tension pylons, and whether there will be people on site. A satellite pin is good; a phone photo of the gate is better.

Insurance

Third-party liability to ₹10 lakh. The certificate goes out with the quote, not after the invoice.

If you have never commissioned a drone shoot

Three things decide whether the day works: the weather, the permission and the light, roughly in that order. Weather I can forecast and work around. Permission takes time — a yellow zone can be days, a monument can be weeks — so the earlier you write, the better the site you can use. Light is fixed by the month, which is why I will tell you that your east-facing house is a morning shoot and there is no point arguing with it.

You do not need a storyboard. You do need to know who will watch the film and what you want them to feel at the end. Everything else — the shots, the route, the altitude — comes out of that.

Response time

Two working days, usually one. If I am on a shoot in a valley with no signal, you will get a short reply the evening I am back and a full quote the day after.
