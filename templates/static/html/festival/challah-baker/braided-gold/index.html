Challah baker · Mile End

Six strands, one loaf, every Friday before sundown.

I bake challah the way my grandmother did in Côte-des-Neiges: bread flour, a long cold ferment, six strands pulled by hand and a double egg wash. The loaves are 1.2 kg before baking and they come out of the oven at 88 °C, which is how you know the crumb has set.

Work out a braid before you mix

Set the flour and the water you plan to use. The bench returns the dough weight, how many loaves it makes, what each strand should weigh, how long that strand has to be, the loaf you end up with and a bake time for a deck oven at 190 °C. The numbers come from the bakery's own bakes: eggs, oil, sugar, salt and yeast at 22.6% of the flour, 3.7 g of dough per centimetre of strand, and a 28% take-up when the strands cross.

    What the numbers mean

    A 60% dough holds a six-strand braid without flattening. Mix at 20:30, retard overnight at 4 °C, braid cold at 06:00 and bake at 07:30.

    The bench assumes a 4 °C retard, a 22 °C bench and a deck oven with 12 mm of steam in the first four minutes. Home ovens run 15 °C hot; drop the dial to 175 °C and trust the probe.
