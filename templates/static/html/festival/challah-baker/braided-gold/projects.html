The loaves · six on the list

Six loaves, and the reasons for each.

The list is short on purpose. Every loaf here has been baked at least two hundred times, which is when the formula stops being a recipe and becomes something you can judge by hand. Shapes change with the week; the dough does not.

6 loaves

01

Friday Six

Every week · six strands

The loaf the bakery is built around. Six strands, sesame on one half and poppy on the other, baked to a deep mahogany. The crumb is fine and slightly sweet, the kind that holds a slice of butter without tearing. This is the one to order if you have never had the bread before.

1,150 g baked
60% hydration
31 min at 190 °C
88 °C core

02

Round Crown

Rosh Hashanah · coiled

A spiral, not a braid: the coil begins at the centre and winds out, so the loaf reads as a crown from above. Raisins on request, never by default. The round shape is for a sweet year, and the bake runs seven minutes longer because the centre is deeper than a straight loaf.

1,200 g baked
58% hydration
38 min at 190 °C
88 °C core

03

Turban

Holidays · spiral knot

The coil is wound from the outside in and finished with a knot set just off centre, so the dome leans the way a turban does. It is the most forgiving shape to bake and the least forgiving to braid: one loose turn and the whole crown slumps in the oven.

1,180 g baked
60% hydration
36 min at 190 °C
88 °C core

04

Rye and Honey

Fridays in winter · five strands

Half dark rye, half bread flour, buckwheat honey and toasted caraway. A denser dough, so it is braided with five strands and proofed an hour longer. It slices thin, keeps for four days and is better on the second day than the first.

1,050 g baked
64% hydration
34 min at 185 °C
90 °C core

05

Za'atar Sesame

Celebration · three strands

A three-strand loaf brushed with olive oil instead of egg and covered in za'atar, sesame and a little sumac. The oil softens the crust, so it is baked hotter and shorter. Best torn, still warm, with labneh and a tomato that has been salted ten minutes.

900 g baked
58% hydration
28 min at 200 °C
88 °C core

06

Water Challah

Celebration · pareve, no egg

No egg and no oil, just flour, water, sugar, salt and yeast. The crust stays pale and the crumb is lighter and chewier. It is the loaf to serve when the table has both meat and dairy, and the one my grandmother would have called a weekday bread.

980 g baked
62% hydration
29 min at 195 °C
87 °C core

On the bench

The bake calendar, twelve weeks out

Friday pickup runs every week except the two weeks the bakery closes in August. Holiday shapes appear a fortnight before the holiday, and orders close on Wednesday at 18:00.

Shapes and when they come

  • Six strands — every Friday, all year.
  • Round crown — from the first week of Elul through Simchat Torah, and again for the second night of Rosh Hashanah.
  • Turban — Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur break-fast and any table that asks for one.
  • Three strands — weekday orders, and the za'atar loaf, which needs a wider braid to hold the topping.
  • Little knots — eighty grams each, forty to a tray, Sunday market only.

What changes a loaf

Three things, in order of effect: the flour year, the weather and the retard. Quebec flour drinks more water in February than in September, so the formula stays at 60% and the bench adjusts by feel. A humid week slows the proof; a dry week speeds it. The overnight retard is the only variable I refuse to shorten.

Seeds and toppings

Sesame, poppy, everything, za'atar, or none. Seeds go on after the second egg wash so they stay put through the oven. Nothing is painted on after the bake; if the topping needs glue, it is the wrong topping.
