The bench · fitting a sher

A couplet has to fit before it can be beautiful.

Nastaliq runs on a diagonal and hangs from the pen, so the width of a line is decided by the word shapes, not by the count of letters. Choose a sher, a script size and a panel, and the bench will measure both misras, tell you whether they fit on one line each, and work out the paper, ink, hours and price. The measurements use the same Nastaliq font these panels are proofed in.

How a panel is made

The pen is cut, not bought.

A qalam is a reed, cut and slit by hand, and it lasts about a month. The nib is trimmed to a width that suits the size of the script: 2 mm for a book page, 4 mm for a wall panel. The ink is lampblack, gum arabic and water, ground on a stone and left to sit for a week. The paper is wasli, seven sheets pasted and burnished until it rings when you tap it.

None of that is romantic. It is the reason a panel can be read in a hall two hundred years after it was written.

The tools and the training
  • 01

    The sher

    Chosen with you, or by you. I will say if the words are too long for the size of the wall.

  • 02

    The proof

    Written at size on newsprint, three or four times, until the diagonal sits right.

  • 03

    The paper

    Cut, sized and burnished. A mistake here cannot be repaired, only started again.

  • 04

    The writing

    One breath per word, no ruling line, no erasing. A couplet takes two to three hours.

  • 05

    The illumination

    Gold, gouache and a squirrel-hair brush, worked around the text after the ink is dry.
