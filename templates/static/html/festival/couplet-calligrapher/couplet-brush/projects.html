Commissions · six panels

Words chosen for a wall, then written for it.

Each commission begins with the space: how far the viewer stands, where the light falls, how much wall there is. A sher that is right for a hall is wrong for a book page. The panels below are the recent ones I can show.

6 commissions

The Nampally Panel

دلِ ناداں تجھے ہوا کیا ہے
آخر اس درد کی دوا کیا ہے

“Foolish heart, what has come over you? What, in the end, is the cure for this ache?”

A wedding gift for a house in Nampally, hung in the hall where the light comes from the left. The sher was chosen by the bride's grandmother, who recited it at the wedding; the panel was written at 24 mm so it reads from the doorway but resolves into the loops only when you stand close.

wasli, gold rule

The Chowmahalla Wall

ہزاروں خواہشیں ایسی کہ ہر خواہش پہ دم نکلے
بہت نکلے مرے ارمان لیکن پھر بھی کم نکلے

“A thousand desires, each one worth dying for; many longings surfaced, and still too few.”

A long panel for a restaurant courtyard, written at 30 mm to be read across the room. The illumination took nine days on its own: gold vine, lapis dots, and a border wide enough to hold the eye at that distance. The owner wanted the whole ghazal; we agreed on one sher and a better wall.

₹64,000Ghalib · 90 × 32 cm
silk-mounted, illuminated

The Bagh-e-Aam Book

پتہ پتہ بوٹا بوٹا حال ہمارا جانے ہے

“Every leaf, every seedling knows of my condition.”

A title page for a small edition of Mir's ghazals, printed from a photograph of the original at actual size. The plain border is deliberate: the book needed no gold, and the publisher had no budget for any. Three hundred copies, sewn by hand.

₹9,500Mir · 30 × 20 cm
ivory board, plain

The Asaf Jah Nameplate

آصف جاہ

A family name, written to be cut in brass.

A nameplate for a house in Banjara Hills, drawn in nastaliq at 40 mm and etched into a brass plate by a karigar in the old city. The drawing had to be simplified for the acid: the hairlines of the script were thickened by a fifth and the nuqtas enlarged. A second version was cut in wood for the gate.

₹18,000nastaliq · 45 × 15 cm
brass, etched

The Tolichowki Scroll

یہ نہ تھی ہماری قسمت کہ وصالِ یار ہوتا

A long scroll written for a nikah, unrolled on the table beside the registrar's book. The couplet is a strange choice for a wedding and the couple chose it anyway, for the last line. Written in one sitting, four hours, with the illumination added over the following week.

The Ghazal Portfolio

بازیچۂ اطفال ہے دنیا مرے آگے

“The world, to me, is a children's game.”

Twelve panels, one for each sher of a ghazal, written over four months and boxed in a cloth portfolio. The same size throughout, the same qalam, the same ink, so the set reads as one hand. Two of the twelve were rewritten from the beginning after a single word drifted off the line.

What a commission costs, and why

A panel is priced by the hours, the paper and the illumination, not by the number of words. A short sher written large can cost more than a long one written small, because the pen is wider and every mistake costs a sheet of wasli. The bench on the front page will give you the same sum for any size and border.

Wedding commissions carry a premium for the calendar: from October to February the studio is full, and a date inside six weeks means working nights. I will say so rather than quietly miss the date. If the date is fixed, tell me at once and I will tell you at once whether it is possible.

Books and editions are quoted separately, because the printing and the binding are not mine. I will work with your printer and check the first proof.

A note on words

I will not write a couplet I cannot stand behind.

If you bring a sher, I will check the metre and the attribution before it goes on paper. If it is misquoted — and many are — I will tell you, and we will decide together whether to correct it or to keep the version your family knows. I have written both, and I have refused twice.
