The qalam · tools and training

The reed is cut to the hand that holds it.

A nastaliq panel is not written with a pen from a shop. The qalam is a length of reed, cut, split and trimmed to a nib the width of the script, and the ink is ground from lampblack and gum. Everything else — the paper, the burnishing, the gold — exists to let the writing last.

Nastaliq is written on a diagonal

Nastaliq hangs from the pen at roughly 45 degrees, with each word descending to the left and the next word beginning above and to the right. That is why a line of nastaliq is not a row of separate letters but a chain of loops, and why the width of a line cannot be estimated by counting characters. The same words can occupy half a centimetre more or less depending on how the kashida is stretched and where the pen is lifted.

The script has no baseline in the Latin sense. There is an imaginary line, the khatt-e-mustaqeem, and a good calligrapher keeps every word sitting on it while the letters cascade below. A panel that looks uneven almost always has one word that has drifted off the line.

I write in the style of the Lucknow and Hyderabad schools, which are less steep than the Iranian nastaliq and a little wider in the bowl. My ustad was Ghulam Hussain Qureshi, who taught in Hyderabad for forty years and would not let a student touch ink for the first six months.

What is on the desk

Qalamقلم
A reed, usually from a species of marsh grass, dried for a year before it is cut. The nib is split with a penknife and trimmed on a bone or a piece of glass. A 2 mm nib for a book page, 4 mm for a wall panel. A qalam lasts about a month of daily work.
Siyahiسیاہی
Ink made from lampblack, gum arabic and water, ground on a stone slab with a flat stone. It is left to sit for a week, then thinned. It should smell faintly of smoke and never of chemicals.
Wasliوصلی
Paper made of several thin sheets pasted together, burnished with a stone until the surface is hard enough that the ink sits on top rather than sinking. Seven layers for a panel, three for a practice sheet.
Nuqtaنقطہ
The dot made by the nib, used as the unit of proportion. The width of an alif is one nuqta, the height of a letter is measured in nuqtas, and a student spends weeks making rows of them before writing a word.
Sherشعر
A couplet: two misras, rhymed and metred. The unit of almost every commission. A ghazal is a chain of shers, and a single one is enough for a wall.
Behrبحر
The metre. A sher is written to a behr, and a calligrapher working from memory will feel at once when a line does not scan. I check every commission against a written scansion before it is proofed.

When the writing is done

The ink has to dry for two days

Not because it is wet, but because the gum in it needs time to settle. A panel that is mounted too soon will bloom at the edges in the first monsoon.

Illumination is a separate craft

The gold is applied with a squirrel-hair brush over a size made from gum and white lead. I do the illumination myself on small panels and work with a naqqash on larger ones. On an illuminated panel the border can take longer than the writing: nine days on the Chowmahalla wall, against three for the sher.

Mounting

Panels are mounted on a rigid board with a linen hinge, so the paper can move with the weather. They are framed under glass with a spacer, or left bare for a wall that will take screws. Silk-mounted panels are sewn, not glued.

How I was taught, and how I teach

I started at fourteen in a classroom in Moghalpura, copying the dots of a senior student while the ustad corrected my grip. For six months I made nothing but dots and alifs. After two years I was allowed to copy a bayt; after four I was given a commission and told to sign it only if I could bear to.

I take two students at a time now, on Sunday mornings, and the terms are the same as they were for me: a year of practice before a finished panel, and no payment until the first commission is sold. One of them is a graphic designer who wants to draw a typeface; the other is a girl of sixteen who already has a better hand than I did at twenty.

I also cut qalams for other calligraphers and teach the cutting, which is the part most people skip. A badly cut nib will ruin a good hand, and no amount of practice fixes it.

The bench

Fit a sher before you commission it.

The panel bench measures both misras in the same Nastaliq face and tells you whether the panel you have in mind will hold them. It is not a substitute for the proof — nothing is — but it saves a conversation about a wall that is too narrow.
