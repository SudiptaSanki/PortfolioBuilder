The morning

Four hours, in an order that cannot be hurried

A muhurtham call time of four in the morning is normal. The running order below is what happens between the front door and the photograph, and it does not change because someone is late.

The running order

  1. 4:00Arrive, set up, lights on the bride
  2. 4:20Skin prep — cleanse, tone, serum, eye patches
  3. 4:45Base, in daylight, checked at the door
  4. 5:20Eyes and brows, one at a time
  5. 5:50Hair, then the jasmine
  6. 6:15Saree, from the fall upwards
  7. 6:45Jewellery, final check, out of the room

Two assistants come with me for a muhurtham: one on hair, one on the family. I do the bride's face myself.

Skin first, colour second, and no surprises at the mandapam

The mistake most brides make is booking the makeup as if it were a photograph. It is not. It is four hours in a hot room with a hundred relatives, and the face has to hold from six in the morning until lunch without turning into a mask.

So the skin comes first. I start with what is already there — hydration, a little colour correction, and a base that is matched at the jaw and checked at the door in daylight rather than under the room's yellow bulbs. If your neck and face disagree, the photograph will tell everyone.

Colour comes after, and it comes from the sari. A Kanjeevaram red is warmer than it looks under a tube light; a pastel one will make a strong lip look painted on. I bring 40 lip colours and use one.

01
Trial, six to eight weeks before

Two hours, in the studio, with the sari. We photograph it in daylight and again under warm light, and change what fails.

02
The morning itself

I arrive before the light does. Two assistants, one case of colour, one of tools, and a steamer nobody asked for but everybody uses.

03
Until the last photograph

I stay for the muhurtham, not just the makeup. A lip needs a touch after the garland, and the veil always moves.

Looks

Three that keep coming back to the studio

Not trends. These are the three briefs I get most often, and each one is a different answer to the same question: how much colour can a face carry at six in the morning.

The six o'clock bride

Muhurtham · Kanjeevaram red

Warm base, soft brown eye, a rosewood lip that survives the garland. The one I do most often and the one that photographs most reliably.

See the brief

The reception eye

Evening · pastel or gold sari

More definition, because the light is worse. A smoked lid in bronze, skin kept luminous, and a lip that is not competing with the jewellery.

See the brief

The ivory bride

Muhurtham · ivory Kanjeevaram

Ivory cloth throws light back at the face, so everything has to be one shade warmer than it looks on the palette. Peach, not pink.
