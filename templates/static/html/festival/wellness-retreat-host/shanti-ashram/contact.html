Before you write

The questions people ask

Is there really no phone signal?

Not reliably. There is a signal at the gate on one network and at the top of the orchard on another, which is enough for a call if you stand still and face the right hill. The rooms have none. Wi-fi in the office runs from nine to six, and we ask that you do not take calls at the table.

Can I come alone?

Most people do. The table seats twelve and dinner is the only social hour, so a solo guest is not left standing. Single occupancy is the only kind we offer; we do not take couples in one room.

What is the weather like?

Warm days and cold nights from March to June, with the orchards flowering in April. Monsoon from late June to September: rain, cloud, and leeches on the lower path. October and November are the clearest weeks of the year. December and January are cold — below freezing at night — and the kutis have tin roofs and thick quilts.

Do I need experience with yoga or meditation?

No. Half the guests have never sat for more than five minutes. The sessions are taught so that a beginner and a long-term practitioner can be in the same room without either being bored or lost; if that stops being true, you are welcome to leave and walk instead.

Is the food vegan or gluten-free friendly?

Yes, with notice. Kamla cooks vegetarian food from the village; milk comes from the two buffaloes below the ridge. Tell us at booking and she will cook around it without making it a separate meal at a separate table.

Can I bring my children or my dog?

Children over twelve are welcome on the walking week and on ordinary stays, if they can keep the morning silence. Dogs are not, though Bhola has views on the matter and will make them known.
