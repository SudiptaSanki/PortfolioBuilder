The work

From a family kitchen to a plaza in Coyoacán

An ofrenda is not decoration. It is a structure with rules, and the rules are what make it legible to the person it is for. The tiers mean the levels of existence; the arch is the door between them; the path of petals runs from the street to the table so that nobody has to guess the way.

I build them in family homes, in museums, in school courtyards and once, memorably, in the back of a cantina in Coyoacán where the owner wanted his father's chair at the table, exactly where it used to stand. That chair is the whole idea: an ofrenda is furniture arranged for an absence.

The work runs for six weeks each autumn. The rest of the year I teach, source materials, and make paper flowers for the following season, because nine thousand stems do not cut themselves.

You do not decorate an altar. You set a table and then you wait to see whether anyone comes.

Lucía Fuentes
