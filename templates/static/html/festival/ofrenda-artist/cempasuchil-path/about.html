The practice

Built by rules,
finished by memory

The structure of an ofrenda is not invented each year. It is a grammar that families and towns already know, and the work is to set it correctly so that the person being remembered is recognisable in it.

I do not build Halloween altars, and I do not put a calavera where a photograph belongs. If a client wants skeletons and candy, I send them to a party supplier and wish them a good night.

My mother set two tiers every year on the sideboard: water, salt, a candle, and a photograph of her father. It took twenty minutes and it was the most serious twenty minutes of the year in our house.

The tiers

An ofrenda can have two tiers or seven, and the number is a sentence about existence. Two is the household: the earth and what is above it. Three adds the road between. Seven is the full reading, the levels of the underworld and the sky, and it is usually only built in public.

The reading of the tiers
TiersWhat it saysWhere I build it
TwoThe earth and the sky. The shortest complete offering.Family homes, small kitchens
ThreeEarth, road, and what is above. The most common.Courtyards, schools
FiveThe full path: the four directions and the centre.Museums, plazas
SevenThe underworld, the earth, and the nine skies, compressed.Public installations

The four elements, before anything personal

Water, salt, candle and cempasúchil go on before a single photograph is placed. Water for the thirst of the road; salt so the soul stays whole; light so the way is visible; marigold because the path has to be scented, not explained.

Only then the personal things: bread, fruit, a cigarette, a bottle of beer, a bowl of pozole, a chair. The personal objects are what make an altar a portrait rather than a display, and they are the part I cannot design alone. I ask the family for three things the person would reach for first.

The path

Petals are laid from the street to the table, in two lines or a scatter, depending on the house. In a flat with a corridor it is a line down the hall. In a courtyard it runs from the gate. The path is the part children are allowed to arrange, and they take it very seriously.

What the work actually is

Two days of building, one day of sourcing flowers, and three visits before that: one to see the room, one to meet the family, and one to bring back the objects they have chosen. The candles are lit on the evening of the first and stay lit until the second, and I come back to put them out.

I am not there for the party. I am there for the twenty minutes before, when the table is finished and the family stands in front of it and works out whether it is right. It is usually the photograph that decides.
