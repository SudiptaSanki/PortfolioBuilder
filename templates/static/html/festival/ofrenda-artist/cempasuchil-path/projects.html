The altars

Four tables,
four absences

Each of these was built around a single object the family could not leave out. The object decides the layout; everything else is arranged to give it room.

Season 2025Four altarsMexico City

The chair in Iztapalapa

Family commission · 3 tiers · 2025

The family wanted their father's chair at the table, in the place it had stood in the kitchen for forty years. That meant the altar had to be built around a chair, not above it: the three tiers sit to one side and the chair faces them.

His hat hangs on the back. His cigarettes are on the second tier, and nobody moved them for the two days.

  • Build 2 days
  • Cempasúchil 600 stems
  • Personal chair, hat, cigarettes

The plaza in Coyoacán

Public installation · 5 tiers · 2025

A five-tier altar in the corner of a plaza, built to be walked around rather than looked at from the front. The tiers turn a quarter turn at each level so the structure reads from every side, and the candles are set in sand so the wind cannot take them.

It was up for five days and lost nine candles to the wind on the second night. We rebuilt the row with glass jars, which is not traditional and is the only reason it survived the third.

  • Build 3 days
  • Height 4.2 m
  • Candles 120

The vitrine

Museum commission · 2 tiers · 2024

A museum asked for an altar inside a glass case, which is the opposite of what an altar is for. We compromised: the case stayed open at the front, the petals ran out of it onto the floor, and a small sign invited visitors to add a flower.

By the last day there were more flowers on the floor than on the tiers. The museum kept the sign.

  • Build 1 day
  • Case 2.4 m wide
  • Added by visitors 600 stems

The courtyard with four hundred children

School workshop · 3 tiers · 2025

A primary school in Tlalpan asked for an altar the children could build themselves. We prepared the crates, the cloth and the paper flowers in advance, and each class was responsible for one thing: water, salt, flowers, photographs.

The photographs were the difficulty. Some children brought grandparents, some brought a dog, one brought a photograph of a friend who had moved to Monterrey. All of them went on the altar, and that was correct.

  • Children 400
  • Photographs 96
  • Build 1 morning
