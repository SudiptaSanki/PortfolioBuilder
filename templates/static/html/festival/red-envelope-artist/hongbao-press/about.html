The craft · 壓印

Pressure makes the line.

A hongbao is a folded red rectangle with one job: it should feel like it was made for the person holding it. Everything in this shop is aimed at that first half-second when a thumb goes under the flap.

Platen: Heidelberg, 1962, rebuilt 2014

Die cutter: Sanchong, two days

Paper: 120 gsm, red dyed through

Foil: Kurz, by the metre

My grandfather's shop sold paper by weight.

He opened a stationery shop on Dihua Street in 1978 selling joss paper, red paper, and the cheap printed hongbao that banks gave away. I grew up doing homework behind the counter and hating the smell of the glue on the envelope flaps.

I studied graphic design in Tainan, worked six years in a studio doing packaging for tea companies, and came back in 2016 when he had a stroke. The shop had four pallets of paper nobody wanted. I bought a platen press from a closing printer in Wanhua for the price of moving it, and taught myself from a 1973 manual and about two hundred ruined sheets.

The first plate was a single character — 福 — printed in one pass of gold foil on red paper, 500 envelopes for a tea shop on the same street. It sold out in a week. The second was for a wedding and took eleven attempts to register correctly.

I still print every order myself. The press is loud enough that I cannot hear the phone, which is why commissions go through email and why the answers come on Mondays and Thursdays.

One plate, four stages

Where the time goes

People assume the printing is the slow part. Printing a thousand is an afternoon. Getting the die to sit flat, and the paper to feed without a crease, is the week.

  1. 01

    Proof and die

    The vector artwork is checked by hand for line weight at 0.3 pt minimum, then etched onto magnesium in Sanchong. Two working days, and a replacement die if a line fills in.

  2. 02

    Make ready

    Packing under the platen with tissue and card until the impression is even across the sheet. On a 440 mm envelope this is about forty minutes of adjusting and testing.

  3. 03

    Foil and feed

    Foil is 640 mm wide and changed every 400 impressions. Paper is fed by hand, one sheet at a time, because an automatic feeder creases red-dyed stock at this weight.

  4. 04

    Fold, glue, count

    Scored on a hand creaser, folded on a bone, glued with starch and counted into tens. The last stage is where a run of 2,000 turns into three evenings.

From paper by weight to plates

  1. 1978

    The stationery shop

    Mei-Lin's grandfather opens a paper shop on Dihua Street, three doors from the tea warehouses.

  2. 2016

    The platen arrives

    A 1962 Heidelberg is bought from a closing printer in Wanhua and winched through a second-floor window with a block and tackle.

  3. 2017

    First plate

    Five hundred gold-on-red envelopes for a tea shop; sold in a week, and the shop starts taking commissions.

  4. 2019

    The wedding year

    Seventeen wedding plates in one autumn. The double-happiness lockup is still the most requested die on the wall.

  5. 2022

    Twenty thousand

    First bank commission. Three weeks of printing, one plate, and a lesson about drying time in a humid October.

  6. 2026

    Forty-six dies

    The wall is full. New plates are cut only for commissions, and the shop closes for two weeks after Lunar New Year.
