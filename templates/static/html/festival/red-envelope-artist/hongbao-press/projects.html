Plate 23 · 2022

Twenty thousand for a bank

A commercial bank in Songshan wanted a red envelope that did not look like a bank: one character, one border, no logo on the front. The logo went on the back flap, printed in one pass of black.

The run took three weeks because of an October humidity problem — the red-dyed stock took the foil unevenly after rain, and we lost 1,800 sheets before the make-ready was right.

  • Edition20,000
  • FoilGold, 2 passes
  • Paper120 gsm, red dyed
  • Press lifts200

Plate 31 · 2023

Double happiness, with the couple's names inside

The most requested die on the wall. The couple wanted the character 囍 on the front and their two surnames inside the flap, printed in one pass so the fold would not break the line.

The first proof was too dense at 132 pt; the foil filled in the centre of the character. We opened the counters by hand in the vector file and cut a second die.

  • Edition1,600
  • FoilGold, 2 passes
  • ExtraSurnames inside flap
  • Dies cut2

Plate 11 · 2024

Spring, for a bakery that opens at six

A bakery on Dihua Street gives a hongbao with every box of pineapple cakes in the month before Lunar New Year. Three thousand, pearl foil, a plum branch that runs off the edge on purpose.

Pearl foil is the hardest to photograph and the easiest to print: it does not tarnish and it does not show a fingerprint. The branch is drawn from the tree in the courtyard behind the shop.

  • Edition3,000
  • FoilPearl, 2 passes
  • Paper120 gsm, red dyed
  • Delivery12 working days

Plate 40 · 2025

Peace, for a temple's New Year table

Longshan Temple gives an envelope with the year's blessing to visitors on the first day of the new year. Five thousand, copper foil, two characters stacked so they read at arm's length in a crowd.

Copper was chosen because gold looked too commercial next to the temple's brass. The lotus border was redrawn from a stone carving in the courtyard.

  • Edition5,000
  • FoilCopper, 2 passes
  • BorderLotus, from stone
  • Delivered28 December

Plate 44 · 2024

The copper one, from a photograph

A client brought a photograph of her grandmother's envelope from 1964: a cloud border, a roundel, one character. The original was printed in Hong Kong on thin paper with a die that had been used for years.

We redrew the border by eye, kept the flaws in the cloud line, and printed 500 in copper on heavier stock. The family gives one to each cousin at New Year, and the plate stays on the wall.

  • Edition500
  • FoilCopper, 2 passes
  • Source1964 photograph
  • DieKept, not retired

“The bank asked for twenty thousand and got twenty thousand, with the logo on the back where nobody would see it. That was her idea, and it was the right one.”

Wen-hua L. · marketing, Songshan
