Rangoli · dry pigment · Pune

Anjali KulkarniI draw on floors, before anyone walks on them.

Fourteen years of rangoli at doorways in Sadashiv Peth, wedding mandaps in Kothrud, the atrium of a hotel on Bund Garden Road, and school courtyards where eight hundred children sit around the edge and wait for the first line.

Every piece is drawn in dry powder and rice flour, on the floor, by hand, in four to nine hours. Nothing is projected, traced or printed.

Before the first line

Three things decide the drawing

A rangoli is not chosen from a book and copied. It is measured to the floor it sits on, and the floor has opinions: the door swing, the drain, the shade at the hour of the ceremony.

  1. 01

    The floor

    Dry, swept, and out of the path of feet for at least six hours. I bring a chalk line and a long tape; if the surface is wet or oiled, the powder will not hold and I will tell you so before you pay a deposit.

  2. 02

    The hour

    Rangoli is drawn at dawn or in the evening, because that is when the floor is cool and the light is low. A piece drawn at 2 pm in April will have a shadow line through it by the time the guests arrive.

  3. 03

    The number of people

    A doorway piece is drawn alone. A 12 ft atrium is drawn with two assistants and a grid of strings, and takes nine hours. Anything over 10 ft needs a second pair of hands on the outline, or the symmetry drifts.

The loom

Set a pattern, a symmetry, a size

This is the same arithmetic I do on the floor with a chalk line and a tape. Choose a pattern and the loom draws it in dry-powder strokes, then tells you the pigment it takes, colour by colour, and how long it will take me to lay it.

    The materials

    Six powders, no paint

    Everything on my floor is dry: stone dust, rice flour, turmeric, indigo, charcoal and flower petal. No acrylic, no rangoli sticker sheets, no glitter. The piece lasts two days outdoors and is swept away with a broom, which is the point.

    What the pigment is, and what it does at the edge of a doorway
    PowderSourceBehaviour on the floor
    Rice flourDry-ground raw riceThe outline. Holds a thin line, lifts under a foot, washes off with water.
    GulalDyed cornstarch, festival gradeStrongest reds. Bleeds into the next colour if the floor is damp.
    TurmericDried root, groundWarm yellow that fades to ochre in sunlight within a day.
    IndigoFermented leaf, groundDeep blue, stains pale stone. Keep it away from the threshold edge.
    CharcoalBurnt tamarind woodThe black that outlines eyes and diya flames. Lightest of all, blows in wind.
    PetalMarigold and hibiscus, driedNot powder: whole petals pressed into the outer ring, swept up first.

    Four floors

    From a threshold to an atrium

    Scale changes everything: a doorway piece is a drawing, an atrium piece is a piece of construction. The same eight-fold grammar holds at both.

    All nine floors

    Ten thresholds

    Ten doorways in one lane, drawn between 5 and 9 am on the morning of Diwali.

    Wedding mandap

    A ten-foot lotus under the seat, drawn the night before, covered with a sheet until the muhurta.

    Hotel atrium

    Fourteen feet in a marble lobby, drawn overnight with two assistants and a string grid.

    School courtyard

    Eight hundred children, one circle, and a rule that everyone draws only in their own square foot.
