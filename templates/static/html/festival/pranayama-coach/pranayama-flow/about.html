The coach · Rohan Iyer

I stopped teaching people to breathe deeply and started teaching them to breathe evenly.

Twelve years, four thousand sessions, and one stubborn finding: almost everyone who comes to me is breathing faster than they think, and almost nobody needs a bigger breath. They need a longer one.

I was a mediocre Carnatic singer with a good ear and no lungs. At nineteen, in a group class in Malleshwaram, a teacher stopped me halfway through a varnam and said, “You are breathing into your collarbones. Nobody can sing from there.” She gave me one instruction and made me do it for six weeks before she let me sing again.

That was the beginning. I studied physical education at Bangalore University, then spent two years at Kaivalyadhama in Lonavala, where pranayama is taught alongside anatomy and physiology and the examinations are as much about what you can explain as what you can do. I left with a diploma, a spirometer, and a useful suspicion of anyone who talks about prana without mentioning the diaphragm.

In 2014 I opened a small practice in Indiranagar. The first clients were singers, because singers talk to each other. Then swimmers, then a school for children with speech difficulties, then a group of software engineers who could not get to sleep. The work turned out to be the same in every case: find the rate, slow it by a fraction, and make the person able to do it without me in the room.

I measure. Resting rate, breath hold, exhale length, and a simple before-and-after on the first and sixth session. Not because numbers are the truth, but because people argue with feelings and accept a chart. When the chart is flat after six weeks, I say so, and we change the approach or stop.

I do not wear a particular colour, I do not chant unless asked, and I do not call what I teach ancient when half of my protocol comes from a 2014 paper on heart-rate variability. The old techniques are good. They are not improved by pretending they are magic.

Bay 02

Twelve years, in order

1998 – now

  • 1998

    The varnam

    A teacher stops me mid-phrase and tells me I am breathing into my collarbones. Six weeks of one exercise before I am allowed to sing again.

  • 2004

    Kaivalyadhama

    Two years in Lonavala: anatomy, physiology, classical texts, and examinations that care more about explanation than performance.

  • 2009

    First measurement

    A borrowed spirometer and a spreadsheet. Resting rate, hold time, exhale length, before and after six sessions.

  • 2014

    Indiranagar

    One room, two chairs, no mirrors. The first four clients are all singers from the same choir.

  • 2018

    The swimmers

    A coach at the Basavanagudi pool sends me three backstroke swimmers who cannot complete a 200 m set without gasping.

  • 2021

    Video sessions

    Two a week, kept on after the pandemic because a singer in Chennai and a nurse in Hubli do not need to drive to Bengaluru.

  • 2024

    The workplace programme

    Six weeks, twenty-five people, one room, no chanting. The average resting rate falls about 1.8 breaths a minute and stays there.

  • Now

    Four thousand sessions

    One-to-one, groups of six, workplaces and the occasional teacher training. The pacer on the front page is the one I use.

Bay 03

Three rules I keep

I.

Measure before you preach

Resting rate, hold, exhale length. If six weeks of practice changes nothing measurable, I say so. A practice that cannot show a result is a hobby, and hobbies are fine, but I do not sell them as therapy.

II.

Never push a hold

The moment the face changes, the hold is over. Retention trains attention; it does not train stamina. I have taught a year of practice with no hold longer than eight seconds and the results were no worse.

III.

Refer out, early

Palpitations, panic attacks, uncontrolled asthma, pregnancy with complications: I send you to your physician first. If you come back with a clearance, we work. If not, we do not.
