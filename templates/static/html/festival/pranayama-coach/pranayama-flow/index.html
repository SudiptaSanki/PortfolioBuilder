Bay 01

Breath is the one automatic thing you can take over

Definition · mechanism · limits

Pranayama is the deliberate arrangement of the breath: how long you draw it in, how long you keep it, how long you let it out, and how many times you do that before you stop counting.

It works because breathing sits at the seam between the voluntary and the automatic. You can leave it alone for a decade and it will keep going. You can also, for six minutes a day, put your hands on it — and the rest of the system notices. Slow breathing at about six breaths a minute increases the variation in the time between heartbeats, which is a sign of a nervous system that can move between effort and rest.

What it will not do is cure anything. I am not a doctor, this is not a treatment, and if you have uncontrolled asthma, a recent abdominal surgery, epilepsy or a panic disorder, you speak to your physician before you hold your breath on my instruction. I will teach you the same thing either way, more slowly.

Bay 03

What the evidence does and doesn't say

Read before booking

01 / Slow breathing

Six a minute is a real threshold

At roughly five to six breaths a minute, heart-rate variability rises and the baroreflex is exercised. Below four a minute, most people are simply holding their breath and calling it practice.

— Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014, review
02 / Holds

Long holds are not the goal

Retention is a tool for attention, not a measure of progress. If the hold makes the next inhale gasp, the ratio was wrong. I have taught people for a year without a single hold longer than eight seconds.

— practice note, 2022
03 / Anxiety

Useful, not curative

Breath work can lower arousal in the moment and give a person a sense of agency. It is not a treatment for panic disorder, and for some people attention to the breath makes anxiety worse. We find that out in session one.

— referral protocol, 2023
