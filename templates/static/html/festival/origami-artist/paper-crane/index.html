I fold paper into birds, flowers and rooms. The work is one sheet, no cuts, no glue, and a crease pattern drawn before anything is touched. I show in Kyoto and Tokyo, take commissions for public rooms, and teach on Thursday evenings.

03 The work

Three ways paper leaves the studio

Exhibition works

Single sheets folded into birds, sea creatures and folded architecture, shown in glass or on a plinth. The largest piece last year was 2.4 metres across, folded from three joined sheets because a single sheet that size cannot hold a crease without tearing.

6–14 weeks · editions of 3

Commissions and installations

Rooms filled with hanging cranes, folded walls for a shop or a hotel lobby, and one wedding hall in Osaka that took 4,000 cranes and eleven days. The paper is specified for the light in the room; a white crane in daylight disappears against a white wall.

4–12 weeks · site visit first

Workshops

Thursday evenings at the studio, six people, from the crane to the bird base and one tessellation. Beginners are welcome and usually make the best cranes. Materials included; patience not supplied.

2 hours · 6 places · ¥4,500

05 The studio

A room in Nishijin, and a bench by the window

The studio is the second floor of a machiya in Nishijin, with a long bench, a lamp that can be moved, and shelves of paper sorted by weight. I fold here five days a week and teach on Thursday. Visitors are welcome by appointment; the stairs are steep and the paper is not for touching.

My grandmother taught me the crane when I was five, on the back of a receipt. I studied design in Kyoto, worked three years in a packaging studio, and came back to folding because paper was the only material I did not have to explain.
