The work · six pieces

Pieces on the frame, and what each one asked for.

Everything here is made to order and nothing is in stock. The pieces below are the ones the studio returns to, with the stitches and the hours they take. Prices are for the embroidery and making; the cloth is quoted separately.

What the hours mean

Forty-two hours is not a marketing figure; it is eight working days for one karigar, at five to six hours of embroidery a day, with the frame set up and taken down each time. A full jaali border of three hundred hours is two months of work, and there are only two people in the studio who can do it without a mistake.

When a piece is delayed, it is almost always because a karigar is ill or a cloth has come back from the printer with the repeat off by two millimetres. We do not substitute a faster stitch. We tell you, and we move the date.

A note on colour

Everything on this page is white.

Chikankari is at its best in white thread on white or undyed cloth, and that is what the studio makes unless asked otherwise. We will work in indigo, madder or a single colour to match a wedding palette, and we will tell you honestly if the stitch will not read in it. Pastel thread on pastel cloth is usually a mistake; the shadow disappears and the embroidery looks like a stain.
