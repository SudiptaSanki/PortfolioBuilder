Chikankari · Lucknow, since 2011

White thread on white cotton, and the shadow does the drawing.

Chikankari is not one stitch. It is six, worked in a particular order: the design block-printed in washable dye, the shadow outlined from the back, the knots counted, the holes cut and bound. A good kurta takes forty hours. A dupatta with a full jaali border takes three hundred.

The bench · thread and hours

What a piece costs before it is cut.

Chikankari is priced by the stitch, not by the metre. Set a motif, a cloth and a density and the bench will count the stitches, the thread and the hours, then put a price on it at the workshop's own rate. The numbers are from our time books: 430 stitches an hour for a karigar working carefully, 1.6 cm of thread per stitch including the turn, and 18% waste at the back.

From the studio

Three pieces on the frame

Each is made to order and takes between six and fourteen weeks. The designs are drawn on paper first, then cut as wooden blocks, then printed in a pale wash that disappears in the first bath.

The Chowk Kurta

A straight kurta with a buti scattered across the front and a bakhiya vine at the hem. White on white, with a single indigo thread in the placket so the eye has somewhere to rest.

The workshop

Fourteen karigars, one table, and the accounts done at night.

I design, cut the blocks and sell; the embroidery is done by fourteen women who work from home or at the long table in the front room. They are paid by the piece, at a rate agreed before the work starts, and their names go on the label beside mine. Chikankari has a GI tag and a long history of middlemen; the only way I know to be honest about it is to keep the payroll small and the names visible.
