Visits
Bench visits by appointment, one object at a time. Please do not bring a textile unannounced — there is no safe surface to put it on.
Waiting list
About twelve weeks for treatment; condition reports within ten working days.
What to photograph Daylight, no flash, on a plain sheet. One full view of the object, one close view of the worst area, one of the reverse, and one of any label, stamp or stitched mark. Include a ruler or a coin for scale.

Describe the object

A few sentences is enough to start. This form does not upload files; photographs come by email afterwards.

Packing, if you must post Roll, do not fold. Interleave with acid-free tissue or, failing that, clean white cotton. Pad the roll at both ends, wrap in polythene only on the outside, and box so the object cannot move. Declare the value for insurance; undisclosed parcels are not covered.
Before you write

Questions that come up every week

Short answers, so that the first email can be about the object rather than about the process.

Can you make it look new again?

No, and you would not want it. A textile that looks new has usually lost its size, its patina and its evidence. What I can do is make it stable, legible and safe to display — which is a different and more useful result.

Will treatment reduce its value?

A documented, reversible treatment usually supports value. An undocumented one, or an aggressive clean, can remove it. The report is the document that travels with the object.

Can you value it for insurance?

No. I record condition, not market value. For a valuation you need an appraiser who works in the trade, and I can suggest two.

How much does a first opinion cost?

Nothing, if it is based on photographs and a short description. The written condition report, which involves the object on the bench, is ₹9,000 and takes ten working days.

Do you work on costume?

Yes — bodices, ghagras, children's wear and men's achkans, but not leather or fur. Shoes and bags go to a leather conservator.

Do you travel?

Within Gujarat for collection surveys, and to other states for exhibitions and storage projects. Travel and accommodation are quoted separately and never bundled into the treatment fee.

If the object is wet, mouldy or shedding, telephone

Do not wash it, do not dry it in sun, and do not put it in a plastic bag. Call the lab on a weekday morning and describe what you see.
