Fourteen ragas I would be willing to be judged by.

Arranged by the prahar they belong to, which is how I was taught to think about them. Aroha and avaroha are written as I sing them, not as the books print them; the difference is small and it is the whole point.

Repertoire by prahar

First prahar

4 am – 7 am · before sunrise

  • Lalitललित

    Āroha: S r G M↑ M P d N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N d P M M↑ G r S

    The earliest raga I sing. Both Madhyams, neither of them resolving, which is why it sounds like a room with two doors open. I keep it for baithaks that begin before the street wakes up.

    Komal Re and Dha · sung at 5:40 in winter

  • Bhairavभैरव

    Āroha: S r G M P d N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N d P M G r S

    Slow, austere, and the raga people ask for when they want the morning to feel serious. The komal Re has to be leaned on just enough; too little and it is a scale, too much and the raga becomes a complaint.

    Bhairav thaat · bandish in Vilambit Ektaal

Second prahar

7 am – 10 am · morning

  • Todiतोड़ी

    Āroha: S r g M↑ P d N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N d P M↑ g r S

    The hardest of the morning ragas to sing without sounding like an exercise. Three komal swaras and a tivra Ma in the ascent; if the tuning slips by ten cents the whole thing curdles. I sing it in Miyan ki Todi, which is a broader raga than people admit.

    Todi thaat · the test piece among my students

  • Jaunpuriजौनपुरी

    Āroha: S R M P d N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N d P M g R S

    Often confused with Asavari, and I have given up correcting people at parties. Jaunpuri leaves Ga out of the ascent and brings it back in the descent, and that single omission is what makes it feel like late morning rather than early.

    Asavari thaat · a fifteen-minute alap, then a bandish

Third prahar

10 am – 1 pm · midday

  • Shuddha Sarangशुद्ध सारंग

    Āroha: S R M↑ P N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N P M↑ R S

    The one midday raga I trust. No Ga, no Dha, and a tivra Ma that keeps the whole thing from settling — which is exactly what noon does to a room. Best with a sarangi, and I have been lucky.

    Kafi thaat · for outdoor afternoons only

  • Multaniमुल्तानी

    Āroha: S g M↑ P N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N P M↑ g S

    Strictly a late-afternoon raga, but I list it here because singers argue about the boundary and I have sung it at one o'clock and regretted it. Six notes; the Pa is a hinge, not a resting place.

    Todi thaat · no Re, no Dha

Fourth prahar

1 pm – 4 pm · afternoon

  • Bhimpalasiभीमपलासी

    Āroha: S g M P N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N d P M g R S

    A raga people think is gentle, and it is, but only if the avaroha is taken slowly through the komal Dha. I built my whole second recording around it and still hear things in it that I have not solved.

    Kafi thaat · my most-requested raga

  • Puriya Dhanashriपूरिया धनाश्री

    Āroha: S r G M↑ P d N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N d P M↑ G r S

    Sits exactly on the edge of evening and I list it in both places depending on who is asking. Tivra Ma and komal Dha with a shuddha Ga: the light is going but the lamps are not lit.

    Puriya thaat · sunset, when it is done properly

Fifth prahar

4 pm – 7 pm · evening

  • Marwaमारवा

    Āroha: S r G M↑ D N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N D M↑ G r S

    No Pa at all. Four years of work and two public performances, both of which I would like back. Marwa is the reason I still practise: it is a raga that tells you the truth about your tuning, and mine is not always good.

    Marwa thaat · the raga I am currently losing to

  • Yamanयमन

    Āroha: S R G M↑ P D N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N D P M↑ G R S

    Where everyone begins, including me. The tivra Ma is not decoration; it is the raga's whole argument. I still sing it at least once a month, because a singer who has stopped singing Yaman has stopped checking the foundations.

    Kalyan thaat · the first raga I was allowed to sing in public

Sixth prahar

7 pm – 10 pm · night

  • Darbari Kanadaदरबारी कान्हड़ा

    Āroha: S R g M P d n Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ n P d M P g M R S

    The raga with the most weight in it, and the one that punishes an ambitious tempo. The oscillation on the komal Ga has to be slow enough to be a thought rather than a decoration. I sing it last in a concert, if I sing it at all.

    Asavari thaat · for rooms with good acoustics

  • Khamajखमाज

    Āroha: S G M P D N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ n D P M G R S

    Light, generous, and the door I use to get from khayal into thumri without the audience noticing the change of room. Komal Ni in the descent only.

    Khamaj thaat · the hinge of an evening

Seventh & eighth prahar

10 pm – 4 am · late night

  • Bageshriबागेश्री

    Āroha: S g M P d N Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ N d M P D n Ṡ — as I sing it, with both Dhas

    A late-night raga that is really about waiting. I sing the vilambit very slowly and let the antara do the work. Audiences either lean in or fall asleep, and I have made peace with both.

    Kafi thaat · after eleven, or not at all

  • Malkaunsमलकौंस

    Āroha: S g M d n Ṡ  ·  Avaroha: Ṡ n d M g S

    Five notes, no Re and no Pa, and nothing to hold on to. The oldest sounding thing I know. It is also the raga my students ask for first, because it is easy to learn and takes a lifetime to make frightening.

    Bhairavi thaat · midnight

Recordings, made at home

Three of the eleven I have finished, in the order they were made. Two microphones, one room, no editing beyond setting the levels. The breathing stays in because removing it would be a lie about how the voice works.

Also in the book

Khayal bandishes

Thirty-two, of which eleven are from Anjali tai and not in wide circulation. Compositions in Ektaal, Teentaal, Jhaptaal and Ada Chautaal. I will send the notation of any of them to a student who asks, on paper, by post.

Thumri and dadra

Nine thumris in Khamaj, Bhairavi, Desh, Tilang and Pilu; six dadras. Poorab ang for the thumris, which means the words sit forward and the ornament goes around them rather than through them.

Bhajans

A small set of Marathi abhangs and Hindi bhajans, sung at the end of a baithak when the audience is still there and the evening has gone soft. Not on the records, and not for festivals.
