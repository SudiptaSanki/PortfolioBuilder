Before you write

What does the space need?

A covered area of about three by three metres for the harmonium and two singers, seating for the room within earshot, and one power point for a lamp or a single microphone. A courtyard with a wall behind the singer is ideal; a hall with a low ceiling works too. No stage is necessary.

How long does a kirtan run?

Ninety minutes for a public night, sixty for a private satsang, forty for a school. I arrive ninety minutes early to set up, teach the refrain and let the room settle. Tea afterwards is part of the practice, not an extra.

What does it cost?

Private satsangs and festivals pay a flat fee that covers travel, harmonium hire and the teaching day I lose. Schools in the district pay half. Public courtyard kirtans are free, with tea by contribution. I will put the number in writing before you commit to anything.

Do you travel?

Yes, for a cluster of two or three dates, or for a festival. I do not fly for a single sixty-minute satsang; it is bad for the voice and worse for the carbon. Train travel within a night's journey is easy.

Can the kirtan be recorded?

For a family archive, yes, with the room told at the start. For publication or broadcast, we agree separately and the fee changes. I would rather a room sing without a camera, and most hosts agree once the night begins.

What I bring

A harmonium, two pairs of kartal, a small oil lamp, and refrains written out for anyone who asks. For rooms over a hundred and fifty, one microphone for the harmonium only; the voices stay unamplified.

You provide the floor, the people and the tea. A chair for anyone who cannot sit on the floor is a kindness, not a rider.

The hour chooses the raga

Dusk is Yaman, early evening Durga, night Kafi, midnight Malkauns, morning Bhairavi. If your kirtan is at four in the afternoon, we will find something that fits; if it is at midnight, we will not be in a hurry.

A note on the lamp

The oil lamp is lit before the first note and stays lit until the last. It is not decoration. It gives the room a single point to rest their eyes on when the singing asks them to be still.
