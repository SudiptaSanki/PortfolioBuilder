Ofrenda season: booking to 20 October

Banners · 2019 to 2026

Six runs, and what the cut had to solve.

A banner is a piece of engineering as much as a drawing: the notch has to hang straight, the border has to survive the wind, and the letters have to be readable from the far end of a street. These are the commissions that taught me something.

Ofrenda, Coyoacán

2025 · Día de Muertos · nine metres

A nine-metre run for a private altar under a jacaranda, with the family's four names cut into each border so that no two pennants are identical. The paper had to be heavier than usual because the arch faced the street and caught the wind; we used a 60-gram tissue instead of the standard 40 and doubled the border cuts.

54 pennants · rosa and naranja · 60 g tissue · 3 weeks

Cempasúchil for a market

2025 · Puebla · six metres

The flower had to read at twenty metres, so the petals are cut as one continuous hole rather than eight separate ones. The stallholders wanted the banner to survive a week outdoors, so we cut it in 80-gram paper and strung it with wire through the top seam.

20 pennants · naranja · 80 g paper · 2 weeks

Palomas for a wedding patio

2024 · Puebla capital · eleven metres

The couple asked for doves, which is not a traditional motif and is harder than it looks: a dove cut too open tears at the wing, too closed reads as a blob. The solution was a single wing line and a separate eye, repeated at three sizes along the run.

36 pennants · verde and blanco · 50 g tissue · 4 weeks

Milagros for a gallery

2024 · CDMX · fourteen metres

A gallery in Roma asked for a banner for an exhibition about votive objects, to hang indoors across a long room. Indoors there is no wind, so we could go thinner: 40-gram paper, a wider run, and the milagro cut at three different sizes in the same sheet. It ran for eleven weeks and came down with every pennant intact.

48 pennants · morado · 40 g tissue · 3 weeks

Nopal for a pulquería

2023 · Tehuacán · four metres

A small commission that taught me the value of a single motif: the owner wanted a nopal, the cactus that gives the pulque, and nothing else. Twelve pennants, one design, two hours on the table. It is still hanging, faded to a good colour.

12 pennants · amarillo · 50 g tissue · 3 days

Stars for a film

2022 · location shoot, Puebla · five metres

A production designer wanted a banner that looked like it had been hanging since the seventies. We cut new paper, then faded it in the sun for four days and tore two corners on purpose. The art department aged it further with tea. It appears for eleven seconds in the film.

16 pennants · rosa · sun-faded · 1 week
