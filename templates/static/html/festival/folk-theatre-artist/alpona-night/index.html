How a night is made

01

The invitation

A village committee agrees on a pala, a date and a budget. Most bookings arrive by phone in the week after Durga Puja, when the harvest money is counted and the winter calendar is fixed.

Oct – Nov
02

The rehearsal

Nine days of music and blocking in a borrowed schoolroom. The musicians set the ragas first; the actors find their pauses inside them. Nothing is fully fixed until the audience is in front of it.

9 nights
03

The stage

Bamboo posts, planks, a painted backdrop of a palace corridor, and four petromax lamps. The backdrop is repainted for each pala by the same family of painters in Ranaghat.

6 hours
04

The night

The overture begins at ten. A jatra runs four hours, sometimes five when the audience calls for a repeat of a song. The last act ends at first light, and the troupe loads the truck before tea.

22:00 – 03:00

The nine rasas

Every jatra scene is built to land on one of the nine rasas, the emotional states codified in the Natyashastra. Choose one to see where it sits in my repertoire and how it is played.

Rasa 01 of 09

Shringara

Love, and the ache of separation

Played as
The slow entrance, a dupatta lifted once and held. The song is sung close to the microphone so the breath is audible.
In the repertoire
Radha–Krishna pala, act two; Behula's wedding night
The cue
A single note on the harmonium, held until the audience settles.
