A ketubah is not a form with a border. It is the first object a couple owns together, and the letters on it are written one at a time, by hand, with a quill cut from a reed and ink that will still be black in a hundred years. I have been doing this in Tel Aviv since 2014.

§01

Twenty-two letters, and no capitals

Hebrew has no upper case and no vowels in the scroll. Everything a reader needs — rhythm, emphasis, breath — sits in the shape of the consonants and in the white around them. That is why a handwritten page reads differently from a set one: the scribe is deciding, letter by letter, where the air goes.

Five letters change shape at the end of a word — kaf, mem, nun, pe and tsadi drop a descender or close a corner. Seven letters wear small crowns called tagin on their heads, remembered by the mnemonic שעטנז גץ. A single letter written out of order can make a scroll unfit for use, which is why the work is slow and why I charge by the letter and not by the hour.

The quill

A turkey feather for Latin, a reed for Hebrew. Cut fresh each morning, split with a blade, tested on the edge of the parchment until the line is even.

The ink

Oak gall, iron sulphate, gum arabic and lamp-black. Mixed in small batches; it darkens for a week and then holds for centuries.

The skin

Klaf — calf parchment, scraped and chalked. It drinks ink differently on each side, and it will tell you within three letters whether it likes you.

§02

Write one yourself, badly, in ink

Choose a letter from the alef-bet. It will be inked from right to left, the way it is written, on ruled parchment. The number is its gematria; the note is what the shape has meant to the people who write it.

Silent. Two arms and a diagonal — the first letter, and the only one that makes no sound.

From the bench

Every letter on this page is set in Frank Ruhl Libre, drawn in Jerusalem in 1908 for a newspaper and still the shape most Israelis read. A hand-written letter will differ: the quill is wider at the top, the corners are cut, and no two are the same.

Gematria is the numerical value of a letter — alef 1, yod 10, qof 100, tav 400. Final forms count 500–900.

§03

What leaves the studio

01 · ketubah

The marriage contract

Hand-written on klaf with a painted border, in Aramaic with the couple’s names and the Hebrew date. I ask for the text two months ahead, and I will not begin until both families have read it.

02 · haggadah

Passover, in ink

Forty pages of hand-lettering, illuminated margins, and a binding by a bookbinder on Herzl Street. One or two a year; each takes about four months and is booked a year out.

03 · lettering

Logos, signs, invitations

A word drawn by hand and delivered as a vector: a wine label, a bakery sign in Neve Tzedek, an invitation suite for a wedding in the Galilee. The letters stay letters, even at 12 mm.

Etc.

Mezuzah cases and the scroll inside them; a memorial plaque for a library in Haifa; one very long quotation from the Song of Songs for a stairwell in Berlin.

«היא כתבה את השם שלי ואז הסתכלה עליי ואמרה: עכשיו תגידי אותו בקול»

She wrote my name, then looked up and said: now say it out loud.

Yael M. · ketubah, 2023
