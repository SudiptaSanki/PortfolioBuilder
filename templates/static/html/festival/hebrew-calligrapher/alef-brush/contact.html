A note on dates

החתונה ב־י״ד בסיון

Hebrew dates are written with letters, not digits: י״ד בסיון is the 14th of Sivan. If you only know the civil date, send that and I will convert it and check it with you.

If it is urgent

A wedding inside four weeks is not a calligraphy problem, it is a printing problem. I will say so honestly and point you to a studio in Jaffa that can letterpress a beautiful invitation in ten days.

§01

What to send, if you are unsure

For a ketubah: both names in Hebrew and in the spelling you use, the Hebrew date of the wedding, the city, and whether either family has a script it expects. A photograph of a grandparent’s document is genuinely useful — I have matched a hand from a 1930s Baghdad certificate twice.

For lettering: the word, where it will live, the size it will be seen at, and one image of something you like and one of something you hate. The second is more informative.

For a haggadah: which edition of the text your family reads, and whether you want the traditional illustrations or none. It is a four-month conversation, so there is no hurry on the first email.

What I need before writing

The final text, the final names, the final date. Everything after that is mine to get wrong, and I would rather it were only mine.
