How a commission runs

1. The text. You send the words, the names, the Hebrew date and the spelling you use. I check the text, query anything that looks like a mistake, and send it back to you and to your rabbi if you have one.

2. The layout. Two weeks later you receive a pencil layout: the size, the line breaks, the border in outline. Nothing is inked yet. Changes are free at this stage and expensive afterwards, which is why I insist on it.

3. The writing. Four to eight weeks depending on the piece. You get one photograph halfway through, and no more, because a half-written page looks like a mistake and always has.

4. The handover. In the studio, or by insured courier. The piece comes with a note of the materials, the date and the script, and a small offcut of the same klaf.

Payment

One third on approval of the layout, the rest on handover. Bank transfer or cash; no card machine, and no deposit before you have seen the layout.

Cancellation

Before the layout: nothing owed. After: the deposit, because the page has been ruled and the skin cut. After writing begins, the full price — a half-written ketubah cannot be sold to anyone else.

Shipping

Israel by hand or courier. Abroad by insured courier, ₪180–420 depending on size, with a tracking number and a customs declaration that says “hand-written artwork”.

Waiting list

Ketubot for spring 2026 are closed. Summer and autumn 2026 are open, and a haggadah commissioned now would be ready for the following Passover, not this one.
