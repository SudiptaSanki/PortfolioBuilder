The dawn concert — Bhairav at its own hour

Case 01 · Solo concert

The brief

A Pune concert series wanted a winter-morning baithak that began before sunrise — a hard sell to an audience used to evening concerts. The ask: make 6:30 am feel like the point, not the problem.

The decisions

Bhairav was the only honest choice — it is a dawn raga, and hearing it at any other hour is hearing it second-hand. The hall lights were set to rise almost imperceptibly across the alap, arriving at full light with the jor. No amplification for the first twenty minutes: a baithak of two hundred can hold a whisper if the room is asked to. The E bass bansuri carried the alap; a shorter flute took the faster gat.

The outcome

Sold out at 200 seats. The series rebooked the format for the following winter and added a second dawn date. The series director’s account of the morning is quoted on the Concerts page.

The film cue — translating a brief into a raga

Case 02 · Film session

The brief

A Marathi feature needed a flute cue for a homecoming scene at first light: the director’s brief, in full, was “relief, but he is not sure he is forgiven.” One 3-hour studio call, cue to picture, no score supplied — the raga choice was the job.

The decisions

Bhairavi was proposed and accepted: its all-komal scale carries exactly that mixture of tenderness and uncertainty, and its custom as a concluding raga suited a homecoming. The cue was recorded in three passes — a plain statement of the theme, a version with meend-heavy phrasing, and a sparse take with long silences — so the editor could grade the emotion in the mix. Played on the E bass bansuri for warmth at low volume.

The outcome

The sparse take was used, nearly whole. The director has since booked two further sessions. Stated honestly: the film is an independent production with a festival run planned; no box-office claims are made for it here.

A year of lessons — the honest outcome

Case 03 · Teaching

The brief

An adult beginner, aged 41, with no prior instrument, wanted to play “something real” within a year — and asked at the first lesson what that could honestly mean.

The decisions

The year was structured in the open: months 1–3 on breath, embouchure and the seven notes in both octaves; months 4–8 on alankars and a first raga, Bhupali, chosen because its five notes forgive a beginner’s fingers; months 9–12 on a full bandish in Yaman with simple improvisation. Practice was set at 30 minutes a day, tracked in a shared notebook — not the two hours a full-time student would give.

The outcome

At twelve months she played a ten-minute Yaman bandish at the students’ baithak, in tune and in tala, with two audible recoveries from mistakes — which the notebook counts as the real achievement. She cannot yet play a concert alap, and was never promised one. She has renewed for a second year.
