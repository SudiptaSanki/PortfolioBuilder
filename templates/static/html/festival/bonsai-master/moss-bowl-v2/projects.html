Work that left the bench

Four pieces of work, told the way they happened: what was asked, what I did, and what came of it. The prices are the prices that were paid, by the same formula printed on the home page.

Six bowls for a tea room in Utrecht

The askA tea room with low wooden tables and north light wanted six living bowls — nothing tall, nothing that would drop leaves into a guest's cup, and all six alive in five years' time, not five months.

What I didSerissa was the only honest answer: low, flowering, and content indoors. I grew six trees for three years on the bench — by the formula, €55 + 3 × €35 + the 18 cm bowl at €40 = €200 a bowl, €1,200 the set — and delivered them in May with the moss already knit, plus a one-page watering card written for the staff, not for a bonsai book.

The outcomeFive years on, all six are on the same tables. Two have been back to Boskoop once, for their three-year repot, which is the visit the calendar promises. The tea room orders its replacement moss from the bench each spring.

“He refused to sell us the maples we asked for — said the room would kill them by Christmas. He was right about the room and right about the serissa. Guests ask about the bowls more than the tea.”

— The tea room's owner, Utrecht — on the six serissa bowls

The law office that had killed four bonsai

The askAn Amsterdam law office had bought four gift-shop bonsai in two years and buried all four. They wanted one bowl for the meeting room that would still be alive at the next partners' dinner — and someone to blame, in writing, if it died.

What I didI gave them the blame in writing and the right species. Dwarf jade — Portulacaria afra — in a gravel-finished bowl with no moss to fail, on a watering rota of once a fortnight, weighed on the office kitchen scale: water when the bowl reads under its card weight. One tree, seven years grown, by the formula on the home page: €45 + 7 × €30 + the 24 cm bowl at €65 = €320, with a guarantee that if it died in the first year under the rota, I would replace it and say why in the letter.

The outcomeThree years, no letter needed. The office now keeps the bowl's weight card in the meeting-room drawer, and the tree has been joined by a second, bought outright from the bench.

“The weight card did it. Caring for the tree stopped being someone's opinion and became a number on a scale. It has outlived two office managers.”

— Jeroen Vos, office manager — on the dwarf jade bowl

The spring repotting weekends

The askEvery February the same letters arrived: my tree is bursting its pot, what do I do? Owners of tired, root-bound trees needed the repotting weeks opened to them, not just to my own stock.

What I didFour Saturday workshops across the repotting window, eight people at the bench at a time, €85 a head. Each person repots their own tree under my hands — root-pruning, fresh akadama, a drilled bowl if theirs has no hole — and beginners take home a young cotoneaster of their own, potted that day, moss and all.

The outcomeLast spring: 32 people, 41 trees repotted, and — the figure I check — 38 of those trees leafed out on schedule in April. The three that didn't came back to the bench free of charge, and two of the three recovered.

“I arrived with a tree I was ashamed of and left with it repotted, a bowl with a hole in it for the first time in its life, and instructions I actually understood. It flowered in May.”

— Els de Jong, workshop participant — on the repotting weekend

An elm for the Genk exhibition

The askThe autumn exhibition in Genk is where Dutch and Belgian growers show their best, and I had not shown in six years. The ask was my own: one bowl worth the table.

What I didI chose the Chinese elm I had been growing for nine years — not the showiest tree on the bench, but the one whose bark had begun to flake into plates, which is the thing elm people cross a room for. It got a full season of preparation: moss renewed twice, no wire after June, and a crackle-glaze bowl chosen because the glaze echoes the bark.

The outcomeThe bowl was shown in Genk in October and bought there by a collector — the €890 elm now listed, marked sold, on the home page. Exhibitions are how a bench like mine is judged; that one paid for the winter glasshouse repairs.
