I am Satoru Mori. For twenty-two years I have been cutting, wiring and waiting on trees in a former kimono store on the Nomachi back street. The work is mostly patience: three cuts a year, one repot in spring, and a bowl of moss that has to stay damp through August.

02 — The year

The calendar is the teacher

Nothing in this workshop happens because I feel like it. Wiring goes on while the sap is slow, repotting while the buds are still hard, and the big cut waits until the tree can answer it.

FEB
MAR

Repot

The root ball comes out of the bowl before the buds break. Akadama, pumice and a handful of charcoal; the old soil goes into the courtyard sieve and comes back next year.

Black pine · late February  ·  Maples · early March

APR
SEP

Feed

Rapeseed cake in a paper pouch, changed every three weeks. Nothing after mid-September, so the new growth hardens before the first frost in the Nomachi lanes.

Weak liquid feed every ten days through the rainy season

JUN
JUL

Wire

Copper on pine, aluminium on anything with soft bark. The wire stays six weeks, no longer; by July the bark will scar and the tree will keep the memory of it.

Check the wire every Sunday through June

OCT
NOV

Prune

The year's decision. Needle thinning on pine, shoot cutting on maple, and the deadwood cleaned with a brass brush. Whatever is cut now is the silhouette for next spring.

Big branch removal · November, when the sap is down

04 — Journal

Notes from the bench

Written in the morning, before the shutters open, in a notebook that also holds the feeding dates.

14 November 2026

The last cut of the year

The kuromatsu on the second bench had been hiding a weak lower branch since spring. Today it came off, in one cut, with the saw cleaned first. The tree is lighter by a third and looks, finally, like the drawing I made in 2019.

2 November 2026

Frost on the courtyard stones

The first frost came early. Beech and stewartia go into the cold frame tonight; the pines stay out. A bonsai is not a houseplant, and a winter indoors will kill it faster than any cold snap in Ishikawa.

Winter care visits →

19 October 2026

One bench, four beginners

Saturday's workshop: a nurse, two students and a retired train driver. All four left with a nursery juniper and instructions to do nothing until March. The hardest part of the first year is the doing-nothing.
