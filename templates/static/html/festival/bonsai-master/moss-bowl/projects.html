02 — Commissions

Three trees that came in as something else

A commission starts with a conversation and a photograph. The interesting ones usually start with a tree the owner has stopped believing in.

2019 – 2024 · Kanazawa

The garden pine

A 40-year-old black pine lifted from a private garden in Utatsuyama before a house was built. Root ball 90 cm, no fine roots to speak of. Three years in a wooden box before it saw a pottery pot.

Result · 71 cm, four pads · now in a garden in Fukui

2021 – present · Toyama

The inherited juniper

Brought in by a daughter clearing her father's greenhouse. Watered daily for nine years, in standing water, in a pot with no drainage hole. The first job was drilling the pot, not styling the tree.

Result · alive, boarding · seventh season

2023 – 2026 · Komatsu

The nursery reject maple

Bought for ¥3,000 at a closing-down sale: a trident maple with a 4 cm trunk and one bad branch. Cut to a stump in the first winter, which is the only dramatic thing that happened in three years.

Result · 38 cm broom, sold 2026 · ¥72,000

How a commission runs

Six steps, and one long wait

1 · Look. Send photographs in leaf and dormant, and tell me where the tree has lived. A visit follows, and I charge ¥8,000 for the assessment, which comes off the work if you proceed.

2 · Decide the roots. A repot, new soil, and often a smaller pot than you expect. This is the year you cannot see progress.

3 · Set the line. One drawing, one conversation, and a decision about the trunk. Nothing is cut yet.

4 · Work the branches. Two to four seasons of wiring, cutting back, and waiting for back buds.

5 · Choose the pot. Last, always last.

6 · Board or collect. Boarding is ¥9,000 a month including soil and summer water. Most commissioned trees stay two to five years.

If a tree on this page is still here in spring, ask about it.

Prices do not include the pot unless it says so, and they do not include delivery outside Ishikawa. A tree leaves the bench only after I have seen it through one full year here.
