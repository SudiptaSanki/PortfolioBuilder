02 — Finding us

A back street, and no signboard

The building was a kimono store for sixty years. The shopfront is 5.4 metres wide and the ceiling is 2.2 metres, which is why the taller trees live in the courtyard.

On foot from the station

Leave Nomachi station by the south exit and turn left onto Nomachi-dōri. Walk two blocks — past the tofu shop, which is the only shop on the street with a queue before nine — and take the second alley on the right. The third door is the atelier. There is a pine by it and no sign.

By car

There is no parking at the atelier. Use the coin lot behind the tofu shop (eight spaces, ¥200 an hour) and walk around. On weekends it fills by eleven, which is another reason to come early.

By bus

Kanazawa's Nomachi loop bus stops at the corner of Nomachi-dōri, four minutes from the door. Buses run every twenty minutes until 19:00 and not at all on the first of January.

Accessibility

The shopfront has a 9 cm step. The courtyard is gravel and not suitable for wheels. If you use a wheelchair, write before coming and we will set up on the raised floor inside, where the light is better anyway.

03 — Questions

Asked often enough to write down

I have never grown anything. Is a workshop too advanced?

No. The first workshop is designed for people who have killed a supermarket basil. You will pot a nursery juniper, learn to water it, and take it home with instructions to do almost nothing until March. About a third of the people who come have no plants at all.

Can I bring my own tree?

Yes, for an assessment (¥8,000). Bring it in a box, not loose, and expect the first answer to be about roots rather than branches. If the tree is in poor health, the assessment may be the only thing I can honestly sell you.

Do you ship trees?

Within Japan, for trees under 50 cm, by a specialist courier, at cost. I do not ship internationally; the phytosanitary paperwork for live material is not something I can do well from a shopfront with no fax machine.

What happens to a boarding tree in August?

It is watered twice a day, shaded from eleven to three, and fed weakly. Boarding includes summer water, winter protection and one repot a year. It does not include styling, which is quoted separately and often not needed.

Do you buy trees?

Occasionally, and only after seeing them through a season. I am not a dealer; a tree bought in spring and sold in autumn has not been improved, only moved.

Friday morning is the quietest time to visit.

The watering is done by nine, the kettle is on by ten, and the courtyard is still in shade. If you come on a Saturday, expect to share the bench.
