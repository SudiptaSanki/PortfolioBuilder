Four plots, one press, and what each acre gave.

The blocks are not equal and we do not pretend they are. Plot B is the good ground; Plot D is shallow and needs the rain early. The press handles what the four of them produce between December and February.

The four plots

A

Plot A — the September sowing

34 acres · KBSH-44 · oil

Our oldest ground, twelve years in rotation, and the block that makes the money. Sown on 12 September, up in six days, flowering in the third week of November. It is the first to be harvested and the first to be visited by parakeets, which is why somebody walks it at dawn from the first yellow petal.

Last year: 486 kg an acre · 41.6% oil · harvested 19 December · 78 litres of oil per acre after pressing.

B

Plot B — seed, and the hives

28 acres · DRSH-1 · seed

The best soil on the farm and the smallest block, which is deliberate: this is the seed crop, and seed wants attention. DRSH-1 is the variety most of Karnataka sows, so we keep it clean and sell it to farmers who would otherwise buy from a shop with a photocopied label.

Last year: 512 kg an acre · germination tested at 92% · two hives at the western edge for the flowering fortnight.

C

Plot C — the one the rain decides

42 acres · KBSH-44 · oil

Late sown on 9 October, which means flowering falls in the third week of December, which means it misses the worst of the parakeets and catches whatever the northeast monsoon has left. In a good year this block out-yields A. In 2019 it gave us 190 kg an acre and a great deal of silence at dinner.

Last year: 421 kg an acre · flowering 16–26 December · the block that fills the press in January.

D

Plot D — for visitors, and for seed

36 acres · Morden · seed & visitors

Shallow red soil over rock, sown late with a short variety so that it flowers in the week between Christmas and the new year, when people are on the road. Morden is open-pollinated, which means we can keep our own seed, and we do: the back two acres are never cut for visitors.

Last year: 368 kg an acre · open to the public 26 Dec – 8 Jan · cut paths mown in the first week of December.

The press, step by step

  1. CleanSeed is winnowed and passed over a screen. A single stone costs a screw and a day.
  2. WarmThe barrel is run for ten minutes empty so the first pressing is not cold. We do not roast; this is a cold press and the oil is meant to taste of seed.
  3. Press40 kg an hour. The oil runs through a cloth into a steel drum; the cake comes out at the other end in a rope.
  4. SettleThree weeks in the drum, then decanted. We do not filter finer than cloth, and we do not add anything.
  5. BottleGlass, 500 ml and 1 litre, filled on Thursdays. The date on the label is the pressing date, not the bottling date.

What we do not do

No refining, no bleaching, no hexane, no blending with rice bran. Which is why the oil is dark, smells of sunflower, and goes thick in the cold — put the bottle in warm water for ten minutes and it clears.

Yield: 40 kg of seed gives about 15 litres of oil and 22 kg of cake. The cake goes to cattle and to a soap maker in Bengaluru; the rest of the loss is moisture and sediment.
