Field notes

Four that keep coming back

Every region tells them differently. These are the versions I was raised on, in a house with a courtyard and a well that was always covered.

01 · Water

Petni

The spirit of a woman who died before marriage. She keeps to ponds and the edges of cremation grounds, and she is recognised by one detail: her feet face backwards. In some tellings she asks for a comb; in others she asks you to look at the water.

02 · Voice

Nishi

A voice that calls your name twice from the dark. You may answer the first time; you must not answer the second. It speaks in the voice of whoever you would most like to hear, which is the whole of its method.

03 · Lane

Skondhokata

The headless one, who walks a narrow lane with his head under his arm. He does not chase. He simply stands in the lane until you turn back, and the turning back is the point of the story.

04 · Tree

Mamdo bhoot

A tall, thin figure seen at dusk among the trees, generally at the edge of an orchard. It is described as gaunt and slow, and it is mostly a story about the moment before you are sure you saw anything at all.

The commission

From brief to board

  1. 01

    The read

    You send the manuscript pages, not a synopsis. I read the whole thing once for the story and once with a pencil, marking where the image should land and where it should not.

  2. 02

    Thumbnails

    Nine to twenty small pencil thumbnails per chapter, sent as a single sheet. We choose together. This is the cheapest place to change your mind, and the last.

  3. 03

    Ink

    Finished plates at 1.5× reproduction size, brush and nib, on 300gsm hot-pressed paper. Scanned at 600 dpi, cleaned, and delivered as layered TIFFs with the line art separate.

  4. 04

    The handover

    Two rounds of revisions are included. After that I charge by the hour, which is a polite way of saying: be sure at the thumbnails.
