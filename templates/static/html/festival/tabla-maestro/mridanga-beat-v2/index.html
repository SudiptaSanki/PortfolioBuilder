Pune · Sawai Gandharva every December

The cycle does not forgive. It also does not lie.

I am Rohan Kelkar, tabla player in Kothrud, Pune. I accompany singers and dancers, teach one student at a time, and spend December where I have spent it for twenty years — on the accompanist's cushion at the Sawai Gandharva Bhimsen Mahotsav, counting other people's music home to sam.

Every season a student arrives who can play teental at 220 and cannot hold it at 60. Speed is the last thing I sell. First you buy the slow theka — and you pay for it in years.

The cycle bench — hear one cycle

Rhythm is learned by ear and hand together, so the bench plays one full cycle of a tala as plain synthesized strokes — a bright stroke on sam, a clap-weight stroke on each tali, a low bayan stroke where your hand would wave. No recordings, no samples; your browser makes the sound only when you press a button, and it stops by itself when the cycle ends.

Featured engagement

Sawai Gandharva, December 2025

The ask was a hard one: accompany a khayal vocalist I had rehearsed with exactly twice, in the festival's evening session, through a vilambit teental that breathes where it wants to. What I played was mostly restraint — theka like a held hand, answering only when the voice left a door open. The outcome the organisers wrote back about was not my solo. It was that the singer took risks she said she would not have taken with a louder drum.

“Rohan does the rarest thing an accompanist can do — he makes the stage feel safe at slow tempo. My vilambit had somewhere to stand.”
— Vidushi Shalini Bhide, khayal vocalist, on the December 2025 Sawai Gandharva performance
