different rooms

The song is what people remember. The numbers are what made it possible. Each of these has the dancers, the weeks, the formation — and one line about the thing that nearly went wrong.

S-61

Shah & Iyer

Juhu, Mumbai · 14 February 2026 · 24 dancers

A four-minute medley for the bride's cousins, most of whom had never been on a stage. Twelve counts of walk-on, a hook everyone could do by the second rehearsal, and a V that closed around the couple for the last chorus. Three weeks, six sessions, one formation change.

We cut the second song on day eleven. The family had learned the first fifteen counts of it and the timing was never going to work — better to lose a song than have the cousins look at each other on stage.

S-58

Nandini & Arjun

The Leela, Mumbai · 9 January 2026 · 34 dancers

The full family item: cousins, friends, and a segment for the uncles that became the reason people stood up. Four songs in five minutes, three formations, and a bow timed to the last beat so the photographer could get the frame from the front.

The uncles' section was rehearsed separately, on Sunday mornings, with a simpler count. They came on at the second chorus, did sixteen counts, and left the stage to applause. Nobody tried to make them do more.

S-54

Aditi & Kunal

Pune · 22 November 2025 · 28 dancers

Five weeks and a stage two feet narrower than the venue's floor plan. We built the item in two rows instead of the V the family wanted, because the V needed four feet of depth we did not have. The circle in the middle of the song gave the couple their moment instead.

The bride's father joined for the last sixteen counts. He had been practising in the kitchen for a week without telling anyone, which is now my favourite story from a rehearsal.

S-49

Meher & Rishi

Delhi · 14 February 2025 · 18 dancers

A groom's-side item with a bhangra base and a lot of shoulders. Eighteen friends, four weeks, and one formation that never changed — a diagonal from stage left to the back right, because the venue's entrance was on the left and the walk-on had to start there anyway.

Two of the dancers were in Bengaluru until the week of the wedding. They learned the whole set from the video references and walked into the last rehearsal knowing the counts better than the people who had been there every week.

S-44

The Menon kids' squad

Kochi · 27 December 2024 · 12 dancers, ages 7–13

Twelve children, ages seven to thirteen, and a song that was already two years old. No formations, no lifts, no tears. I put the tallest at the back, gave every child one line to remember, and marked the stage with tape in the shape of a circle so they could find their spot without looking at me.

One child froze at the first note. The others kept going, and she joined at the chorus. In a children's item, that is a success.

S-40

Sana & Vikram

Bengaluru · 16 August 2024 · 40 dancers

The largest set I have staged: forty dancers, three groups, one stage. It only worked because each group rehearsed separately for four weeks and met for two combined sessions, and because the transitions were built as walk-ons rather than formation changes. Group A leaves as Group B enters, both to the same eight counts.

The DJ changed the edit two days before the wedding, which moved every count. Kabir rebuilt it overnight from the rehearsal recordings. We rehearsed once more at 7 a.m. on the day.
