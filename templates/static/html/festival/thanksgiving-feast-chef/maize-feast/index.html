I'm June Hollis. I cook harvest feasts in Winneshiek County, Iowa, and my whole year is aimed at one week: Thanksgiving. The menu is the crop itself — corn on the cob grilled in its husk, creamed corn, polenta stirred for forty-five minutes, cornbread from the skillet, hominy I nixtamalise myself — cooked over one oak fire, in the field the corn came out of, whenever the field allows.

The Menu Logic

Five things corn can be

  • 1On the cob, in the husk. Soaked, grilled over coals, husk pulled back as the handle. Salt at the table, not before.
  • 2Creamed corn & succotash. Kernels cut from the cob, cooked in their own milk with beans from the same field.
  • 3Polenta. Stone-ground dent corn, forty-five minutes of stirring, finished with butter and age.
  • 4Skillet cornbread. Coarse meal, a ripping-hot skillet, a crust you can hear.
  • 5Hominy & beans. Field corn nixtamalised in lime water — the oldest dish on the table, and the one that keeps all winter.

From the Field Notebook

Two things I hold to

Sweet corn is a vegetable. Field corn is an ingredient. Cook them the same way and you have understood neither.

Butter is a seasoning. Salt is a decision. I make both on purpose, at the end, where you can taste them.
