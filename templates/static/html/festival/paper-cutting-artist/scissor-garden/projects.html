The drawer,
opened.

Every sheet here was cut in the same room and tested on the same tank. Some were sold, some were floated once and kept, and two were cut for no client at all — those are usually the ones I still look at.

6 of 6 sheets

Sheet 06 · 2025 · 42 cm · temple

Radha’s tank

Cut for the Kartik float at the Potra Kund. The sheet is laid on the water at four in the morning, dusted with six colours, and lifted before the first bath. This is the stencil that made me stop signing the back — the water does not care who cut it.

Held in the workshop drawer. Not for sale; a second cut is possible.

Sheet 41 · 2024 · 60 cm · wedding

Mayur, for a courtyard

Four sheets joined at the tail so the fan crosses the bride’s side and the groom’s. The family had used the same motif at three generations of weddings; the grandmother brought her own 1968 sheet to the workshop so I could copy the eye.

Edition of three. One floated at the wedding, two kept by the family.

Sheet 78 · 2024 · 48 × 62 cm · portrait

Kalpavriksha, for a birth

Cut overnight for a naming ceremony. The tree holds five birds, one for each of the women who stayed awake with the mother. The child’s name is not on the sheet; the family wanted the tree to be read before the name was spoken aloud.

Private commission. A tree with a different count of birds can be cut in six weeks.

Sheet 19 · 2023 · 36 cm · temple

Venugopala

The flute is a single unbroken cut, 11 cm long. If the blade stutters once the note is lost and the sheet goes into the practice pile; this one took four attempts in a single afternoon before the line held.

Two sheets in the drawer. A third can be cut to order.

Installation · 2022 · nine panels · 140 × 100 cm

Nine windows

A gallery piece for a winter show: nine sheets hung in a grid, each with the same star cut, each lit from behind at a slightly different warmth. As you walk past, the stars appear to move because the paper does not.

Shown once in Bengaluru. The panels were separated afterwards; three are still with the gallery.

Sheet 63 · 2025 · 74 cm · wedding

Baraat, at dusk

A procession in one cut: palanquin, two drummers, a lamp-bearer, and the groom’s car reduced to its roof. Made for a family in Agra who wanted the whole street on the tank rather than a single god.

Edition of two. The larger version is cut in two halves and joined at the palanquin.

What a commission involves

Tell me the occasion.
I will tell you the fold.

Most sheets begin with a conversation of about twenty minutes and a photograph of the wall or the tank where the stencil will be used. I then cut one small trial sheet, usually within a fortnight, and send a photograph before the full sheet is started. Nothing is charged until the trial is approved.
