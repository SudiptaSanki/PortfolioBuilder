Studio / Sessions

Six series, and the one rule behind all of them.

The rule is simple: photograph the room as carefully as the subject, and never make a child perform. Everything below came out of that.

I

The Chair

Portrait series202536 frames

One chair, one light, thirty-six people who sat in it across a December. Ray chose the chair in 1994 and it has been reupholstered twice. The series is about the way people hold themselves when they are told they may stay as long as they like: the shoulders come down at different speeds. Some never do.

36 frames

II

Sleigh at Dawn

Outdoor series202418 frames

Six mornings starting at 6:40, when the light comes over the Cascades and the snow turns the colour of a peach. The sleigh belongs to a farmer outside Peshastin and is older than the town’s Bavarian renovation. Nobody smiles on command at that hour, which is why the pictures work.

18 frames

III

The Fireplace Room

Interior series202424 frames

The back room of the shop, with a real fire and a mantel that has been in the photographs since 2017. It is the only set I have that requires no light at all: the fire is the key, a white board bounces it back, and the ISO goes to 1600. The grain is the point.

24 frames

IV

Mrs. Claus, Alone

Portrait series202312 frames

A series made in the half hour after the last family leaves, with the woman who has played Mrs. Claus at the shop for eleven years. She sits in Ray’s chair, takes off her glasses, and stops performing. It is the only work of mine that has been in a gallery, and the only one where nobody is smiling in any frame.

12 frames

V

Dogs of December

Studio series202341 frames

Forty-one dogs, one chair, and a Santa who is allergic to two of them. The rules are different: the dog decides everything, the session lasts four minutes, and the treat is held by the owner behind my left shoulder. Half the frames are of a dog leaving the frame, and three of those are the best in the series.

41 frames

VI

The Last Session

Documentary series2022–20259 frames a year

One family, photographed at 5:30 on Christmas Eve for four years running, after the shop has closed and the street is empty. The parents started it because the father works Christmas Day. The series is four Decembers of the same corner of the same room, and the children growing out of the frame.

Four Decembers
