Bamboo first. Cloth second. Light last.

We build pandals for committees across north Kolkata: sixty-two last season, from a 24-foot neighbourhood mandap to a 90-foot theme pavilion. The frame is cane and bamboo, tied by hand in our yard. The theme is whatever the para argues about for three months, and we have learned to wait for that argument to finish.

03 The work

Four things we build

01

Theme pandals

A committee gives us a story, a budget and a footprint. We give back a drawing in June, a frame in September, and a pandal that can take an evening crowd of forty thousand without a queue collapsing at the steps. The theme work is in the panels and the light, not in a printed backdrop.

6–14 weeks · 18–90 ft frontage

02

Light installations

Bulb arches, rice-light curtains and LED walls for the pandal approach and the lane beyond it. Everything on its own circuit, everything earthed, everything switchable in sections so that one failed strip does not black out a doorway.

3–5 days on site · 12–40 kW

03

Wedding mandaps

Smaller frames, four posts and a canopy, built for one evening and dismantled the next morning. We do eleven or twelve a season, mostly in Baranagar, Dum Dum and Belgharia, and we do not take one during the last three weeks before Puja.

1 day build · 12–24 ft

04

Repair and re-dressing

Old frames that have been in a club storeroom for eleven months, re-tied and re-covered instead of replaced. Since 2018 we have re-dressed thirty-eight pandals this way, which keeps a committee's money in labour rather than in new cane.

2–3 weeks · yard or site
