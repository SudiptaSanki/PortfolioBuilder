The sala on Chembukkavu Hill

Four rooms, five people, and a practice kept deliberately small. A consulting room with the garden door open, a therapy room with two wooden tables, a dispensary, and the courtyard where patients wait and argue about the rain.

The house Built in 1962 as a family house. The consulting room was the front parlour until 2004, when the practice outgrew the dining table.

A house that became a clinic, slowly

Dr. Sarojini Warrier opened the sala in 2004 after thirty years of government service, using the front two rooms of the house she had lived in since marriage. The pharmacy came second, in what was the store; the therapy room came last, in 2011, when a neighbour sold the strip of land behind the kitchen.

Her daughter, Anjali, joined in 2008 and took over the consulting room in 2016. Nothing was rebuilt or renamed. The tiles in the waiting room are the original ones, worn through in a line where people walk to the door, and the shelf in the dispensary still holds her mother's handwritten labels, in Malayalam, for the eighteen oils the house uses most.

The practice sees about forty patients a week across six half-days. That number is a decision, not a limit of space: a first consultation is seventy-five minutes, and there is one physician in the room.

Training Ayurveda in Kerala runs through families and through colleges; Anjali was trained in both, and does not pretend the two are the same.

Dr. Anjali Warrier

BAMS from Vaidyaratnam Ayurveda College, Ollur, 2001. MD in Kayachikitsa from the Kottakkal institution, 2006, with a dissertation on the management of grahani — chronic, irregular digestion — in patients who had already been treated for two years or more.

Between 2006 and 2008 she worked in the panchakarma unit at a hospital in Ernakulam and learned how a therapy room runs when there are thirty patients and one physician: badly, unless the schedule is brutal and the diet is enforced. She brought that schedule back to Thrissur and then slowed it down.

Rules of the house

We do not ask you to stop a medicine prescribed by your physician. We ask to know what it is, so the plan does not fight it. Blood pressure, thyroid, diabetes and psychiatric medication continue unless the prescribing doctor changes them.

Case records are written the same day and kept for seven years. A record is published in the programmes section only with the patient's written consent and with every identifying detail removed. Every patient gets a copy of their own plan.

No treatment is sold as a package before an assessment. If a course is not suitable — pregnancy, active infection, recent surgery, uncontrolled blood pressure — we say so and refer you back.

Fees are on the board and do not change with the course. If a plan is not settling after two reviews, we say that too, and either change the approach or send you to a specialist.

The pharmacy dispenses only what is prescribed here. We do not sell oils or tablets across the counter, and there is no shop.
