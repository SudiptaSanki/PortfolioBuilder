The playing

The instrument does not get easier

Twenty-six years of practice and the same two problems: the meend is not smooth enough, and the tabla player is waiting. That is the work, and it does not finish.

Four hours before the city wakes

I begin at four in the morning, in a room with one window and a cool stone floor. Alap first, without tabla, for an hour. Then the gat, with the metronome at half the speed I will perform it. The metronome is not musical, and that is the point: it tells me when I am lying.

My guru, Ustad Nazir Ahmed Khan, taught in the Senia way, which means the raga comes before the composition and the breath comes before both. He would stop me in the middle of a tihai and ask what the raga was doing at that moment. If I could not answer in words, I did not understand it, and the phrase would not survive a bad evening.

I still cannot answer that question every time. That is why I practise.

A raga is not a mood

Every programme note says a raga is “serene” or “yearning”, and every one of those words is a shortcut that hides the raga. Yaman is not calm because of a feeling; it is calm because it moves in whole tones and its tivra Ma opens the middle of the scale. Malkauns is not sad because it is midnight; it is dark because it has no Re and no Pa, and the ear has nowhere ordinary to rest.

So I will tell you the notes, the vadi, the time, and the phrase that identifies the raga, and leave the adjectives to you. If you want to know what a raga feels like, listen to a musician hold one note for forty seconds at the right hour. That will do more than a paragraph.

What I will not do

  • Play a night raga at noon. A festival slot at 14:00 is not a reason to play Bageshri; it is a reason to play a different raga. If the organiser insists, I decline the concert and say why.
  • Do a twenty-minute medley of four ragas. Four ragas in twenty minutes is a demonstration, not a concert. I would rather play one for an hour.
  • Add a band. A sitar with a drum kit and a synthesiser is a different instrument, played by other people, and they are welcome to it. It is not what I trained for.
  • Play through a sound system that clips. The sitar's jawari lives in the overtones, and a clipped system deletes them. If the hall's system cannot carry an acoustic instrument, I will ask for two microphones and a quiet hand on the desk, or play unamplified.
  • Teach by video. Four students at a time, in the room, by introduction. The instrument needs hands on it and a person to move them.

The instrument

A 1978 Hiren Roy sitar with one tumba, nineteen frets and eleven sympathetic strings. I bought it in 1999 from a man in Lucknow who had stopped playing after his hearing went. It has been re-fretted twice, the jawari has been filed four times, and the second string has a wolf tone at the fifth fret that I have learned to play around.

The taraf are tuned to the raga before every concert. Eleven strings retuned between two ragas in a programme is ten minutes of a hall listening to a musician tune, which is part of the concert and not a delay.
