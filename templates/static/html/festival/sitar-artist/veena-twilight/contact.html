Invite me

Tell me the room, the hour, and who else is playing

I take about twenty engagements a year, and most of them are in rooms smaller than a hundred seats. The hour decides the raga, so it is the first thing I will ask you about.

What a booking needs

A room that is quiet enough to play unamplified, or a system with a sound engineer who understands an acoustic instrument. Two microphones and a hand on the desk is better than eight and nobody watching.

A tanpura player, if you have one. If not, I bring a tanpura app on a phone and a small speaker, which is honest and not ideal.

Fee
₹60,000 for a baithak or temple concert, ₹1,10,000 for a festival set with sound and travel, studio work by the day. Travel outside Varanasi at actuals.
The hour
Sunset and after, or before 07:00. A midday set is a different raga, not the same one at a worse time, and I will say so before you book.
Length
One raga, forty-five to seventy minutes, with an alap. If the evening is long, two ragas with an interval and ten minutes of tuning between them.
What I bring
The 1978 Hiren Roy, two of my own microphones in case yours are wrong, and a tabla player if you do not have one in the city.
Teaching
Four students at a time, by introduction. I do not teach online, and I do not take beginners who have not heard a live baithak.

If the date is inside a month, write and I will tell you honestly whether it is possible.

The invitation

Seven fields. The last one is the one that decides whether the evening will work.

The invitation is with me

Thank you, —. I reply within three days, and the first question is always the hour. If the date is close, telephone rather than write.
