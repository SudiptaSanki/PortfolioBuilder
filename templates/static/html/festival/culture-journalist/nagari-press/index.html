Theatre Reporting · 3,100 words

They kept the lights on for eleven years. Now the lease is up.

A 90-seat theatre off Prince Anwar Shah Road has run on donations, one grant, and the labour of forty volunteers since 2015. Its landlord wants the floor back in March. The artists have four months to argue that a room can be infrastructure.

By Rukmini Sen · Photographs by Arko Das · Read time 14 min

The rehearsal began, as it has for eleven years, with somebody moving a chair. The chair is the theatre's whole architecture: it becomes a throne, a hospital bed, a border post, a grandmother's lap. On a Tuesday in October it became a barricade, because the actors were rehearsing a play about a blockade and the room is only nine metres wide.

The lease expires on 31 March. The landlord, a printing firm that has owned the building since 1978, wants the floor for storage. The theatre's accounts show ₹11.4 lakh in annual running costs and ₹9.8 lakh in income, a gap closed each year by a benefit performance and by the director's own salary, which is zero.

What the numbers do not show is the queue outside on a Sunday: sixty people for ninety seats, half of them under twenty-five, paying ₹150 a ticket to watch a play in a room with one fan.

“A city measures its culture in monuments. Artists measure it in rooms they can afford to be wrong in.”

— from the lease submission, October 2026

The submission to the municipal heritage committee runs to forty pages and argues that the theatre is not a heritage building but a heritage practice — a distinction the current rules do not recognise. A decision is expected in February. The full dispatch is in the archive →

Craft 2,600 words

Nine yards, one loom, no apprentice

In a village in Nadia, a weaver of tangail sarees is the last in her family to work the pit loom. She has three years of orders and nobody to leave the pattern to.

Film 3,400 words

The prints nobody will project

Forty-one cans of 35 mm Bengali film sit in a room at the state archive with no working projector. A projectionist has offered to fix one, if someone will pay for the parts.

Music 2,100 words

A baul archive with no heirs

Six hundred hours of field recordings, made between 1972 and 1994, are on reel-to-reel tape in a flat in Bansdroni. The collector is eighty-one.
