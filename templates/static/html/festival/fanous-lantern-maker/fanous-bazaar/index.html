The glazier's table

Set the body, the glass,

A fanous is measured by the panel, not by the centimetre: six sides, three tiers, one door. Change the silhouette or the palette and the shop returns the brass, the glass, the weight and the price in Egyptian pounds.

What leaves the workshop

Three kinds of work pay for the brass: the Ramadan fanous, the commission for a hotel or mosque, and the repair nobody else will take.

01

The Ramadan fanous

Standard bodies, six sides, three tiers, in red and amber glass. Sold from the shop and from a stall in Khan el-Khalili from the first night of the month. Families buy them in threes: one for the door, one for the child, one for the balcony.

From £E 850 · ready in 3 days
02

Commissioned pieces

Hotels, mosques and restaurants ask for a body drawn to their arch and a glass palette matched to the room. The largest we made was 1.4 m for a hotel in Zamalek, hung in a stairwell, lit from inside by nine bulbs.

From £E 9,500 · 4–6 weeks
03

Repair & re-glass

Every January the shop fills with lanterns that lost a pane in storage. We keep the old patterns, so a 1970s body can be re-glazed in its original red without recutting the brass. Bring the pieces, even the broken ones.

From £E 180 · 2–10 days
