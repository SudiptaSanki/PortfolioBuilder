Nine frames that survived the edit

Every year I shoot about four thousand frames and print nine. These are from the archive: the ones where the light, the crowd and the moment happened to agree. Frame numbers are from the negative sleeves.

Parade frames

Show
Frame 118 · 202535 mm

The shoe

Muses throws a decorated shoe, and each one is somebody’s winter. This is a close frame shot from the second row, after the throw.

Frame 041 · 202485 mm

Flambeaux

Orpheus, on the route. The flame is the light source, so the exposure is a guess and then a correction. f/1.8 at 1/125, ISO 1600.

Frame 207 · 202350 mm

The coconut

Zulu, Mardi Gras morning. Painted by hand and handed down from a float. This one was still warm from the sun.

Frame 314 · 202535 mm

Corner, before dusk

Black Masking Indian, Uptown. One porch light, a suit a year in the making, and permission asked before the frame.

Frame 088 · 202224 mm

Rex at Lee Circle

The oldest parade, shot wide because the crowd is the picture. The float lights are designed for television; the faces are not.

Frame 402 · 202435 mm

Second line

A Sunday afternoon that ran into dark. The umbrella turned; the shutter was 1/30 and the spokes became a drawing.

Frame 233 · 202685 mm

The catch

Hands are the hardest thing to photograph in a bead storm. This one is sharp because the thrower was close and the flash was off.

Frame 356 · 202124 mm

The sousaphone

A brass band warming up under a porch before the parade stepped off. The bell catches every light on the street.

Frame 019 · 202050 mm

Ash Wednesday, 6 am

The morning after. Ladders still tied to the neutral ground, confetti in the gutter, and nobody in the frame.
