Pookalam artist · Thrissur

A pookalam is a prayer you can walk around. Four thousand flowers to a square metre, laid by hand before the house wakes.

I am Devika Nair. From Atham to Thiruvonam I lay flower carpets on verandahs, temple flags and wedding mandapams across central Kerala, and for the rest of the year I make them for housewarmings, memorials and the odd stubborn client who wants marigold in June.

Ring 01 · What it is

A floor that is only good for one day

The word is pookalam: poo, flower, and kalam, a place marked out. It is a drawing on the ground, made of nothing but flower heads, and it is meant to be walked past, looked down at, and then swept up. A pookalam that survives the week has failed.

The ground is cleaned and smeared with a thin paste of cow dung, which darkens it and holds the flowers without water. The design is marked with a stick and a length of thread, then the flowers go down ring by ring from the centre out. Nobody draws the whole thing first. You keep the circle true with your arm.

How a commission runs

I arrive before four in the morning with the flowers, because a pookalam laid in the afternoon heat is a pookalam that will be brown by the time the guests come.

  • Size1.5 m for a doorway up to 12 m for a temple courtyard Priced by the metre and the flower
  • TimeFour hours for three metres, nine hours for six Two assistants beyond five metres
  • FlowersThumba, marigold, chethi, aripoo, chrysanthemum, rose petals Cut in Thrissur and Ollur the evening before
  • WeatherUnder a roof, or under a shamiana Rain on an open pookalam is the one thing I cannot plan for

Ring 04 · The ten days

From Atham to Thiruvonam

The pookalam grows by a ring each day and is swept away after Thiruvonam. The Gregorian dates shift every year; the Malayalam almanac decides, not me.
