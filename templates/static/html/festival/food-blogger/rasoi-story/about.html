The cook · since 2014

Aditi RaghavanI am not a chef. I am a person who has cooked dinner about five thousand times in the same small kitchen, and taken notes.

Born in Matunga, 1983. Mother from Palakkad, father from a house in Girgaon where the Sunday food was Maharashtrian. Both kitchens are in the recipes, usually in the same pot.

At a glance

Journalist for nine years, food writer for twelve. Two children who eat most things. One mother-in-law who eats fewer. A husband who does the shopping and is therefore consulted on all quantities.

Vegetarian by household, not by sermon.

The kitchen the recipes come from

Two burners, one window facing east, a counter 2.1 metres long, and a pressure cooker that is older than my marriage. There is no oven. Anything on this site that claims to be baked has been tested at my sister's flat in Andheri and is marked as such.

The window matters more than it should. It faces the light well, so from seven in the morning there is a stripe of sun across the counter that moves from the spice box to the sink in about forty minutes. I do most of the photograph-free work in that stripe, which is why the recipes are written for the morning.

Two rules before a recipe is published

It has to be cooked twice. Once without notes, to see what I do by habit, and once with the notes, to see whether they are true. If the second time does not produce the same pot, the recipe is not ready and it does not go up. About one in four never passes.

Every quantity has a weight. Cups are in the recipe as well, because that is how most people cook, but the weight is the one I have tested. A cup of toor dal is 200 grams in my kitchen and 180 in my mother's, and that is a difference worth knowing about.

The recipes do not photograph well, and I have stopped trying. A steel dabba of amti under a kitchen bulb looks like a steel dabba. What I can give you is the weight, the order and the mistake I made the first time.

What this is not

  • Not a restaurant. Nothing here is a restaurant dish recreated at home, and none of it is "restaurant-style".
  • Not sponsored. No brand has paid for a mention, and the one time a company sent a box of utensils, I gave it to a school kitchen and said so.
  • Not authoritative. My grandmother's Palakkad amti is not the only amti, and if yours has coconut in it, yours is also right.
  • Not illustrated. No photographs of hands, no flat-lays, no cheese pulls. The words have to do the work or the recipe is not worth writing.

A short history of the pot

  1. 2014

    The first forty recipesWritten on a blog with a grey background, mostly for my sister in Toronto who kept ringing to ask how much tamarind.

  2. 2017

    The index startsA spreadsheet with a column for the weight of every ingredient, because a reader wrote to say her amti was sour and it turned out her tamarind was a paste, not pulp.

  3. 2021

    The Thursday letterOne recipe a week, no images. Seven thousand people read it now, which is roughly the population of the building I live in times twenty.

  4. 2024

    The zineTwice a year, printed at a shop near Sion, sold at the Matunga market on the first Sunday. It exists because a printer offered a good rate and I wanted to see the recipes on paper.

  5. 2026

    412 recipesThe index, searchable, with the family notes kept in the margins where they were written.
