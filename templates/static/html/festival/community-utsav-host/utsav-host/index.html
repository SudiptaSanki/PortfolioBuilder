      How these numbers are worked out

      Volunteers: one for every 45 guests, plus six fixed roles. Chairs: 40% of the crowd, in tens, because a third of the colony stands at the back. Prasadam: the festival’s own portion weight per head plus eight per cent for the kitchen and the volunteers, rounded up to a five-kilo batch. Water: 0.6 litres a head and 60 litres for the crew. Power: 3 kW of house load, the sound rig, and the venue’s lights. Waste: 40 grams a head, 60 on the street. The 22:00 cut-off is the Noise Pollution Rules, not my preference.

      What I take on

      Four things I will not hand back to the committee

      1. 01

        The programme

        One sheet, printed and stuck at the gate: who stands where, which child sings when, what happens at 19:15. If two people are holding different sheets, the evening is already late.

      2. 02

        The money

        One treasurer, one ledger, receipts read out at the closing meeting. I collect nothing myself, which is why I can ask a vendor to leave the pandal.

      3. 03

        The sound

        One operator, one meter reading at the boundary wall, the volume marked on a strip of tape. Off at 22:00 even when the last bhajan is going well.

      4. 04

        The cleanup

        Sweepers and a skip booked before the pandal goes up, not after. Flowers, prasadam leaf cups and the flower-bed of a neighbour who did not sign up for any of it.
