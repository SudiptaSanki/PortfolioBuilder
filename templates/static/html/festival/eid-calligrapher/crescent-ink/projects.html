Commissions · a working index

What left the
desk this year.

Commissioned pieces are private by default. The entries below are shared with the families' and shopkeepers' permission, and every photograph was taken before mounting, on the day the ink dried.

  1. Plate12
    Nikkah nama · 2026

    A contract for two doctors, written in one breath

    Forty-two by thirty centimetres, thuluth for the names and Nastaliq for the conditions, with a border borrowed from a 1911 Hyderabad deed. The couple asked for the witnesses' lines to be left wide enough that an elderly uncle could sign without folding his hand.

    Hyderabad42 × 30 cmSized hammam, lamp-black

    Eight weeks, two trial sheets.

  2. Plate11
    Eid panel · 2026

    One word for a bakery on Lad Bazaar

    عيد مبارك in thuluth, gilded rosette, sized for the wall behind the counter where the cash box sits. The owner wanted it to be readable from the street through a crowd, so the strokes are heavier than I would normally draw at this size.

    Lad Bazaar60 × 45 cmTea-toned paper, shell gold

    Three weeks, one revision.

  3. Plate10
    Shop board · 2025

    Enamel on teak for a boarding house

    The sign for Sabri Boarding House, sixteen feet above the pavement and facing the monsoon. The lettering was drawn on paper first, then transferred and painted in three coats of enamel. The Urdu is in Nastaliq, the English in a stripped-down serif that can hold its own beside it.

    Begum Bazaar240 × 60 cmTeak, enamel

    Painted on site over four days.

  4. Plate09
    Bookplate · 2025

    An ex libris for a private library

    Six hundred bookplates for a collection in Banjara Hills, each stamped from a block cut by hand after the original drawing. The design is a small eight-fold geometric device with the owner's name in naskh and a line of descent in a lighter hand.

    Banjara Hills9 × 12 cmInk on laid paper, block-printed

    Fourteen days for the drawing, three weeks to print.

  5. Plate08
    Nikkah nama · 2025

    A Kashmiri border for a wedding in Srinagar

    The text was standard; the border was not. The bride's family sent a photograph of a shawl that had come down through four generations, and I drew its chinar-leaf repeat around the sheet in gold and a single line of pomegranate red. It took longer than the writing.

    Srinagar50 × 35 cmShell gold, pomegranate lake

    Eleven weeks, four border studies.

  6. Plate07
    Eid panel · 2024

    A pair of panels for a hallway

    Two sheets, hung as a pair: عيد and نور, the second added at the client's request after seeing the first. The moon in the upper margin of each is drawn with a single loaded stroke, which is either the easiest or the most nerve-racking mark on the sheet.

    Mehdipatnam30 × 30 cm eachHammam paper, lamp-black

    Six weeks for the pair.

  7. Plate06
    Menu board · 2024

    A Ramzan menu, written fresh each evening

    A restaurant near Charminar wanted the day's menu written by hand on a slate at four o'clock, every day of Ramzan. I did it for twenty-nine days. The handwriting changed by the third week, and the owner asked me to keep the change.

    Charminar90 × 120 cmSlate, chalk and brush

    Daily for one month.

  8. Plate05
    Bookplate · 2023

    A bookplate for a school library

    Four hundred and fifty plates for the library at a girls' school in the old city, printed from a drawing of a lamp and an open book. The school could not afford shell gold, so the lamp is left as bare ink, and I think it is the better for it.

    Old City8 × 11 cmInk on cartridge, block-printed

    Pro bono; the block was donated.
