How we work

The cook is Nikhil. The system is the stall.

Nikhil Verma learned chaat the way it is usually learned — standing next to somebody better at it. His uncle ran a thela in Sarafa for thirty years; Nikhil started at fourteen washing plates, graduated to the mint chutney at sixteen, and was trusted with the tikki tawa at nineteen. The stall under his own name opened in 2016, the prep kitchen followed in 2019, and events in 2021. What changed across those years is not the food. It is that everything the uncle kept in his head got written down — the measures, the order of assembly, the chart — so that a plate built by any of the four people on this counter is the same plate.

The day, in order

How a plate comes to exist

  1. 05:30 — Chutneys first

    The morning starts with the two chutneys, because everything else is built on them. Imli from whole tamarind blocks, soaked and pulped in the kitchen; pudina ground fresh — mint chutney made yesterday is a different, duller thing, and it is not served. Both are tasted against the reference jars before anything else happens.

  2. 07:00 — Bases and fillings

    Potatoes boiled and held whole; chole on; papdi dough rolled and fried; puris puffed and stored dry, away from steam. Pani puri water is made now, chilled, and never carried to a second day — yesterday's water is poured away, and the board would rather run out at 9 pm than serve it.

  3. 11:00 — The stall opens, nothing pre-built

    Not one plate exists before it is ordered. Tikkis fry when the ticket lands; samosas are crushed hot, never before. This is slower than a display counter and it is the entire business model.

  4. The assembly order — the same on every plate

    Base, hot. Chutneys, by the spoon, at the level on the ticket. Yoghurt if it is on the ticket. Then — in the last twenty seconds, and not before — the sev. The order is printed in the kitchen because crisp and wet are enemies, and the sev's twenty seconds are the truce.

  5. Allergen orders change the setup, not just the plate

    Say the word allergy at the counter and the plate is built on a washed board with a clean spoon, from the covered containers, by one person start to finish. Peanut, dairy and gluten are handled on this counter all day — sev carries gluten, yoghurt is dairy, the peanut finish is peanut — so the flags on the menu are a starting point, and the conversation is the real system.

The honest page

Four things we would rather print than imply

Heat is personal

The wall chart defines what goes in — it cannot define what you will feel. Chillies vary by season, and so do people. So the chart is a guide with exact measures, not a promise with exact effects. Taste at the counter, say “one level down”, and the plate is rebuilt without argument and without charge. Nobody has ever been teased at this counter for a level 0.

Chaat waits for no one

Ten minutes. That is the honest life of a built plate, and it is why we do not deliver assembled chaat, do not box it mixed, and retired a profitable lunch box over it (the story is on the Events page). If food must travel, it travels as a kit and is assembled where it is eaten.

“Vegan” means the whole plate

Yoghurt is dairy. A plate with yoghurt on it is vegetarian, not vegan, however plant-based everything else is — so the board never calls it vegan, and the builder on the home page takes the word away the moment yoghurt goes on. Most of the menu is vegan as built once yoghurt is off; sev and puris are the gluten to watch, and they are flagged.

The kitchen, plainly

The prep kitchen is FSSAI-licensed and the licence hangs on the stall board, next to the chart. Water is municipal, filtered at the kitchen; the pani puri water is made daily from it. Ready-to-eat assembly is gloved. Raw and ready-to-eat never share a board. None of this is remarkable — it is the floor, not the ceiling, and it is printed because a customer should not have to ask.

Terms

Events, in writing

Booking

  • Packages and minimums exactly as printed on the home page — no second price list exists.
  • The date is held with a 50% advance; the balance is due on the day of the event.
  • Travel within Indore is included; outside Indore, a flat ₹2,500, agreed before booking — never discovered after.
  • The final dish list and cover count are confirmed 5 days before; covers can rise after that, and we will always try, but cannot be promised.

Cancellation & changes

  • Cancel 10+ days out: the advance returns in full.
  • Cancel inside 10 days: the advance covers the prep already bought; anything unspent returns.
  • Weather moves an outdoor counter under cover or to a new date once, free — chaat under a tarpaulin is still chaat; chaat in the rain is not.
  • Allergen counts for an event are taken at confirmation and the counter is briefed from that list, not from memory.
