The wall chart · 01

Heat, defined by the spoon — not by bravado

“Medium” means nothing until somebody prints what is in it. This chart hangs beside the counter and it is the whole of our heat policy: five levels, each one a measure the person building your plate can repeat exactly.

Table scrolls sideways on a phone — the measures are the point.

LevelNameWhat goes in, per plateWhat it means
0Bina mirchNo chilli at allNo green chilli, no chilli powder. Tang from imli and amchur only — the plate for children and the cautious.
1Halki¼ tsp Kashmiri chilli powderColour more than heat. Kashmiri chilli is bred for red, not fire.
2Beech ki½ tsp chopped green chilli + ¼ tsp chilli powderThe counter standard. Warm all the way through, gone in a minute.
3Teekhi1 tsp chopped green chilli + ½ tsp chilli powderProper heat. Most regulars who say “spicy” mean this.
4Sarafa teekhi2 tsp chopped green chilli + 1 tsp chilli powder + a fresh chilli on topThe night-market level. We will ask once — “pakka?” — and then build it exactly so.
A word on Scoville: Scoville units measure the heat of a chilli or an extract, diluted in a lab. They describe an ingredient, not a finished plate — a level-3 plate and a level-3 plate at the next stall are different food. So the kitchen scales by the spoon, which is the measure the cook actually uses, and prints it.

The order ticket · 02

Build your plate

This is the ticket the counter works from. Choose a base, the chutneys, a heat level from the chart, and the finish — the ticket writes the build in assembly order, works out the allergens from the components themselves, and prices it from the printed menu card below. Nothing on the ticket is typed twice.

    The event counter · 04

    The stall, brought to your venue

    Chaat travels well exactly one way: unassembled. We bring the bases hot, the chutneys cold, and build every plate in front of the guest — the same ticket system, the same wall chart, on a counter at your wedding, office do or festival.
