A costume is a performance before it is a garment.

I cut and build for bodies that have to move eight times a week. That means a toile before the silk, a seam that gives where the shoulder turns, and a fitting on a person, never on a mannequin. The workroom is on East Passyunk; the kettle is usually on.

02 · the workroom

Four tables, two machines, one long mirror.

The atelier takes theatre, dance, film and the occasional private commission. Everything starts with measurements and a toile; nothing starts with a sketch alone.

01

Measure

Fourteen measurements, taken over the clothes the performer will wear under the costume. A dance costume is measured in fifth position, not standing still.

Allowance: 2 cm at the shoulder, 4 cm at the hip.

02

Draft and toile

A flat pattern from the block, then a muslin toile built to it. The toile is where the design changes; the silk never hears about the first idea.

Two to four toiles per principal look.

03

Build

Cut, stay-stitch, bone, bind. Period pieces are built on a corset or a corselette so the outer layer can be thin and still hold its line.

Hand-finishing on anything that will be seen close.

04

Fit and move

The final fitting is a rehearsal. The performer walks, sits, lifts both arms, does the choreography. A seam that fails here is changed here.

Nothing leaves the workroom untested.

03 · the drafting table

Draft a skirt block, to scale.

This is the same quarter-scale exercise I set for a first fitting: enter a waist, a hip and a length, choose a silhouette, and the bench draws the front panel with its dart, grainline, notches and seam allowance. The numbers are real; the fabric is not.

Quarter scale. The solid line is the cutting line, the dashed line the stitching line, and the two notches mark the hip. Every pattern in this workroom is drafted by hand first; the computer only copies it.

04 · the fitting

Three fittings, and then it is costume.

A first fitting is in toile and pins, with the performer in the shoes they will wear. The second is in the real fabric, unfinished. The third is in the rehearsal room, under the lights, with the hat and the wig on. Between each one there is a week of work and a list on the table.
