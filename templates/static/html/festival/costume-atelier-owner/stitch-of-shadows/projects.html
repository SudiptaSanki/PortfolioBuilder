Six builds, and what each one cost the pattern.

Every production changes something in the workroom: a new block, a different bone, a faster quick-change. These are the builds that taught the most, with the numbers that mattered.

The production book

No. 118
Spring 2024

A Midsummer Night's Dream

Twenty-three costumes for a regional theatre with a three-week build. Oberon's coat was the problem: the director wanted it to move like smoke, the actor wanted to be able to fight in it. We built it in a washed silk organza over a boned corselette, with the skirt panels cut on the bias so they lifted with the arm rather than dragging. Two toiles, then a rehearsal fit with the fight choreography, after which we took four inches out of the back panel.

Costumes 23 (11 principals)
Build 3 weeks, 3 people
Toiles Oberon 2, Titania 3
Fabric Silk organza, wool suiting
Note Fight call fitted before first preview

No. 121
Dec 2024

Fourteen tutus for a Nutcracker

A civic ballet's Sugar Plum and snow scene. Each tutu is a different body, so each is a different pattern: the plate is built to the dancer's hip, and the net is tacked in place while she stands, because a tutu that looks right on a form can sit wrong when the dancer lifts a leg. Fourteen in nine weeks, with the last three delivered the morning of the dress rehearsal.

Pieces 14 tutus, 6 bodices
Net 8 layers, hand-tacked
Fit 2 per dancer
Note Plates matched to each dancer's hip

No. 109
2023 · film

A 1920s film, with multiples

A feature shooting in Philadelphia needed a 1920s wardrobe for a cast of nine, with three multiples of the lead's day dress because the schedule had her wet, muddy and bleeding by turn. The original dress was drafted from a photograph and a museum pattern; the multiples were cut from the same block, but the third was built on a slightly different bias so the mud would sit differently on camera.

Pieces 31, including 3 hero multiples
Period 1923–1926
Research 2 museum patterns, 40 photographs
Note One dress aged by hand, not machine

No. 126
2025

Three quick-changes for a drag act

A performer with a 90-second change between numbers. The costume had to open down the back, step out of, and be stepped into on the other side of a wing with one dresser in the dark. We built the second look with magnets at the shoulder and a concealed zip at the hip, and rehearsed the change eleven times in the workroom before it went to the theatre.

Looks 3, one quick-change
Change 90 seconds, 1 dresser
Fastenings Magnets, concealed zip
Note Rehearsed 11 times before opening

No. 130
2025 · museum

A 1912 walking suit for a mannequin

A small museum needed a study reproduction of a 1912 walking suit for a mannequin that would be dressed once and left for a year. No body inside, so the shape had to be built: a padded underform, a boned inner belt, and a skirt that hangs from the belt rather than the waistband, so it would not collapse over twelve months. The pattern was taken off the original with a muslin rub-off.

Pieces Suit, blouse, underform
Method Rub-off from the original
Display 12 months, no re-dress
Note Skirt hung from an inner belt

No. 134
2026

An opera coat, cut twice

A mezzo-soprano wanted a coat that could be worn open for the aria and closed for the curtain call, in a velvet that weighed more than she expected. The first cut was too heavy at the shoulder; the second moved the seam back and took the weight onto the waist. We kept the first as a rehearsal coat, and it is still in the rail at the back of the workroom with a tag that says “heavy — do not repeat”.

Pieces 1 coat, 2 cuts
Fabric Silk velvet, 6 m
Fits 3, one after the orchestra dress
Note First cut kept as rehearsal coat

How to start a commission

Tell me the opening night first.

The date decides everything else: the number of toiles, whether a fabric can be dyed, and whether the workroom can take the job at all. Send the date, the venue and the design, even if the design is a sketch on a napkin.

Ask about a build →
  • 01

    Read the design

    A conversation about the production, not just the garment. I will tell you what is possible in the time.

  • 02

    Quote and calendar

    A fixed quote per look, a build calendar, and the dates of the fittings before anything is cut.

  • 03

    Measure

    Every performer in the workroom, or on site if the company is out of town.

  • 04

    Build to the rehearsal

    The last fitting is always in the room where it will be performed.
