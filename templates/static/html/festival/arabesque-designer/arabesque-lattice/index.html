Girih · arabesque · Isfahan

A pattern is a promise that repeats.

Nastaran Farhadi draws girih and arabesque patterns for tile, plaster and paper, from a studio three streets from Naqsh-e Jahan Square. She starts with a compass and a straightedge, because the compass does not lie about symmetry. The computer comes later, to repeat what the hand has already proved.

The lattice bench · 02

Draw a wall, one fold at a time

A girih pattern is a small number of shapes repeated on a grid, with the lines crossing at angles the masons can cut. Choose a family, a palette and a repeat; the bench draws the lattice and measures what it would take to cover a square metre.

The method · 04

Compass first, computer second

The order matters. A pattern proved on paper with a compass can be built in tile by a mason who has never seen a screen.

01

Set the circle

Everything begins with a circle and its radius. The radius is the unit; the repeat is a multiple of it.

02

Divide the circle

Eight, ten or twelve equal parts, stepped off with the compass. No protractor, no measurement in millimetres.

03

Draw the rosette

Connect the division points into a star, then into petals. The rosette is the atom of the pattern.

04

Find the repeat

Place rosettes on the grid until the gaps ask for their own shape. The interstitial star is discovered, not invented.

05

Cut the strap

Decide the width of the line. On tile it becomes the grout; on plaster it becomes the raised ridge; on paper it is the gold.

06

Enlarge on the wall

A 1:5 drawing is chalked onto the surface at full size, then corrected by eye where the wall is not true.

"The pattern is not decoration on the building. It is the building explaining how it was measured."

The studio

Naqsh Studio is one room with a north window, two drawing tables and a wall of plaster samples. The samples are the archive: every pattern that has been built is on the wall in a square the size of a hand.

Commissions are taken for architectural surfaces, print and single plates. The studio works with one tile workshop and one plasterer, both within walking distance.
