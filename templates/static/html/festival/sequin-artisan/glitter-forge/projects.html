Eight things that left the bench

Not a gallery — an order book. Each entry carries the cloth, the sequin, the count and the hours, because that is how the work is priced and how I remember it. Client names are withheld where the costume house asked.

No. 091 · 2026

Ramlila crown and chest panel, Ayodhya company

Six crowns and six chest panels for the principal actors of a month-long Ramlila. The crowns are built on a cane frame with a cotton base, so they sit light on a boy of sixteen who has to wear one for four hours a night. The chest panels carry a sun in katori and a border in badla that reads from the back of the maidan.

Cloth cotton canvas over cane Sequin 4 mm katori, 6 mm tikkadi Count 41,600 across six crowns Hours 46 h, two hands

No. 088 · 2026

Durga tableau drape, a barowari committee in Howrah

Three drape panels that frame the pandal and a cover for the dhak stand. The brief was one sentence long: “It must be visible from the road, in the rain.” Tikkadi discs do the work at distance; the close detail is 3 mm sitaara in the folds, where the committee’s own people stand.

Cloth tarpaulin-backed cotton Sequin 10 mm tikkadi, 3 mm sitaara Count 96,400 across three panels Hours 108 h, four hands

No. 084 · 2026

Teej dupatta border, a family in Gomti Nagar

A four-metre border in mukaish and 3 mm sitaara, worked to be read by the family sitting close, not by the crowd. The motif is the client’s grandmother’s: a vine with seven leaves, taken off a khaka in the archive dated 1974 and re-pricked at the original scale.

Cloth client’s own Chanderi silk Sequin badla, 3 mm sitaara Count 18,200, border only Hours 21 h, one hand

No. 079 · 2025

Nautanki dancer’s shoulder cape, Kanpur

A cape that has to survive being caught on a curtain, folded into a trunk and worn again the next night. Every sequin is seated through two layers and knotted on the reverse; nothing is left with a loose tail. The rose finish was mixed from two suppliers to match a photograph the client brought in.

Cloth cotton velveteen Sequin 6 mm katori, rose Count 12,800 Hours 15 h, one hand

No. 072 · 2025

Ganesh mandal banner, a lane committee in Pune

A 2.4 by 1.2 metre banner carried at the head of a procession through lanes two metres wide. It is mounted on a bamboo pole with a canvas sleeve, because a stitched sleeve tears at the first turn. The lettering is in badla line work, so it survives being folded against itself for eleven days.

Cloth heavy canvas, bamboo sleeve Sequin badla, 8 mm tikkadi Count 34,900 Hours 38 h, two hands

No. 066 · 2024

Sehra band for a groom, old city

A narrow band worn across the forehead under the sehra, worked in badla so it lies flat under the turban. The family asked for the moon in the centre; the moon is 6 mm katori, and the two stars either side are the only tikkadi on the piece, because anything larger presses into the forehead after an hour.

Cloth silk on muslin backing Sequin badla, 6 mm katori, 8 mm tikkadi Count 2,140 Hours 4 h, one hand

No. 058 · 2024

Re-seat of a 1962 mukaish odhni

Brought in folded in newspaper by a family clearing a house. Roughly a third of the badla had gone; the rest was tarnished black. I matched the wire gauge from the surviving border, re-seated 4,300 stitches, and left the blackened sections alone where the cloth underneath was sound — a repair should be visible on the reverse, not invisible on the front.

Cloth original muslin, conserved Sequin badla, matched gauge Count 4,300 new stitches Hours 19 h, one hand

No. 041 · 2023

Tableau rider’s armour, a Ramlila company in Varanasi

A chest and back plate in tikkadi and mirror, built on buckram so it holds its curve off the body. The actor plays Ravana and has to turn on a moving tableau; the plates are laced at the sides, never buckled, so they give when he turns. The ten heads are carried separately, each with its own crown band.

Cloth buckram, cotton lining Sequin 10 mm tikkadi, mirrors Count 27,500 Hours 31 h, two hands
