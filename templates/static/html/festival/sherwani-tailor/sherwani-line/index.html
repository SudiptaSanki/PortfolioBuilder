01

A sherwani is built
from the inside out.

The visible cloth is the last thing that happens. First comes the canvas — a chest piece of cotton and horsehair, padded by hand with thousands of small stitches, which is what gives a sherwani its hold across the front and its soft collapse at the shoulder. A machine cannot pad a chest; it can only quilt one flat.

Then the collar, cut on the bias so it stands without a tie, and the sleeve head, eased in by hand over the shoulder point. By the time you see the brocade, the garment has already been fitted on a dummy in the workshop for four days — and on you twice.

My father opened the shop in 1987 with two tailors, a hand-cranked Singer, and a rule that has not changed: no garment leaves without a third fitting. Not a delivery appointment — a fitting, with a mirror, in daylight, and time to be honest.

The house rules

  • One garment at a time, per client.
  • No fusion fits. If it has a zip, it is not ours.
  • Cloth is bought after the second fitting.
  • The tailor signs the inside facing.

03

The cloth comes
from four looms.

Bhagalpur · raw silk

Raw silk, undyed

Slubby, honest, and it creases where you sat. Takes tilla beautifully and shows every stitch that is not straight, which is why we only use it for clients who can be fitted twice in the same week.

₹4,200 / metre · 3.2 m for a calf-length sherwani

Banaras · katan brocade

Brocade, oxblood

Woven in Varanasi on a pit loom, with the gold run through the weft rather than printed on top. Heavy enough to hang without a lining at the body, which is how the old sherwanis were made.

₹9,800 / metre · 3.4 m for a calf-length sherwani

Surat · cotton velvet

Velvet, forest

The winter choice. Cotton velvet, not silk, because silk velvet crushes in a car and a wedding involves a car. Cut with the pile down so it reads almost black at the shoulder and green at the hem.

₹6,400 / metre · 3.6 m for a calf-length sherwani

Lucknow · handloom linen

Linen, sand

For the men who marry in April. A linen sherwani needs a half-canvas and a softer collar or it looks like a kurta that has been promoted; we cut it two inches closer and leave the hem unlined.

₹3,100 / metre · 3.0 m for a calf-length sherwani

04

Eight weeks,
in five parts.

  1. 01

    The measure

    Forty minutes, twenty-two measurements, taken in shirt and trousers. We photograph the shoulder line from behind, because a shoulder either slopes or it does not and no tape will tell you.

    Week 1

  2. 02

    The toile

    A cotton shell, cut to the measurements and stitched roughly. You wear it standing, sitting, and with a hand in your pocket. This is where the armhole is fixed — everything after is cloth.

    Week 3

  3. 03

    The cloth, and the second fitting

    Cloth is bought only after the toile is approved. The chest canvas is padded, the collar cut on the bias, and the garment is tried on with the sleeves basted, not set.

    Week 5

  4. 04

    The work

    Tilla, chikankari, or zardozi, done in the lane by three women who have worked with the shop for twenty years. Buttons, buttonholes, and the inside facing, signed by whoever closed the garment.

    Week 7

  5. 05

    The last fitting

    In daylight, with the shoes you will wear. We check the break of the shoulder, the hang of the hem, and whether you can raise both arms. Then it is pressed, boxed, and you are told to sit carefully in it once before the wedding.

    Week 8
