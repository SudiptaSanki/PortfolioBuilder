Asked: reset nine polki from a damaged 1960s necklace into a choker for a December wedding in Jaipur. Did: built a new gold cup for each stone by hand, keeping their uneven shapes rather than polishing them flat. Outcome: delivered six days before the wedding; the old pendant was reset as a maang tikka to match.

She refused to polish my mother's stones flat to make her own work easier. The choker sits exactly the way the old necklace did.
— Sunita Agarwal, Jaipur, mother of the bride, on the Gulab Polki Choker

The Merchant's Necklace, Restored

Year
2023
Medium
Antique kundan necklace, 31 stones; foil replaced on 7
Turnaround
5 weeks
Price
₹86,000, restoration

Asked: make a ninety-year-old necklace wearable again — eleven stones loose, the foil blackened. Did: photographed and mapped every seat before opening a single cup; replaced foil on seven stones and left four original seats untouched. Outcome: two stones were too fragile to reset; they went home unset, in a lined box, with a written note saying why. I will not set a stone I cannot seat properly because a date is close. A loose polki at a wedding is a lost polki.

  1. 01

    The stone sitting

    We look at your stones together — yours or mine — under the lamp. Stones with weak seats are refused here, not discovered later.

  2. 02

    Drawing and cups

    The piece is drawn at full size and a gold cup is raised for each stone. You approve the drawing before any foil is cut.

  3. 03

    Foil and setting

    Each stone is bedded on gold foil over a lac core and pressed home by hand. This is the slow part: a choker takes nine to twelve days at this stage.

  4. 04

    The back and the finish

    Meenakari enamel on the reverse if you want it, edges burnished, cord and clasp fitted.

  5. 05

    Fitting and delivery

    The piece is tried on in the atelier and adjusted on the spot. You take home the stone map and the care card with it.
