02 · The display log

Displays, frame by frame.

A fireworks photograph on its own proves nothing — anybody can get lucky once in twelve minutes. So the desk publishes whole displays: the brief, the survey, the cue sheet, and the frames in the order they were fired. Three recent commissions, logged.

The ask

One campaign image for Hogmanay 2026 and a complete, legally-shot record of the midnight display — from inside the event safety plan, not from the crowd.

What I did

Surveyed both positions in November, agreed fallout distances with the firing team, and fired to a 64-cue sheet — one frame every ten seconds per position, each logged against its cue as it happened.

The outcome

Frame 044 leads the 2026 campaign. The archive set was delivered in six days, and the desk is held for Hogmanay 2026 — the survey is already booked for November.

▸ Forth Pyro Ltd · Display company

A brochure that had to be honest

The ask

A display company tired of licensing stock fireworks photographs — pictures of other people's shells — wanted its own August harbour display photographed for its 2026 brochure and tender documents.

What I did

Surveyed the pier and the barge line at low tide and high, because the reflection changes with both. One position on the pier end, one remote camera on the harbour wall, fired from the desk by radio. Every frame in the set is their display, their shells, their night.

The outcome

22 frames delivered; the set leads their 2026 brochure and two council tenders. Rebooked for August 2026 before the edit was a week old.

“Tender panels can smell a stock photo. Ours now open with our own shells over our own barge — and Ewan's survey notes are in the safety file, which our insurers noticed before the clients did.”

— Pete Sanderson, Display Director, Forth Pyro Ltd, on the Harbour Lights commission

FRAME 023 · Cue 23 — the reflection frame, from the remote camera on the harbour wall. f/9 · 7 sec. The brochure's back cover.

▸ Private clients · Estate wedding

Six quiet minutes

The ask

A couple marrying at a family estate wanted the surprise display photographed — but the display was low-noise by licence, the house had livestock in the next field, and the one photograph that mattered had to include the house itself.

What I did

One position only, on the lawn axis the estate factor and I paced out in September, lined up so the shells break over the roofline. A short cue sheet — 31 cues in six minutes — and a second, wider frame running for the one print the couple had already chosen a wall for.

The outcome

The 150 cm print hangs in the estate hall. The couple's set — 14 frames — was delivered before they left on honeymoon.

“He was invisible all evening and then produced the exact second the sky opened over the house. Our guests took four hundred phone photos. We framed his one.”

— Hannah & Rob Mercer, on their October 2025 estate wedding display

FRAME 028 · Cue 28 — the closing pair, red over gold. f/8 · 4 sec. The last frame of the night, and the last one in the couple's album.
