▸ City of Edinburgh · Festivals office

Hogmanay 2025 — the castle display

The ask

The city's festivals office needed one image to carry the 2026 Hogmanay campaign, plus a complete record set of the midnight display for the archive — shot legally, from inside the event's own safety plan.

What I did

Walked two positions with the firing team in November — Calton Hill and North Bridge — agreed the fallout distances, and built a 64-cue sheet from the display script. On the night: two cameras, two radios, one frame every ten seconds, each logged against its cue.

The outcome

Frame 044, the midnight salvo, leads the 2026 campaign. 38 frames delivered inside a week; the midnight edition of 12 sold out by February; the desk is held again for Hogmanay 2026.

“He handed us the cue sheet before he showed us a single photograph. Then the photographs were better than the display felt — because he was standing exactly where the shells were aimed to be seen from.”

— Fiona Calder, Festivals & Events Manager, on the Hogmanay 2025 commission

How a display gets photographed

03 · The desk method

STEP 01

The survey

Six weeks out, I walk every candidate position with the firing team, in daylight, with the site plan. Distances are agreed, marked, and written down — I never shoot from inside a fallout zone, and I never guess one.

STEP 02

The cue sheet

The display script becomes a numbered cue sheet: which shell, which second, which lens, how long the shutter stays open. By midnight I am not deciding anything. I am executing a plan.

STEP 03

The night

Two radios — one to the firing desk, one to my second position. Bulb exposure, cable release, one frame every ten seconds. And my phone stays in my pocket: four hundred lit screens in a foreground have ruined more finales than rain ever has.

STEP 04

The log & the print

Every frame is logged against its cue before I sleep. The edit is delivered inside a week; edition prints are made by hand, signed, numbered, and never reprinted once the edition closes.
