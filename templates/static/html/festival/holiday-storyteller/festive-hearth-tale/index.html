Eight nights, one room, and a story told close enough to warm it.

I perform Hanukkah stories for families, classrooms and long-term care homes across Toronto. No screen, no soundtrack, one small table of props and an audience close enough to see the candle burn down.

A story is a room we agree to sit in

Most of the year I teach Grade 3 and 4 in the Toronto District School Board. In December I take three weeks off to tell the same eight stories I heard from my grandmother in Dollard-des-Ormeaux, first in French, then in Yiddish, and finally in the English I could understand.

My sets are built for a real room: a library corner with bad acoustics, a temple social hall with a coffee urn running, a resident's lounge at four in the afternoon. I bring the props, the candle table and a cloth that has been in the family since 1961. Hosts bring the audience and the chairs.
