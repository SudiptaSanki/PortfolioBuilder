The repertoire, and where it has been told.

Ten Hanukkah stories I can tell without a book, six sets built for a specific kind of room, and a short list of the places they have been told since 2008.

Eight rooms in eight nights

In December 2025 I told a different night at a different Toronto Public Library branch for eight consecutive evenings. Each set was 25 minutes, each one ended with a question, and each branch got the night that suited its audience: the Fairview branch asked for the dreidel night because the after-school crowd was eight to eleven; the Palmerston branch, with a mostly adult audience, took the Judith story and stayed forty minutes past the end.

The series taught me something I now build into every December: a host who books all eight nights gets a different holiday than a host who books one. The stories start to talk to each other by the fourth night, and the room starts finishing the blessings.

The library asked for the cue sheets in advance so branch staff could set up tables. That is why the builder on the home page exists.

Outside December

Three sets that are not Hanukkah, for hosts who ask what else is in the crate.

Megillah, out loud

A Purim telling for family rooms, built around the four noises the audience makes and the one line everyone waits for. I bring groggers, a crown that does not fit anyone, and a scroll I can unroll without dropping.

The seder as a story

A 20-minute telling for families who want the youngest at the table to have something to hold on to before the reading starts. Told before the meal, never instead of the seder.
