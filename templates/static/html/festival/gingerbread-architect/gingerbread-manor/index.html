The problem with gingerbread houses

Most of them are a bag of walls leaned against each other and glued with icing that never sets hard enough. They stand for a photograph and collapse by the second week. The fix is not more icing. It is the same fix as a real building: thickness, bracing, and letting the walls dry before the roof goes on.

Wall thickness
8 mm for anything over 30 cm tall. 6 mm is a biscuit, not a wall.
Dough
Rye and wheat, low butter, baked hard and flat. A soft dough moves under its own weight.
Mortar
Royal icing at 8 parts sugar to 1 part egg white, whipped until it holds a peak and stands a minute before you place the panel.
Bracing
Interior corner gussets in spare dough, and a full interior wall on anything wider than 30 cm.
Drying
Six hours per joint, minimum. The roof goes on the next day, never the same afternoon.
Base
A board thicker than the walls and wider than the eaves. If the base flexes, the house cracks.
