The year · 2026

Six kitchens,
one shed.

The Sunday annadanam is the spine, but not the whole body. In a bad December the same crew runs a flood kitchen; on Pongal the street itself becomes the dining hall; on school days a smaller pot goes out at seven. Open any line below to read what it took.

Every Sunday Annadanam, Kutchery Lane3:40 am · Mylapore 1,014

The ordinary Sunday: sambar rice, poriyal, curd, and a sweet if the sweet fund is healthy. Forty-six volunteers, three gas cylinders, ninety kilos of ponni rice. The queue starts forming at six, though the first ladle is not until a quarter to seven — that gap is filled with coffee and, increasingly, with people who come early just to talk.

Dec 2023 Flood kitchen, ThoothukudiNine days · temporary site 12,400

After the December floods we loaded the urli, two gas stoves, and eleven volunteers into a rented van and drove south. The kitchen ran out of a marriage hall with no running water for the first two days; we bought cans and cooked rice in the hall’s own vessels. Twelve thousand four hundred plates in nine days, plus 400 packets sent up the flooded lanes by boat.

The lesson we brought home: a kitchen that travels needs its ledger more than its recipes. We now keep a laminated cost sheet and a packing list in the van at all times.

  • SiteMarriage hall, Thoothukudi
  • Crew11, rotating
  • Cost₹9.20 a plate
Weekdays since 2024 School breakfast, Mylapore7:10 am · 60 children 11,700

A corporation school two lanes from the shed had children arriving without breakfast. We cook a smaller pot on weekday mornings — upma, idli, or tomato rice — and two volunteers carry it over in insulated boxes before the first bell. Sixty children, five days a week, term time only.

The headmistress keeps the attendance register; we keep the plate count. In the first year, attendance on Mondays rose by nine children. That number is the whole argument for this kitchen.

  • Served7:10 am
  • Children60
  • Cost₹9 a plate
14–16 Jan Pongal street kitchenThree days · tank steps 2,480

For Pongal the kitchen moves outside and the street becomes the dining hall. Sakkarai pongal is cooked in the urli after the rice, in three batches, and served on banana leaf with a spoon of ghee on top. The first pot is offered at the temple, the second goes to the queue, and the third is kept for whoever is still standing at ten.

This is the one weekend a year when we accept help from people who have never cooked before. They are put on serving, never on salt.

Since 2022 Wedding surplus runSaturdays · by arrangement 6,900

Chennai weddings cook for a number that is always a little more than the guests. Since 2022 we collect untouched surplus from three caterers who call us on Saturday night, and it goes to two shelters and the night queue at the shed. Nothing that has been on a plate is accepted; only what is still in the vessel.

The arrangement is simple and written down: the caterer calls by nine, Arjun collects in the van by ten, the food is served by eleven, and the vessels go back washed by Sunday noon. Six thousand nine hundred plates last year, and not one rupee spent on food.

  • Caterers3
  • CollectionSaturday, 9 pm
  • CostFuel only
Nov – Feb Night shelter meals9:30 pm · 40 plates 1,760

In the cool months a smaller pot goes out at half past nine to the night shelter behind the market: forty plates, usually rice and a thick dal, carried in the same insulated boxes the school uses in the morning. It is the quietest kitchen we run, and the one where a warm plate matters most.

Two volunteers do it, on a rota, and they are usually the same two. We would like more; the walk back at ten is not everyone’s idea of a Saturday.

  • Served9:30 pm
  • Plates40 a night
  • Crew2, rota
