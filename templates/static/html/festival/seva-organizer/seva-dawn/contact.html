What to bring

Closed shoes, a hair tie if you have long hair, and a bottle of water. We provide an apron and a cloth. Please do not bring food to give away — the kitchen has a rule about that, and it is a good one.

If you would rather give

₹14 is a plate. A Sunday costs about ₹12,600 to run, and the school breakfast ₹540 a day. Bank details for transfers, and 80G receipts above ₹1,000, are sent on request — write to accounts@dawnlangar.in with your PAN.

Before you come

Honest answers

I cannot cook at all. Is that a problem?

No. Most of the work is not cooking: it is carrying, cutting, counting, packing, washing, and talking to people in the queue. If you are put on the sambar you have either asked for it or been coming for three years.

What time do I actually have to arrive?

Four in the morning for cutting and setup, five-thirty for the second shift, six-fifteen if you want to serve. Many first-timers come at six and stay until nine. Tell us which and we will not expect you earlier.

Can I bring my children?

Yes, from about eight years old, with a parent. They are usually put on packing spoons or handing out buttermilk, away from the fire and the knives. There are chairs by the window, and the morning is over before it gets hot.

What if I sign up and cannot come?

Tell us by Friday night if you can. If it is Sunday at four and you are unwell, stay home and message after eight. We plan for two or three no-shows every week; it is not a crisis, but the onions do not cut themselves.

Do you take groups or corporate volunteering?

One group of up to ten a month, on the second Sunday, and we ask that the group stays for the whole morning rather than an hour of photographs. Write with the date and number; we will confirm and send the ground rules.

Is the food vegetarian?

Always. No egg, no onion or garlic on festival days, and no food from outside is served. The kitchen is attached to the temple trust and the rule is older than any of us.
