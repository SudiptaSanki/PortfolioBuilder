The Performance Record

Evenings I have danced

Two hundred and fourteen performances in nineteen years. These four stand for the rest: a temple festival, a college stage, a wedding eve, and a debut I presented. Each is told the way organisers ask about it — what was wanted, what I did, and what came of it.

Thrissur Pooram · April 2026Vadakkunnathan temple groundsFull margam · 93 min + intervalAudience ~3,000

The closing item at the Pooram

The askThe Pooram cultural committee wanted a full margam to close the festival's cultural programme — the quiet item after a day of melam and panchavadyam, when the grounds are ringed with lit lamps and nobody is in a mood to be hurried.

What I didAll six items, in order, with a fifteen-minute interval after the varnam. When the programme slipped forty minutes late I refused the suggestion to trim the padam; we started late and finished late, whole.

The outcomeThe crowd stayed past midnight. The committee wrote the same closing slot into next year's programme before this year's lamps were out — the only item they have ever renewed on the night itself.

The musiciansVocal, mridangam, edakka and veena — my regular four. Temple stages take no recorded music from me; the Pooram least of all.

"The grounds were loud all day. She made three thousand people quiet with one raised eyebrow — that is the Pooram performance we will measure the others against."

— K. R. Venugopal, programme convener, Thrissur Pooram cultural committee

Palakkad · September 2025College cultural festivalShort programme · 49 minAudience ~600 students

A first margam for six hundred students

The askA college arts festival wanted classical dance on a bill of film songs and fashion walks — fifty minutes, an audience of students who had mostly never seen Mohiniyattam, and a union secretary honest enough to say so.

What I didCholkettu, varnam and tillana — and before each item, ninety seconds of spoken introduction: what to watch for, whose story it is, where the joke lands. No item cut, nothing dumbed down; only the map was handed out before the journey.

The outcomeThe hall stayed. The varnam's second verse got the silence you cannot rehearse, and the college has since started a classical arts circle that invites one traditional performer every term. Four of my present students first saw the dance that evening — as audience.

The lessonYoung audiences do not dislike classical dance. They dislike being locked out of it. Ninety seconds of welcome is not dilution; it is hospitality.

"We booked her expecting a polite half-empty hall. Students were sitting in the aisles by the varnam. Our arts circle exists because of that evening."

— Fathima Noor, general secretary, college students' union, Palakkad

Kochi · November 2025Wedding eve · Private functionShort programme · 39 minAudience ~250 guests

The forty-minute wedding eve

The askA bride who had learned dance as a child wanted Mohiniyattam at her wedding eve in Kochi. The hall's changeover gave us forty minutes exactly, with the dinner service queued behind us.

What I didDropped three items rather than shorten any: cholkettu, padam, tillana — 39 minutes. We rehearsed the programme against a stopwatch twice, musicians included, so the last salutation landed with a minute to spare.

The outcomeFinished in the slot. The bride's grandmother, a dancer in her youth, asked for the padam's last verse again as an encore — the one time I have ever repeated an item, and I would do it again for her.

Note for organisersThis is what the planner on the home page is for. Give me your true slot and I will give you a programme that fits it whole — never a long programme squeezed.

Irinjalakuda · February 2026Arangettam presentationFull margam · Student debutAudience ~200

Presenting my student's arangettam

The askDevika had studied with me for six years and was ready for her arangettam — the debut that presents a student as a solo dancer. Her family wanted it traditional and modest: no banners, no chief-guest speeches, the dance first.

What I didTwo years of arangettam preparation behind one evening: full margam, my own musicians, myself compering — introducing each item, naming its ragam, and telling the audience what a debut means before she began.

The outcomeShe danced the whole margam without a falter, and the hall rose for the tillana. Devika now assists my Saturday beginner class. Eleven of my students have made this crossing; each one is the performance I am proudest of that year.

If you are a parentAn arangettam is priced from ₹60,000 all-in — musicians, rehearsal weeks and the evening itself. It is a serious sum and a serious day; both should be planned two years out, not two months.

"She did not just teach my daughter to dance. She stood beside her on the stage of her debut and gave her away to the audience like a bride. We could not have asked for a better guru."

— Smt. Rema Devi, mother of the debutante, Irinjalakuda
