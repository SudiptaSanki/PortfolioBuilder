Three builds, weighed and signed.

Each of these went over the public weighbridge at Cabo and came back with a ticket. The weight on the drawing is the weight in the parade, not the weight we hoped for in September.

Boneco carrier 07

Two giants on a standard 7.5 tonne chassis for the Bloco Carnavalesco Pitombeira. The brief asked for the giants to face opposite sides of the street so the bloco could turn a corner without the float turning with it. That meant two truss towers, two pivot rigs and a deck long enough to keep the towers apart.

The heads were carved from a single billet each, hollowed to 30 mm and skinned in silk, painted with casein so the colour stays matte under the parade lights. The rear giant is 3.6 metres; the front one is 3.9 to look level from the pavement, which is the kind of thing nobody notices until it is wrong.

Reef float

A municipal float for the Olinda carnival opening, built around the theme of the reef and the mangroves. One giant, a shoal of smaller fish on wire, and a deck that had to carry a school band of fourteen children with instruments.

The constraint was the route, not the sea: three low branches on the hill up to the Alto da Sé. The float was designed at 6.1 metres so it could pass under them without pruning a tree the city has protected since 1998.

Trio float with sound deck

A float built for a bloco that wanted the band on the move. The deck carries a sound system, three musicians, a generator and a 4.2 metre giant at the front. The engineering problem was the generator: 340 kilograms in one corner, which meant ballast on the opposite corner and a revised axle load.

We ran the sound on its own circuit, fused separately, with the generator in a ventilated box that could be reached from the deck without climbing over the speakers. The fire brigade signed it on the second inspection, which is one better than usual.
