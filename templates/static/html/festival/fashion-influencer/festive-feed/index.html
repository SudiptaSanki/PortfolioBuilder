The look planner

Start with the budget.
Then the clothes.

Most festive styling starts with a photograph and ends with a bill. This starts with the bill. Pick the occasion and the money you actually have; the planner returns a three-piece look, the palette it lives in, and what is left over for the tailor.

Occasion
Budget

From ₹8,000 to ₹1,50,000. The planner spends on the outfit first, then the jewellery you will keep.

Mood

Prices are Hyderabad retail in 2026, before alterations. The planner never spends more than the budget; if the three pieces do not fit, it tells you what to drop.

Diwali · 4 min read

What ₹25,000 actually buys in Hyderabad

One organza saree, one blouse tailored twice, a pair of jhumkas from a shop near Koti, and juttis you can walk in. The saree was ₹12,400; the blouse cost ₹1,800 including the second fitting.

Wedding season · 6 min read

The lehenga that survived three fittings

A Banarasi skirt bought at a trunk show, altered twice at the waist and once at the hem. The trick was refusing the blouse the shop wanted to sell and having one cut from the same silk instead.

Jewellery · 3 min read

Buy the jhumkas, rent the necklace

Kundan necklaces photograph beautifully and sit in a locker for eleven months. A rented set for ₹3,500 and a pair of jhumkas you own is the arithmetic I recommend to almost every client.

Getting ready · 2 min read

The forty-minute getting-ready plan

Hair first, then jewellery, then the blouse, then the skirt, then the dupatta last. Photograph the pinned dupatta before you leave the house, because it will not survive the car.
