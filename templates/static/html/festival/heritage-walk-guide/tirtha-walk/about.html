The guide

I was born in the lane behind the temple, and I still get lost in it.

My name is Shalini Mishra. I grew up in a house off Vishwanath Gali with a courtyard, a hand pump and eleven other people. I became a licensed guide in 2004 because a French couple asked me the way to the river and then asked me eleven more questions, and I found that I liked answering them.

Why eight

Ten years ago I took a group of nineteen along the ghats. It worked, in the sense that nobody was lost, but it did not work as a walk. In a lane two shoulders wide, the last person hears a different city from the first. A story told to nineteen people has to be simplified; a story told to eight can change depending on who is standing in front of it.

So the group is capped at eight. It costs more per person and it is the only way I know to walk the old city without turning it into a parade. On the craft visits the cap is four, because a pit loom room is small and the weaver is working.

The other rule is that I do not carry a flag, a microphone or a folder of photographs. The city is the document. If a door is open and the family is outside, we might be invited in; if it is closed, we walk past it.

What I will not do

A few things people ask for that I decline, and the reason each time.

Photograph a cremation

Not from the boat, not from the platform, not with a long lens from the other bank. At Manikarnika we stand on the upper path and we do not raise a camera. This is the one rule with no exceptions.

Take you into a house uninvited

Some guides have arrangements with families. I do not. If a door is open and someone says come in, we go in; otherwise the courtyard stays private.

Guarantee the aarti view

The evening aarti at Dashashwamedh is crowded, and the front row is not mine to sell. We watch from the steps or from a boat, whichever the group prefers, and we arrive early enough to stand.

Walk in the afternoon heat of May

Between May and June the walks start at five and end by eight. A three-hour walk at two in the afternoon is not brave, it is unkind to everyone on it.

03

Practical things

What to wear, what to carry, and what the walk costs.

  • Clothing

    Covered shoulders and knees. Loose cotton is better than technical fabric; nothing dries faster in Varanasi humidity. Shoes that slip off easily, because they will come off at two temples.

  • Carry

    Water, a cloth for the dust, and small notes. Leave the tripod behind; it changes how people react to you and there is nowhere to put it down in a lane.

  • Cost

    ₹1,800 per person for the two-and-a-half-hour walk, ₹2,600 for the craft walk with the weavers. Children under ten free. The fee includes chai and the boat crossing where the route needs one.

  • Access

    The ghat route is stone steps and uneven in places. Tell me in advance if anyone in the group cannot manage forty steps; there is a flatter version of every route, and one walk is entirely on the upper path.

Walk with me

Mornings except Monday, and evenings in the cooler months. Tell me the date and what you want from the city.
