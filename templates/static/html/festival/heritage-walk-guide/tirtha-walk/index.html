The city is a set of steps. You have to walk them in the right order.

Varanasi is not arranged for a visitor. The lanes are three feet wide, the temple is behind a shop, and the best view of the river is from a flight of stairs that looks like a place to sit. A walk is a sequence: which ghat first, where to stop, when to be silent because a family is carrying someone down to the water.

    02

    How a morning runs

    A walk is built around the river's own schedule. The timings below are the ones that work in November; in June everything moves an hour earlier.

    1. 05:40

      Meeting point

      By the wooden boat landing at Assi, not the main steps. There is a chai stall; I am the person with the canvas bag and no clipboard.

    2. 05:55

      The first light

      We walk north on the upper path while the river turns from grey to copper. This is the only hour when the ghats are quiet enough to hear the temple bells separately.

    3. 06:35

      Bathers and wrestlers

      At Tulsi Ghat the akhara is already working. We stop at a distance; the wrestlers are not an attraction and a camera changes the session.

    4. 07:15

      Chai, then the lanes

      A ten-minute stop at a shop that has been boiling milk since four. Then into Vishwanath Gali, single file, past the brass sellers.

    5. 08:05

      The last stop

      Manikarnika, from the upper platform only. No photographs, no commentary unless the group asks. We finish there and walk back together.

    03

    Walking in someone's front room

    The lanes of Varanasi are not a heritage zone; they are where people live. A doorway with a chalk mark is a household, and a courtyard with a cow in it is a working courtyard. Six rules keep a walk welcome:

    1. Walk single file in the galis and step into a doorway to let a scooter pass.
    2. Ask before photographing anyone. A nod is not a yes.
    3. No photographs at Manikarnika or Harishchandra, at all, from any distance.
    4. Remove shoes at the temple, and cover shoulders and knees.
    5. Do not give money to children; it keeps them out of school.
    6. If a procession comes, stand aside and wait. It will not be long.
