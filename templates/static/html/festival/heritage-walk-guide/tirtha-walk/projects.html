Six routes

Six walks, and the hour each one belongs to.

A route is not just a line on a map; it is a time of day. The ghats are one city at five in the morning and another at six in the evening. Each walk below is written for the hour when it makes sense.

6 routes

  • 01

    Ghats at first light

    05:45 · 3.4 km · 2 hr 30 · ₹1,800

    The classic. Assi to Manikarnika on the upper path while the river turns from grey to copper, with a stop at the Tulsi akhara and chai in the lanes. Ends before the heat and before the crowds.

    DawnSteps
  • 02

    Temple and the lanes

    07:00 · 1.9 km · 2 hr · ₹1,800

    Vishwanath Gali before the shops open, the brass sellers setting out their trays, and the small temples that sit inside ordinary doorways. Ends at Man Mandir with the observatory instruments explained.

    MorningLanes
  • 03

    Silk and brass

    09:30 · 2.2 km · 3 hr · ₹2,600

    Madanpura, where the pit looms are, and the brass workshops behind Thatheri Bazaar. Four people maximum, because a loom room holds four. One family has been weaving here since 1958.

    MorningCraft
  • 04

    The morning market

    06:30 · 1.6 km · 1 hr 45 · ₹1,800

    Kachori Gali at its busiest: vegetables, flowers, jalebi fried to order, tea boiled with cardamom. Three tastings included, and a stop at the flower market under the bridge.

    DawnFood
  • 05

    The aarti, from the steps

    17:30 · 2.4 km · 2 hr 15 · ₹1,800

    Dashashwamedh in the hour before the lamps are lit, with the seven priests and the brass lamps explained before the crowd arrives. We watch from the steps, not the water, unless the group books a boat.

    EveningRitual
  • 06

    Sarnath at opening

    06:00 · 4.2 km · 4 hr · ₹3,200

    A car to Sarnath, then the deer park, the Dhamek stupa and the museum at opening time, before the coach groups. The walk back along the Varuna is optional and usually declined in June.

    DawnCar

All routes start and end within a ten-minute walk of Assi Ghat unless noted. Prices are per person and include chai, tastings and any boat crossing on the route.

02

A route is a sequence, not a list

The order of stops matters more than the number. A group that climbs to Man Mandir before the lane walk arrives at the observatory fresh and reads the instruments properly; the same group at the end of three hours reads a staircase.

So every route has a shape: one climb, one long flat stretch, one place to sit. The sitting place is not a rest stop; it is where the conversation usually turns from what the city looks like to what it is like to live in it, and that is the part people remember.
