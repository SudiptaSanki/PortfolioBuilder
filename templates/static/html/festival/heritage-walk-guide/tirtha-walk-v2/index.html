Heritage walks · Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh

Varanasi, read at walking pace.

I am Nikhil Bhatt, a licensed heritage walk guide. I lead five walks through the ghats, lanes and weavers' quarters of my own city — and on Tuesday 24 November 2026, one festival walk for Dev Deepawali, the night the gods are said to come down to bathe in the Ganga and a million lamps are lit along the steps.

Everything I run is in the index below — route, distance, start time, group cap and price. There is no sixth walk hidden behind a phone call. I don't hand out audio-guide wands, either. A wand talks at the city through one ear while the city happens in the other. On my walks you get a person who stops, points, waits for the aarti bell, and answers back.

Before you come

House rules, stated plainly

These are not fine print. They are how the walks stay welcome in a living, worshipping city.

  • We start when the index says

    The dawn walk leaves at 05:30 because the city is honest before the heat and the crowds. Arrive at 05:45 and you will see our backs. I wait five minutes, no more — the aarti does not wait at all.

  • Shoes off, shoulders covered

    At temple thresholds and in weavers' homes we leave shoes at the door and cover shoulders. I carry two spare cotton wraps; better to bring your own.

  • Photography has a hard line

    Ask before photographing a person, always. At Manikarnika and Harishchandra — the cremation ghats — no photography at all, and no lingering. We pass quietly and I will ask you to put the phone away before we arrive, not after.

  • The sanctum is not a viewpoint

    Entry to the Kashi Vishwanath sanctum is for Hindu worshippers; that is the temple's rule and I keep it without argument. Walk 2 uses the corridor viewpoint, from where the temple and its story are fully visible.

  • No shopping stops, no commissions

    If a weaver's family shows you silk, it is because their work is the walk — buy or don't, at the price they name. Nothing you spend anywhere on my route pays me anything.

  • Groups stay capped

    The caps in the index are set by the lanes, not by my diary. A group of 14 cannot hear a guide in a two-metre gali, and a guide who takes 14 anyway is selling you a walk he cannot give.
