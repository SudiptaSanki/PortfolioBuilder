Route sheet

Design starts with the street, not the drawing.

Before a single sheet of ply is cut I walk the route with the organisers, at the hour of the march, and measure four things: the narrowest carriageway, the lowest overhead, the sharpest turn and the place a float can be abandoned if it dies. These are the three routes I know by heart.

  1. km 0.0

    Azad Maidan, assembly

    Seven gates, one wide enough for a trailer. Floats reverse in from the Mahapalika Marg side between 06:40 and 07:20, before the crowd fills the maidan. Ground is level tarmac; the water point is 30 m east of gate 4.

    7.4 m carriageway4.5 m canopylevel

  2. km 1.4

    Metro junction, the 90° turn

    The one place a long trailer fails. The corner radius is 6.2 m, so anything past 8.5 m has to be walked round by hand with the tow released, or built in two halves and pinned on site. I keep a hinge kit in the truck for exactly this.

    6.2 m radiustow release8 walkers

  3. km 2.6

    Girgaon Chowpatty, dispersal

    Sand. A loaded trailer sinks to the axle within four metres, so the last stretch is on foot: costumes, handheld props, one small cart with the sound. The float waits on the road with two people and the fire extinguisher.

What the shop does

Four things, done properly

01

Floats

Plywood deck, bamboo crown, sewn vinyl, tractor paint. Built to be repaired at the roadside with a staple gun and a roll of gaffer, because that is where repairs happen.

6–9 build days · 4–24 riders

02

Walking contingents

Choreography for 18 to 300 people who have never marched together: spacing, a signal whistle, two people at the back who know when to close the gap. Costume patterns that survive 34 °C.

3 rehearsals · patterns included

03

Staging and sound

One stage, one generator, and a sound plan that keeps the speeches audible over the march arriving. I bring the dB meter and I write the quiet-hour schedule into the permit.

2 generators · 96 dB cap

04

Route and permissions

The unglamorous part: police letter, traffic branch, fire officer, municipal ward. A route pack with measurements, a tow plan, and the name and number of who signs at each desk.

From 11 weeks out

From brief to boulevard

Nine weeks, give or take a monsoon

Small-town first marches run shorter: a hand-pulled cart and forty people can be ready in three weeks if the permissions desk is kind. Anything with a tractor needs the full nine.

How the shop works
  1. Week 1

    Walk the route

    With you and the traffic officer, at march hour. Photograph every overhead, measure every turn, find the abandonment point.

  2. Weeks 2–3

    Draw to the street

    Elevation, plan and a section through the deck. You see the float at 1:60 before any money is spent on ply.

  3. Weeks 4–6

    Build in Lalbaug

    The mandal workshop is empty after Ganpati visarjan, which is why the rates work. Frame, deck, crown, cloth, paint.

  4. Week 7

    Trial assembly

    Full build on the maidan, once, at night. Every float I have built has needed one thing moved after this night.

  5. Week 9

    March, then strike

    I ride the tow vehicle with the toolbox and the fire extinguisher. Strike happens the same night; the ply goes back to the mandal.
