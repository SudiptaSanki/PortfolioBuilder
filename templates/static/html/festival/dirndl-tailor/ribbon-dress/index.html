Schnitt 01The fitting

Four measurements do most of the work

A dirndl is a fitted bodice on a gathered skirt, and each half is drafted from a different measurement. Get these four right and the rest is taste.

01

Bust

Around the fullest part, tape level at the back, in the bra you will wear. This sets the size and the front panel width.

02

Waist

At the natural waist, not at the top of the jeans. The bodice is tight here; it is the measurement that decides whether the lacing works.

03

Hip

Around the widest point, about 20 cm below the waist. The skirt is gathered to twice the hip, so this decides the panel count.

04

Height

Without shoes. It sets the waist position on the pattern and the skirt length before we shorten anything.

What happens in the atelier

The first appointment is half measuring, half conversation: what the dirndl is for, whether it will be worn at a festival or a wedding, and how you feel about your arms. There is no wrong answer, but there is a wrong bodice.

The pattern is drafted from your measurements onto 60 g pattern paper and cut in calico. At the second fitting we pin the calico on you and move the seams with chalk. The bodice is the hard part — the skirt is generous and forgiving — so most of the tweaking happens above the waist.

A good dirndl does not make you stand differently. It lets you stand the way you already do.

Then the cloth is cut, with 1.5 cm seam allowances and 3 cm at the side seams, because bodies change and a dirndl should be alterable. The apron is cut last, after the skirt hangs for a day, so the hem is level.

Schnitt 04The order

From first measurement to the last hook

Most dirndls take six weeks. A wedding dirndl in silk takes eight, and in July everything takes longer.

  1. Measurement, 45 minutes

    Bust, waist, hip, height, plus back length and arm. We talk about the occasion and look at cloth in daylight.

  2. Pattern and calico, 2 weeks

    The pattern is drafted to your numbers and cut in calico. You try it on; I pin and chalk. No cloth is cut before this fitting.

  3. Cloth, 1 week

    You approve the cloth and the apron. If it is silk, we cut a sample bodice first, because silk behaves differently at every seam.

  4. Making, 3 weeks

    Bodice, lining, lacing, skirt panels, apron. The skirt hangs for a day before the hem is marked on you, standing, in shoes.

  5. Final fitting, 30 minutes

    Hooks, lacing, apron ties, hem. The blouse is fitted last because it sits under the bodice and changes the line.

What is not in the price

The blouse, unless you order one; the shoes; the jewellery. I do not sell either, and I will tell you if a chain you already own will do.

What is

Cloth, lining, ribbon, hooks, thread, three fittings, and alterations for a year if your body changes by a size — which happens, and is not a failure of the dirndl.
