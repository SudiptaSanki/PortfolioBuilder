Five dirndls, and the fitting that changed each one

A commission is a conversation with cloth. Here is what was asked for, what the calico showed, and what was finally cut. Measurements are given as they were taken.

Auftrag 214Wedding · June 2025

A blue silk dirndl, and a bodice that had to work sitting down

The bride wanted blue silk, a high neck, and a skirt to the floor. She was marrying in a church with no aisle to speak of and a two-hour dinner afterwards, which meant the bodice had to be comfortable seated — the thing most wedding dirndls are not.

The calico fitting showed the problem before the silk was cut: with the waist seam where she wanted it, the bodice rode up two centimetres when she sat. We moved the seam down, lengthened the back by three, and cut the skirt with a hidden pleat at the centre back for the chair. The silk was cut only after the second calico.

Eight weeks, three fittings, and one small alteration a month later when she had lost weight from the stress of the wedding, which is normal and which the side seams were cut to allow.

Cloth
Blue silk dupion, 4.2 m, cotton lining
Measurements
Bust 94, waist 76, hip 101, height 171
Fittings
Three, the first in calico

8 weeks

The bride

“She made me sit down in the calico. Twice. I thought she was being fussy; she was the only person in the whole wedding who thought about the dinner.”

Auftrag 189Rebuild · November 2024

A grandmother's bodice, rebuilt onto new cloth

A bodice from 1958, green wool, with hand-worked eyelets and a lining that had gone to dust. The granddaughter could not wear it — two sizes too small and cut for a shorter back — but did not want it turned into a cushion either.

I unpicked it on the table over two evenings, photographed every seam, and kept the original hooks, eyelets and the maker's label. The pattern was traced from the pieces, then altered to her measurements. New wool was dyed to match the original under the arm, where the colour had been protected from light for sixty years.

The old bodice is now a labelled study piece in a box, with the photographs and the story. The new one is worn. Both are in the family, which is the point.

Original
Green wool bodice, Rosenheim, 1958
Kept
Hooks, eyelets, maker's label, original pattern
New
Wool dyed to the unworn shade, cotton lining

5 weeks

The granddaughter

“I expected to be told it was impossible. Instead I got two dirndls: one I can wear and one I can show my daughter.”

Auftrag 203Birthday · February 2025

A high-neck dirndl in wool for a seventieth

A customer of twenty years wanted a dirndl for her seventieth birthday lunch, in February, in a cold village hall. Wool, high neck, long sleeves under the blouse, and a skirt she could sit in for four hours without it creasing into a fan.

The cloth was the easy part: a light grey-green wool from a mill in Austria, which presses beautifully and does not shine. The hard part was the neckline. A high neck on a body that has softened with age needs a shaped collar stand, not a straight band, or it gapes at the front when the wearer looks down at her plate.

We drafted the collar three times in calico. The third one sits flat. She wore it for six hours, ate everything, and reported that the bodice did not need adjusting once.

Light wool, Austria, 3.8 m

Auftrag 176Pair · August 2024

Mother and daughter, matching but not identical

A mother and her eight-year-old wanted the same dirndl for a family wedding. The same print, the same apron colour — but a child's body is not a small woman's body, and scaling an adult bodice down produces a garment that pulls at the armhole and rides up when she runs, which she will.

So the child's dirndl got its own draft: a higher waist, a wider armhole, a gathered bodice instead of a fitted one, and an apron that ties at the side rather than the back so she can do it herself. From two metres away they read as a pair. Up close, the seams are entirely different.

Auftrag 158Stage · October 2023

Twelve dirndls for a stage production

A theatre in Munich staged a Bavarian folk play with a cast of twelve and a quick-change scene in the second act. The dirndls had to look period from the front row, survive eight shows a week, and come off in under forty seconds.

I cut them in a sturdy cotton that reads as wool under stage light, with hook-and-loop tape under the lacing and a hidden zip in the side seam. The aprons were pinned rather than tied, and each bodice had the actor's name in the lining. The whole run took nine weeks, and I went to the première to watch the quick change, which was the real test.

Quantity
12 dirndls, 6 blouses, 12 aprons
Cloth
Heavy cotton, 38 m, machine washable
Design
Period silhouette, hidden side zip, taped lacing
Time
9 weeks
Price
€6,400 for the set

The costume designer

“Eight shows a week and not one seam failed. The name in the lining saved us in the dark.”
