01The practice

Three moves, repeated slowly

Ayurveda is often sold as a mood. In a treatment room it is closer to joinery: a sequence of measurable steps, each one preparing the body for the next. The old texts name six upakrama, six therapeutic measures. Snehana and svedana — oiling and sweating — are two of them, and almost everything I do begins there.

i. Snehana

Warm oil, applied in long strokes along the direction of the hair and the muscle, then short circles at the joints. Twenty minutes of it, at 38–40 °C, is enough to soften the superficial tissues; less than twelve and the body simply registers a stranger’s hands.

ii. Svedana

Steam, in a wooden box, from the shoulders down and for no longer than it takes the skin to turn pink. Svedana moves what snehana loosened. It is the step most often skipped by studios in a hurry, and the one most often missed by a body that has been carrying a knot for years.

iii. Rest

A hot bath, a cotton wrap, twenty minutes with nothing to do. The treatment is not over when the oil comes off. It is over when the pulse has come back down to the rate it had before you walked in.

    03The ninety minutes

    What actually happens in the room

    1. 20 min

      Nadi pariksha and talk

      Pulse at three positions, tongue, eyes, nails, sleep, digestion, work. I would rather hear about your commute than your symptoms: a body that sits for nine hours needs a different oil from one that lifts boxes.

    2. 35 min

      Abhyanga

      Seven strokes, head to foot, on a wooden droni. The first four are broad and warming; the last three are small and specific. Pressure is yours to set with a word or a raised hand.

    3. 15 min

      Svedana

      Steam from the neck down, with a cold cloth folded on the forehead. If you have never had it before I stay in the room and talk you through the first three minutes.

    4. 20 min

      Bath and rest

      Warm water, a green-gram paste for the oil, then a cotton wrap and a cup of jeera water in the courtyard. No phone in the room; the ceiling fan will dry your hair eventually.

    Bring nothing but yourself and the name of any medicine you take. Leave with oil still in your hair, an hour of quiet, and a written routine. Come back in three weeks, not three days.
