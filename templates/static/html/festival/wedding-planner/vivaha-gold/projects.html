Selected weddings · 2019–2026

Six weddings,
with the numbers left in

Guest counts, halls and money, because those are the parts other planners leave out of the photographs. Each of these has one line about what nearly went wrong — that is usually the more useful story.

W-41

Priya & Karthik Santhome, Chennai

320 guests · four-pillar mandapam · February 2025

A doctor's daughter and an architect, both from families that had already built two houses each and did not intend to be told how to build a wedding. We kept the ceremony inside the family's own agraharam and put the reception in a hall four streets away, which meant moving 320 people at 11 a.m. in February heat.

What made it work was a convoy of six cars with a printed route card on each dashboard, and a second sadhya sitting at the reception hall so nobody arrived to an empty leaf. The oonjal was borrowed from the bride's grandmother's house and re-strung with fresh rope the day before.

W-38

Meera & Senthil Madurai, near the temple

610 guests · temple mantapam · August 2024

The largest wedding I have run in Madurai, held in a leased mantapam with stone pillars that could not take a single nail. Every garland had to be tied with rope to the pillar bases and every light run along the floor in covered channels. The family wanted the muhurtham at the temple itself, so we did the kanyadaanam there and the sadhya in the mantapam.

Six hundred and ten guests, two hundred of them arriving from Theni on four buses. We fed the bus drivers first, which is the only reason the return buses left on time.

W-36

Lakshmi & Arun Coimbatore, RS Puram

180 guests · open-air pandal · December 2024

A small wedding with a large opinion about flowers. The bride's mother wanted only jasmine and only from a farm she had used for thirty years; the farm could deliver two hundred metres, not four. We built the mandapam decor around what could be delivered — dense jasmine on the four pillars, and marigold garlands only at the entrance and the swing, which is where the photographs happen anyway.

Open-air pandal in December in Coimbatore is a risk and we took it. The rain held until the last leaf was cleared, then came down for two hours while we loaded the truck.

W-33

Divya & Rohan Jayanagar, Bengaluru

420 guests · kalyana mandapam · June 2024

A Tamil-Maharashtrian wedding where the two families had a genuine argument about the order of the morning, settled by adding a forty-minute segment rather than removing anyone's ritual. We printed the run sheet in two languages with a column for each family's expectations, which sounds bureaucratic and was in fact the thing that kept the peace.

The hall's kitchen could only hold one sadhya service at a time, so we ran two sittings with a forty-minute gap and a separate coffee and fruit room for the first sitting's overflow. It worked because the seating was pre-assigned by family group, not by who arrived first.

W-30

Anitha & Vignesh Mylapore, at home

96 guests · house wedding · April 2023

The smallest wedding I have planned, and the hardest logistically: a house on a lane too narrow for a lorry, a courtyard that holds sixty chairs if nobody breathes, and a grandmother who would not leave her own home for her granddaughter's wedding. Everything came in by handcart or on two-wheelers.

We borrowed the neighbour's courtyard for the sadhya, hung a single row of banana stems across the lane, and put the mandapam where the car normally parks. Ninety-six guests, twenty-two items, and the best payasam I have tasted at a wedding.

W-28

Kavya & Naveen Adyar, Chennai

540 guests · hotel ballroom · November 2022

The one hotel wedding in this list. A ballroom cannot be a mandapam — the ceiling is too low for a toran, the carpet is too loud for a homa, and the hotel will not let you bring a fire inside without three permits. I told the family that in the first meeting; they booked it anyway, and we designed around it with a raised platform, a portable homa in a copper vessel approved by the priest, and flowers brought down low rather than hung.

It was a beautiful wedding and not a traditional one, and the family knew the difference. The reception the same evening is why they chose it, and I would make the same trade again.
