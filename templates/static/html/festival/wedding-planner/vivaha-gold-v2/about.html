About

Seventeen seasons of other people's big days

I came into weddings from the floor, not from a design studio. In 2009 I was a caterer's floor manager in Hyderabad, the person who counts chairs at midnight. A colleague asked me to plan her daughter's wedding because, she said, I was the only one at her sangeet who knew where the extra plates were. That wedding had 300 guests and one working microphone. It went well. I have planned 214 since.

I work with a team of four and one senior associate who has run sixty weddings beside me. We take at most fourteen weddings a season and only one per weekend — a wedding is not a shift that can be handed over at six o'clock. Most of my weddings are in Hyderabad; two or three a season travel, usually because one family is from here and the wedding is not.

Families hire me for the same reason, mostly: I tell them the true number early — for the budget, for the guest list, for what their date will cost in the middle of the season — and then I hold to it in writing.

Method

The order I book in

The calendar is built backwards from the muhurat; everything else is arithmetic. This is the order, and it does not change because a vendor is a family friend: the things that run out get booked first.

Peak-season weekends are the scarcest thing in a wedding. A good venue for a December Saturday in Hyderabad is spoken for eleven to thirteen months ahead — before the couple is, sometimes. If your date is fixed and the season is close, the venue conversation is this week, not next month.

House rules

What I will not do

A short list, kept short on purpose. Each item on it cost somebody something once — a guest, a family, or me — and I do not pay twice for the same lesson.

  • Entry pyrotechnicsCold pyros at the couple's entry, refused since 2019. The front row is grandmothers.No
  • Vendor commissionsMy fee comes from the family alone. A planner paid by both sides works for one of them, and it is not you.₹0, ever
  • Two weddings in one weekendIf my associate takes your weekend, you are told before you book, not after.Never
  • A hidden contingencyEvery budget I write carries its 5% contingency as a printed line, and what is left of it comes back.Printed
  • A muhurat I cannot stand behindIf I cannot be there for the ceremony hour, I will say so and offer the associate honestly — or decline.Stated upfront

The season

November to February, and the diary

The muhurat calendar concentrates the year's weddings into about sixteen weekends. This season, three of mine are already gone — 21–22 November 2026, 12–13 December 2026 and 23–24 January 2027 — and the planner on the home page will tell you so the moment you enter one of those dates. Fridays and Mondays either side of a booked weekend are often the better wedding anyway: same light, calmer venues, caterers at their best.
