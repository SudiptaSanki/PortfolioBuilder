Fees

How I charge, in full

My fee comes only from the family. I take no commission from any vendor — no percentage from the caterer, nothing from the decorator for sending you to him. That is why my vendor list is short and why I can tell you, in writing, when a quote is too high.

Every wedding I plan gets a written budget with a contingency line of 5% shown on its own. Weddings need it: a train runs late, a guest list grows, a generator is hired at midnight. If a planner will not show you the contingency line, they are spending it.

Recent wedding · Hyderabad · December 2025

The 9:42 muhurat

A Telugu wedding, 620 guests, three days. The muhurat was fixed at 9:42 in the morning by the family priest, and the bride's family was arriving from Vijayawada on a train due in at 8:10. I told them in October that the train would be late. It was — by fifty minutes. The cars were still at the station, the hall doors closed at 9:20, and the muhurat was met at 9:42.

The days before

Haldi at nine, mehndi by four

The wedding day runs itself if the days before it are placed properly. Haldi belongs in the morning, while the light is kind and the bride can still rest after it. Mehndi starts by four, because a full bridal mehndi takes five hours and the artist's hands tire before the bride's patience does — I book two artists for any guest list over three hundred, and I feed them before the guests arrive.

The sangeet is the one evening I let run loose, with one condition: it ends by eleven. Every wedding I have seen suffer did so because the night before ran long.

Guest lists

Two numbers for the caterer

Every wedding adds guests after the cards are printed. Every single one. So the caterer gets two numbers from me: the printed one, and the true one — printed plus fifteen per cent, which experience says is closer to who actually comes.

I plan for the wedding you will actually have, not the one in the spreadsheet. It costs less than the alternative, which is a buffet running out in front of four hundred people.
