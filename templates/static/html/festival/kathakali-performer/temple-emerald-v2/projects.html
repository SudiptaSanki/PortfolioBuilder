Performances · Three nights, told whole

A performance is a promise kept until dawn.

These are three recent nights — a temple courtyard, a school ground and a family's own yard. Each is written the way committees judge them: what was asked, what we did, and what came of it.

Case 1 · Guruvayur · Nalacharitam, Night Two · December 2025

The Ekadasi night

The ask. The utsavam committee at Guruvayur wanted Night Two of Nalacharitam for the Ekadasi crowd — the night Nala loses his kingdom at dice and leaves Damayanti sleeping in the forest. It is the saddest stretch of the four nights, and the one committees most often ask me to shorten. This committee did not ask.

The work. Nine of us travelled. Make-up from four in the afternoon; the kelikottu drums at dusk; the story from nine at night until five in the morning, the singers spelling each line twice so the hands could be followed from the back of the courtyard. My work in the last two hours is mostly stillness — a ruined man deciding to walk — played to a courtyard that had gone completely quiet.

The outcome. Booked again for Ekadasi 2026 before we had washed the green off, and Night Three commissioned for 2027 so the courtyard can follow Nala into the forest and out of it.

“When Madhavan plays Nala, nobody leaves at the dice game. Nobody. The young ones stayed this year — that is the thing the committee keeps repeating.”
Suresh Menon, utsavam committee, Guruvayur

Case 2 · Thrissur · Kalyanasaugandhikam · February 2026

The school that chose the whole night

The ask. A school in Thrissur wrote asking for forty minutes of Kathakali — “the colourful parts” — for four hundred students on a school afternoon. It is the request I refuse most often, and I refused it again, in writing, with the reasons.

The work. I offered two honest things instead: my one-hour demonstration, where I narrate the make-up and the hand-language as it happens, or the full Kalyanasaugandhikam on their ground on a Saturday night. The school chose the night. We printed a one-page story sheet for the students; the senior class ran the lamp and the curtain. Bhima's walk to fetch the saugandhika flower began at nine.

The outcome. Of four hundred students, the teachers counted perhaps thirty asleep by two in the morning — on a story four centuries old, in a language of hands. The school has made it an annual night, and the demonstration now runs in their classrooms each October, before the season opens.

“He would not sell us forty minutes, and he was right. Our students did not need Kathakali made smaller. They needed to be trusted with the whole of it.”
Deepa Krishnan, headmistress — on the Kalyanasaugandhikam night above

Case 3 · Palakkad · Keechakavadham · January 2026

The villain, for his eightieth birthday

The ask. A family in Palakkad commissioned a full night in their own courtyard for their father's eightieth birthday. As a young man he had played Keechaka in village performances; they wanted the villain's story, played by somebody who would not make him a cartoon.

The work. Kathi vesha — four hours in the chair while the family came and went with coffee. We played Keechakavadham entire: the boasting, the dance hall, the assignation, the fall. A kathi character is not evil in one colour; he is charm first, and the charm is what makes the courtyard complicit before it turns.

The outcome. In the last hour the old man began, very quietly, to sing the lines along with the singers — he still knew them. His family has since endowed one courtyard performance a year in his name, for any village committee that asks.

“He played our father's part back to him — the pride of it, not just the wickedness. At the end my father raised his hand to the actor the way actors greet a guru. We will never be able to say what that night was worth.”
Sankar, son of the house — on the Keechakavadham above
