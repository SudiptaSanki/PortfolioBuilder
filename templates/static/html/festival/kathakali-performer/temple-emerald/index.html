KathakaliKalamandalam, 2009–2020

The face is painted at dusk. The story is danced until dawn.

Kathakali has no curtain and no interval. A temple committee commissions an attakatha, the drummers arrive with the chenda, and three to five hours later a hero who has been dead for two thousand years walks off the stage. Everything in between is preparation I began four hours before the first note: rice paste along the jaw, lime, lamp black, and a green so dense it changes how the lamp falls on the face.

I

Three attakathas that carry a season

A festival does not ask for a show. It asks for one story, told properly, with the verses sung in the order they were written. These are the three most requested.

  1. 3 hr 40 min

    Kalyanasougandhikam

    Bheema goes into the forest to find a flower for Panchali and meets his brother Hanuman, who is lying across the path as an old monkey. The whole middle of the night is a test of whether a strong man will bow. I play Bheema in pacha; the Hanuman is vella thadi, and the two make-up rooms share a single mirror until eleven.

    PachaBheema
  2. 3 hr 10 min

    Duryodhanavadham

    The Kathi role. Green face, white moustache, a red knife-mark across the cheek, and a walk that arrives before the character does. The temptation in this part is to play the villain as loud; the better reading is a man who believes he is right, which is slower and lasts longer in a courtyard.

    KathiDuryodhana
  3. 2 hr 50 min

    Poothanamoksham

    Poothana comes to kill the infant Krishna and is killed instead. Kari make-up: black face, red mouth, two teeth, and no chutti at all. It is the shortest piece in my repertoire and the hardest to leave, because the last ten minutes are played to children sitting on the temple steps.

    KariPoothana
II

The aharya studio

Six character types, six different ways of building a face. Choose one and a curtain time; the studio shows you the make-up and the green-room clock that goes with it.

Pacha · the noble green

Character type

Pacha

Green face, full white chutti, red lips, no beard. The colour says the man is in the right, and the play will not test it.

The clock assumes a village koothambalam with no proscenium, a five-piece chenda–maddalam ensemble, and a lamp lit at the north-east corner. For a proscenium stage, add forty minutes for the longer walk to the wing.

III

A night, in five movements

A temple festival performance is an all-night form. This is the shape of one, from the moment the trunks are opened to the moment the wick is pinched out.

  1. 17:30

    Trunks open

    The crown box is unpacked first. Wooden pieces are checked for cracks; a broken mudi cannot be repaired at seven in the evening.

  2. 19:00

    Chutti

    Rice paste and lime are pressed into a ridge from ear to ear. It sets as it dries, so the jaw stops being a jaw and becomes a mask.

  3. 20:40

    Eyes

    Chundappu seed and castor oil redden the rims. It stings for a minute. After that the eyes can hold a hall without widening.

  4. 21:15

    Melappadam

    The drummers play the opening. I am behind the curtain, listening for the third variation, which is the cue to enter.

  5. 04:30

    Dawn

    The last verse is sung, the lamp is pinched out, and the chutti comes off in warm water. The face underneath is always a surprise.

Eleven years to stand still

Kalamandalam training begins at the kalari at five in the morning with the body, not the face. Two years of chuvadu — the basic steps — before a student is allowed to hold a character. Then the eye exercises, which are the part nobody warns you about: tracking a lamp wick from corner to corner without moving the head, for twenty minutes, while your legs ache from the previous hour.

The make-up comes last because the make-up is a conclusion. A student who has not learned to stand still will only look painted. The face has to be a place where the story can happen, not a costume that announces it.
