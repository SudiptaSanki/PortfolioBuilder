Night after night, the same four hours.

Kathakali does not rehearse a new piece for every occasion. A festival picks a story from the repertoire, and the company performs it the way it has been performed for two centuries: verses sung in order, gestures held long enough to read, the drum deciding the pace. Below are the nights I can offer, and the ones already played.

II

How a festival commission works

Three conversations, then a night. The committee decides the story; the company decides what the stage must be.

  1. 01

    The story

    A committee names the attakatha, or asks for a recommendation from the season's calendar. We confirm the date, the start time and whether the performance runs to dawn or to a fixed hour.

  2. 02

    The stage

    I visit or ask for measurements: covered courtyard or koothambalam, the lamp position, the distance to the green room, and where the audience will sit. A Kathakali stage needs a level floor and a place to hang a curtain, nothing more.

  3. 03

    The night

    The company arrives five hours before curtain. The committee provides the green room, the lamp oil, and a person who can unlock the temple at four in the morning.

Which story is yours

Tell me the festival, the date and the audience, and I will suggest two or three attakathas that suit the night.
