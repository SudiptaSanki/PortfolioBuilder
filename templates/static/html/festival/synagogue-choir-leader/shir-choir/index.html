01 — The choir

Four parts, one breath

The choir rehearses on Tuesday nights in the social hall, between the folding tables and the book cart. Nobody auditions. What I ask for instead is attendance and the willingness to be wrong out loud for a few weeks.

S C4 – A5

Soprano

Carries the melody when the mode opens and hands it back at the repeat. In Kol Nidre the line is deliberately unornamented: the longing sits in the interval, not in the decoration.

A F3 – D5

Alto

Holds the third, which means the alto decides whether a chord sounds major or minor. It is also the part most likely to drift sharp when the congregation sings louder than anyone expected.

T C3 – G4

Tenor

Sings the nusach line an octave below the soprano when the mode opens. A good tenor is not a loud tenor; it is one who can make the melody audible without covering the congregation.

B E2 – D4

Bass

The floor of the room. In Kol Nidre the bass barely moves, and that stillness is what makes the melody above it sound like it is asking for something.

02 — Nusach

Three modes I keep coming back to

Nusach is not a fixed score. It is a set of melodic habits that tells a congregation where it is in the service before a word is sung. These three carry most of our year.

Mode I

Kabbalat Shabbat

קבלת שבת

Begins on the dominant, settles onto the minor third, and should feel like a door opening. The choir enters on L’cha Dodi after the congregation has already found the tune — never before it.

Mode II

Yamim Noraim

ימים נוראים

Ahava Rabbah, with its augmented second. The interval is the point: it is meant to sound unsettled. Kol Nidre is sung three times, each time lower in the chest rather than lower in pitch.

Mode III

Shabbat Morning

שבת בבוקר

Adonai Malach, the major mode of Saturday morning. The Kedusha is the peak of the service; after it the choir steps back and lets the congregation finish the line on its own.

“The choir does not sing to the congregation. It sings so the congregation can.”
Lucia Kovacs · rehearsal note, September 2026
