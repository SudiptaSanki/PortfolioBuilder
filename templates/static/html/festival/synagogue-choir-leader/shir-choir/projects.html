Six things the choir has done, and one it does every week.

Some of these are concerts with tickets. Most are the ordinary work of a synagogue: a Friday night, a Saturday morning, a student learning a phrase that has been sung in this building for a hundred years.

6 programmes

I

Eight Nights, Eight Voices

ConcertDecember 2026New Hazlett Theater

Forty singers from four congregations, a string quartet, and a programme built around the eight nights rather than a single tune. Each night gets one short movement: candle, blessing, argument, latke, dreidel, gift, exhaustion, light again. The new setting of Ma’oz Tzur is by Nadia Rosen, who wrote it for amateur choirs and refused to make the soprano line cruel.

Tickets $18Pay what you can at the door
Sun 6 Dec, 4:00 pm

II

Kol Nidre: four variants, one night

Workshop & lectureSeptember 2026Kehillat Or Hadash

A two-hour session before the High Holy Days comparing four ways the Kol Nidre melody is sung — an Ashkenazi setting from Poland, a Sephardic version from Salonica, an early Reform arrangement with organ, and the loose, speech-like version many small congregations have kept because nobody could teach them anything else. We sing all four, badly and then better.

42 attendedRecording and handout available on request

III

Kabbalat Shabbat, every Friday

ServiceWeekly6:15 pm, main sanctuary

The service I care most about. The choir sings four settings a month and the congregation sings everything else. In winter we start in the small chapel, which seats eighty and makes the choir sound twice its size; from April we move into the sanctuary and everyone has to listen harder.

Open to allNo ticket, no booking
In person and streamed

IV

The volunteer choir, year fourteen

ChoirOngoingTuesdays, 7:30–9:00 pm

Eighteen singers, four parts, no auditions. We spend the first twenty minutes on breathing and the last twenty on the piece that is not working. The middle hour is spent learning to hold a line while the part beside you moves. New members are welcome in September and January; the only requirement is that you come back the following week.

18 singersNew members: September and January

V

Nusach coaching for b’nei mitzvah

TeachingBy arrangementSix to ten sessions

Most students can learn the words in a few weeks. The music takes longer, because trop is not a tune you memorise but a grammar you apply. We work in short sessions, often with a parent in the room, and we record each phrase on the student’s own phone so practice at home is not a guess. I do not require a beautiful voice. I require curiosity.

Six to ten sessionsIn the chapel or at home

VI

Notes from a choir loft

Recording2024Six tracks, 28 minutes

Recorded over two evenings in the social hall with two microphones and a borrowed interface, because the sanctuary was being rewired. Six pieces, mostly unaccompanied, with the room’s own reverb left in. You can hear a chair move in the third track and someone laugh at the end of the fifth. We kept both.

Six tracksStreaming and a short run of CDs

Bringing one of these somewhere else

Workshops travel. The choir does not — eighteen volunteers with day jobs are not a touring ensemble — but I can bring a smaller group, a lecture with recordings, or a rehearsal plan your own singers can use.
