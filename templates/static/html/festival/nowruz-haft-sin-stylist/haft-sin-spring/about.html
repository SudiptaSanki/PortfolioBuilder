Method

Three weeks of work for one evening.

A haft-sin is not a centrepiece. It is a table that has to hold seven meanings, stay standing through forty guests, and be cleared without anybody's glass falling over.

Studio: 34 Shariati alley, Tajrish

Working since 1394 · 11 Nowruz seasons

Tables dressed: 218 · largest: 40 guests

I learned it on a floor in Rasht.

My grandmother set her haft-sin on the floor of the guest room, on a cotton cloth she had embroidered before she was married. The table had a rule I have kept: no two items at the same height. Sabzeh stood tallest at the left; samanu sat low in a copper pot; the mirror rose behind everything, and the candles were the only thing above it.

I moved to Tehran in 1391 to study textile conservation. The conservation work taught me to look at the back of things — the seams, the repairs, the places where a piece has been made to last. I started dressing haft-sins for friends in 1394, mostly because they had bought beautiful things and could not make them sit together.

Now the studio does 20 to 24 tables each Nowruz. I keep it at that number because sabzeh cannot be rushed and because I build every table myself. The rest of the year is spent sourcing: copper from a workshop in Zanjan, linen from Yazd, sumac from a family in Khorramabad, and the clay saucers I have made each autumn in a small kiln in Shahreza.

Eleven seasons

  1. 1394

    The first table

    One table for a friend's apartment in Vanak, built from borrowed dishes and a dish of sabzeh grown on the kitchen sill.

  2. 1397

    Copper and linen

    Bought the first set of seven copper pots from a workshop in Zanjan. Switched from shop cloth to Yazd linen after one table on a vinyl runner slid under a candle.

  3. 1399

    The year of small tables

    Twenty-two sofrehs, most of them on one shelf or one side table. I designed the 60 cm layout that is now half of what I build.

  4. 1401

    Courtyard weddings

    First aghd sofreh in Kashan. Weighted linen, no glass, and a mirror carried from the bride's family house in a wooden crate.

  5. 1404

    Teaching the sprouts

    Started a spring workshop for eight people on growing sabzeh and reading the height of a table. It runs twice each Esfand.

  6. 1406

    This Nowruz

    Bookings open for 24 tables. Sabzeh start date is 3 Esfand; anything booked after that comes with shop-grown sprouts, named as such.
