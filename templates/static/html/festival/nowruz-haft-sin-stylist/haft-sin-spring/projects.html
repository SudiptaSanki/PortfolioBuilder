Selected sofrehs · 1403–1405

Six tables, and what each one had to survive.

Not styled photographs. Each entry lists the cloth, the heights, the quantities and the one problem that shaped the build.

Six shown here, chosen for the problem each one posed

1405Niavaran40 guests

A sofreh for forty in a room with one window

The room had one north window and a table that could not be moved. Sabzeh needs light or it leans, so it went into nine shallow dishes along the window wall rather than one big pot, and the table took the seven heights in three steps instead of two.

The client wanted the mirror at the centre. I argued for the end and lost, which turned out fine: the reflection doubled the candles and the room read brighter in photographs than it did in person.

  • ClothLinen runner, 210 × 45 cm
  • Tables3 joined, 4.2 m total
  • Sabzeh9 dishes, sown 19 days out
  • ProblemOne window, fixed table
1405ElahiehOne shelf

The apartment that had 60 cm to give

A one-bedroom flat with no dining table and a plaster shelf above a radiator. The radiator decided everything: no candles near it, nothing that would dry out in a day. We put the sabzeh on the cooler end and kept the samanu in a teacup so the pudding would not skin over.

The result was the smallest seven I have built and the one I use as a reference when someone says their home is too small for a haft-sin.

  • ClothPressed cotton, 58 × 22 cm
  • Dishes7, all under 9 cm
  • SabzehOne dish, 11 cm tall
  • ProblemRadiator below the shelf
1404KashanAghd

A wedding sofreh that had to survive a courtyard

Outdoors, April, with a breeze that came through the garden every twenty minutes. Everything was weighted: the linen sewn with a pocket of rice at each end, the copper sat on damp cloth so it would not slide, and there was no glass anywhere on the table except the mirror.

The mirror came from the bride's grandmother's house in a wooden crate, and the crate was the only thing that left the courtyard before the photographs.

  • ClothWeighted linen, 3.6 × 50 cm
  • Guests72 (table dressed for 12)
  • GlassMirror only
  • ProblemCourtyard breeze
1404Vanak6 guests

A seven that leaves room for the rice

The brief was simple and impossible: a full Nowruz dinner on the same round table as the haft-sin. The sofreh had to fit inside a 60 cm circle without taking the serving space.

I built it as a centre island with the mirror at the far edge and put the seven in two rows rather than a fan. Everything had to be liftable with one hand, because the host would be moving dishes around it all evening.

  • ClothRound linen, 60 cm
  • Guests6, plus 4 for dinner
  • Height spread18 cm
  • ProblemDinner on the same table
1404TajrishWaiting room

The sofreh nobody was allowed to touch

A small clinic asked for a haft-sin in the waiting room. Hospital rules meant nothing could be fresh in water, nothing could be a choking risk, and everything had to be gone in the morning.

Sabzeh grew in paper cups on the windowsill for eighteen days before the equinox, the samanu was a sealed jar with a paper lid, and the hyacinth was cut paper on a wire stem. It stayed up for eleven days and was given away cup by cup on Sizdah Bedar.

  • ClothCotton, 80 × 30 cm
  • Fresh itemsPaper cups only
  • Life on view11 days
  • ProblemClinic hygiene rules
1403Darband20 guests

A table that had to wait for the dark

A rooftop, twenty guests, and a sunset at 18:22. Candles lit before then would look like nothing, so the table was dressed in daylight and held until the mountain went dark.

The wind came up the valley at dusk, so the seven were set low and the mirror was tied to the table leg with linen tape. The photograph the host remembers is not the table; it is the moment twenty people waited in silence for the first flame.

  • ClothLinen, 2.4 × 50 cm
  • Guests20, standing
  • Light2 candles, 4 lanterns
  • ProblemValley wind at dusk
