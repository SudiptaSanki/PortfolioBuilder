I am Aaliya Mir. I play the Kashmiri santoor — a hundred strings over twenty-five walnut bridges, struck with two light mallets. My grandfather's instrument had a crack in the soundboard that he never repaired, and I learned to play around it before I learned to play the ragas.

01

The instrument

Walnut, wire and one old crack.

A Kashmiri santoor is a shallow trapezoid of walnut with a hundred steel strings stretched over two rows of wooden bridges. It is played with two light mallets called kalam, one in each hand, and it rings far longer than most people expect.

Strings100

Four per bridge, tuned in unisons and octaves.

Bridges25

Two staggered rows on the soundboard; movable for tuning.

WoodWalnut

Kashmiri walnut for the body, mulberry for the bridges.

MalletsTwo

Kalam of light walnut, tipped with felt or hard cloth.

02

The repertoire

Four ragas, and the hour they belong to.

The santoor came to me through Sufiana kalam, not the concert hall, so the repertoire keeps one foot in the shrine and one in the evening raga. Choose a raga to load it into the practice room below.

A note on the names. The santoor does not belong to one tradition. In Srinagar it carries the muqam and the Sufiana kalam; in the concert hall it carries the raga. I play both, and I no longer think they are two instruments.

03

Practice room

Twelve strings, no audience.

A working sketch of the instrument: one octave over twelve strings, with the bridges drawn in. The strings outside the chosen raga are muted. Sound begins with your first pluck — nothing plays on its own.

The practice room

Choose a raga, set Sa, and pluck. The gold marks are the bridges; the upper octave doubles every string. The aochar plays the raga's ascent so you can hear its shape before you try it.

Raga Yaman

Kalyan thaat · early evening

N R G M′ P D N S

The first raga most students meet, and the one that teaches the ear to reach for tivra Ma without squeezing. On the santoor the tivra Ma rings longest because the bridge is nearest the soundboard edge.
