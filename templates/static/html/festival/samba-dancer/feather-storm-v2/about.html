Sobre — the dancer, the ladder, the terms

Worthy of the costume's four months

First school

Larissa Peixoto learned samba no pé the way the avenue teaches it: at the back of a quadra, copying feet. She grew up two streets from the Estrela da Serra rehearsal court in Rio de Janeiro, joined the school's community ala at fourteen, and was a face in a crowd of three thousand before she was ever a name in a programme. That order of things — school first, name later — is the whole of her professional ethics, and this site is built to keep it.

She is, by the school's own ladder, a dancer of the ground positions: passista, then destaque de chão. She does not choreograph the school's parade, direct its harmonia or speak for it — those are other people's offices, held by people whose names are on the school's own documents. What she holds is a position, a rehearsal standard and a teaching practice, and those are what this portfolio sells.

Nº 6

How a booking runs

1 · The position offer, in writing. School, parade or show, date, and the exact position — destaque, passista, soloist, teacher. A position is a job description on the avenue; nothing is agreed on a corridor conversation.

2 · The costume conversation. Class agreed from Table 1 (home page), with the atelier's build hours and fittings count written into the engagement. If the window fails the planner's arithmetic, the booking is refused or the position changes — that refusal is a term of trade, not a negotiation.

3 · The fitting schedule. Fittings one per week across the final weeks of the build, at the atelier. She attends every fitting herself; a costume fitted on a stand-in is a costume fitted for somebody else.

4 · Rehearsal discipline. Thirty-six sessions in position before parade day, the school's standard, logged by the harmonia team — the same number the planner uses, because there is only one number.

5 · Technical rehearsal, then the avenue. Full costume, full tempo, one week out. Then seventy minutes that everyone has already danced thirty-six times.

Nº 7

The honesty page

The avenue judges the school. Every score a judge writes is the school's score. A destaque who markets a school's title as her own achievement is selling something that was never hers; this site therefore prints school results as the school's, positions as positions, and her own small record — parades, seasons, classes — as hers.

The costume is not hers. It is designed by the school's carnavalesco, built by its atelier, owned by the school, and returned after the parade. She wears four months of other people's work for seventy minutes; the job is to be worthy of the four months. The build hours quoted on this site are the atelier's hours and are credited to the atelier every time they appear.

Materials, stated plainly. Her 2026 destaque costume used farmed rooster and pheasant feathers, dyed, as the atelier states its sourcing; no wild-bird feathers. Where a school or client asks for synthetic alternatives, she dances them without a speech — the 2025 ala wings were synthetic, and the record does not pretend feathers and substitutes are the same thing, in either direction. No adjectives do this work; the materials list does.

The opinion she is known for: a passista is not made by the costume, the costume is not made by the deadline, and a school that cannot count back from carnival should not be booking destaques. Everything on this site — the planner, the printed fees, the injury file — is that opinion with its sleeves rolled up.

Nº 8

Engagement terms

Fees follow the printed rate card; estimates from the home-page estimator are estimates, confirmed in writing per engagement. Travel is charged at the printed flat bands, once per engagement.

Costume ownership: parade costumes are the property of the engaging school and its atelier, before, during and after the parade. Stage costumes built for a tour remain the property of the production that funded them unless the contract says otherwise, in writing.

Injury and stepping back: if she is injured, she steps back early and at no penalty to the school, and will say so as soon as a clinician does. A position danced on an injury costs a school more than a season costs her; File 03 is the precedent, and it is a term, not a favour.

Weather: street parades and blocos are danced in the rain the organisers accept; a parade called off is recorded as called off — the 2024 bloco on the home page is in the record at twenty minutes, and so will yours be.

Teaching: workshops are ninety minutes, capped at thirty students, with the tempo tool's tempos used openly. Private coaching begins with a written assessment; if she judges a student not ready for the audition they are preparing for, the assessment says that, and the fee for it is the session's, not a course's.
