Nº 2

O Planejamento — count back from the avenue

A parade booking is arithmetic before it is glamour. The costume is built by the school's atelier, by hand, at a stated number of hours a week — and carnival does not move for anyone.

The planner below works exactly the way the booking conversation works: pick the costume class, count the weeks left, count the rehearsals. Every figure comes from the tables printed here — the atelier's build hours and the school's readiness standard — so the page and the tool cannot disagree. When the window is too short, the tool says so, and says what the honest options are.

The figures are the atelier's, quoted as the atelier states them: build hours are the atelier's numbers, fittings are held one per week across the final weeks of a build, and a costume must be finished one week before parade day, because the technical rehearsal is danced in full costume.

Bateria Tempo — a practice metronome

For workshop students: the school's parade tempo, as a teaching pattern. Synthesised clicks — a low surdo thump on beats 1 and 3, a rim click on every beat. Not a recording of any bateria. It starts only when you press Start, plays at a fixed low level, and stops itself after 8 bars.

What moves the price

Costume class first. A parade booking is priced with its costume window: a Class A fantasia commits the atelier for nine weeks, and a booking inside that window is priced — and usually refused — accordingly. The planner above is the same arithmetic.

Travel second. Flat bands, printed left, charged once per engagement however many shows it holds.

Not charged: the school's costume (it is never the client's to fund or keep), rehearsal attendance inside the 36-session standard, and the technical rehearsal. Percussionists for stage shows are contracted and paid by the organiser at the printed line in the engagement terms.
