The passista · 2009–2026

Eleven carnavais,
one needle.

I learned to dance in a samba school courtyard in Vila Isabel, barefoot, behind a woman who had been passista since before I was born. I learned to sew in the same courtyard, because if you dance you will eventually have to fix your own costume at two in the morning.

Rio de Janeiro Unidos da Gamboa, passista Atelier since 2019

Vila Isabel, barefoot

Samba school rehearsals happen in a concrete courtyard with a tin roof, and the children dance at the edge until someone lets them in. I was six. My mother was in the baianas, my uncle played surdo, and my job was to hold the water bottles and watch the passistas. I learned the step by copying it badly for years.

The passista is not the whole school; she is the soloist who dances in front of the bateria, the one the crowd watches when the ala passes. It is a role of stamina rather than tricks: forty minutes of samba no pé, in a costume that weighs as much as a small child, on a runway that is wet with sweat by the second school.

“A backpiece is a second body. You do not wear it; you negotiate with it for the length of the parade.”

From the avenida to the bench

I started sewing because I could not afford to commission what I wanted. The first backpiece took three months and fell apart in the second pass; I still have one of its feathers pinned above the machine. By 2019 I was making pieces for other dancers in the school, then for a bloco, then for a shop in Gamboa that sells to tourists and does not know the difference.

The atelier is one room with a long table, two machines, a wall of feather boxes and a balcony where the glue gun lives because the smell is unbearable inside. In November the room disappears under plastic sheeting; in March it is empty and I sleep for a week.

What the body has to carry

A passista's costume is a negotiated weight. The school's designer will draw a backpiece the size of a door, and the dancer has to explain, politely, that a three-kilo frame will not survive the second school in the parade. My own limit is 3.2 kilos; above that, the shoulders bruise before the first turn.

Eleven carnavais, briefly

  1. 2009

    First avenida

    Danced in the children's ala of a school in Vila Isabel. Costume made by my mother, heels borrowed, one feather lost before the gate.

  2. 2014

    Passista

    Promoted to the front line after a season as understudy. The first backpiece weighed 2.1 kg and left a bruise shaped like a wing.

  3. 2019

    The atelier

    Rented the room in Gamboa and took commissions from other dancers. The first paid piece was a headdress for a queen of the bateria.

  4. 2026

    Unidos da Gamboa

    Eleven carnavais, one school, three kilos of feathers on the shelf and a waiting list that starts in September.
