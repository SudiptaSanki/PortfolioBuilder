Carnaval · 2019–2026

Six nights
in feathers.

A costume only exists for one night, and then it lives in a box under a bed. These are the six I still think about: what they weighed, what they cost, and what broke on the avenida.

11 carnavais Largest piece: 3.1 kg All feathers dyed in-house
01

Unidos da Gamboa · 2026

Noite de Vento

The school's theme was wind, so the backpiece had to move even when I was still. We built it on a curved carbon spine with two layers of ostrich that open on a pivot; the outer layer swings a beat behind the body. It weighed 2.8 kg and lost nine feathers between the first and second school, which is fewer than I feared.

Bloco · 2025

Verde Que Te Quero

A commission from a bloco in Lapa that wanted green without looking like a football club. We used three shades of dyed ostrich and kept the frame short so the dancers could move through a crowd. The costume was danced four times in one night and came home smelling of beer and rain.

96 ostrich

03

Queen of the bateria · 2024

A coroa de Marina

The queen of the bateria needs a headdress she can wear for ninety minutes while directing two hundred drummers. It had to be light, visible from the stands, and stable enough to survive her turning to cue the surdos. The crown is brass wire wrapped in gold lame, with ostrich plumes on a quick-release base so she could take it off between schools.

Atelier commission · 2023

Prata da Casa

A pair of shoulder pieces for a dancer who wanted something she could wear twice: once in the parade, once at a wedding in Niterói. We built them detachable, on a harness that hides under a jacket, and used cream ostrich instead of white so they would not yellow in the box. She wore them to both, and the bride was not amused.

05

Street bloco · 2022

Costura de Rua

For a bloco that parades at nine in the morning in February heat, feathers are a liability. We used short rooster feathers on a light wire frame and sewed everything twice, because a street crowd will pull at anything within reach. Twenty-two dancers, twenty-two pieces, one week, and a lot of coffee.

Learning piece · 2019

The first headdress anyone paid me to make: a small wing for a dancer in a bloco who could not afford the shop prices. The feathers are uneven and one side sits a centimetre lower. She wore it for two carnavais and still asks me to repair it instead of replacing it, which I take as a compliment.
