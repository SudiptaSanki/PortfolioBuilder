Talasari · Palghar district · the new rice is in

I paint the year my village lives.

When the new paddy comes in, we thank Kansari, the goddess of the grain. When a daughter marries, we paint her a chauk. When the harvest is home, we dance the tarpa till the gourd gives out. I paint all of it — white on geru earth, the way my grandmother painted the inside walls of our house — on walls, on cloth and on paper.

I am Sunita Bhoye, a Warli painter. My people have drawn this way in the villages of Palghar for longer than anyone here can count. The figures are two triangles and a circle for a head, and they carry the whole of our life.

How a wall commission goes

From bare wall to wedding morning

  1. 1

    I come and see the wall

    Light, damp, plaster — a wall tells you what it can hold. We talk about the scene standing in front of it, not from photographs.

  2. 2

    The ground is prepared

    Patch the plaster, wash the wall with geru. Two days, and the wall must dry fully — paint on a damp ground will not stay.

  3. 3

    The chauk is drawn first

    For a wedding, the lagna chauk goes up before anything else, and the family sees it and approves it before I paint a single guest.

  4. 4

    The painting days

    About 10 sq ft a day, working from the chauk outward. The family is welcome to watch — children usually end up holding the bamboo sticks.

  5. 5

    Care, told plainly

    Keep water off it, dust it dry, and if a corner ever flakes, call me — the first repair visit is part of the price.
