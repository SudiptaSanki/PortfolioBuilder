The painter

I learned to paint
on the wall I was
told not to touch

My mother painted our house every April, before the heat came. I was six and I was allowed to watch. By the time I was fourteen I was mixing the rice paste and being shouted at for making the goddess too small.

Warli painting is not a style I chose. It is the way the women in my family have marked a marriage, a harvest and a good monsoon for as long as anyone can name. The wall of a Warli house is not a canvas; it is a record. A chauk on the wall says this family married this year, and the figures around it say who came.

I grew up in Karanjwadi, eleven kilometres from Dahanu, in a house with a mud wall and a tin roof. My mother, Janaki, painted the lagna chauk for every wedding in the hamlet. She used rice soaked overnight, ground on a flat stone, mixed with water until it ran like milk. The brush was a bamboo stick chewed at the end. There was no white paint in the house and no need for any.

What changed for me was a visit to Mumbai in 1998, when I saw a Warli painting in a gallery, framed, with a price on it. I stood there a long time. It was the same painting my mother made on a wall that would be washed away by the next monsoon, and it was being sold as art by people who had never stood in a Warli house. I went home angry and then I went home practical: if the wall can travel, the painter can eat.

The tradition, plainly

Warli is the painting of the Warli community of the Palghar and Thane districts of Maharashtra. It is line work, almost always white on an earth ground, built from a vocabulary small enough to learn in a week and deep enough to spend a life in: a circle, a triangle, a square, and the human figure made from two triangles joined at the waist with a circle for the head.

The chauk — a square, often with a smaller square inside — is the marriage square, and the goddess sits at its centre. The tarpa dance is painted from above, a spiral turning around the musicians. The harvest and the fishing are painted as rows and bands, because that is how they are seen from the field. There is no perspective, no shadow and no horizon; the wall is the world.

Since the 1970s the tradition has moved onto canvas and paper, and the government has listed Warli painting as a Geographical Indication. Both of those things are good for the painters and dangerous for the painting. A canvas does not need repainting every year, so the wall stops being a record. I still paint the wall at home every April, and I teach my daughter the order of it.

Materials & method

Why the white is not paint

The paste

Rice, water, a little gum from the babhul tree. Ground on stone, never in a blender — the blender cuts the grain and the line goes grey. Mixed fresh each morning, because by evening it sours. A wall of twelve feet takes about two kilos of rice over four days.

The brush

A green bamboo stick, split at one end and chewed to a soft brush. Widths are made by chewing more for a thick border and less for the fine ticks. I carry twelve in a cloth roll. There is no shop that sells them; you make them the night before.

The order

Border, then the centre, then the rows, then the animals and trees, then the birds and the sun. If a client asks me to start with the figures I will say no. The border is the measure of the wall, and everything else is measured from it.

The wall

Earth and cow dung, three coats, a day between each, then a wash of water and lime. The wall has to be cool and dry before the paste touches it. In the monsoon nobody paints; the paste will not hold and the wall sweats it out by August.

What a commission actually involves

  1. 01 · The visitA photograph is not enough. I need the wall, the light in the room and the height of the people who will stand in front of it. In Mumbai I come for an hour; outside, I ask for a video walk with a measuring tape.
  2. 02 · The sceneYou choose from the four, or tell me what the room is for and I will tell you which one belongs there. A restaurant is not a wedding house; a fishing scene in a boardroom is a decision somebody has to defend.
  3. 03 · The drawingI draw the arrangement on paper at one-tenth size and send a photograph on WhatsApp. You can move a tree. You cannot add a figure, because the count is what the price is built on.
  4. 04 · The wall or the canvasFor a wall, the preparation takes three days and the painting four to nine. For a canvas, I paint in the studio and ship it stretched and crated. Both are the same painting; only the ground is different.
  5. 05 · The drying and the starchA day to dry, then a thin starch coat that disappears into the wall and holds the paste. Photographs, the balance payment, and a note on how to clean it — a dry cloth, no water, and never a detergent.
