Selected commissions · 2017–2026

Walls that left
their houses

A commission is a negotiation between a room and a village. Here are six, with the size, the days on the wall and the scene — and one line on what I refused to paint.

Bandra restaurant, west wall

Mumbai · 12 × 8 ft · canvas on site · March 2026

A monsoon fishing scene across a restaurant wall, painted in place over seven days while the kitchen was being fitted. The room is dark wood and low light, so the paste line had to be thicker than usual or it disappeared at dinner.

The owner wanted a chauk in the centre because he liked the shape. I said no — a chauk belongs where a marriage happened, not in a restaurant. We put the fishing net at the centre instead, and the boats at eye level.

A wedding chauk, Dahanu

Palghar district · 8 × 8 ft · mud wall · December 2025

The wall of a house where a daughter was married in December. The chauk went up three days before the ceremony, and the outer figures were painted with the family standing in the courtyard telling me who to include.

It is still there. In April the mother will paint over the border and refresh the goddess, and the marriage will still be in the wall.

Museum education corridor

Pune · 40 ft × 7 ft · wall · August 2025

A long corridor in a museum, painted as four scenes in sequence: sowing, harvest, tarpa, marriage. The height was the problem — seven feet meant the figures had to be small enough to read from three feet away and still hold the wall from the far end.

Two painters from my village worked with me for eleven days. The museum wanted labels explaining the scenes; I wrote them, in the first person, and they are still there.

School corridor, Karanjwadi

Palghar · 20 × 6 ft · wall · June 2024

The zilla parishad school my daughter attends. I painted the tarpa dance along the corridor because the children see it every October and did not know it was painted from above.

No fee. The school paid for the rice and the scaffolding, and the sixth standard helped mix the paste for two days. One of those children now paints with me on Sundays.

A collector in Delhi

Delhi · 4 × 3 ft · handmade paper · February 2023

A small harvest scene on four sheets of handmade paper joined wet. The collector had bought a Warli painting at an auction and wanted to know whether it was real. It was painted by a man in a workshop in Sawantwadi who had never seen a harvest.

I painted the real thing for him on paper, at the size he could hang, and told him what to look for: the border drawn first, the figures built from two triangles, the paste sitting on the surface rather than soaked in like paint.

Festival pavilion, Lyon

France · 16 × 10 ft · canvas panels · September 2017

Four canvas panels shipped to a festival of Indian crafts, painted with the tarpa and the chauk and assembled on site by a French carpenter who had never seen Warli before and followed my drawings exactly.

The panels came back with a corner scuffed and a note from a child in Lyon asking why the people had no faces. I wrote back: because a face would decide who they are, and they are everyone.
