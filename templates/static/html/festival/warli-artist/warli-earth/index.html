What the four scenes mean

Nothing in a Warli wall is only a picture

Lagna chauk

Painted for a wedding on the wall of the house, a square within a square, the goddess at the centre and the couple inside. It stays for the length of the marriage ceremony and is often repainted every year. If you want a chauk, the painter needs the date of the wedding — the arrangement of the outer figures follows the family, not the studio.

Tarpa

The tarpa is a trumpet made from a dried gourd. At harvest the village dances in a spiral around the players until the music stops, and the spiral is what I paint: two players at the centre, everyone else turning inwards. The dance is the only part of Warli life that is drawn from above.

Rice harvest

Rows of figures bent at the waist, sickles in the right hand, sheaves tied and stacked. The sun is drawn as a wheel, the moon as a crescent, and the field runs to the edge of the wall without a horizon, because the wall is the horizon.

Monsoon fishing

The rivers run in July. Figures stand in water to the waist with nets, fish below the surface line, and the boats drawn flat, as if seen from the bank. This is the scene most people ask for in a hotel, and the one I am asked to change most often — the fish are not decorative, they are dinner.

From wall to wall in eleven days

  1. 01 · The mixRice soaked overnight, ground on a stone, mixed with water and a little gum. No acrylic, no white paint. It dries to a chalk line that holds for years indoors.
  2. 02 · The groundFor a mud wall: cow dung and earth slurry, three coats, a day between each. For canvas: hand-stretched khadi and a coat of earth-coloured primer mixed by the same recipe.
  3. 03 · The borderThe chauk frame goes up first, in one continuous line, with a bamboo brush cut to a width. If the border is crooked the whole wall reads wrong.
  4. 04 · The figuresTwo triangles and a circle. The order is always the same: the centre, then the rows, then the trees and the animals. I paint standing, and I do not sketch in pencil first.
  5. 05 · The last dayThe birds, the sun, the small marks between the figures. Then a day to dry, and a thin coat of starch that disappears. Photographs before the client walks in.
