Objects · 2024 — 2026

Six things that
were made to be used.

Nothing here is behind glass. A menorah with wax on it, a cup that has held wine, a mezuzah that has been touched on the way in every morning for twenty years — these are the marks I am aiming for.

Menorah · O-114 · 2026

The stepped base

Silver
925, 1,240 g
Height
31 cm
Finish
Brush, rouge highlights
Price
€2,850

Raised from six discs, with the base weighted by a steel plate set under the silver floor so the whole thing sits like a stone. The branches were first bent as straight rods and then curved over a wooden form; the curve is repeated eight times and there is no jig, so each pair is a millimetre or two different. I like that. A machine would have made them identical and the object would have lost its breath.

Menorah · O-109 · 2025

The straight line

Silver
925, 860 g
Height
26 cm
Finish
Matte, no rouge
Price
€1,950

For a client in Tel Aviv who wanted the opposite of her grandmother’s ornate piece: no curves, no ornament, every joint visible. It is much harder to make than it looks, because there is nowhere to hide a solder line. The cups are turned, not raised, and the stems are square tube, which is the only way to keep the edges honest.

Mezuzah · O-121 · 2026

The door piece

Silver
925, 78 g
Length
11 cm
Scroll
Klaf, by a sofer
Price
€420

The case is raised in two halves and soldered along the spine, with a sliding capsule that lets the scroll be taken out and checked without removing the case from the door. The klaf comes from a sofer in Kraków who writes on parchment made from a kosher animal; I will not sell the case without the scroll, because an empty case is a decoration and not a mezuzah.

Kiddush cup · O-102 · 2025

The Friday cup

Silver
925, 210 g
Height
19 cm
Interior
Gilt
Price
€860

The bowl is raised, the stem is drawn from a single tube, and the foot is spun — three techniques in one small object. The inside is gilded because silver and wine turn each other black, and a cup you cannot see the wine in is a cup you stop using. The gilt is 24-carat and will wear at the rim after twenty years. That is honest wear, not damage.

Spice tower · O-117 · 2026

The pierced tower

Silver
925, 165 g
Height
16 cm
Piercing
Hand-sawn, 180 holes
Price
€740

Havdalah needs a smell, so the tower must breathe. The dome is sawn with a jeweller’s blade, 180 holes in a repeating pomegranate pattern, and each is filed from both sides. The flag on the top turns in a draught; the hinge is a piece of the same wire, flattened and curled, and it took four attempts to make one that did not stick.

Tzedakah box · O-124 · 2026

The little chest

Silver
925, 320 g
Size
14 × 9 × 8 cm
Lid
Hinged, friction fit
Price
€980

A commission for a family who wanted a box their children could use without being told to be careful. The lid is hinged with a friction fit rather than a clasp, the slot is wide enough for a folded note, and there is no lock — the point of a tzedakah box is that the money can leave. The engraving inside reads צדקה, tzedakah, in a hand I copied from a 1930s Polish stamp.

How an object is ordered

We begin with a conversation about the room it will live in and the hands that will touch it. Then a paper drawing to scale, a deposit of a third, and three weeks at the bench. Engraving is quoted separately, because a name in Hebrew can take an hour or a day depending on the hand.
