01

The hammer decides
where the light goes.

Silver is raised, not cast. A flat disc is annealed over a flame until it goes dull red, then hammered over a stake in circles from the centre outward, cooling and hardening as it goes. Each round of hammering raises the walls a little and leaves a line of planishing marks; each round must be annealed again, or the metal cracks at the rim.

A menorah has thirty-one separate pieces before it is one piece: the base, the stem in four sections, eight branches, eight cups, the shamash and its stem. They are hard-soldered in order — base first, then stem upward, then branches outward — because heat travels and a later joint will undo an earlier one if you are careless.

The finish is the part clients notice and the part that takes least time. A matte brush, then a rouge polish on the high points only, leaving the recesses darker. Silver should look like it has been touched.

02

Eight nights,
one flame first.

On each night of Hanukkah the shamash is lit first, and from it the night’s candles — one on the first night, eight on the last. Choose a night below, light the helper, then the candles. The blessings appear when the night is complete.

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה יְיָ אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְו‍ֹתָיו וְצִוָּנוּ לְהַדְלִיק נֵר שֶׁל חֲנֻכָּה.

Baruch atah Adonai, Eloheinu melech ha’olam, asher kid’shanu b’mitzvotav v’tzivanu l’hadlik ner shel Hanukkah.

Blessed are You, Eternal our God, Sovereign of all, who makes us holy through the commandments and commands us to kindle the Hanukkah light.

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה יְיָ אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, שֶׁהֶחֱיָנוּ וְקִיְּמָנוּ וְהִגִּיעָנוּ לַזְּמַן הַזֶּה.

Baruch atah Adonai, Eloheinu melech ha’olam, shehecheyanu v’kiy’manu v’higianu laz’man hazeh.

Blessed are You, Eternal our God, Sovereign of all, who has kept us alive, sustained us, and brought us to this season.

Click the shamash first. Tradition lights the candles from it, right to left, and lets them burn down rather than blowing them out.

a house needs.

Raised stem and branches, eight cups level to the millimetre, shamash set higher so it can be told apart. Base weighted with a hidden disc of steel so it does not ring when the table is knocked.

Mezuzah · from €340

The door piece

A raised case with a sliding capsule, so the scroll can be checked without taking the case off the door. The klaf is written by a sofer in Kraków and sold with the case; we do not sell one without the other.

Kiddush cup · from €780

The Friday cup

Held at the stem, not the bowl, so the warmth of a hand does not sit in the wine. The interior is gilt, because silver and wine turn each other black over a decade of Fridays.

05

Bring me an object
that has stopped working.

A bent branch, a base that no longer sits flat, a cup that has been soldered by someone in a hurry. I repair what I make and, when I can, what others made — there are too few of these objects left to throw one away.
