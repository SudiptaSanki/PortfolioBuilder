구성 · Anatomy

Four pieces, one line.

A hanbok is not a dress with a jacket over it. It is a short top with a curve at the sleeve, a skirt pleated into a band, a collar that shows white at the neck, and a ribbon that does the work of a button.

What each part does

The top is cut short on purpose so the skirt's waistband shows a clean line. The collar is a separate strip, starched, and it is the piece that makes a cheap silk look expensive or the other way round.

  • 1 · 저고리Jeogori. The jacket. Wide sleeve, short body, closed by the ribbon rather than buttons.
  • 2 · 고름Goreum. The ribbon. One long tail, one short, tied left over right. A child's goreum is short so it cannot be chewed through.
  • 3 · 동정Dongjeong. The white collar strip. Detachable, starched, replaced when it yellows.
  • 4 · 치마Chima. The skirt, pleated into a band that wraps the chest, not the waist. This is why a hanbok flatters different bodies than a dress.
  • 5 · 밑단Hem. Turned and hand-stitched. On a rental it is marked at the fitting and re-hemmed in two days.

The atelier is on the second floor of a 1930s house in Bukchon, up a stair that is too narrow for a bolt box. Everything comes up by hand.

How I came to this

I studied clothing and textiles at Hongik and then spent three years in a rental shop near Gyeongbokgung, where the job was to get forty tourists into hanbok in an hour. I learned speed there and I learned what a badly fitted chima does to a person's posture: they hold their arms still, they do not walk, and the photographs show it.

I opened this atelier in 2012 with fourteen bolts and a sewing machine inherited from my mother. The rule was one fitting per client, free, with no obligation to rent. It is still the rule.

Silk, and what happens to it

The bolts come from a mill in Gyeongju that weaves for hanbok only. Silk is bought in autumn for the next spring, because the dye lot changes and a red that matches in October will not match in March. Each piece is aired for two days after a rental, spot-cleaned with distilled water, and dry-cleaned once every six wears.

I keep 90 bolts. When a bolt falls below four metres it goes to the children's rack or to the hem pile, which is why the doljanchi pieces often match a wedding from two years earlier.

Rental or made

Most clients rent. A made hanbok takes three weeks and two fittings and costs between 1.4 and 3.2 million won depending on the silk. I only take a made order if the client can come twice, because a hanbok cut from measurements alone will not sit right at the shoulder.

  1. 2012

    The atelier opens

    Fourteen bolts, one machine, one fitting room behind a paper screen. First season: 60 rentals, mostly palace days.

  2. 2015

    First doljanchi

    A friend's daughter's first birthday. The striped jeogori from that day is still in the children's rack, in a smaller size and a copy.

  3. 2018

    The Gyeongju contract

    A direct order with the mill, four dye lots a year, and the end of buying from the wholesale market in Dongdaemun.

  4. 2021

    Ninety bolts

    The back room is converted into a silk store with cedar panels. The atelier stops taking walk-ins and moves to appointments only.

  5. 2024

    Wedding fittings

    Weddings become half the calendar. The second fitting with shoes is introduced after a bride walked badly down a stone stair in Samcheonggak.

  6. 2026

    This season

    Twelve fittings a week, ninety bolts, and a waiting list for April that starts in January.
