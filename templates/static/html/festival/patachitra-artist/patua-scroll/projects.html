What leaves the workshop

Nothing is sent rolled in a tube without its singer's notes. A pat is a performance object, and it arrives ready to perform.

The cloth

Mill cotton, washed twice and sized with tamarind kernel and chalk. It will cockle slightly in humidity; that is the cloth breathing, not a fault.

The pigments

Conch shell, vermilion stone, indigo, lamp soot and turmeric, ground on stone and bound with gum from the bel tree. No acrylic, no marker.

The song sheet

Each scroll comes with the pater gaan written out in Bengali and a transliteration, panel by panel, so a new singer can learn it.

The roll

Rolled around a bamboo pole with a cotton sleeve, never folded. A folded pat leaves a white line across the faces that never comes out.

The hanging

Two brass rings at the top edge, stitched with waxed thread. Hang from a single nail; the rod takes the weight.

The care

Keep out of direct sun. Dust with a soft brush, never a damp cloth. If the cloth must travel, unroll it in the new room for a day first.

Ask for a story

If a story is not in the catalogue, it can usually be painted. Send the episode you remember, even in one sentence, and I will tell you whether it belongs on a pat or on a single panel.
