The story is older than the cloth. I keep it moving.

I am a patua. My family unrolls a painted scroll, I sing the panels in order, and the village hears a story it already knows but has not heard this year. The cloth is cotton sized with tamarind seed and chalk. The white is conch shell, the red is vermilion, the black is soot from a mustard-oil lamp.

A scroll is made in seven sittings

No part of a pat is bought ready-made except the mill cotton. Everything the brush touches, we prepare ourselves, the way my grandmother Meenakshi did.

  1. 01

    Wash the kapor

    Mill cotton is washed twice in the pond behind the house and beaten on a stone until the weave closes. Dry in shade, never in sun, or the ground cracks.

  2. 02

    Size with tamarind

    Seed kernels soaked overnight, pounded, strained. Mixed with chalk powder and rubbed into the cloth with the palm. Two coats, one day apart.

  3. 03

    Burnish

    A smooth river stone is dragged over the dried size until the surface shines like a drumhead. The paint must sit on top, not sink.

  4. 04

    Grind the colour

    Conch shell for white, vermilion stone, indigo cake, lamp soot, raw turmeric. Each ground on its own flat stone with water; the brushes are separate per colour.

  5. 05

    Draw the border

    The floral border goes first, because it decides the size of the panels. Two fingers wide, drawn freehand, no ruler.

  6. 06

    Paint the panels

    Figures before ground colour. Faces last, so the eyes are the final mark and the panel is finished in one breath.

  7. 07

    Learn the song

    A pat without its pater gaan is wallpaper. I learn the couplets for each panel from my uncle and sing them standing, the scroll held open on a bamboo pole.

A scroll for a room

Most commissions are one story, four to seven panels, for a wall that can take 2 metres. Tell me the story you want kept and the height of the wall; I will tell you what fits. A commissioned pat takes eight to ten weeks, because the cloth must cure between coats.
