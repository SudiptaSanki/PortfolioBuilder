Alebrijes · third generation · Oaxaca

An animal that was never in the field.

Nadia Cruz Santiago carves alebrijes in San Martín Tilcajete, the Oaxacan village where the woodcarving trade took hold in the 1980s. Every beast begins as a green copal trunk and ends six weeks later as something with wings it never used and a tail it never needed.

Four choices make an animal

A carver works from a drawing, then lets the wood argue. The bench below skips the argument: choose a body, a pair of wings and a palette, and it draws the beast and estimates the weeks at the bench. Nothing is ordered, nothing is priced.

The six weeks · 04

How a trunk becomes a beast

Copal is soft when it is green and hard as a chair leg when it dries. Everything in the order below exists because of that one fact.

01

Choose the trunk

Copal blanco from planted trees in the village. The trunk is split and left to dry in the yard, cut side up, for six to ten weeks before anyone draws on it.

02

Draw the animal

Pencil on the flat face: body, neck, one raised paw, the tail as a single line. The drawing is a suggestion; the grain gets a vote.

03

Rough out

Machete and chisel remove everything that is not the animal. This is the loud part, and the part where a knot can decide the piece is a rabbit instead of a deer.

04

Carve and sand

Knife work for the ears, eyes and feet, then four grits of sandpaper. The surface has to be smooth enough that the brush never catches.

05

Seal and paint

A thin sealer first, so the pigment sits on the surface instead of drinking into the grain. Base colour, then the pattern, then the fine dots and the eyes last.

06

Wax and rest

Beeswax and copal varnish, buffed by hand. The piece rests a week before it travels, which also gives the paint time to stop smelling of turpentine.

The animal is already in the wood. My job is to take away the parts that are not the animal, and then to convince you it was always there.

Visiting the workshop

The workshop is a covered yard behind the family house, four blocks from the village square. Visitors are welcome Tuesday to Saturday, 9:00 to 17:00, and the best hour is the morning when the carving is still loud.

No appointment is needed for the shop. For a demonstration at the bench, write first, especially in the weeks before Día de Muertos.
