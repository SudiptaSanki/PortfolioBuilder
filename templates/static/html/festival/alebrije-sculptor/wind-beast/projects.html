Forty animals a year, no two alike.

A selection from the last three seasons: commissions, exhibition pieces and the stock that lives on the workshop shelf. Each one started as a drawing on a flat face of copal.

Selected work · 07
2026

Jaguar de copal

A commission for a private collection in Oaxaca de Juárez. The brief was one word, "jaguar", and a request that the animal look as if it had just stopped walking. The rosettes are painted in two passes so the ink ring sits under the jade centre.

The block had a knot in the left shoulder. Rather than cut the shoulder away, Nadia carved a small scar into the pattern, which is now the part the owner likes best.

Venado nocturno

Made for the lobby of a small hotel in the centre of Oaxaca, where it stands against a white wall and is therefore seen mostly as a silhouette after dark. The heron wings were the hotel's idea; the antlers were Nadia's answer to them.

The indigo base is mixed from three pigments and looks nearly black in daylight. The turquoise wing edges were painted last, in one sitting, because the colour dries faster than it can be corrected.

Iguana de lluvia

The largest piece of the season: a metre and a half of copal, carved from a trunk that had been drying for four months. The crest runs the whole spine, and the tail curls into a spiral that took three attempts to carve without splitting.

It hangs above the bar of a restaurant in Jalatlaco. The scales are painted in two colours that alternate along the body, a pattern Rosa calls "rain on a roof".

2024

Armadillo de mercado

One of a set of six small animals made for the workshop shelf. The armadillo is the piece visitors pick up first, because the bands make it look like it should open. It does not open; the seam is painted on.

The animal sold in the first week and was replaced by a second, which sold in the second. There is now a waiting list for armadillos, which nobody in the family expected.

Conejo de la esquina

A small piece made for a child's birthday and then copied four times for the shop. The ears are carved thin enough to flex, which is a risk in copal and the reason the first two attempts became firewood.

The dots are the workshop's signature border, painted last and always by Rosa. On this piece they are larger than usual because the animal is small and the brush was the same size as always.

Commissions · 09

How a commission works

Three drawings, one block of wood, and a date that depends on how the wood dries. The workshop takes six commissions a year.

1

The animal

Tell the workshop the creature, roughly how tall, and where it will stand. Photographs of the room help more than references of other alebrijes.

2

The drawing

Nadia sends three pencil sketches within two weeks. One is chosen, or the idea goes back to the start. No deposit until the drawing is agreed.

3

The wood

César chooses a trunk from the yard and starts drying it for the piece. This is where the schedule comes from, not from the carving.

4

The carving

Photographs at the rough-out stage, then again before painting. Changes are possible at the first stage and very expensive at the second.

5

The paint

Palette agreed from painted swatches, not from photographs. Rosa paints the pattern; Nadia paints the eyes, which is the only part she will not delegate.

6

The crate

Pieces travel in a wooden crate with foam corners. Within Mexico the workshop uses a courier; abroad, the buyer arranges the freight.

Six commissions a year, and one is open

The next bench window starts in February 2027. Small pieces can sometimes be fitted in sooner, especially rabbits.
