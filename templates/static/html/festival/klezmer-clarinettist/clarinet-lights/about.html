About

Taught by a wedding player who never read a note.

Yaakov Feld, born in Chernivtsi, playing in Vienna since 1998. One clarinet, two cimbaloms, and a repertoire learned by ear in kitchens.

My first teacher was Motl Shtern, who played weddings in the villages around Chernivtsi for forty years and could not read music. He taught me by singing a phrase and waiting until I found it on the instrument. If I found it too quickly, he changed the phrase.

I was nine. What I learned in that kitchen was that the note is the least interesting part of the tune. What matters is the year in which the phrase is bent, the breath before the high note, and where you choose to let the clarinet crack a little on purpose. The old players called that the krekhts — a sob — and a clean recording of it is usually a failed one.

From Chernivtsi to Vienna

We came to Vienna in 1991. I studied clarinet properly at the conservatory, which taught me to sit straight and play in tune, and then spent ten years unlearning some of it in the klezmer bands of the second district. Both trainings are in the playing. The discipline keeps the tone honest; the unlearning keeps it alive.

Since 1998 I have played around eighteen weddings a year, a handful of concerts, and one annual Hanukkah night in Kraków that has become the fixed point of the winter. The quartet is cimbalom, double bass, accordion and clarinet. Sometimes a violin; never a drum kit unless the floor can take it.

“Play the doina as though the room has not decided whether to cry. Then the first freylekhs decides for them.”
Motl Shtern, in his kitchen, 1988

How I work a room

I walk the room before the guests arrive. Where the floor is, where the tables are tight, where the older guests will sit, where the sound dies. The set list is written after that walk, not before. A band that plays its favourite programme at a room that cannot hold it is a band playing to itself.

For weddings we meet once, in person or by call, and I ask what the families actually want: the tune the grandmother hums, the one the couple danced to first, the one that must not be played because of a funeral. All three go on the list, in that order of priority.

The 1928 Albert clarinet has the crack in the upper joint that Motl put there with a chair. It still plays; I use it for doinas because the tuning is impolite in exactly the right way.

Short history

  1. 1988

    First phrase found by ear

    In Motl Shtern’s kitchen in Chernivtsi, aged nine, on a borrowed instrument with one working key.

  2. 1991

    Vienna

    Conservatory clarinet, and evenings in the second district playing for anyone who would listen.

  3. 1998

    First wedding under my own name

    Eighty guests in Baden, an hour of rain, and a bulgar that lasted nineteen minutes.

  4. 2009

    The quartet settles

    Cimbalom, bass, accordion and clarinet. The same four players, more or less, since.

  5. 2019

    Kraków, every December

    The Hanukkah concert becomes an annual night, and the band learns to play in a room with a stone floor.

If you have a room and a date, I can tell you if it will work.
