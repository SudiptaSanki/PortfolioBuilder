Installation · light on the ground

Five thousand small flames, and one direction of wind.

I build installations out of earthen diyas, mustard oil and the open air. The work is planned like a lighting rig: a count, a spacing, a burn time, and a calculation of what the wind will take before the public arrives.

Instrument · array planner

Diya array bench

The bench works out what an array of earthen lamps will do on the ground: how much oil it drinks, how long it burns, how far the light reaches at ankle height, and whether the wind will take the flames before the ceremony does. The constants are from the studio's own test burns.

Selected works

Three arrays, three winds

All works →
  1. 01

    5,400 — Sabarmati riverfront, 2024

    A grid of 5,400 diyas on the stepped ghat, lit by forty volunteers in eleven minutes. Two industrial fans stood by; the wind stayed under 1.2 m/s and they were never switched on.

    1 night · 68 L oil
  2. 02

    Well Ring — Adalaj, 2023

    Three concentric rings around a stepwell, sized so the innermost ring's light reaches the water and the outermost only reaches the first step. 240 diyas, no electric light for 400 metres.

    9 nights · 3.6 L
  3. 03

    Path of Small Lights — Kutch, 2022

    A spiral a kilometre long, walked in the dark, with the flames set at 1.8 m intervals so the path is legible from the walker's own shadow. Commissioned for a crafts fair.

    4 nights · 21 L

Statement

An electric lamp is a decision someone else already made. A diya is a decision you have to keep making — the wick, the oil, the wind, the hour.
