Works · 2015 – 2026

Every work begins as a plan and ends as an hour of watching.

Each installation below lists its count, its oil and the wind it was built for. The plans are drawn to scale; the photographs are not, because a camera cannot hold five thousand small flames at once.

5,400

Sabarmati riverfront, 2024. A grid on the stepped ghat lit by forty volunteers in eleven minutes. Two industrial fans stood by; the wind never reached 1.2 m/s.

Well Ring

Adalaj stepwell, 2023. Three rings sized so the innermost light reaches the water and the outermost only reaches the first step. Nine nights, no electric light for 400 metres.

20239 nights3.6 L

Path of Small Lights

Kutch, 2022. A kilometre of spiral, walked in the dark, diyas at 1.8 m intervals so the path is legible from the walker's own shadow. 640 diyas.

20224 nights21 L

Step Edge

Chandrabhaga, 2025. Nine hundred diyas along a single step of a river ghat on the night of Kartik Purnima, with a 0.6 m sandbag shelter against a 3.8 m/s wind.

20251 night11 L

Courtyard of Four Gaps

Ahmedabad, 2021. A walled courtyard filled to the walls, with four gaps left at the entrances so that people walking in carried their own shadows instead of a torch.

20213 nights15 L

Constellation, Ground Level

Baroda, 2019. Three hundred and eighty diyas placed where the winter sky's brightest stars would fall if the sky were a field — plotted from a star chart, checked on site with a compass.

What the works are about

Not devotion, exactly. The diya is a unit of attention: a small flame that must be tended or it goes out. When five thousand of them are arranged in a field, the arrangement is the artwork and the tending is the performance — by the volunteers, and then by the public, who always want to help.

I am interested in the moment the public stops taking photographs and starts watching the wind.

Commissions

Works are commissioned by festivals, museums, civic bodies and occasionally a family for a wedding. Lead time is eight weeks for anything over a thousand diyas, because the terracotta has to be thrown and dried before it is fired.

The studio does not use electric lights, LEDs, or any flame that cannot be put out with a hand.
