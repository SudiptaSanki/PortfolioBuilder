What a reading is

Three things I will do, and one I will not

Most of the fear around astrology comes from people using it as a court. I use it as a calendar with a long memory, and I am careful to say which part is tradition and which part is advice.

01

Read the hour

Where the slow planets sit, which dasha is running, and what the tradition says that period is for. This is timing, not fate: a season is not an order.

02

Name the pattern

A chart is a vocabulary for tendencies — the thing you do when you are frightened, the way you spend money, the kind of work that keeps you interested. We check the vocabulary against your life.

03

Suggest a practice

Small, concrete things: a day of the week for a difficult conversation, a direction for a desk, a discipline for a difficult planet. Practical, cheap, and reversible.

✕

Not a verdict

No predictions of death, illness, divorce or the sex of a child. No “remedy” that costs more than a book. If a reading is making you afraid, it is a bad reading — mine included.

Method

Tradition first, then the person.

I was taught in the Paranelli line, in Chennai, by my mother and then by her teacher — a man who read charts for sixty years and refused to read for anyone he had not met. I keep that rule: a chart is read with a person in the room, not at them.

My work is in Tamil and English. I use the Lahiri ayanamsa, whole-sign houses, and the Vimshottari dasha. I do not mix systems to make a nicer answer; if two techniques disagree, I say they disagree.
