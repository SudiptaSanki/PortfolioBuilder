CASE 118 · 2019M.
Career · one sitting

The family business

M. had run his father's printing press for eleven years and wanted to leave. The chart showed Saturn transiting his tenth house, with the dasha lord in the sixth — a period for work that feels like duty, not for cutting loose.

“I told him the chart would support a slow handover, not a resignation. He left in six weeks anyway.”

He called a year later. The press had been sold, he was freelancing, and he was sleeping badly but happier. The chart was right about the difficulty and wrong about the form. I wrote that down.

OUTCOME: LEFT ANYWAYNO CHARGE FOR FOLLOW-UP
CASE 204 · 2020R.
Timing · muhurta

A visa appointment

R. had three possible dates for a visa interview. Two were in a period I would have avoided for a “first approach to authority”; one was dull but clean. I gave all three with reasons and said the difference was small.

“He chose the dull date. The interview took four minutes and he was asked one question.”

I do not think the date got him the visa. I think the preparation did. But he asked for a date, and a date is what the tradition can give — the rest is his paperwork.

OUTCOME: VISA GRANTEDNOT ATTRIBUTED TO CHART
CASE 231 · 2021S. & A.
Marriage · compatibility

Two charts, one wedding

Both families wanted a match read. The charts had a difficult Moon-Saturn contact, which the tradition reads as patience being required rather than doom. I said that plainly, twice, because the grandmother had already decided otherwise.

“The wedding happened in the month I suggested. The first two years were hard, in the way I had described.”

They are still married. They came back in 2024 with a question about a house, not about each other. That felt like the best possible outcome of a compatibility reading.

OUTCOME: MARRIED 2021FOLLOW-UP 2024
CASE 147 · 2018K.
Study · second attempt

The second attempt

K. had failed an entrance exam once and wanted to know whether to try again. The chart's fifth lord was strong but the current dasha was not supportive until the following spring. I said: prepare now, sit in spring, and expect the preparation to be the hard part.

“She sat in spring and passed by nine marks. She says the chart gave her permission to take the year.”

I would rather take credit for the advice than the timing: study for nine months instead of three is what changed the result.

OUTCOME: PASSEDONE FOLLOW-UP
CASE 262 · 2022P.
Travel · relocation

A move to Singapore

P. had an offer and a sick parent. The chart showed a strong ninth-house period, good for a foreign move, but the fourth house was under Saturn — the tradition's language for responsibility at home. I did not tell her what to do; I told her the chart was describing the tension she had already described.

“She went, came back every six weeks for a year, and her father died in the following spring.”

The chart saw the pull of both houses. It did not see the ending, and I would not have said it if it had.

OUTCOME: MOVED, RETURNED OFTENNO PREDICTION MADE
CASE 288 · 2023J.
Family · household

The joint family

J. asked about a mother-in-law moving in. The chart's fourth house was crowded; the Moon was in a nakshatra associated with care that becomes over-care. I suggested a separate entrance and a fixed hour for the household accounts.

“She laughed at the suggestion and did it anyway. Six months later she said the hour was the thing that saved them.”

This is the part of the work I trust: not prediction, but a vocabulary that makes a practical suggestion sound like common sense.

OUTCOME: HOUSEHOLD HOLDSONE FOLLOW-UP
CASE 176 · 2017T.
Career · sabbatical

The year off

T. wanted to leave a law firm for a year. The chart's tenth house was strong enough that I said the year would not damage the career, but the dasha was poor for “beginning a new thing”. I suggested he call it a sabbatical, not a resignation.

“He took the year. He came back to a different firm, one level up.”

The naming did the work. A sabbatical is a story an employer can accept; a resignation is a door. The chart gave us the vocabulary for the difference.

OUTCOME: RETURNED SENIORONE FOLLOW-UP
CASE 241 · 2021V.
Timing · a filing

A property dispute

V. asked for a date to file a case. I gave two, with the caveat that a chart cannot see a court's calendar and that his lawyer's advice outranks mine. He filed on the second date.

“The case ran four years and settled.”

I do not think the date mattered. I think the lawyer mattered. I have stopped taking this kind of question except as a conversation about patience.

OUTCOME: SETTLED 2025NO FURTHER TIMING WORK
CASE 156 · 2018N.
Family · naming

A name for a daughter

The parents had two names and wanted the nakshatra syllable. The chart gave a pada and a syllable; the parents chose the name that fitted both, and then called her by a shortening that had nothing to do with either.

“The name on the certificate is the chart's. The name in the house is the grandmother's.”

This is the tradition at its best: a small ritual of attention, not a claim on a life. I sent a book and no bill.

OUTCOME: NAMEDGIFT, NO FEE
