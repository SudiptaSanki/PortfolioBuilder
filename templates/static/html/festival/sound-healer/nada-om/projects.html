Two hours a week at Sahyadri Hospital, Pune, working bedside with patients and, separately, with the nurses who ask. No charge, by arrangement with Dr. Leena Kurien. This is the practice that keeps the rest of it honest.

Six bowls for a side room

For the Kochi-Muziris Biennale's ancillary programme: six brass bowls suspended at ear height in a bare room off Bazaar Road, one instruction on the wall. Visitors struck a bowl and had to wait for it to finish before leaving. Average stay, forty minutes.

Winter retreat: four days on a lake

Twelve people, a house on the water at Naukuchiatal, two sessions a day and long silences in between. No phones after the first evening. Walking, sitting, and one night of bowls in the dark with the windows open, which is either wonderful or very cold.

A room for arrivals

A commissioned installation for the new wing of the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya, for people coming into the museum from a loud street. Four bells, one bowl, and a bench. The staff call it the quiet room, which is the correct name.

Sound & sleep, a lecture

An hour at Jnanapravaha with Dr. Aditi Rege, a sleep physician, and not enough chairs. She presents the data; I demonstrate what a bowl actually does to a room and then let the room sit in it. No claims beyond what the evidence supports, which is the point.
