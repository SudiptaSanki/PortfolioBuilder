01 Installations · 2019–2026

Work made for a particular corner.

Commissions are site-specific: the light, the height, the colour of the wall and how close people will stand. These six show what changed and why.

The commission book

No. 19
Vasastan · ongoing

A hotel lobby, four times a year

A 28-room hotel in Vasastan with a high, north-facing lobby and a great deal of pale oak. The brief was to make a single arrangement that could be seen from the reception desk and from the door, and that would not look tired by the third week. We settled on one tall vessel, one dominant branch and a low companion piece, with the materials changed seasonally: pine and forced tulips in February, cherry in April, hosta and grass in July, maple and seed heads in October.

Vessel Stoneware cylinder, 62 cm
Height 1.9 m including branch
Change Four times a year
Light North, no direct sun
Note Low piece on the counter, never on the same plinth

No. 23
Rosendal · June 2021

A wedding that was not a bouquet

Two long tables under the apple trees at Rosendals Trädgård. The couple wanted flowers on the tables but did not want centrepieces that blocked the view. I made twenty small suiban compositions, each one three stems and a stone, placed off-centre so the sightlines stayed open. The materials came from the garden itself, cut the morning of the wedding, which meant the arrangements were slightly different in every place setting.

Pieces 20 suiban, 24 cm
Materials Apple, iris, sedge
Height Under 40 cm
Note All cut on site that morning

No. 27
Nationalmuseum · Nov 2022

A stair, a branch, and a shadow

The museum asked for a temporary piece for a stair landing during an exhibition of Japanese prints. The constraint was that nothing could touch the wall or the balustrade. I built the arrangement in a low bronze basin on the landing, with a single long willow branch leaning across the space and a low pine line holding it. The piece was lit from one side so its shadow became part of the work — visitors mostly photographed the shadow, which I took as a compliment.

Vessel Bronze suiban, 70 cm
Materials Willow, pine, moss
Duration 11 weeks
Note Nothing fixed to wall or balustrade

No. 31
Södermalm · since 2023

Nine small pieces for a restaurant counter

A twelve-seat restaurant with a concrete counter and a very small pass. The chef wanted something alive in the room but no flowers on the tables. We put nine small vessels along the back counter, one for each course change, each holding a single stem or a stone. They are replaced twice a week; in winter they hold nothing but a branch and a pine cone, which the chef says makes the food look warmer.

Pieces 9 stoneware, 12–18 cm
Materials Seasonal, single stem
Change Twice weekly
Note Nothing on the tables

No. 36
Nacka · April 2024

A memorial for a courtyard

A family asked for an arrangement for a memorial in the courtyard of a care home where their mother had lived for eleven years. It was April, cold, and there were no flowers to be had that suited the occasion. I used bare hazel, forced white tulips and moss from the garden, in a shallow basin set on the bench where she used to sit. It lasted four days; the family took the moss home in a box.

Vessel Shallow basin, 48 cm
Materials Hazel, white tulip, moss
Duration 4 days
Note Made on site, in the courtyard

No. 41
Östermalm · Jan 2026

Two branches for a lawyer's office

A law firm with a reception desk and a wall of grey felt. They wanted something formal that would not need attention. Two tall cylinders, one on each side of the desk, each with a single arm of pine leaning into the room and a low stone. Nothing changes for three weeks at a time; the pine dries and holds its colour, and the stones stay. It is the least ikebana thing I have made, and the one that has taught me most about restraint.

Vessels 2 cylinders, 70 cm
Materials Pine, granite stones
Change Every three weeks
Note No flowers at the client's request

How a commission runs

See the space before you see a sketch.

Every commission begins with a visit, at the hour of day the piece will be seen. Then a paper proposal with three material options, then the work itself, made on site where possible.

Ask about an installation →
  • 1

    Visit

    An hour in the room, at the right time of day, with a measure and a camera.

  • 2

    Proposal

    Three options on one page: vessel, materials, height, cost. No mood board.

  • 3

    Build

    On site if the room allows; in the studio if the piece is small enough to carry.

  • 4

    Tend

    Seasonal changes and water, either by me or by a member of staff I have taught.
