01 Studio Södermalm · since 2014

Three lines, and the air between them.

Ikebana is not a bouquet. It is a decision about what to leave out. I arrange branches, flowers and one or two stones so that the empty space does some of the work — the Japanese call it ma, and it is the hardest material to handle.

02 The grammar

Shin, soe, hikae.

Three lines, three jobs. Everything else is material and weather.

  1. 真

    Shin the truth

    The tallest line. It sets the height of the whole arrangement and leans about ten to fifteen degrees. In Sogetsu it is the line that decides the room.

  2. 副

    Soe the support

    Two-thirds the height of shin, leaning about forty-five degrees the other way. It is the line that keeps the arrangement from being a single gesture.

  3. 控

    Hikae the reserve

    The shortest, low and wide at seventy-five degrees. It holds the base down and gives the eye somewhere to rest.

The studio year

Materials follow the Swedish growing season, not the calendar in a book. What is available in February is not what a Kyoto textbook assumes.

Jan–Mar

Bare branches

Pine, willow, hazel catkins, the first forced tulips. The season of line only.

Apr–Jun

Flowering

Cherry, apple, lilac, iris from the allotment. The hardest season to leave space in.

Jul–Aug

Green and water

Hostas, grasses, water lilies from a pond in Nacka. Vessels get bigger and shallower.

Sep–Nov

Colour and seed

Maple, seed pods, rosehips, late chrysanthemums. The studio fills with things that dry.

Dec

Evergreen

Pine, fir, moss, one red berry. December arrangements are the most requested and the least free.

05 The class

Six people, one long table.

Classes run on Tuesday evenings and Saturday mornings in the studio on Bondegatan. Six students, one theme, a great deal of silence, and coffee at the end. Beginners start with a branch and a single flower; nobody makes a bouquet.

Ask about a place →
  • 6

    Students at most

    Each arrangement gets looked at twice: once while it is being built, once when it is finished.

  • 2½

    Hours per class

    Two hours of work and half an hour of looking at everyone else's, which is where most learning happens.

  • 4

    Classes per term

    A term runs over eight weeks so there is time to fail at home between meetings.

  • 1

    Rule

    Nothing comes into the studio already arranged. You build it here, from the branch up.
