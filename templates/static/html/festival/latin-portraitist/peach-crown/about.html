A front room with good north light

The house is from 1942 and was built for a family with five children. It has been a dentist's office, a dance school, and since 2015, a portrait studio.

Why a wall, not a window

I paint in a room with one north window and a door to the courtyard. The light does not change much between ten and two, which is the whole reason I took the lease. The wall behind the sitter is painted peach because a white wall turns every skin tone slightly grey, and I got tired of correcting it.

I trained at La Esmeralda, then spent four years painting theatre sets, which taught me more about faces than the academy did. A set has to read from the back row. A portrait has to read from a metre away, and then keep reading from thirty centimetres.

A portrait should look like the person on a Tuesday, not the person at a party.

The ex-votos came later. A woman asked me to paint a small thanks for her son's recovery, on tin, the way her grandmother had done. I made it badly. Then I made eleven more, and learned that the form is stricter than it looks: a disaster, a rescue, a witness, and a sentence that does not explain too much.

What is on the bench

Four pigments, one medium, and a lot of masonite. The palette is small on purpose; a face mixed from fewer colours holds together better in a room than one built from twenty.

  • MasoniteTempered hardboard, 3 mm, gessoed twice and sanded between coats. It does not flex the way canvas does, so the paint sits still and the surface photographs without a weave.
  • CochinealDried insects from Oaxaca, ground and laked onto alum. The red in the cheeks and lips, and the only red I trust in a face.
  • MarigoldPetals steeped in linseed oil for three weeks, then strained. A warm yellow that is not cadmium and does not shout.
  • IndigoA stone ground with oil. Used in shadows instead of black, because black in a face reads as a hole.
  • Lead whiteNothing else has the same body. I keep it away from the edges of the panel and wash my hands like a surgeon.
  • Damar varnishOne thin coat after ten days of drying. It deepens the shadows and then stops moving.
  • TinFor retablos: roofing tin, cut to 18 × 24 cm, edges folded over so nobody cuts a finger. Two hanging holes punched with a nail.

Eleven years, briefly

  • 2011Graduated from La Esmeralda, moved into a shared studio in Santa María la Ribera, painted sets for a theatre company.
  • 2014First portrait sold, to a friend, for the price of the materials and a good dinner.
  • 2015Moved into Casa Melocotón. Repainted the front room peach. The first sitting was in March, with the shutters half closed.
  • 2018First ex-voto commission, then a wall of twelve at a gallery in Roma Norte. Sold eight, kept four.
  • 2021The mural in Xochimilco, six metres long, painted in nine days with two assistants and a very patient family.
  • 2024Began teaching a Saturday class in the courtyard: eight students, charcoal and one model, no phones.
  • 2026One hundred and forty portraits, forty-one retablos, and a waiting list that has finally stopped growing.

The dog, the courtyard, the door

Nico is a twelve-year-old rescue with opinions about visitors. He is kept in the courtyard during sittings, which he considers a personal insult. The jacaranda drops purple flowers on everything in March, so if you are allergic, come in April.
