Painted from the chair, not from the photograph.

I am Ximena. I paint people in a room with the shutters half closed, over three sittings, in oil on masonite. The first sitting is conversation. The second is drawing. The third is the mouth, which is where a face decides whether it is telling the truth.

The ex-voto bench

Paint a small thanks

Ex-votos are the other half of my work: a tin panel, a story of something survived, and a figure who is usually not a saint at all. Choose a ground, a crown and a witness, and the bench will compose one for you. The dedication is set in the lower margin, the way my grandmother's retablo from 1968 was lettered.

A sitting, hour by hour

Three sessions, ninety minutes each, a week apart. Nothing is painted from a photograph, though I take one at the end of the first sitting to remember the light.

  1. 0:00

    Coffee, and no drawing

    We sit at the table under the window. I ask what you do on a Sunday, and I do not open the sketchbook. The face I need is the one that appears when you are not being looked at.

  2. 0:30

    Three charcoal studies

    Head and shoulders, quick, on brown paper. You can keep them. One is for the pose, one for the shape of your jaw, one to get the likeness wrong so the painting can get it right.

  3. 1:00

    The ground and the shape

    Masonite, gessoed twice, toned with a thin wash of burnt sienna. The first layer of the face is only three greys. If the structure is wrong, no colour will save it.

  4. Sitting two

    Colour, in daylight

    Same hour of the day, same shutters. I mix from four pigments: cochineal, marigold, indigo, lead white. Your skin is somewhere in those four.

  5. Sitting three

    The mouth, then the eyes

    The last hour is the mouth and the eyes, in that order. You will see the painting finished before the varnish; then it rests for ten days and is varnished with damar.

Come to the house

The studio is the front room of a 1940s casa in Coyoacán, two blocks from the plaza. There is a jacaranda in the courtyard and a dog named Nico who is not allowed in during a sitting, and knows it.
