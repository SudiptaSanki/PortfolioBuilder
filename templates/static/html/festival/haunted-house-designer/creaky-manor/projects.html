Attractions

Six houses, each with its own way of creaking.

Selected builds from the last four seasons. Each entry carries a plan thumbnail, the room count, and one thing that made it work.

6 of 6 cases shown

  • CASE 01 · 2025

    The Ashby Hall Cellars

    Ashby-de-la-Zouch · heritage · 9 rooms

    A real vaulted cellar under a stately home, with drainage that only works at low water. We built the whole route on raised decking to protect the floor; the echo did the rest.

  • CASE 02 · 2024

    The Winter Fairground

    Glasgow · fairground · 6 rooms

    Built inside a disused ride, with the machinery turning slowly overhead. The scares are timed to its rotation, which means the room is never quite the same twice.

  • CASE 03 · 2024

    Charity haunt, St Mary’s

    Bristol · charity · 5 rooms

    Nine hundred pounds and forty volunteers. Blackout cloth, two good scares and a tea urn. It raised more in six nights than the previous three years combined.

  • CASE 04 · 2023–24

    The Long Corridor

    London · theatre · 11 rooms

    The route passes through the same corridor twice without anyone noticing. It depends on one door being four inches offset from the one before it, and on nobody counting doors.

  • CASE 05 · 2023

    Mrs Pellow’s Parlour

    York · theatre · set piece

    Six people at a time, one actor, one table, and a tea service that does something it should not. Fourteen minutes, no jump scares, and the most letters I have ever received.

  • CASE 06 · 2022

    Fog on the Pier

    Brighton · outdoor · 7 rooms

    An open-air route beneath the pier at low tide. The tide table was part of the safety plan, and on two nights it was part of the show. Salt destroys everything; we rebuilt in marine ply.

“I have been through forty haunts. This is the only one where I could not work out where the wall had moved, and I build sets for a living.”

Priya RamanScenic carpenter, after The Long Corridor
