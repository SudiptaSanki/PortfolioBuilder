The method

Five hours, and not one of them is a shortcut.

A brezel is a slow thing wearing the costume of a fast one. The dough is mixed in the dark, the lye does in thirty seconds what steam cannot do in thirty minutes, and the salt goes on last so it stays loud.

01 / 05

The dough

Type 550 wheat flour, cold milk, butter, 2.2 per cent salt, a spoon of malt. Mixed at 03:40 and left to rest for 45 minutes, until it stops fighting the hand. We keep the dough at 22 °C. Warmer and the arms collapse in the oven; colder and the belly stays pale.

02 / 05

The shape

Every piece is weighed to 95 grams. Rolled to 58 centimetres with a thick middle and thin ends, then knotted in one movement: arms up, cross, tuck. A good shaper finishes forty brezeln in ten minutes and still has flour on the wrist by the end of it.

03 / 05

The lye

Thirty seconds in 3.5 per cent food-grade sodium hydroxide, then straight onto the tray. Lye is what makes the crust mahogany, gives it the faint mineral smell, and turns a pale bread into a brezel. It is also the reason we wear goggles at five in the morning.

04 / 05

The bake

240 °C on the stone floor, thirteen minutes. The belly should sound hollow when tapped; the arms should be nearly black at the tips. We bake three trays at a time and never open the door in the first six minutes.

05 / 05

The salt

Coarse rock salt, scattered from a height so it lands unevenly. Never table salt — it dissolves into the crust and disappears. The salt is the last thing that happens and the first thing anyone tastes.

A note for home bakers. Sodium hydroxide is caustic. Gloves and glasses, always, and add the lye to cold water — never the other way around. If you would rather not keep it in the kitchen, bake bicarbonate of soda at 250 °C for an hour and use it at 4 per cent; the colour will be lighter, but the snap is still there.
