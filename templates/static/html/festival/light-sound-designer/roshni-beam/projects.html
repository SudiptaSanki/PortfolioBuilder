Show notes · five productions

What each room allowed, and what it refused

A design is a negotiation with a building. These are the five rooms I have spent the most time in, with the constraint that shaped each one and the cue that mattered.

Bonbibi · 2025GD Birla Sabhagar · 460 seats

A river, one boat, and a wall of water

The play is set in the Sundarbans, and the design had to make a proscenium stage feel like open water without a single projection. The set was a boat, a jetty and a cyclorama; the rest was light and sound.

We lit the cyclorama from below with two 250 W floods in deep green, and kept the fronts almost unlit for the first twenty minutes. The audience had to lean in. By the time the storm came — cue 21, an 8 Hz strobe for three seconds, warned at the door — they were already inside the story.

The sound was mostly recorded: river, insects, a drone that runs under the whole second act. One live element: a conch, played offstage, for the dawn cue. It was the only sound that was not in the desk, and it was the one people mentioned afterwards.

Role
Light and sound design, operator for two previews
Rig
5 profiles, 2 movers, 2 floods, 1 haze
Cues
46 light, 31 sound
Constraint
No projection; cyclorama only 4 m from the front row
Run
12 shows, March 2025

The director

“She asked to see the play with the lights at half. That was the whole design, and it took her one rehearsal to find it.”

Bagbazar Durga Puja · 2025North Kolkata · pandal

Ten nights, forty thousand people, one unstable generator

The pandal was a bamboo structure the size of a small house, with the idol at the centre and a queue that ran around the block. The brief was simple: make it beautiful, keep it safe, and survive the power.

The generator dipped for about half a second whenever the crowd surged and someone opened a drinks fridge. We put the whole rig on a UPS with ten minutes of battery and wrote the show control so that after any power loss the cue stack resumed at the last state, not at the beginning. Twice in ten nights it saved the show.

Lighting was warm and low — 2,700 K on the idol, a slow 40-second breath in the fibre optics, and nothing that flashed. A pandal is not a concert; people stand in front of it with their families and their phones, and the light has to be kind to a face.

Role
Lighting design, show control, on-site for ten nights
Rig
24 fixtures, 2 universes, fibre optics, UPS
Cues
18, on a 40-minute loop
Constraint
Unstable power, no rigging above the idol
Visitors
About 40,000 a night

The organiser

“The lights came back on their own both times. Nobody in the queue knew anything had happened, which is exactly what we paid for.”

Basement 404 · 2024–26Park Street · club

A ceiling a foot above the dancers' heads

A basement room, 120 capacity, a ceiling at 2.4 m and a resident DJ who plays eight hours on a Friday. The rig has to be small, cool and out of reach of a raised hand.

Four moving heads recessed into the ceiling, two lasers aimed only at the back wall above head height, and haze on a timer rather than a switch. The sound was tuned by ear at 11 p.m. with the room full, because an empty room lies about bass.

The design has twelve looks and no cue stack: the DJ calls the energy, and I change the look by hand. The best compliment I get is that nobody notices the lights changing, only that the room feels different.

Role
Lighting and sound, Friday residency
Rig
4 movers, 2 lasers, 6 LED bars, haze
Looks
12, hand-operated
Constraint
2.4 m ceiling, residents above, noise curfew at 2 a.m.
Run
Weekly since 2024

The DJ

“He knows the room better than the sound meter does. When the bass gets loose he fixes it before I hear it.”

Chaar Adhyay · 2024Academy Theatre · four plays

Four worlds, one rig, four changeovers

Four short plays by four directors in one evening, each with its own set and its own world. The rig was eight profiles and a cyclorama, and it could not be re-focused between plays.

The solution was to give each play a colour temperature and a direction: the first cold from the side, the second warm from the front, the third a single top light, the fourth a low backlight through the cyc. The changeovers were lit as scenes, with a recorded transition, so the audience never sat in the dark listening to furniture move.

Sixty-two cues in ninety minutes, which is a lot, and about a fifth of them were written to cover a scene change rather than to light one. That is the invisible work of theatre lighting.

Role
Light design, operator
Rig
8 profiles, 1 cyclorama, 2 floods
Cues
62 in 90 minutes
Constraint
No re-focus between plays; 6-minute changeovers
Run
9 shows, August 2024

One of the directors

“Our changeover used to be four minutes of blackout and coughing. Now it is part of the evening. I did not know lighting could do that.”

Kolkata International Festival · 2023Maidan · main stage

Five acts, one afternoon, a stage the size of a tennis court

An outdoor main stage, five acts from 3 p.m. to 10 p.m., and a sun that did not set until the third act. The first two shows had to be designed for daylight, which means forgetting most of what you know about contrast and designing in shape and colour instead.

We used the truss as a frame, with strong side light and a deep backdrop so the stage read from 200 metres in the afternoon. By the fifth act it was fully dark and the same rig became a different design: narrow beams, low fronts, a lot of black.

The monitor mix was mine as well, which is a second job on a festival day. I had a local crew of six and about four hours of get-in, and the whole thing worked because the plot was simple enough to explain in ten minutes.

Role
Stage light and monitor mix
Rig
24 movers, 12 LED bars, 8 blinders
Cues
30, five acts
Constraint
Daylight for two acts, 200 m throw, 4-hour get-in
Crew
Six local, one operator

The stage manager

“Four hours of get-in, five acts, no rehearsal for the last two. She called the cues from memory and nothing was late.”
