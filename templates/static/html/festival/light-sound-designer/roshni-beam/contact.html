Before you write

The questions that decide a quote

If you can answer these four, the reply will be a fee rather than a list of questions.

A

Can the room be dark?

Windows without blackout, a skylight, or a hall that shares a wall with a bright corridor will change the design more than any fixture choice.

B

Where can I hang a fixture?

A front truss or four rigging points. Without either, the design becomes floor-based, and the plot and the fee change.

C

What power is there?

Two separate 16 A circuits, and whether they are on the same phase as the lifts or the kitchen. A generator is fine if it is steady.

D

How long is the get-in?

Four hours is comfortable, two is possible, one means a simpler plot. The crew is part of the design, not an afterthought.

E

Is there a script?

If yes, send it. If no, send the running order and the three moments that matter. I design from the moments outward.

F

Who operates?

I can operate, or write a cue stack for your operator and sit with them for a tech. The second is cheaper and often better for a long run.

One show at a time

I take one production a month, and two in a festival week. Anything more and the cues start arriving late, which is the only unforgivable thing.
