A festival stage is lit twice: once in the plot, once on the night. Only one of those can be rehearsed.

This page opens at the desk because the desk is the work. Below is a working model of a real plot — the Kaveri Riverfest main stage — with its cue sheet, its rig schedule and its power budget printed beside it. Step through the cues: the desk adds up every channel from the printed tables, checks the total against the printed budget, and refuses one cue. That refusal is the most honest thing on this page.

How the desk adds. Desk levels are percentages of full. LED and mover channels draw level × rated watts (driver draw, measured at the distro, ±5%). The tungsten festoon obeys the filament law — draw = rated × (level ÷ 100)² — so halving its level quarters its draw. That is why the festoon is this plot's shock absorber, and why Q5's fix costs the audience nothing it can see.

Why a budget at all. Power on a festival site is finite and shared. A plot that only works at full distro is a plot that trips the show. The load sheet is drawn before the first fixture is hung, and it is the document the site electrician signs — see Case File 01.
