What it includes

  • Ninety minutes with the guide, six stops.
  • A small brass lantern to carry if you want a turn.
  • Tea at the end, at a shop near the ghat. Included, because it is cold.
  • No recordings, no actors, no jump scares.

Cancellations

Rain cancels; the walk is refunded or moved to the next dry evening. If you cancel more than 24 hours ahead, nothing is owed. Within 24 hours, the ticket is held for another date.

Is it suitable for children?

Ten and up, usually. The stories are told, not performed, and nothing jumps out. Younger children tend to be bored by the third stop, which is about a printing press.

How dark does it get?

The lanes have streetlights, unevenly. The lantern is for atmosphere and for the two stretches where the light fails. Bring a torch if you need one; nobody will mind.

Can we take photographs?

Of the lanes, yes. Not through doorways or windows, and not with a flash. The guide will say so at the start, and again at the two houses where it matters.

Is there a lot of walking?

1.8 kilometres over ninety minutes, flat, with six stops of three to five minutes each. The paving is uneven in places and one lane is narrow enough to need single file.

What if I am late?

Come to the second stop at Kumortuli ghat — six minutes down the lane from the gate. If you cannot find it, call the number in your confirmation and the guide will send someone back.
