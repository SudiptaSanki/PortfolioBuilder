The walk · 1.8 km · 90 minutes

Six doors, and what stands behind them

The stops in walking order, with the distance from the one before, the spirit the neighbours name, and the one instruction that ends each tale. Read this before you book, or after — it is not a spoiler; the walk is better when you know what to listen for.

Practical Starts 19:30 · 19:15 in winter
Meets at the Rajbari gate, Shobhabazar
Ends at Nimtala ghat, 21:00
1.8 km, flat, some uneven paving
12 walkers maximum
01Shobhabazar · start

The Rajbari gate

The walk begins where the old household still keeps its gate shut at nine. Two figures are said to stand inside the arch, and the stories disagree about whether they are guards or the people they once guarded. Either way, nobody counts them.

  • Distance0 m, meeting point
  • SpiritGuardian / pair
  • InstructionDo not stop, do not count
02Kumortuli · 340 m

The ghat where the idols are counted

Potter's quarter, and the steps where unfinished Durga idols are brought down to the water before the festival. A petni counts them from the fourth step. The potters have a system: they count aloud on the way down, and again on the way up, and the two counts must agree.

  • Distance340 m · 6 min
  • SpiritPetni, unmarried woman
  • InstructionStay on the top step
03Marble Palace lane · 260 m

The lane that calls your name

Shoulder-width, one lamp, and a nishi with a good ear. It calls in the voice of someone you love, once, from behind. The guide keeps talking through this stretch on purpose: a group that is talking is a group that is less likely to answer.

  • Distance260 m · 5 min
  • SpiritNishi
  • InstructionDo not answer or turn
04Rabindra Sarani · 410 m

The press that still corrects proofs

Shuttered since 1998, with one window lit on most nights. A brahmadaitya is said to read the galleys left on the desk and fix the errors in a careful hand. The watchman leaves one mistake deliberately, because a spirit with nothing to correct might look for other work.

  • Distance410 m · 9 min
  • SpiritBrahmadaitya
  • InstructionNo flash through the window
05Beadon Street · 300 m

The house with the upper verandah

A zamindar house with a dry fountain, a locked gate and a figure that walks the first-floor verandah after eleven. The skondhokata is a debt story: the family keeps a lamp burning and a plate set out on the anniversary, and the figure has never come downstairs.

  • Distance300 m · 6 min
  • SpiritSkondhokata
  • InstructionStay in the lane
06Nimtala · 490 m · end

The ghat with the patient ferryman

The burning ghat, and the quietest ten minutes of the night. A ferryman offers a crossing for nothing to anyone not ready to be on this bank. The guide tells the story, stops mid-sentence, and walks the group back together. The rule that matters is the last one: nobody goes home alone.

  • Distance490 m · 11 min
  • SpiritThe ferryman
  • InstructionNobody walks home alone
PrivateSix or more

Walks made for a group

Families, offices, school history groups and the occasional sceptics' club. A private walk is the same route at the same hour, with the group's own questions shaping how long each stop lasts. Six to twelve people; the price is the same per head, with a minimum of six.

Two custom routes are also available on request. The first is a history walk with no ghosts at all — printing presses, merchant houses, the old tram depot — for groups who want the neighbourhood without the stories. The second is a Bengali-language walk, which is the version most of the neighbours would tell you is the real one.

Both custom routes are guided by Ritwik, run on the same evenings, and need a week's notice. Write with the date and the number of people, and say which of the three you want.
