About · The photographer & the method

The camera is the easy part. The sheet is the work.

I'm Arjun Mehta. I came to weddings sideways: two years as a newspaper photographer in Pune, then, in 2013, a friend asked me to second-shoot his sister's wedding because the booked studio's second photographer had fallen ill. I spent that day doing what news work teaches — watching the edges of the room, never asking anyone to repeat anything — and the family asked for the second photographer's frames by name when the gallery arrived.

I second-shot for four seasons after that, for three different studios, and learned the trade's real curriculum: how a baraat actually moves, where a photographer may stand at a mandap and where he may not, how to work a greeting line without becoming its bottleneck. Shaadi Frame opened in 2017. The practice's own count, kept on the booking ledger: just over ninety weddings since, in eleven states, from sixty guests to nine hundred.

The run-sheet system on this site is not a marketing device. It is the document I genuinely work from — blocks, hours, a family list with a named wrangler, delivery weeks in writing — because a wedding is the one commission that cannot be re-shot. Everything that can be decided in advance is decided in advance, in print, so that on the day itself the only job left is paying attention.

I take fourteen weddings a year. That number is not scarcity theatre; it is the edit. Every gallery is edited by me, frame by frame, and a three-day wedding is six weeks of evenings. Fourteen is what fits without the standard slipping — the Hyderabad story on the Weddings page shows what happens to the promise when a couple asks for more: the date moves, in writing, before the work starts.

How booking runs

Five steps, each with a date on it

01

Enquiry & a straight answer

Send the dates and the shape through the enquiry form or by email. You get a reply within two working days: free, not free, or free-but-tight — and if I'm not free, two names of photographers I trust, in the first reply, not after a sales call.

02

Meeting — one hour, your questions first

We meet in person in Mumbai or by video call. You see two complete galleries — whole weddings, not selections — and the booking sheet with every figure from this site repeated on it. Nothing is quoted that isn't printed.

03

Booking — 30% holds the dates

The booking fee is 30% of the package total. On receipt, your dates leave the calendar — I hold no second enquiry against a booked date, ever. The balance is due seven days before Day 1.

04

The run-sheet call — ten days before

A one-hour call to build your actual sheet: block timings against your venue's schedule, the family list (capped at 20 groups, in printed order), one named wrangler per family, and where I may stand during the ceremony — agreed with your priest or officiant where possible. You get the sheet as a document the same evening.

05

The days, then delivery

Coverage as sheeted. A preview set of about forty frames arrives within a week of the last day; the full gallery lands in the package's printed weeks — 4, 5 or 6 — with film two weeks after that where booked.

Your files

What happens to the photographs before you ever see them

Dual cards, always. Every camera body I work with writes each frame to two memory cards simultaneously. A card failure — it has happened twice in ninety weddings — costs nothing, because the second card holds the same frame.

Same-night backup. Before I sleep on any wedding night, the day's cards are copied to two separate drives, and the copy is verified against the card count. One drive travels home with me; the other stays with my assistant or in a different bag, in a different vehicle.

Nothing is deleted early. Cards are not formatted until your gallery is delivered and you have confirmed receipt. The full raw archive of your wedding is kept for two years; after that I write to ask before anything is removed.

The family list, as a method. Group photographs fail at weddings for one reason: hunting for people. So the list is printed, capped at twenty groups, ordered largest-first, and one named person from each family — the wrangler — owns the gathering. My job is ninety seconds per group: arrange, two frames, done. At Jaipur it ran eighteen groups in eighty minutes; the ten spare minutes went to the couple, in the good light, unplanned and unhurried.

What I won't do. I won't ask a priest to pause a ritual, move a guest mid-ceremony for a cleaner frame, or stage a “candid” moment. If a photograph can't be taken honestly inside the event, it isn't taken.

A note on privacy: your gallery is delivered by private link and is never shown in my portfolio, on social media, or to another couple without your written permission — the two full galleries shown at our meeting are shown by couples who gave it. The stories on this site are fictional composites precisely because real couples' days are theirs, not my advertising.
Terms, in plain language

The booking sheet's small print, printed large

Booking fee
30% of the total, payable to hold the dates. It is a booking fee, not a deposit against cancellation: if you cancel, it is not refunded, because the dates were held against other couples.
Balance
70% due seven days before Day 1. Extra hours agreed on the day are invoiced after delivery at the printed ₹6,000 per hour.
Date change
One date change is free if made six months or more before the original date and the new date is free. Inside six months, the change is treated as a cancellation and a new booking.
Cancellation
More than six months out: the booking fee is retained, nothing further is owed. Inside six months: 50% of the balance is also due. Inside one month: the full balance. These figures are on the booking sheet you sign — there is no other version.
If I cannot work
If illness or emergency stops me covering your wedding, I first offer a named replacement of equal standing at the same price; if you decline the replacement, every rupee paid is refunded in full. This has been needed once in nine years; the couple accepted the replacement and their gallery is one of the two shown at meetings.
Delivery
Photographs in the package's printed weeks (4 / 5 / 6), counted from the last coverage day. Film, where booked, two weeks after the photographs. If a re-grade or change you request moves a date, the new date is agreed in writing before the work starts.

Questions the sheet doesn't answer?

Ask them on the enquiry form — or read the planner first and bring its numbers with you. I answer every enquiry myself.
