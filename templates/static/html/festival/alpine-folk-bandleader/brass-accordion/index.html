Musikkapelle Falkenbach · founded 1898

Forty players, one downbeat.

Sepp Hauser has led the village brass band for nineteen years: three generations of players, a book of marches, waltzes and polkas, and a standing argument about the tempo of the Radetzky. He writes most of the arrangements himself, which is why the tuba never gets bored.

The voicing desk

Every brass arrangement is a stack of the same three notes, placed at different heights. Move the middle voice and the whole chord changes character. Set a key, a chord and an ensemble below; the desk draws the stack, spells the transpositions, and stays quiet until you ask to hear it.

The book

Four binders, two of them handwritten, one held together with electrical tape. What the band actually plays, in the order it usually gets played.

  1. 01 Falkenbach MarchHauser, 2011 The band's own march, written for the village feast and played at the start of every outdoor engagement. Opens with the horns alone, which is a gamble in the wind. E♭ major
    2/4 · 112 bpm
  2. 02 Grüße aus dem Taltraditional, arr. Hauser A slow waltz from the Zillertal, learned from a 78 rpm record and a very patient grandmother. The tuba carries the melody in the second strain, which is not traditional and is now expected. B♭ major
    3/4 · 168 bpm
  3. 03 Almabtrieb PolkaHauser, 2016 Written for the cattle drive and therefore required to be audible across a meadow. The clarinet doubles the melody an octave up; the accordion stays out of the way until the trio. A♭ major
    2/4 · 138 bpm
  4. 04 Stilles Lichttraditional, arr. Moser The Advent piece. Four verses, no percussion, and a flügelhorn solo that the band has never once played the same way twice. F major
    4/4 · 72 bpm
  5. 05 Radetzky, but slowerJ. Strauss I The standing argument. Sepp holds it at 116; half the band wants 132. The audience claps at whichever tempo it gets, which settles nothing. D major
    2/4 · 116 bpm
  6. 06 HeimwegHauser, 2023 The last piece of the night: a chorale that starts with the tuba and adds one instrument per phrase. By the end all forty are playing, and then they stop on the same beat. E♭ major
    4/4 · 66 bpm

Who stands where

The band is a village institution, which means the seating plan is partly acoustic and partly diplomatic. This is the current arrangement.

6 players

Flügelhorns

The melody, mostly. Softer than a trumpet and easier to blend, which is why the village band uses them and the military band does not.

4 players

Tenor horns

The middle of the chord and the harmony line. In this band the horns also tune the room before anyone else plays a note.

5 players

Trombones

The slide section, and the only part of the band that can bend a note properly. They are seated behind the clarinets, which is a compromise.

4 players

Clarinets

Woodwind in a brass band, a Tyrolean habit rather than a rule. They double the melody and fill the third when the horns are busy.

3 players

Accordions

Not standard in every Tyrolean band, and essential in this one. They cover the chord when the band is marching and the harmony is spread thin.

3 players

Bass tubas

The floor of the chord. One plays the root, one the fifth on the afterbeat, and one is usually a farmer who cannot read music and does not need to.

4 players

Percussion

Bass drum, snare and a pair of cymbals. The snare sets the march tempo, which is why the drummer and the Kapellmeister have a weekly conversation.

11 players

The rest

Trumpets, a flugelhorn soloist who also conducts when Sepp plays, and two percussionists who are technically retired.

"A village band is not a concert hall. The job is to be loud enough for the back of the meadow and quiet enough for the blessing."

Sepp Hauser

Rehearsal

Wednesday at 19:30 in the parish hall, September to May, and outdoors behind the school from June. The first twenty minutes are tuning and gossip, in that order.

New players are welcome at any level, but the band asks for one season of Wednesdays before a uniform is ordered. The uniform is dark green with brass buttons and has not changed since 1974.

Book the band for a village day

Feast days, weddings, processions and the kind of birthday where the neighbours expect a march. Tell us the date and the size of the meadow.
