Hours are bench hours, not calendar days — a twenty-one bangle bridal set takes a week of mornings because colours have to be dyed in sequence and rest overnight. Dates are the date the order was collected, not the date it was booked.

What a commission includes

A fitting at the counter or, for brides who cannot come to the old city, a video call with a printed measuring tape sent by post. Both hands measured, not one; most people are half a size apart and never notice until they wear a stack for an hour.

Then a paper drawing of the stack — colours in order, sizes, widths — signed before any lac is warmed. You can change the drawing twice. After that the work has started, and a change costs a bangle.

Collection is at the shop, with a final try-on and any re-sizing done while you wait. Orders are couriered across India in a padded box with each bangle in cloth; the courier charge is at cost and we will tell you what it is before dispatch.

What it does not include

We do not photograph bridal stacks for our own use. If a set appears in this list, the family asked for it to be there and the name is reduced to initials unless they said otherwise. Three commissions this year are in the book and not on this page because the families declined.

We do not guarantee a colour against a photograph. Screens lie, and a rani pink on a phone is not a rani pink on silk. That is why the silk comes to the shop, or the shop sends three lac chips by post for nothing.
