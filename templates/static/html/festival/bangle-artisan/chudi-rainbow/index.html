    THE WORK

    What a lac bangle actually is

    Not glass, not metal. Lac is a resin secreted by an insect on the twigs of the palas tree; in the workshop it arrives as dark amber sticks that soften at about 50°C and can be wound like warm toffee. Everything you see in a Chudi Rainbow bangle — the colour, the mirror, the gold line — is set into that resin while it is still soft.

    A bangle takes forty minutes of handwork before it is polished. A bridal stack takes a week, because the colours have to be dyed in sequence and the sizes have to match to a millimetre.

    “A machine can make a circle. It cannot make a circle that fits one hand.”

    Zohra Begum, third-generation lac bangle maker, Laad Bazaar.

    HOW IT IS MADE

    Seven steps, all by hand

    The same order every time. The only thing that changes between a five-bangle stack and a bridal set is how long you stand at the flame.

    1. 01

      Melting

      Lac sticks are warmed over a low flame until they take a fingerprint without sticking. Too hot and the colour burns; too cool and the bangle cracks at the seam.

    2. 02

      Winding

      The resin is wound around a wooden mandrel in a flat ribbon until the ring is the right thickness — between six and eight millimetres for a bangle, wider for a kada.

    3. 03

      Joining

      The seam is pressed and rolled while warm, then rubbed with a palm-full of oil. A good seam is invisible from both faces and holds when the bangle is dropped on stone.

    4. 04

      Colouring

      Pigment is worked into the surface in thin layers, not painted on. Rani pink takes three passes; peacock green takes two and a rest.

    5. 05

      Inlay

      Mirror, sequin or foil is set with a hot needle. Each piece is pressed into the soft surface and sealed with a drop of clear lac.

    6. 06

      Sizing

      The bangle is tried on a wooden gauge and burnished from the inside until it slips over the knuckles without a struggle and sits without turning.

    7. 07

      Polishing

      Palm wax and a soft cloth. No varnish — lacquer that is sprayed on dulls within a month, and cannot be repaired in the shop.
