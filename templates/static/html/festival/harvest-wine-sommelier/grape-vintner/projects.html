What is in the cellar, and what it cost the year

Every wine here was picked by this vineyard and made in this cellar. Nothing is bought in. The figures are the ones written on the tank, not a tasting note written afterwards.

Lot 01 · 2026

Sauvignon Blanc, stainless

Picked on 18 February at night, pressed by ten, fermented at 14 °C for sixteen days. No oak, no fining, filtered once. It smells of guava and cut grass, which is what this block does in a dry year, and it is the wine I would put in front of someone who thinks Nashik only makes heavy reds.

Picked 18 Feb 2026 · 21.8 °Brix Acid 6.4 g/L · pH 3.24 Vessel stainless, 14 °C Bottles 5,200 · ₹850

Lot 02 · 2026

Chenin Blanc, barrel

Four barrels, a third new, fermented in oak and left on the lees until June with a stir once a fortnight. Chenin is the grape that holds acid in this heat, so it can take the oak without going soft. Drink it now or in four years; it will be a different wine and neither is wrong.

Picked 24 Feb 2026 · 22.6 °Brix Acid 5.9 g/L · pH 3.31 Vessel 4 barrels, 1 new Bottles 2,100 · ₹1,450

Lot 03 · 2026

Rosé of Shiraz

Four hours on the skins, pressed before the colour went too far, fermented cold. The colour of a watermelon and the acid of a white wine, which is the whole point and the reason it sells out by April every year. Filtered, because it has to survive a car boot in 38 °C.

Picked 21 Feb 2026 · 21.2 °Brix Acid 6.1 g/L · pH 3.28 Vessel stainless, 12 °C Bottles 1,800 · ₹780

Lot 04 · 2026

Shiraz, barrel

Picked on 6 March, two weeks after the first white, and fermented with 15 per cent whole bunches because the stems were ripe this year. Twelve months in French oak, a third new. Pepper, black fruit and a tannin that is still settling — it will be better in 2028 than it is now.

Picked 6 Mar 2026 · 24.1 °Brix Acid 5.2 g/L · pH 3.62 Vessel 18 barrels, 6 new Bottles 6,300 · ₹1,900

Lot 05 · 2026

Cabernet Sauvignon, ridge block

The last fruit in every year and the smallest berries. Picked 11 March at 24.4 °Brix, macerated for twenty-one days, then eighteen months in barrel. It is the most structured wine we make and the one that needs food, a decanter and an hour of your patience.

Picked 11 Mar 2026 · 24.4 °Brix Acid 5.0 g/L · pH 3.68 Vessel 12 barrels, 5 new Bottles 3,000 · ₹2,300

Trial · 2026

Chenin, second fermentation

Eight hundred bottles of the April-pruned Chenin, picked in September at 18.9 °Brix with 8.4 g/L of acid, put through a second fermentation in the bottle and left on the lees for eleven months. It is a trial, not a product; half of it will be disgorged in March and the other half kept to see what happens.

Picked 22 Sep 2026 · 18.9 °Brix Acid 8.4 g/L · pH 3.12 Vessel bottle, 11 months on lees Bottles 800 · not for sale

Library · 2024

Shiraz, library release

The cool year. Picked late, at 23.6 °Brix, and the wine is lighter than the 2026 with more pepper and less alcohol. We kept forty cases back and open one every February to see whether the decision to hold it was right. So far it is.

Picked 14 Mar 2024 · 23.6 °Brix Acid 5.6 g/L · pH 3.58 Vessel 14 barrels, 4 new Bottles 480 left · ₹2,600

Library · 2024

Chenin Blanc, three years on

Kept from the 2024 barrel lot as an argument against drinking everything young. Honey, quince, a waxy texture and the acid still doing its work at the back. There are ninety bottles and I am not in a hurry.

Picked 8 Mar 2024 · 22.2 °Brix Acid 6.2 g/L · pH 3.3 Vessel 3 barrels, 1 new Bottles 90 left · ₹2,100

How a tasting runs

Six wines, an hour and a half, at the long table in the cellar with the barrels behind you. It is not a tour and there is no video.

What you taste

Two whites, the rosé, two reds and whatever is open in the barrel that week. Bread, cheese and a plate of something salty, because six wines on an empty stomach at two in the afternoon is a mistake people make once.

What you pay

₹1,400 a person, refunded against any purchase of six bottles or more. Groups of more than eight need a week’s notice and a different table, which we set up between the tanks and which is honestly the better room.

When

Friday to Sunday, at 11.00 and 16.00, from October to March. In February and early March the cellar is working and the tastings stop — there is no room, no clean glass and no one with time to talk to you.
