The Diwali diya

A diya is a small promise: it must hold oil for an evening and not crack at the flame. Mine are thrown on the wheel — a thrown wall is even, and an even wall does not split when the oil heats. Soak them two hours before the festival night and they will drink less oil and burn longer.

The matka that sweats

People glaze a matka to make it pretty and kill the very thing it does. An unglazed matka sweats — water seeps to the wall and evaporates, and that is what cools what is inside. Seal the pot and you have a bucket with a story. My matkas stay bare clay, and on a May afternoon the water in them sits about four degrees below the shade.

Planters & garba pots

Planters with the drainage hole thrown into the base while the clay is wet — a hole drilled after firing is where the crack starts. And for Navratri, the perforated garba pot: the holes are cut at the leather-hard stage, one by one, so the lamp inside throws its pattern on the wall for nine nights.

Commissions

How an order moves through this yard

No forms with twelve fields, no advance beyond what the clay costs. The waitlist is honest: in October it is long, in February it is short, and I will tell you which before you pay anything.

STEP 1

Tell me the order

Piece, count and the day you need them — by the order form, by email, or at the workshop. I reckon it on the planner's figures and answer within two working days.

STEP 2

Deposit 30%

Thirty per cent books your place in the kiln queue. The rest is paid when the pieces are fired, counted and sound — never before.

STEP 3

Drying & firing

Your pieces are thrown, dried their full days, and fired. If a load goes wrong I say so the same week and re-throw at my cost.

STEP 4

Collect or dispatch

Collect from Khavda, or I pack in straw and send by transport to Bhuj, Ahmedabad and beyond. Breakage in transit on orders over ₹5,000 is replaced free.
