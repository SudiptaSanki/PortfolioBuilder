00Who pours

I learned to taste
in a room that smelled
of wet earth.

My grandmother’s palenque was behind her house in Tlacolula, under a roof of corrugated tin, and the still ran when the mash told her it was ready. Nothing about that room appears in a tasting note. Everything I know about mezcal came from it.

Fifteen years, three rooms

I was born in Tlacolula de Matamoros and spent every school holiday at the palenque, mostly carrying things and being told not to run near the oven. My grandmother, Elvira, sold mezcal by the litre to neighbours and by the bottle to a man from the city who came on Tuesdays. She never called herself a producer. She called herself a cook who also made mezcal.

I studied gastronomy in Puebla, worked two years in a restaurant kitchen in Mexico City, and came home in 2011 because my mother was ill and because I had begun to notice that the mezcal on the city’s bar lists tasted like the label rather than the place. I started writing down what my grandmother did, in order, with times. She found this funny and then useful.

The tastings began by accident. A group of cyclists came through Tlacolula in 2013, stayed for lunch, and asked to try the bottles on the shelf. I charged them 150 pesos each, poured five, and talked for two hours. Two of them are still customers. The room on Macedonio Alcalá opened in 2017, after a bank loan and a great deal of arguing about chairs.

In 2019 I passed the Consejo Regulador del Mezcal’s certification for sensory evaluation — CRM, level 2, which is mostly an examination of your nose under pressure. I am also, since 2021, one of the people who taste for a small collective of nine palenques in the Valles Centrales, deciding what is good enough to carry a label and what goes back to the family to drink.

What I will not pour

  • Anything with a worm in it, sold as a novelty. The gusano lives in some agaves; it is a snack, not an ingredient. It became a marketing device in the 1940s and it has done mezcal no favours since.
  • Mezcal that has been aged in whisky barrels for a year to taste like whisky. Ageing in glass or clay to rest it is one thing; dressing it as bourbon is another.
  • Anything I cannot name the village, the agave and the person for. If the label says “artisanal” and nothing else, the word is doing the work of a missing fact.
  • Flavoured “mezcal” with fruit syrup and a low ABV. There is a place for a cocktail. It is not this table.

The house rule

No shots. No salt-and-lime ritual. A copita is 40 ml and it should take you twenty minutes. If you want to get drunk quickly, there are cheaper places on the zócalo and I will point you to one.

“El mezcal no se toma, se escucha.”

Elvira Bautista, 1931–2018 · my grandmother, who would have charged you for the second copita

How a tasting actually goes

You arrive at seven. There is bread, salt and a glass of water at your place, and nothing else. I pour the first copita before I explain anything, because the first impression is yours and I would rather not put words in your mouth. Then we talk about what you noticed. Then I tell you where it came from.

We work from the lightest to the heaviest, which usually means espadín first and jabalí or pechuga last. Between the third and fourth there is a pause of ten minutes with food, because alcohol numbs the nose before it numbs the tongue and a fourth pour taken too quickly is money wasted.

Nobody is asked to describe “notes of leather and mango” unless they want to. “It smells like my uncle’s garage” is a better tasting note than most of what is printed on the back of a bottle, and I will write it down and use it.

The room seats eight because that is how many people can hear each other without raising their voices.

What is on the table

Clay copitas from Atzompa — the wide mouth lets the smoke out, which is why mezcal is not served in a whisky glass.

Water, bread, Oaxaca cheese, and a small dish of sal de gusano for the last pour.

What I ask of you

Eat first. Arrive on time. Tell me if you do not drink alcohol before I pour, not after. And if a mezcal tastes of nothing to you, say so — I will not be offended and it helps me choose the next one.

Come on a Thursday.

That is when the new bottles are open and the room is at its quietest. Bookings are by message, confirmed by a person, and cancelled without drama if the weather keeps your flight in Mexico City.
