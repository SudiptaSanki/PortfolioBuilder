01Six ways to sit at the table

Tastings, prices
and what is poured.

Everything below is per person, in Mexican pesos, and includes the copitas, water, bread and the food named. The bottles change with what the palenques bring me; the shape of each tasting does not. Book three days ahead for the table, two weeks for the palenque.

01

The Agave Flight

2 hours · Thu–Sat, 19:00 · 9 seats $1,450

The one to book first. Five copitas across the cabinet — espadín, tobalá, tepeztate and two that change weekly — poured light to heavy, with a ten-minute pause and food in the middle. You leave knowing what smoke is and what it is not.

02

The Wild Agave Table

2.5 hours · Fridays, 19:00 · 6 seats $2,600

Six pours, all wild or semi-wild: tobalá, tepeztate, jabalí, a madrecuixe, a bicuixe, and whatever Don Chucho sends that month. We talk about why these take eighteen years to mature, why the price is what it is, and what happens to a village when the agaves are cut before they flower.

6 copitasWild agavesChocolateAllocation talk
03

Market to Copita

4 hours · Saturdays, 10:00 · 8 people $3,200

We start at the Abastos market with a shopping list — chapulines, quesillo, a pineapple, a bar of chocolate — then come back and taste four mezcals against them. The pairing is the point: fat against smoke, acid against heat, sweetness where a mezcal has none.

04

Palenque Day

8 hours · On request, Tue–Thu · up to 6 people $9,800

Matatlán and back, in a car with a driver. You stand in the pit, turn the tahona if you like, and taste at three stages from the still, then three finished mezcals with the family who made them. Lunch is cooked by whoever is in the kitchen that day. Two weeks’ notice.

05

The Sommelier’s Hour

75 minutes · Thursdays, 21:30 · 4 seats $2,100

After the main table clears, four seats at the bar for people who already know what they like. Three rare pours and one blind. The blind one is usually older than the person drinking it, and it is usually the one they end up buying a bottle of.

06

Private Table

2–3 hours · Any evening · 6–14 people $2,200 p.p.

The room closed for your group, the flight built around what you already drink, and a short talk about agave in Oaxaca if the group wants one. For birthdays, work trips, and the occasional wedding party that has behaved well enough to be trusted with the good bottles.

Room closedCustom flightFood on requestMinimum 6

Fine print, in plain Spanish and English

Payment. Card or cash at the end of the night. A deposit of 30% holds a private table; the rest is charged on the evening. Prices include tax.

Cancellations. 48 hours for the table, one week for the palenque day, because the family buys food and takes the day off. Under 48 hours the deposit is kept, and I will try to move you to another night if the seats fill.

Age and sobriety. No under-18s, and I will stop pouring for anyone who has had enough. This is not a moral position; it is the only way the room stays worth sitting in.

Groups. The main table seats nine and is shared. If your group is louder than the room, I will ask you to bring it down, once, politely.

Pregnancy, medication, driving. Tell me before the first pour. There is water, coffee and a very good tepache for anyone not drinking.

Best value

The Agave Flight at $1,450 is the one I would send a friend to. The Wild Agave Table is the one I would send an enthusiast to.

Bottles to take home

I sell what I pour, at the same price as the palenque, with no markup over the case I bought. Expect $700–$1,900 for 750 ml. I will write the village and the year on the label for you.

Also on the shelf

Sal de gusano, clay copitas from Atzompa, and a small book of tasting notes in Spanish that I wrote and printed badly in 2022.
