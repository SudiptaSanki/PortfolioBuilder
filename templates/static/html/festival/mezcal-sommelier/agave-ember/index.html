01The four days

Nothing here is distilled from a syrup.

01 / jima

The cut

A jimador opens the rosette with a coa and takes the piña — forty to ninety kilos of it, depending on the species. Espadín is farmed and comes out in eight years. Tobalá and Tepeztate are wild or semi-wild and take twelve to twenty-five; nobody plants them on a schedule.

02 / horno

The pit

A cone of volcanic stone is heated with oak for a day, the piñas go in, a petate and earth go over, and four to five days later the sugars are roasted rather than steamed. This is where the smoke in the glass is born — not added, not flavoured.

03 / tahona

The crush

A stone wheel pulled by a horse turns in a circular trough until the roasted agave is a fibrous mash. It is slow, it is loud, and it leaves the fibres intact for the ferment. A mechanical mill is faster and makes a flatter mezcal; you can taste the difference at the second sip.

04 / still

The double pass

Wild yeast ferments the mash in pine or clay for four to twelve days, then a copper alembic is run twice. The first pass is ordinaire, the second is mezcal; the head and tail are cut by smell and by the bead, not by a thermometer.
