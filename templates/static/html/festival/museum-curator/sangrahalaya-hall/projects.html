The Cloth and the Comb

84 garments · 31 pattern books · 6 recorded voices

Chikankari from court embroidery to household economy. The exhibition follows one stitch family, bakhiya, across 90 years and three markets, and ends in the room where the embroidery is still done.

12 loans18,400 visitors to date
2 Oct 2026 — 18 Jan 2027Gallery 1

Paper Rooms

46 drawings · 9 lenders · 3 site models

Awadhi architectural drawings from the 1870s to the 1950s, including a set of elevations for a house in Kaiserbagh that was never built. The room is hung low, at drawing height.

9 loans11,200 visitors to date
PermanentGallery 4

The Loud Room

212 objects · brass, bells and betel

Our most-used gallery: ewers, lotas, temple bells and betel boxes, shown at the height they were used. Visitors are allowed to ring three of the bells, which is why the room is called what it is.

Photographs of a Musicians' Quarter

120 photographs · 4 loans · 1 listening room

Studio portraits and street photographs from a neighbourhood of musicians, with 18 oral histories cut to three minutes each. In preparation; the walls go up in January.

Reading room

For the people who want the footnotes.

Object lists, condition reports, catalogues and the accession register are available to researchers on Tuesdays and Thursdays, 11:00–16:00, by appointment.

What to ask for

An accession number helps. If you do not have one, write with a description and a photograph, and Ira will search the register before you come.

Photography without flash is allowed for study; publication needs a written request.

What to bring

Photo ID and a pencil. Ink, food and water are not allowed near the drawings.

Lockers at the desk; laptops welcome; the wifi is unreliable.

Book a visit

Use the enquiry form and choose “Research visit”.

Requests are answered within three working days. Several objects need 48 hours' notice to move.

Borrow something, or lend us something.

Loan requests are read against the next three years of the programme. We would rather say no early than keep an object waiting in a crate.
