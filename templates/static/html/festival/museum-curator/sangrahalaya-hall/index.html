The collection

What we keep, and why.

Five collecting areas, each with a named keeper and a storage plan. An object enters the collection only if we can say where it came from and what it will teach.

01

Textiles

Chikankari, zardozi, jamdani and household cotton, stored flat in acid-free tissue and rotated every eighteen months.

1,840 objects · keeper: R. Ahmed
02

Awadhi brass

Ewers, lotas, bells and betel boxes, mostly nineteenth century, much of it still capable of being used.

612 objects · keeper: S. Mirza
03

Miniatures

Murshidabad, Lucknow and Company-school works on paper, kept at 20 °C and 50 per cent relative humidity.

388 objects · keeper: A. Bhatt
04

Photographs

Studio portraits, street photographs and family albums from the 1880s to the 1980s, with 2,410 negatives scanned.

2,410 objects · keeper: N. Qureshi
05

Oral histories

Recordings with embroiderers, brassworkers and musicians, transcribed in Hindi and Awadhi, with English summaries.

96 recordings · keeper: Dr I. Sengupta

Accession book

Three objects, with their paperwork.

Provenance is not a formality. It is the difference between a museum object and a beautiful thing in a case.

SGH.1962.14.3Textile

Chikan sari

Lucknow · c. 1900 · cotton muslin, white thread

Acquired from the family of Begum Nasir Fatima, who wore it at her wedding in 1904. The embroidery is bakhiya, worked from the reverse, with 32 stitch varieties counted. The pallu carries a grapevine border and a small repair in the corner, made by the same hand.

SGH.1978.4.1Metalwork

Brass ewer

Awadh · c. 1850 · brass, cast and engraved

Bought at a house sale in Kaiserbagh with its original lid, which is rare. The engraved band shows a chinar leaf repeat; the spout has been repaired twice, once with a lead solder that we have left in place rather than erased. A vessel that has been mended is a vessel that has been used.

SGH.1954.9.7Painting

Garden miniature

Murshidabad · c. 1780 · gouache on paper, gold leaf

One of four leaves from an album broken up before the museum existed. Two figures sit in a walled garden; the tree is in flower out of season, a convention rather than a mistake. The frame is later, added in 1911, and is catalogued separately.

Hang the wall

Plan an elevation before you lift a hammer.

A curator's working tool: set the wall, the works and the eye line, and see where every frame hangs. Distances are in centimetres, measured to the centre of each work.

Wall planner

Standard practice: hang to a 150 cm centre line unless the room or the work argues otherwise. Fixings are counted at two per frame; add a third for anything wider than a metre.
