Issue 14 · Hemanta · the cold half of the year

Winter is a season for the middle of the day

In the old calendars the year turns at the winter solstice, and the body turns with it: heavier food, earlier nights, and a kind of deliberate slowness that the rest of the year will not allow. This issue is about that slowness, and about the four things I do between November and January that are worth the trouble.

By Ananya Rao · 12 November 2026 · 9 min read

The first winter I kept a proper routine, I was living in a flat with one window that faced a wall. I had been told that the season called for warmth, oil, sleep and slow food, and I did all four badly for about six weeks before anything changed. What changed first was not my digestion or my skin. It was that I stopped dreading the evenings.

Nine years later the routine is smaller and less impressive than it was at the start. Two of the old instructions turned out to be things I had read rather than things I needed. The rest are here, in the order I do them.

The winter routine

The version I actually keep between November and January. Times are for Pune; the order matters more than the clock.

  1. 06:15

    Warm water, then nothing

    No phone for the first half hour. I used to think this was a moral position; it is simply the only way I have found to notice how I woke up.

  2. 06:45

    Oil before the bath

    Sesame oil, warmed, on the limbs and the soles of the feet, ten minutes before hot water. Skip it on a day you are unwell or in a hurry. The floor will be slippery.

  3. 08:00

    A cooked breakfast

    Usually a millet porridge with ghee and jaggery, or two eggs and a roti. Cold cereal in November is a habit from a different climate.

  4. 12:30

    The main meal

    Rice or roti, dal, a cooked vegetable, curd if the day is not damp. The largest meal at midday, when the sun is doing the work.

  5. 16:00

    Something warm

    Tulsi and ginger, or a small cup of soup. This is the hour I used to spend on biscuits, and the change did more for my evenings than anything else on this list.

  6. 21:30

    Lights low, feet warm

    Screens off, a lamp on, socks on. I read paper in winter because a screen at that hour keeps me awake and I have stopped pretending otherwise.

From the letters

“You wrote that you stopped taking the ashwagandha because you could not tell whether it was working. I have been taking it for two years and I also cannot tell. What I wanted to ask is whether the not-knowing is the point.”

Meera, Kochi · letter printed with permission, shortened

My answer, in short: no, the not-knowing is not the point, and it is a good reason to stop paying for something. The routine works because it is a routine. That is a less interesting sentence than the one about herbs, and it is the true one. The full reply is in the archive.
