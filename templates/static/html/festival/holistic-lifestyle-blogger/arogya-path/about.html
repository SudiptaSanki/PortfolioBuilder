About the journal

Nine years of one small routine,
written down honestly

Arogya Path began as a notebook I kept for myself, to remember which winter habits actually helped and which I had read somewhere and never tested. It is still that, with an editor and a deadline.

Ananya Rao · Pune

I am not a doctor. I have a degree in food science, eight years of working in a test kitchen, and a family that has cooked by the season for four generations. When I write about what to eat in November, I am writing from those three places: the training, the work, and the kitchen I grew up in, in that order of confidence.

The tradition I draw on is Ayurveda, and I use it the way I use any old body of knowledge: carefully, with attention to which parts are testable and which parts are poetry. The poetry is not worthless. A routine that gives shape to a day is doing something real, even when the explanation attached to it is older than the evidence. What I try not to do is dress the poetry up as proof.

The three rules of this journal

  1. Say which part is tradition and which part is me. Every essay notes where an instruction comes from and whether I have tried it.
  2. Never sell the thing being recommended. No brand, no affiliate link, no supplement I have a stake in. If I mention a shop, it is because I buy there.
  3. Do not turn a habit into a treatment. Nothing here is offered as a cure, and any essay that touches on a condition links to a clinician's explanation as well as mine.

“The routine works because it is a routine. That is a less interesting sentence than the one about herbs, and it is the true one.”

How an issue is made

Six weeks per issue. Two weeks of reading and taking notes, one week of trying the thing properly, two weeks of writing and cutting, and a final week with a friend who is a physiotherapist and reads everything for claims I cannot support. The reading list for each issue is in the archive, including the sources I decided not to use and why.

Photographs are rare because I am a poor photographer and refuse to use stock. The drawings are mine, or they are by a friend in Nashik who lets me use them for the price of lunch.

What this journal is not

  • It is not a substitute for a doctor, a dietitian or a physiotherapist, and it does not diagnose anything.
  • It is not a panchakarma clinic, a retreat, or a place to buy herbs.
  • It is not written for people managing a serious illness. Some of it may still be useful, but the useful parts are the routine, not the remedies.

— Ananya

Kothrud, Pune · November 2026

About the writer

Ananya Rao, 38. Grew up in Nashik, studied food science in Mysore, worked eight years in a test kitchen in Pune. Writes, cooks, and teaches a Saturday morning cooking class that has a waiting list she is embarrassed by.

Editorial independence

The journal has no advertising and takes no payment for mentions. It is supported by subscriptions and by the cooking classes, which are listed nowhere on this site because they fill up by word of mouth.

A note on language

I use the Sanskrit terms where they are more precise than the English, and explain them the first time. If a word is only there to sound authoritative, it gets cut.

How I got here

  1. 2008

    A notebook in Nashik

    My grandmother's routine, written down because I was leaving home and wanted the recipes in her handwriting.

  2. 2013

    The test kitchen

    Eight years developing packaged food, which taught me exactly how much sugar can hide behind the word “traditional”.

  3. 2019

    The first issue

    Twelve pages, forty copies, printed at a shop near Deccan. Eleven people replied, which felt like enough to continue.

  4. 2022

    The correction

    I removed an essay about a herb I had recommended without evidence. The correction is still published, with the original essay, because deleting it would make the archive a liar.

  5. 2026

    Issue 14

    This one, written in a flat with three windows and a balcony full of tulsi that the pigeons keep attacking.

Please read this

Arogya Path describes traditional practice and one person's experience of it. It is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, nursing, taking medication, managing a chronic condition, or simply not sure, speak to a qualified clinician before changing what you eat, how you sleep, or what you take.

If something in an issue helped, I am glad. If something in it contradicted your doctor, your doctor is right.
