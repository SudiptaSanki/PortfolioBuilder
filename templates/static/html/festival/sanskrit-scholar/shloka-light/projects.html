Five texts, and what happened to them.

Editions are listed with their witnesses, because an edition without a list of witnesses is an opinion. Where a book is out of print, the note says so rather than pretending otherwise.

१

Mṛcchakaṭika, act four

In progressCritical edition with apparatusSince 2023

The act everyone skips, and the one where the play’s language changes most between copies. Three Deccan witnesses, one of them missing two folios at the point where the merchant’s house is described. The apparatus records spelling as well as substance; the orthographic variants are the best evidence for the scribal networks around Pune and Kolhapur in the eighteenth century.

तीन3 witnesses, one incomplete Sanskrit, PrākritWith English apparatus 2027Expected from the institute press

२

Āryabhaṭīya, chapter two

PublishedEdition and translation2023

The chapter on the measurement of time, edited from two manuscripts and collated against the 1874 printed text. The translation keeps the verse numbers in the margin and does not modernise the units. A short appendix explains the arithmetic for readers who, like me, need to see a worked example before believing a rule.

द्वौ2 manuscripts + 1 printed edition 148 pp.Hardback, Sanskrit and English In printInstitute press, Pune

३

A Deccan anthology of stotras

PublishedCatalogue and edition2021

Twenty-two hymns gathered from fourteen manuscripts in six collections, most of them copied for private use and never printed. The introduction is deliberately short; the bulk of the book is the texts, the variants, and an index of first lines that took a year longer than I told anyone it would.

चतुर्दश14 manuscripts, 6 collections 22 hymnsWith first-line index 2021Out of print; PDF on request

४

The chandas chapter of the Agni Purāṇa

PublishedTranslation with commentary2019

A working translation of the chapter on metre, with the gaṇa tables laid out as tables rather than as verse. The commentary is aimed at a reader who has a verse in front of them and no teacher: it explains each definition twice, once in words and once with a scanned example, because the second explanation is the one that works.

छन्दःTranslation + commentary 212 pp.Paperback, English 2019Second printing 2022

५

Śiśupālavadha, canto one

PublishedSchool edition2017

Made for the first-year class at the institute and still used there. Each verse is given with its metre named in the margin, a word-by-word gloss, and a note that explains the compound only as far as the grammar the class has already met. It is not a scholarly edition and does not pretend to be; it is a book that gets a student to the end of a difficult canto without despair.

प्रथमःCanto one, 96 verses 134 pp.Paperback, Sanskrit and Marathi 2017Fourth printing 2024

A specimen

How a variant is recorded

From the Mṛcchakaṭika apparatus, act four, verse 12. The base text reads कथमपि; the Kolhapur copy reads कथमिव. The difference is one vowel and it changes the tone of the line.

कथमपि कृतवानसि त्वं मयि स्नेहम् ॥

katham api kṛtavān asi tvaṃ mayi sneham — “somehow you have placed affection in me.”

Apparatus: katham iva K (Kolhapur, 1782) · katham api P B · metre requires katham api, and the Kolhapur reading is therefore either a correction or a scribal memory of a different recension. I have kept the base reading and noted the alternative in full.
