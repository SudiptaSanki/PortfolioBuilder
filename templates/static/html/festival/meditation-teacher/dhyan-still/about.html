The practice · since 2012

Nothing here needs
you to believe
anything.

I teach attention and breath. The vocabulary is Buddhist and the frame is Indian, because that is where the training came from, but the instructions are plain enough to test for yourself this afternoon.

I came to meditation through insomnia, not through seeking. In 2008 I was working as a physiotherapist in Pune, sleeping four hours a night, and a patient gave me a copy of a book I did not finish. What finally worked was a ten-day course in Igatpuri in 2010, which was boring, uncomfortable and the first thing in two years that had made any difference.

The training since then has been unglamorous. A two-year teacher programme in the Vipassana tradition, finished in 2015. Yoga nidra training in Dharamshala with Swami Atmapriyananda, whose name is grander than his manner. A great deal of anatomy, because I had come from physiotherapy and wanted to understand what I was asking bodies to do when I told them to sit still.

The shala in Tapovan opened in 2016, two rooms and a courtyard above a bookshop. It holds twelve people on cushions, which is the number at which the room still feels like a room. I teach one-to-one on Tuesdays and Thursdays, the six-week course twice a year, and I keep Friday for visiting practitioners who need the space.

What I am careful about: I am not a therapist. Meditation is not a treatment for depression, trauma or grief, and I have referred more people to a psychologist than I have taken onto a course. If you are in crisis, please use the numbers at the bottom of this page rather than a booking form.

What I am not careful about: the incidental things. Tea is always on. People arrive early and leave late. The shala has a cat called Aur, who has never once respected a silence.

I keep a list of local psychologists in Rishikesh and Dehradun for anyone who needs more than a meditation course can offer.

What a first session is like

An hour, in four parts

MinutesWhat happens
0–10We talk. What your days are like, what you have tried, what you are hoping for, and what would tell you this is not working.
10–20Posture and breathing mechanics, on a chair if that is easier than a cushion. No lotus, no forcing, no pain as a teaching tool.
20–40A guided sit, mostly silence, with one or two instructions when they are needed and not more.
40–60What you noticed. Then a practice small enough to do on a Tuesday when nothing is going well, written down on a card for you to take.

v

What I ask of you

Come on time, sit as still as you comfortably can, and tell me the truth about whether it is working. The rest is negotiable.
