Eleven bottles, five flowers, one fire.

The house makes eleven attars. These five carry the range: a rose, a jasmine, a kewra, an earth and a winter blend. Each bottle is dated by lot, because a lot is a season and no two seasons smell the same.

2026 · LOT KB-200-SRB

Gulab

Rose attar from a March lot, distilled into sandalwood over four charges. Picked at five in the morning, in the third week of the season, when the oil is highest and the petals are still cold.

The first smell is green and almost sharp; ten minutes later it is the rose everyone recognises, and by evening it is warm wood with a faint jam sweetness. It is the house's most requested bottle and the one Rehana is most critical of.

2025 · LOT KB-150-JSC

Chameli

Jasmine, picked at night when the flower opens, distilled the same night into vetiver. The combination is drier than the rose and a little green at the edges, which is what the vetiver does to a white flower.

The lot was small — one hundred and fifty kilos — because the June rain came early and flattened two fields. What was left was good, and it is nearly gone.

2025 · LOT KB-120-MSB

Mitti

The smell of the first rain on ground that has been baked all summer. Discs of earth are fired, broken and distilled into sandalwood; what comes out is not earth and not sandal but the moment they meet.

The 2025 lot came from a single afternoon of rain in July. Two days later would have been too late, which is why the bottle is dated by the week and not the month.

2024 · LOT KB-180-KRC

Kewra

Screwpine, distilled into sandalwood with a little saffron in the receiver. Kewra is used across north India in food and drink, and the attar carries that association: it smells cool and slightly sweet, like the inside of a sharbat glass.

It is the least understood bottle on the shelf and the one Rehana recommends to people who say they do not like rose.

2024 · LOT KB-300-SHM

Shamama

The winter blend: about twenty herbs, flowers and spices, distilled in stages and rested for a year. The recipe is written in the 1938 ledger and has been changed twice since, both times by Rehana's father and both times noted in the margin.

It is not a single flower, so it is not a single smell. On the wrist it moves from spice to wood to something almost medicinal, and then stays there for a day.

Wearing · 10

How to wear attar

ONE DROP

On the wrist

One drop, warmed between the wrists, not rubbed. Attar is oil; rubbing it drives the top note off and shortens the whole thing.

Best for rose, jasmine
Lasts 8–12 hours
A TOUCH

Behind the ear

A smaller touch behind the ear or at the base of the throat. The smell rises with the body's heat instead of sitting on the skin.

Best for shamama, kewra
Lasts a day
A CLOTH

On a scarf

A drop on the hem of a scarf or the inside of a collar will outlast the day and come back when the cloth moves.

Best for mitti
Lasts weeks on wool
