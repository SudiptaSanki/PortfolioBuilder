さとう屋Sato-ya, under the lanterns

I am Sato Kenji, and this is my stall. Fukuoka, the Nakasu riverbank, the second week of July — Hakata Gion Yamakasa, when the floats race past at dawn and my lanterns are lit before they are even dressed. Fourteen seasons I have fed this city's festivals: takoyaki first, yakisoba second, grilled corn when the families come.

One rule hangs over my grill: sauce goes on with a brush — a hake (a wide, flat brush). A squeeze bottle draws a stripe. A brush paints the whole ball.

The menu board

お品書き

Takoyaki

たこ焼き — octopus balls

Eight balls, crisped outside, soft in the middle, a real piece of octopus in every one. Sauce painted on with the brush, then aonori (green seaweed flakes) and katsuobushi (bonito flakes) that move in the heat.

¥600per boat of 8

Yakisoba

やきそば — fried noodles

Noodles fried on the flat top with cabbage, pork and pickled ginger, sauced dark and finished with aonori. Cooked in small pans of six — never a mountain of noodles going soft.

¥700per tray

Yaki-tōmorokoshi

やきとうもろこし — grilled corn

A whole cob grilled and brushed with soy sauce until the edges catch. The children's dish, and the one that sells out first when the families come to Hōjōya.

¥400per cob

The festival prep planner

仕込み計算

Every stall owner does this sum the night before, on the back of a pitch receipt. This is mine, printed in full: tell it how many people will pass the stall and how many hours the festival runs, and it works out the prep — portions, batter, sauce, gas and the night's expected takings — from the rates in the table. No hidden numbers; the table is the planner.

Last Yamakasa, in short

山笠の記録
Hakata Gion Yamakasa · July 2025

Nine nights, and the last ball went at 21:40

The street association asked for a stall that would still be serving when the yoiyama (the eve of the race) crowd came off the floats. I prepped for 1,900 portions across nine nights and sold 1,940 — the corn ran ahead of its share, as it always does when children are up past their bedtime. On the final night the takoyaki sold out at 21:40, and I wrote it on the board in chalk rather than let people queue for nothing. A queue deserves the truth early.
