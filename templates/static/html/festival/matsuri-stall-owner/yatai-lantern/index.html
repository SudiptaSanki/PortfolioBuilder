One night, hour by hour

The cart has its own clock

  1. 3.30 p.m.

    Batter

    Flour, dashi, egg, grated nagaimo, mixed in the back of the shop and left to rest for an hour. The octopus is cut the night before, into pieces that fit one hole exactly.

  2. 4.40 p.m.

    Push out

    Four hundred metres from the shop to the corner, by hand, because the cart has no engine and the street is closed to cars by five. Two of us on the handle, one walking ahead with the lanterns.

  3. 5.00 p.m.

    Light

    The lanterns go on and the pan goes on. The first batch of the night is never sold — it seasons the pan and goes to whoever is standing closest.

  4. 7.00 p.m.

    Peak

    The queue is longest at seven and again at nine. One person turns, one person sauces and boxes, one takes the money. Nobody does two jobs at once after seven o'clock.

  5. 9.40 p.m.

    Last order

    Called out, not written. After that the pan is cleaned while it is still hot, which is the only way to clean it, and the gas is shut at the bottle.

  6. 10.20 p.m.

    Home

    The lantern comes down last. If it is a festival night with fireworks, add two hours and subtract one temper.
