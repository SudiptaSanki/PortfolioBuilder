Nights · 2024–2026

Six nights,
counted in portions

A night is remembered by three things: how many portions went out, when the last order was called, and what went wrong. These are six of them, including the wet ones.

Sumida River fireworks

26 July 2026 · Asakusa side · festival eve

The biggest night of our year. We push the cart out at four instead of half past, because by five the street is closed by the police and the corner is full. Three pans, five of us, and the queue reaches the corner three times before the first rocket.

We sold 1,180 portions between five and ten, and turned people away from nine forty. The gas ran out at 9:12 and Sota cycled home for the spare bottle, which took eleven minutes and cost us about sixty portions.

Sanja Matsuri, third day

17 May 2026 · Kaminarimon-dori · festival

The mikoshi come past the corner at eleven in the morning and the street does not empty until dark. We opened at noon, which we only do twice a year, and Michiko ran the money from noon to nine without sitting down.

A television crew filmed the pan for twenty minutes and bought nothing. A man from Sapporo bought six portions, ate two, and asked us to post the rest to his hotel. We did not.

A school festival, Katsushika

3 November 2025 · hired cart · 400 visitors

A primary school hired the cart for its autumn festival. We cannot legally sell on school grounds, so the school bought 400 portions in advance and gave them out for ¥100 a plate, with the difference from the PTA.

Children under six do not want takoyaki; they want the toothpick and the sauce. We made 160 portions of yakisoba that day, the most we have ever made in one place, and taught four teachers how to turn a ball.

A wet Tuesday in June

11 June 2025 · Asakusa · weekday

Rain from four o'clock, the kind that does not stop and does not get heavier. We opened anyway, put the awning out, and sold 96 portions in five hours to people who were already wet and did not want to go home yet.

Three of them were neighbours. One stayed until closing and helped carry the pan in. A weekday in the rain is not a night you make money on; it is a night you keep the corner.

A wedding after-party

19 April 2025 · Yanaka · cart hired by the hour

A couple who had their first date at the cart hired it for the party in a courtyard in Yanaka. We pushed it there at three, which took fifty minutes and one argument with a parked car, and served 260 portions from six to nine.

The bride's grandmother asked for the sauce recipe. Michiko said it was a family secret and then wrote it out for her anyway, which is the most Michiko thing that has ever happened.

Yokohama food festival

5 October 2024 · two-day event · 14 hours of service

Our only trip out of Tokyo. The organisers wanted the cart itself, not a stall, and paid to move it on a flatbed. Two days, 1,640 portions, and a queue that never ended because the site was twice the size of our street.

On the second morning we found the pan had warped overnight in the cold. Hiroshi spent forty minutes with a mallet and a block, and the pan has been slightly uneven ever since — you can see it in the first batch, which cooks half a minute faster on one side.

“Eight hundred portions and the pan never stopped. What I remember is the boy at the front of the queue who had been saving his pocket money and could not decide between takoyaki and yakisoba. He got both.”
