Biography · 1990–2026

Taught by a skirt
that would not turn.

I was eleven the first time I wore a falda that belonged to someone else. It was too long, too heavy, and it kept falling against my legs because I was turning with my shoulders. Señora Aguirre stopped the music and said the skirt would open when my hips learned to lead. It took two years.

Guadalajara, Jalisco Escuela de Danza Folklórica, 1998 Compañía Falda Fire, founded 2014

Guadalajara, first steps

I grew up four blocks from the Teatro Degollado, in a house where my mother played the same four LPs until the sleeves wore white. Ballet folklórico was not an art form to me then; it was the sound of Saturday morning and the smell of ironed cotton. I entered the Escuela de Danza Folklórica at seven and stayed eleven years.

At the school we learned the regions in order: Jalisco first, because it is the showpiece, then Veracruz, Michoacán, Nayarit, Chiapas, Yucatán. Each one gives the body a different gravity. Veracruz wants quick feet and a loose knee; Michoacán wants a flat back and a heavy heel; Jalisco wants the whole body to make a circle around the hips.

“The skirt tells the audience where the beat is. If it lies, they will not hear the music at all.”

The company

Falda Fire started as a group of five in 2014, dancing at a wedding in Tlaquepaque for the price of a hired van. We now tour fourteen dancers and five musicians: two violins, a vihuela, a guitarra de golpe and a trumpet. The company keeps a small studio in Colonia Americana with a door wide enough for the twelve-metre skirt, which is not a joke — we refused three locales because of that door.

We rehearse Tuesday to Friday, four hours a night. Saturdays are for children's classes and for pressing skirts, which is the least glamorous and most decisive part of the work: a falda that was ironed lazily will fall out of its pleats by the third dance.

What I teach

Beginners learn the six steps before they learn a choreography. The first month is done without music, on a wooden floor, counting out loud. The second month adds the skirt, which is when most students discover that their arms have been doing nothing.

Twenty-two years, briefly

  1. 1998

    Escuela de Danza

    Entered at seven. First public dance at the Teatro Degollado, fourth from the left in a green skirt.

  2. 2009

    First solo

    Danced the Son de la Negra at the Fiestas de Octubre. Broke a heel on the second remate and finished on the flat.

  3. 2014

    Falda Fire

    Five dancers, one van, a wedding in Tlaquepaque. The company name was written on the side of the van in masking tape.

  4. 2026

    Fourteen on tour

    Two programmes, eleven cities, a children's class in every city we play. The van has been replaced twice.

Study with the company.

Saturday classes run in two groups: beginners at nine, intermediate at eleven. Six students to a floor, and the first month is always without music.
