The workshop

Plan the skirt
before the first pleat.

A falda is measured in metres of hem, not in sizes. The wider the hem, the more the skirt holds its circle when you turn; the heavier the cloth, the more the pleats stay where the iron put them. Set a dance and a fabric below — the workshop returns the cloth, the pleats, the ribbon and the peso.

Three things
the skirt is known for

Every region plays the falda differently. These are the three I am asked for most.

I.

El faldeo

The turn that opens the skirt into a full circle. It is not a spin — the hips close first, then the skirt follows a beat behind. In Jalisco the company holds the fan for four counts so the audience can read the colours.

Jarabe Tapatío · 8 counts
II.

El zapateado

Heel, toe, heel, flat, on a hollow stage. The sound is the percussion, so the foot has to land like a tuning fork rather than a hammer. My students practise on a wooden door for a month before they touch the stage.

Son de la Negra · 6/8
III.

El remate

The final stamp that ends a phrase and tells the musicians the verse is closed. Get it late by half a beat and the whole company hears it. Get it right and the skirt falls still on the same instant.

La Bamba · closing
