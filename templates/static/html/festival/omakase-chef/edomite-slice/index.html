    The room

    One slab of hinoki,
    ten people, no music

    The counter is a single piece of hinoki from Nagano, sanded every Sunday and left bare. There is no music, no decoration and no second room. You will sit beside people you have not met, and by the third course you will probably be talking to them.

    Two things are asked of you: no strong perfume, and arrive on time. Both are about the fish. A scent in the room flattens the nose, and a late guest makes the rice wait.

    • 01

      Eat each piece within ten seconds of it being set down.

    • 02

      Use your fingers. The towel is there for a reason.

    • 03

      Tell me at the start if you cannot eat something. I would rather change the menu than watch you manage it.

    The shari

    Rice is the hard part

    Fish is a matter of buying well and cutting straight. Rice is a decision every night: the blend, the water, the red vinegar, the temperature of your hand, the two degrees between warm and cold. I use a three-year-old akazu from Chiba and cook in a pot, not a machine, because a machine will not let you feel the grain.

    The neta is aged, sometimes for days, sometimes for minutes. Kohada is salted and vinegared for four days; akami is cured seven; uni is not touched at all. There is no principle that covers all of it except the tide that morning.

    Reservations

    Ten seats, and they go on the first of the month

    Reservations open at ten in the morning, Tokyo time, one month ahead, by telephone or through this site. The counter is closed on Sundays and the third Monday of each month, when the fish market rests.
