02

A season, from quote to storage

Christmas lighting is a five-month job that looks like a three-week one. The dates below are the ones that actually matter in Kolkata.

  1. By 31 Oct

    Survey

    A site visit in daylight: roofline length, gutter type, which walls take a clip, where the power point is, and whether a ladder can stand safely. No quote without it.

  2. By 10 Nov

    Design

    A single drawing of the elevation with the runs marked, the bulb count and the circuits. Most clients change one thing: the colour of the windows.

  3. 18–26 Nov

    Install

    Two to four days, weather permitting. Everything is tested at dusk on the day it goes up, not the day of the switch-on.

  4. Dec

    Maintenance

    Two visits, plus call-outs within 24 hours. A monsoon gust takes out a run; we re-clip it and check the RCCB before we leave.

  5. 7–20 Jan

    Takedown and storage

    Every strand labelled with its run, coiled on a reel, and stored in our godown until November. Broken bulbs are replaced before storage, not after.

03

The load maths, honestly

Most houses can carry a roofline on one circuit; the trouble starts when the tree, the gate and the veranda are added to the same point. The arithmetic is simple and we do it in front of the client.

RunBulbsLoadCircuit
Roofline, C9 LED9255 WAny 6 A point
Roofline, G40 globe92184 W6 A point, RCCB
Two palms, mini600300 WSeparate 6 A point
Shop fascia, retro140420 WDedicated 16 A, RCCB

A 16 A circuit at 230 V carries 3.6 kW, but we never load a domestic point past 1.8 kW for a seasonal install. The limit is not the wire; it is the old wiring behind it.

Two rules we do not bend: everything outdoors is IP65 or better, and no run is daisy-chained past three strands. The second rule is the one that melts cheap sets in a Ballygunge December.
