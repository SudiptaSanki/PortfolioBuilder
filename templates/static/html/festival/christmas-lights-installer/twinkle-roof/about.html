The crew

Four people, two ladders, and a rule about the monsoon.

I started in 2014 with one ladder and a Maruti van, lighting three houses in Ballygunge for a friend who ran a hardware shop. The crew is still four: two on the roof, one on the ground, one checking the load and the earth before anything is switched on. Nobody works alone, and nobody works in rain.

The monsoon decides the schedule

Kolkata gets its rain in the wrong months for our trade, which is lucky, and its damp in the right ones, which is not. December is cool and mostly dry; the problem is the humidity between the rain, which finds every unsealed joint in a cheap set. Everything we hang outdoors is IP65, and we open and dry the connectors in the godown before storage.

The second local problem is power. Old houses in Ballygunge and Bow Barracks have wiring from the 1970s, a single 6 A point on the veranda, and no earth in the socket. We test the earth continuity before we hang anything; if it fails, the client gets an electrician's number and the install waits. That conversation is unpopular in the second week of December and always worth having.

The third is access. A Park Street shopfront can be lit from a ladder; a three-storey façade cannot. For anything above the first floor we scaffold, and scaffolding on a pavement needs a corporation permission that takes ten days. That is why the diary closes on 31 October.

How we work on a roof

Five rules, written after an incident in 2019 that nobody was hurt in and everybody remembers.

  • 01

    Two points, always

    Feet on the ladder and a harness clipped to the ridge, or feet on the roof and a harness to the chimney. A ladder alone is not a platform.

  • 02

    No climbing in rain

    Wet terracotta is a skating rink. If it rains on install day, we reschedule; a delayed switch-on is cheaper than a fracture.

  • 03

    Test before you climb down

    Every run is switched on at dusk, checked bulb by bulb from the ground, and only then is the ladder packed.

  • 04

    The client's wiring is the client's

    We do not rewire a house. If the point is not earthed or the RCCB trips, we stop and hand over the number of an electrician we trust.

  • 05

    Leave nothing behind

    Clips come off with a flat blade, not a hammer. The wall is inspected on takedown day and any mark is filled before we leave.

03

The godown

Every strand is ours, labelled and stored. Clients do not buy lights; they rent a season from a stock that is tested each October.

Reels, not bags

Each strand is coiled on a labelled reel with its run number, so next year's install starts from the drawing rather than from scratch.

Tested in October

Every reel goes on the bench: 100 per cent of bulbs checked, failed ones replaced, connectors dried and greased.

Dehumidified

Kolkata air keeps a garage damp for eight months. The godown runs two dehumidifiers from March to September, which is most of the overhead.

Stock, not sales

About 4,000 m of C9, 1,800 m of mini and 300 G40 globes. When a colour is out of stock, we say so before quoting.

Talk to the person who climbs

The survey is done by whoever will be on the roof, not a salesperson. That is why the quote is accurate and the diary is short.
