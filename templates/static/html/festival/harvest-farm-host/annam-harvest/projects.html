Harvest & table

What comes off the field,
and what goes on the leaf

The farm sells very little. Most of the rice is ours for the year, the black gram becomes batter, and the sesame goes to the mill at Kallanai and comes back as oil. What we do offer is the table, and a place to sleep after it.

01 · From the field

Four things, in the order they ripen

01

Ponni rice

October · 2 acres · about 4 tonnes

Short-duration Ponni, transplanted by hand in July and cut in October. Milled at Kallanai with the bran left in, and eaten here all year. We sell a hundred kilos to the village and keep the rest.

02

Black gram

December · 1 acre · about 180 kg

Sown into the paddy stubble once the field is dry. It becomes the urad for idli batter and, in a bad week, for the whole village's idli batter.

03

Sesame

January · half an acre · about 90 kg

Pressed at the Kallanai mill for cold-pressed oil. One harvest gives the kitchen enough oil for eight months and a little to give away at Pongal.

04

Coconut and banana

All year · 40 palms · 2 rows

Poovan bananas and coconuts along the canal bund. The coconuts go into the sambar and the chutney; the bananas are mostly eaten standing up in the yard.

02 · The table

A Pongal morning, on a banana leaf

One menu, no choices, served at seven and again at half past twelve. The leaf is from the two trees behind the kitchen, cut the same morning. Eat with your right hand; there is a spoon if you would rather not.

Served on the thinnai · Thai month

  • Sakkarai pongal new rice, jaggery, moong dal, ghee, cashew
  • Ven pongal rice and dal, pepper, cumin, more ghee than is sensible
  • Kootu whatever vegetable the garden gives, with coconut and dal
  • Rasam tamarind, tomato, pepper, curry leaf from the bush by the well
  • Vadai urad from the December crop, fried to order
  • Appalam and curd appalam from the shop, curd from the two cows
  • Filter coffee in a steel tumbler, poured high, twice

₹450 a head, children under eight half. The kitchen cooks for a minimum of six and a maximum of thirty; beyond that we cook in the yard and it costs less per head, not more.

03 · Ways to come

Three visits, all of them slow

The Pongal morning

January · 05:30 – 09:00 · up to 20 people

Watch the pot, say the words when it boils over, eat on the leaf afterwards. This is the one morning of the year we do not take bookings after the eighth of January.

₹450 a head

A day on the field

July – October · 06:00 – 17:00 · up to 6 people

Transplanting, weeding or harvest, depending on the week. You will be given a mat, a hat and a job that is genuinely useful, and lunch in the field.

₹1,200 a head, lunch included

Two nights at the farm

October – February · 2 rooms · 4 guests

The thinnai room and the back room, shared bathroom, all meals, and the canal bank at six in the evening. No wi-fi; the mobile signal is on the roof.

₹3,600 a room, all meals

Book

The table seats twelve, the yard seats thirty

Tell us the date and the number of people. If it is Pongal, tell us early; if it is a Tuesday in February, tell us the day before and it will be fine.
