Pongal, and the four days around it

Rice is not a crop here.
It is the measure of everything.

We farm three acres of Ponni paddy in the Kaveri delta, between the Grand Anicut canal and a village with two temples and one bus. In the Thai month we cook the new rice outdoors, in a pot wide enough to need two people to lift it, and we keep a place at the table for whoever is passing.

01 · The four days

Bhogi, Thai Pongal, Maattu Pongal, Kaanum

Bhogi

The day before

The house is cleared and the old things burned in the yard before dawn. The kolam is drawn on a floor washed the night before, in rice flour, so the ants get their share first.

Thai Pongal

First day of Thai

New rice, new jaggery, and a pot that boils over on purpose. When the milk rises and spills east, the whole yard says pongalo pongal. We have never once had to pretend it boiled over.

Maattu Pongal

The third day

The cattle are washed, painted on the horns and fed sweet pongal before anyone eats. Our two bulls are called Karuppan and Murugan and they know the order of the morning.

Kaanum Pongal

The fourth day

Visiting, and food carried to relatives who could not come. The leftovers of the pot are finished at the canal bank, which is the closest thing this village has to a picnic spot.

02 · The pot

How much pongal for how many

One pot of sakkarai pongal, the sweet kind, measured for the number of people actually coming. The ratios are the ones my mother uses: one part raw rice to a third of moong dal, jaggery a little less than the rice by weight, and ghee until it looks right, which the card guesses at.

12

For a temple festival we cook in three pots rather than one; the card tells you how many. Nothing here is a recipe for a restaurant, where the ghee is always less than it should be.

03 · What the fields give

Three acres, in four rotations

  • Jun – Oct

    Ponni paddy

    Two acres of short-duration Ponni, transplanted by hand in July. Most of it goes to the local mill and comes back as our own rice for the year.

  • Nov – Jan

    Black gram and sesame

    The paddy fields are dry from December, so we sow black gram in the stubble. It fixes a little nitrogen and gives us the urad for the winter idli.

  • All year

    Coconut and banana

    Forty coconut palms along the canal bund and two rows of poovan banana. The coconuts are for the kitchen; the bananas rarely leave the village.

  • Feb – May

    Vegetables for the table

    Brinjal, okra, country tomato and three kinds of greens, grown only for what the kitchen needs and what the neighbours ask for.

04 · Visiting

A morning here runs on the canal timetable

Breakfast at six, because the field is worked before the heat. If you stay, you will be given a mat on the thinnai and a steel tumbler of filter coffee, and nobody will ask you to help. You may, and it will be noticed.
