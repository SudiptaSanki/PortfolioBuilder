What was ordered, and what it took to cast it

Seven entries from the workshop ledger. Open a row for the oil capacity, the burn test and the thing that went wrong — because something always does, and the customer deserves to know which lamp they are buying.

27 Pair of samais for the sanctum door Bhimnagar temple committee 2026 Temple

Two 620 mm samais, cast a week apart so the committee could see one before the second was poured. The brief was simply “heavy enough not to walk away”, so the base is 1.0 mm sheet with a lead-filled core, 2.9 kg each. We burned them for fourteen hours on mustard oil and the level fell to a finger’s width above the wick — good, but the buyer’s priest prefers ghee, which burns slower and needs the cups cleaned weekly.

Mistake: the first rim was spun too thin and sang when the door opened. I re-rolled it, which cost four days and one sheet.

26 Akhand jyot for a housewarming Kothiwal family, Majhola 2025 Household

Four cups, 180 ml, a wide lip because the grandmother would be filling it herself with a spoon. The family asked for a plain bowl and no engraving; the only decoration is the turned band on the stem. First burn test ran thirteen hours on sesame, then another on coconut oil so they could decide which smell they wanted in the room. They chose sesame.

I kept the seam visible on the underside, as they asked. Their father had owned a lamp with the same seam and remembered it.

25 Nine jhoomars for a veranda Hotel Riverview, Ram Ganga bank 2025 Hospitality

Nine hanging lamps for a covered veranda, each 330 mm across and hung 2.1 m above the floor so nobody’s head would find the rim. The hotel wanted them lit before sunset and out by eleven, which is a six-hour burn: the seven-cup jhoomar at 300 ml does that with oil to spare, and the staff only refill every second evening.

Two of the chains were replaced within a month because the ceiling anchors were fixed into old lime plaster. That was the builder’s fault, not the lamp’s, but I went back and re-hung all nine with a back plate.

24 Panchmukhi for the offering table Sai mandir, Kundarki Road 2025 Temple

A low five-cup lamp for a marble table, 196 mm across, so the priest could move it with one hand while holding a bell in the other. It holds 90 ml and runs a little over five hours, which suits the evening aarti and the early morning one; between them it is wiped and refilled rather than left burning.

The marble was cold enough that the base rang for the first ten minutes every evening. I soldered a thin felt pad into the foot, which fixed it and is now standard on all panchmukhi work.

23 A grandmother’s lamp, split at the seam Bringi family, Sambhal 2024 Repair

The reservoir had opened along the soldered seam and had been used anyway, so the underside was black with old oil. I did not replace the bowl. I cleaned it with a soft flame and a brass brush, closed the seam from the inside with a low-tin solder, and pressure-tested it with water for an hour. The dents stayed. The family had asked for the dents to stay.

What I could not fix: the stem had been shortened by someone else, so the lamp now sits 34 mm lower than it was made. I told them, and they said it had been like that since 1971.

22 Twelve small diyas for a wedding Agarwal wedding, Moradabad 2024 Household

Not my usual work: twelve throwaway-size diyas, each holding 18 ml, for a courtyard lined with them at a wedding. The father wanted them in brass rather than clay so the family could keep them afterwards. We cast them from one sheet, twelve at a time, and they burned four and a half hours each on mustard oil.

They were delivered unpolished, with the file marks still on them, because the bride’s mother liked the look. Two were kept back as spares. I still have one on the shelf.

21 Table lamps for a reading room Kotwali library, Moradabad 2023 Hospitality

Six akhand jyot lamps, each set in a shallow brass dish to catch drips, for a reading room that had lost power more often than the librarian liked. They asked for a low centre of gravity and a handle; we added a small loop to the stem, which is not traditional but has proved useful. Each burns about fourteen hours on sesame.

One lamp came back after a year with a wick cup worn thin from cleaning. I replaced the cup and left the rest, and it is still in use.
