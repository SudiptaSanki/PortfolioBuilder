Festival and wedding orders

Big orders are a scheduling problem.

A tray of mithai for a wedding is not a bigger box. It is a count of pieces, a departure time, a vehicle, and a person who stays until the last tray is on the table.

Largest order: 400 boxes, Diwali 2025

Heaviest tray: 120 kg, a wedding in Punjabi Bagh

Notice we ask for: 5 days, 10 for wedding season

2025DiwaliCorporate

Four hundred boxes for a Karol Bagh office

One order, four hundred boxes, nine pieces each — three thousand six hundred pieces from a kitchen that sells forty kilos a day. We closed the counter at three for two afternoons to cut and pack, which the regulars forgave because the laddoos were still warm at pickup.

The mistake I would not repeat: the client changed the ribbon colour on the fourth day. Four hundred boxes had to be retied.

  • Pieces3,600
  • Weight74 kg
  • Notice given11 days
  • Delivery2 vans, 06:00
2024Wedding120 kg

A wedding tray, weighed in three parts

The family could not decide on one sweet, so we built a tray of three: kaju katli on the left, motichoor in the middle, besan barfi on the right, with the fourth tray of pista roll arriving separately so it would not soften in the car.

I went with the trays. A wedding is the one place where a mithai is looked at before it is eaten, and nobody in that family had ever seen a tray set by the person who made it.

  • Weight120 kg
  • Trays4 brass
  • Notice given9 days
  • On site2 h
2025School200 boxes

Two hundred boxes at ninety-five rupees

A school in Civil Lines wanted a Diwali box for every child, at a price that worked for a government-aided budget. We cut the box to four pieces, dropped the gold leaf, and used a paper band instead of a ribbon.

It is the only order where I changed the recipe to fit the money rather than the money to fit the recipe, and the laddoo was still the same laddoo.

  • Boxes200
  • Pieces each4
  • Price₹95 / box
  • Notice given16 days
2023Shop opening6 kg

The platter that had to arrive before the ribbon

A cloth shop opening in Sadar Bazaar needed one platter on the counter before the chief guest cut the ribbon at nine. It had to look full at arm's length, survive a scooter ride, and not be too heavy for one person to lift.

Six kilos on a brass platter, packed in a wooden crate with two cloths, delivered at eight by my cousin on his scooter. The ribbon was cut at 9:10 and the pista roll was gone by 9:30.

  • Pieces184
  • Delivery08:00, scooter
  • Notice given3 days
