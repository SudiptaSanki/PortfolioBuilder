File 03 · The podcaster

Maren Holloway, in her own voice.

I spent six years producing other people’s radio before I started telling my own stories. In 2023 I hung a microphone in the coat closet of my Sleepy Hollow apartment — the only room in the building where the radiator doesn’t tick — and recorded ten stories into the coats. That was Season One. The closet has not been upgraded. The coats have been briefed.

Why horror, and why this quiet

People assume horror podcasting is about volume. It isn’t — it’s about proximity. A podcast is the only medium that speaks from inside the listener’s head, through headphones, usually at night, usually alone. You don’t need to shout in a place like that. You need to be believed. So everything on Whisper Reel is written to be believed first and feared second: real bus routes, real tides on the Hudson, rent books and parish ledgers and all the paperwork of ordinary life — with one detail wrong.

I record where the stories happen whenever the story allows it: foley is gathered in stairwells, church halls and one very cooperative undertaker’s back room here in the village. The narration is always the closet. A treated studio sounds like a studio; a closet sounds like somebody hiding — and in my stories, somebody usually is.

I am often asked whether I record in a real abandoned place for authenticity. No. Abandoned places have terrible acoustics and excellent lawyers. I record in a closet and write the abandoned place properly.
— Maren Holloway, answering the same question for the fortieth time

The record

The studio, stated plainly

The room
A coat closet, 1.2 × 1.5 m, cedar-lined, in Sleepy Hollow, New York. Heavy coats on three sides; the fourth side is a door with a towel over the gap.
Recording chain
One large-diaphragm condenser microphone on a boom arm, into a two-channel interface, recorded at 24-bit / 48 kHz. No live processing — the performance is the processing.
The script
Every episode is fully written before recording: roughly 5,200 words for a 38-minute episode, with held beats timed in the margin (1.5 s before a door, 3 s after a name).
Foley
Recorded, never library. Doors, gravel, river water, a real night bus on Route 9. Each season’s foley log runs to about 300 clips.
Release cadence
First Friday of the month, February to September; weekly through October, when the village fills up and so does the inbox.
The live show
One seated Halloween show a year in Sleepy Hollow, performed with a live foley table. 2025 sold out its 412 seats; 2026 adds a second night.

The mix

Four layers, in order of importance

Every episode is built from the same four layers. The bench on the Episodes page lets you hear three of the ingredients; the fourth can’t be demoed, because it’s the absence of the other three.

01

Room tone
Ten minutes of the empty closet, recorded before every session and laid under the whole episode, so a cut never sounds like a cut.

−48 dB under voice
02

Voice
One take per scene wherever possible. No pitch tricks, no whisper-boost: if a line isn’t frightening at speaking volume, it gets rewritten, not processed.

−16 LUFS, true peak −1 dB
03

Foley & place
The recorded world — doors deciding, water keeping things, gravel under one pair of shoes. Placed where the script’s geography says it must be, never for decoration.

placed, not padded
04

Silence
The loudest layer. Held beats are written into the script and defended in the edit against every instinct to fill them.

1.5–3 s, timed

The method

How an episode gets made

Wk 1–2

Write
The story is drafted as prose first, then marked up as a script: geography, timings, where the listener is standing in every scene.

~5,200 words
Wk 3

Gather
Foley trips: the real door, the real stairwell, the real river. Then one evening per scene in the closet.

~300 clips / season
Wk 4

Build
Edit and design: four layers, in the order above. The sting library is opened only to be closed again.

0 stings used
Wk 5

Rest & release
A week away from the episode, one cold listen at midnight, then out on the first Friday. Distance is an editing tool.
