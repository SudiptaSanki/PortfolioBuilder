The craft · Nikhil Bhatnagar

Learned at this counter, from the man who learned it here.

My grandfather weighed with brass weights at this spot in Sarafa; my father taught me the syrup stages on the same karahi I use now. What follows is the whole method — there is no secret recipe. There is only the date, the stage, and the refusal to lie about either.

Two dates on every tray

Every tray ticket at this counter is stamped twice: the morning the sweet was made, and the day you should stop eating it. Most counters print only a “best before” date, chosen kindly. Mine are shorter and true — a malai barfi here gets four days, because on the fifth day it is not the sweet I sold you.

This is not fussiness. A mithai's life is set by what is in it: milk sweets live for days, ghee-and-flour sweets for weeks, and anything claiming more than that has been helped by something you did not order. When you buy from me, the stamp is the contract. When I take an order, I plan backwards from your date — and if the dates cannot be made to hold, I will tell you what to swap instead of quietly selling you a stale box.

Syrup has stages

Sugar syrup is not one thing. It is a road, and each sweet gets off at a different stop. The test is done the way my father did it: a drop cooled between finger and thumb, drawn apart, and counted in threads — taar. A thermometer can tell you the temperature. It cannot tell you the thread.

Half a string too far and the katli turns hard; half a string short and the mysore pak weeps syrup in the tin. The stages are why the same three ingredients — flour, sugar, ghee — can be six different sweets on one counter.

Milk is a morning decision

Malai barfi begins as ten litres of morning milk and ends, hours later, as about two and a half kilos of barfi. That reduction is the price, and it is also the shelf life: there is nothing in the tray that could keep it past four days, and I will not put anything in it that could.

So milk sweets are a decision made at dawn, against orders already in the book. If your wedding is on Saturday, your barfi is cut at 6 a.m. on Saturday — not on Thursday to “get it out of the way”. Getting it out of the way is how barfi dies.

Jalebi is fried when you arrive

The batter ferments overnight — that sour edge is the flavour, and it cannot be hurried or faked on the morning. The spirals go into hot oil in front of you, come out crisp, take thirty seconds in warm one-string syrup, and should be eaten before they have finished steaming.

A jalebi that has waited an hour is not a worse jalebi; it is a different object wearing the same name. This is why the counter keeps no jalebi tray, why the planner on the home page will refuse it for any date but today, and why the night market queue waits for the next batch instead of taking the last one. It always waits.

Keeping sweets at home

The stamp tells you the days. This table tells you the how — clip it to the tin.

SweetKeep itHow
Kaju Katli15 daysAirtight tin, cool and dry. Never the fridge — the cold dries it out.
Motichoor Ladoo7 daysSealed tin away from sunlight. If the ghee smells tired, so is the ladoo.
Besan Ladoo21 daysSealed tin. The traveller — it forgives trains, parcels and hostel shelves.
Malai Barfi4 daysFridge, always. Bring it out twenty minutes before eating. Day five is not a suggestion.
Mysore Pak12 daysTin kept shut. Every opening costs it a little of its melt.
JalebiTodayEat it warm, standing up if possible. There is no storage advice for jalebi.

The counter's year

The year has two mountains: the wedding season, and Diwali. From Dussehra the counter changes gear — the order book fills, the karahi starts earlier, and the closing dates below are not marketing. They are the last days on which the making schedule can still be honest.

Diwali — Sunday 8 November 2026. Shipped gift boxes close Sunday 1 November. Counter and wedding orders close Thursday 5 November. On Diwali morning the jalebi karahi lights at 7 a.m. and pre-orders are collected by noon.

When you are ready, tell me the date first. The sweets we can decide together.
