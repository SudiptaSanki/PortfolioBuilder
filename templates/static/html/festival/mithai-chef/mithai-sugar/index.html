Diwali begins at the sweet counter.

I am Nikhil Bhatnagar, third-generation halwai. Every tray on my counter carries two dates — the morning the sweet was made, and the day you should stop eating it. If a sweet cannot keep its promise, I will not sell it to you. That is the whole business, and it has been enough for three generations.

How an order works here

Step 1

Tell me the date first

Not the sweets — the date. The day the mithai will be eaten decides everything else: what can be made, what must be swapped, what I will refuse.

Step 2

I work backwards

Barfi is made on the last morning, katli the week before, ladoo in between. Your confirmation carries the making schedule, so you can check my working.

Step 3

Weighed in the open

Collect at the counter or take it shipped in 1 kg boxes. Either way the taraju reading is the bill — if the scale and the slip disagree, the scale wins.

From the order book · Diwali 2025

Three hundred boxes that had to survive the train

A firm in Bhopal sent 300 gift boxes to its people in four cities, two to three days in transit. Only sweets keeping twelve days or more were allowed into a shipped box — and the malai barfi they asked for was refused for shipping, in writing, with the dates shown. The full story is on the Orders page.
