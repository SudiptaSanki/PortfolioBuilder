The walks · four winters

Work that has walked in the dark

Every commission below started as a question somebody asked in October, usually beginning with “is it too late to…”. It was not too late. Here is what was asked, what I made, and what happened on the night.

Asked October 2025 · Walked 21 December 2025

The Canal Park Solstice Walk

The ask. The city's recreation office wanted a solstice walk a seven-year-old could light, on a route with lake wind on one side and a harbour on the other, and it had to be safe enough that the fire marshal would sign it off without a second meeting.

The work. Six public workshop Saturdays where 212 people built their own walkers; 88 repaired loaners off my rack for anyone who arrived empty-handed; LED tea lights in every carried lantern, no exceptions; two marshals a corner, spare batteries in every marshal's pocket.

The night. 340 walkers carried 412 lanterns along the lakewalk in falling snow. The wind took exactly zero flames, because there were no flames to take. The marshal's report used the word “uneventful”, which from a marshal is a love letter.

Asked November 2025 · Lit 19–21 December 2025

Remembrance Row, for a Hospice Garden

The ask. A hospice asked for a garden vigil: one lantern for every patient who had died in their care that year, each carrying a name, standing along the garden path for the three longest evenings. Families would walk the path. Some would want to stand a long while.

The work. Forty-six remembrance lanterns, white on white, each name hand-lettered in ink on the front panel. Because these lanterns stand still, on stakes, in glass-shielded cups, they burned real votive candles — the one job in my year where real flame is the right light.

The nights. The garden stayed open until ten. Staff told me families came back the second and third evenings with thermoses, and that two families asked for their lantern afterwards. Those two went home in tissue and boxes, with a spare skin each.

Asked October 2025 · Paraded January 2026

The Whale, for Harbor School

The ask. A harbour-side school wanted a centrepiece for its January parade: something from the lake, big enough to read from across the schoolyard, light enough for two sixth-graders to carry — their words, and a fair engineering brief.

The work. A five-foot whale on a bent-willow frame, skinned in blue-grey tissue, with a spout of folded stars. Fourteen making days, most of them drying time. Two carry poles, padded where small hands grip, and a practice session in the gym so the carriers learned its turning circle before the street did.

The night. The Whale led 190 children down the hill in the January dark and finished with its skin intact, which no one who has carried a five-foot paper animal through a school parade will take for granted. It hangs in the school library now, lit on reading nights.

Asked September 2024 · Lit December 2024

A Street of Windows, Lester Park

The ask. One street in Lester Park wanted to do something together for December that was not a competition. Their words again: “Not bigger than the neighbours. The same as the neighbours, on purpose.”

The work. Eighty-four window lanterns from a single pattern — a spruce tree cut into the tissue, flat bamboo backs, timer candles so every window lit at dusk and darkened at midnight without a single ladder or extension cord.

The outcome. The street lit as one thing for thirty-one nights. The following September, before I had written a single reminder, 61 of the 84 households ordered again — most of them a second lantern for the back window, “for the alley”.
