Winter Solstice · Monday 21 December 2026 · Duluth, Minnesota

Lanterns for the longest night

I am Aila Rantala. I make paper-and-willow lanterns by hand in a harbour-side loft in Duluth, and every December my whole year leans toward one evening: the solstice, when the sun sets at twenty past four and the night runs nearly sixteen hours. We do not curse that dark here. We carry lights through it.

The order planner

Price it the way I price it

The planner reads the same two tables printed below it — the ones pinned above my bench. Pick a lantern, a light and a night, and it works out the price, the making days and the light you are buying. If the arithmetic says an order cannot be ready for the solstice, it says so, and names the January walk instead.

1 · The lantern

2 · How many

I build in batches of six. Orders of ten or more take 10% off the lantern price — the light add-ons stay full price, as printed below.

3 · The light inside

Real flame: I fit a votive only to lanterns that stand still — window and remembrance lanterns on a shelf or a stake. A paper lantern that gets carried gets a battery, and that rule has no exceptions.

Work that has already walked

December 2025 · Canal Park, Duluth

The Solstice Walk of 412 Lanterns

The city asked for a walk that a child could light. Six Saturday workshops, 212 lanterns built by the people who would carry them, 88 repaired loaners off my rack — and on the longest night, 412 lights moving along the lakewalk in the snow. Every carried lantern burned a battery. Nothing caught, nothing blew out, nobody went home early.
