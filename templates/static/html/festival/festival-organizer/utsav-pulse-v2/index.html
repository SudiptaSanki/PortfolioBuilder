01

Deccan Riverfront Food Festival

The ask. The Riverfront Cultural Trust wanted a food festival that proved the grounds could hold a crowd: 96 stalls, three days, on a site that floods every monsoon.

What I did. Laid the vendor grid on the high terrace in rows of twelve with one-way food lanes, put the stage at the far end so queues never crossed the music, and wrote the rain plan before a single stall was sold — every pitch had a numbered terrace twin.

The outcome. 38,000 people ate over three days, stall holders reported their best weekend of the year, and the Trust rebooked the site before the fences came down.

What went wrong. On day two a thunderstorm dropped 40 mm in an hour and the low row took water. Fourteen stalls moved to their terrace twins in 90 minutes — because the twins were marked, powered and numbered in advance, not improvised in the rain.

02

Hillside Echoes

The ask. A first-time promoter with a hillside site above Lonavala, three stages, and a headliner flying in from Delhi on show day. Make it look like they had done it ten times before.

What I did. Rebuilt the running order so no stage ever depended on the airport, contracted a Pune band as the standing cover act, and put the headliner's fee on a landed-not-booked clause.

The outcome. 12,500 people across two days — and when the flight was cancelled, not delayed, the cover act played the sunset slot and the headliner drove down the expressway and closed the night. Refund requests: 41 out of 12,500 tickets.

03

Old City Lantern Fair

The ask. Five evenings of street fair in Pune's old city, on real streets, under a licence condition most producers refuse: every street reopens to traffic by 23:00, every night.

What I did. Built the fair as a kit that assembles and strikes in 100 minutes — stalls on wheels, lighting on battery, a teardown crew of sixty walking a printed route behind the last visitors.

The outcome. 61,000 visitors across five evenings. The streets reopened at 22:52, 22:47, 22:55, 22:49 and 22:51. The licence was renewed without a hearing.

What it costs to hire me

The fee, in plain figures

Production fee

12% of budget

My fee is 12% of the production budget, with a floor of ₹4,00,000. For a single-day festival under 3,000 people I work on a flat ₹2,50,000 production fee instead — percentage maths gets silly at that size, and I would rather say so.

Artist fees, stall income and sponsorship sit outside the production budget and outside my percentage. The budget we agree at the site visit is the budget in the post-show report; if it moves, you hear it from me before it moves.

What the fee buys

  • Licences & permissions — police, fire NOC, local body, music licences (PPL / IPRS), filed on the 90-day runway
  • Site plan & safety case — capacity maths, gate and lane plan, the walk-through with the authorities
  • Artist contracting — advances, travel, cover acts, and contracts that survive a cancelled flight
  • Vendor grid — stall layout, power and water per pitch, one-way food lanes
  • Show-calling — my team runs the gates, the stages and the radio net on the day
  • Rain plan & post-show report — written before, and written after: attendance, incidents, accounts
