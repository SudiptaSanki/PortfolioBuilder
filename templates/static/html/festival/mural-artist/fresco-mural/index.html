How a wall gets painted

A fresco is not a picture applied to a wall. It is pigment pressed into lime plaster while the plaster is still wet, so the colour becomes part of the surface. That changes everything about how the work is planned.

  1. Read the wall for a day

    Where the water comes from, where the sun lands at four, which corner the children already use as a bench. The design response is usually a colour and a height, not a subject.

  2. Draw at full size

    Cartoon drawn on paper in the workshop, then pricked and pounced onto the wall with charcoal dust. On a 20-metre wall this takes two days and a lot of string.

  3. Lay plaster and paint it wet

    One section at a time, about two square metres, because lime goes off in forty minutes in Goa. Three coats of lime putty and sand, then pigment brushed into the last coat.

  4. Let it cure, then leave it alone

    Lime cures by absorbing carbon dioxide, not by drying. Three weeks before anything touches the wall, no sealer, no varnish, and a conversation with the caretaker about not washing it.

The Mapusa market wall

Eighteen metres on the vegetable market's west wall, painted over eleven days in November 2025 with two assistants and a very patient fishmonger. The register is a river of fish and baskets, because that is what the market sells, and the palette came from the laterite under the plaster.

The wall was cement render that had been patched four times. We ground the patches back, damped it down, and painted in three sections so the lime never went off before the pigment was in it.

Read the wall notes

The market wall gets touched more than any gallery piece I have made. By the end of the first week there were handprints at knee height, and I decided they were part of it.
