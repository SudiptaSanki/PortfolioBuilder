Commissions, Halloweens past

THE WORK, WHILE IT LASTED

None of these pumpkins exists any more. That is the trade. What remains is the record: what the client asked for, what I carved, and what happened on the night. Every figure below is the figure from the job book.

1 · The Night Orchard Walk

The ask. A harvest festival wanted a walk-through trail of lit pumpkins through its orchard — three nights, hundreds of visitors a night, and no open flames anywhere near the trees.

The work. Forty-six pumpkins, cut-through faces, carved in three waves across three days so no face on the trail was ever more than three days old. Battery candles throughout, wired to rails so a kicked pumpkin could not roll. The last wave was carved the morning of the final night, in the barn, by lantern light of the earlier waves coming through the door.

The outcome. Three sold-out nights. The festival extended the trail a second weekend with a fresh set of twenty, and the oldest faces went to the compost heap in front of a school group, which is the correct ending and the children understood it perfectly.

Pumpkins
46 + 7 spares
Carve time
74 hours over 3 days
Design
Cut-through faces
Lifespan used
3 of 4 best days

“She carved in waves so the last night looked as fresh as the first. Our visitors photographed the trail more than the orchard, the cider and the band combined.”

Maren Oduya, director, Hollow Creek Harvest Nights

2 · The Hollowbrook Inn pair

The ask. A Tarrytown inn wanted its founder's face on a pumpkin for its Halloween weekend — and wanted to know, before paying anything, exactly how many evenings it would last. A fair question, asked in August.

The work. A pair. The portrait was shaded into a 90 lb display pumpkin across two days — the wall never cut through, so the face is drawn by light passing through flesh of different depths. Its companion, a classic gatekeeper face, was cut through for the gate post. I gave the inn the schedule in writing: portrait peaks nights two to four, gate face nights one to four, both done by the fifth.

The outcome. The portrait peaked on the Saturday, as scheduled, and the inn took its booking photographs that night. On the fifth evening it began to slump, and the inn composted it with a small ceremony I was not invited to improve upon. They rebooked for the next October before the month was out.

Pumpkins
90 lb display + 34 lb porch
Carve time
14 hrs portrait, 2 hrs face
Design
Shaded portrait + cut-through
Lifespan used
5 of 6 best days

“Eliza told us on the first phone call exactly which night the portrait would peak and which night it would start to go — and she was right to the evening. Guests planned their photographs around her schedule. That honesty is rarer than the carving.”

June Pruitt, innkeeper, The Hollowbrook Inn

3 · Crooked Ladder Brewing

The ask. A taproom in Peekskill wanted its snarling-ladder mascot and its wordmark on pumpkins for a two-week run from mid-October to Halloween — a fortnight being longer than any carved pumpkin has ever lived.

The work. We split the difference honestly. The mascot went on twelve pumpkins as shaded work — the wall intact, six good days each — in two sets of six, the second set carved as the first faded. The wordmark was cut through on porch pumpkins for the final four nights only, when brightness mattered most. One live carving night in the taproom, me at a bench by the bar, carving the spare set while the queue watched.

The outcome. Two weeks of window display with no day on which the taproom showed a tired pumpkin. The live night filled the bar on a Tuesday. The brewery's owner now tells other businesses the refresh is the whole trick, which it is.

Pumpkins
12 shaded + 6 cut-through
Carve time
36 hrs + 3 hrs live
Design
Shaded logo & mascot
Lifespan used
6 of 6 days, twice over

“She refused to sell us one set for two weeks, and explained why with a knife and a torch in the actual pumpkin. Then she sold us the truth instead: two sets. The window never had a bad night.”

Tom Halloran, taproom manager, Crooked Ladder Brewing

4 · The school fundraiser carve

The ask. A Sleepy Hollow primary school wanted a live carving at its autumn fair and a finished pumpkin worth raffling — something the children would remember longer than the pumpkin itself lasted.

The work. Three hours at a bench in the middle of the fair. The children voted on the faces by shouting, which is a sound design process: a grinner, a worrier, and one with its tongue out. I drew each face on the skin while they watched, cut the first two through, and shaded the third so they could see both kinds of carving. The raffle pumpkin was carved last, in the final hour, so it would be fresh for its winner's porch.

The outcome. The raffle raised $1,840. The winner, aged seven, named the pumpkin and sent me a photograph of it every day for five days. On the fifth it had, in her words, “gone to sleep.” Correct.

Pumpkins
4 porch classics
Carve time
3 hours, live
Design
Faces chosen by vote
Raised
$1,840 in raffle tickets

“The children still talk about the pumpkin that went to sleep. Eliza never once pretended it would last — she made the short life part of the show, and the fair was better for it.”

Alisha Beckett, PTA chair, Sleepy Hollow Primary
