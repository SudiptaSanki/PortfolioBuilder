Plates · selected work

Six pieces, and the hours inside them

Not a catalogue. These are the pieces the atelier keeps photographs of, with the cloth, the stitches, the karigars and the time each one actually took. Prices are given for the four that can still be commissioned.

Plate II · bridal

Noor — wine velvet lehenga

A kamdani ground so dense that the velvet reads as bronze in daylight and black at night, with salma borders and a scatter of sitara. The motif came from a 1974 photograph, redrawn at four times the size.

  • GroundWine velvet
  • StitchesKamdani, salma, sitara
  • Hours1 400 h · 4 hands
  • Weight9.2 kg finished

₹8 50 000 · commission closed for 2026

Plate III · couture

Chand — raw silk sherwani

A groom's sherwani with a single vine running from the left shoulder to the hem, worked in dabka for relief and nakshi for the curve. The vine is continuous: one thread path, no joins, 520 hours.

  • GroundRaw silk, ink blue
  • StitchesDabka, nakshi
  • Hours520 h · 2 hands
  • Weight2.4 kg finished

₹3 20 000 · four-month lead

Plate IV · bridal

Mehtab — organza odhni

A veil in pale gold organza with sitara scattered by hand, denser at the border and thinning towards the centre so the cloth still moves. Worked on a frame with a muslin backing that is removed stitch by stitch at the end.

  • GroundOrganza, pale gold
  • StitchesSitara, kora border
  • Hours310 h · 2 hands
  • CareTissue, no folding

₹1 40 000 · six-week lead

Plate V · small work

Katra — a velvet clutch

The smallest piece the atelier makes, and the one that teaches new apprentices proportion. A kora border, four sitara accents and a single dabka centre. Everything on it fits in a hand.

  • GroundVelvet, aubergine
  • StitchesKora, sitara, dabka
  • Hours90 h
  • EditionMade to order

₹38 000 · three-week lead

Plate VI · interior

Bagh — a wall panel

Commissioned for a Delhi drawing room: three flowers and a vine on deep blue silk, mounted on a stretcher so it hangs without a frame. The design is deliberately sparse, because the panel is seen at four metres in low light.

  • GroundSilk, midnight blue
  • StitchesSalma, nakshi, sitara
  • Hours640 h · 2 hands
  • Size1.4 × 0.8 m

₹2 60 000 · eight-week lead

Plate VII · restoration

A shawl from 1890

Private collection. The metal had oxidised to black and the ground was splitting along two folds. The work was support, not replacement: silk crepeline behind, missing stitches left missing, and every intervention recorded on the reverse in a single line of thread.

  • GroundPashmina, faded
  • WorkSupport and stabilisation
  • Hours220 h
  • StatusNot for sale
