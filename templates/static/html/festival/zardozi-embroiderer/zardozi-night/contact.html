What to bring

  • Any photograph of a motif you love — a faded one is fine.
  • The jewellery you will wear with it, or a picture of it.
  • The shoes, if it is a lehenga; the hem is set to them.
  • Your mother, if she is the one who will decide.

How it runs

First meeting, motif drawing, cloth selection, then a calico toile for fit. The embroidery begins only after the toile is approved, because metal cannot be let out at the seam. Two fittings follow, then the finishing, which is the slowest part: the backing removed, the reverse lined, the edges bound.

Payment is in three parts — a third on the order, a third when the embroidery starts, a third on delivery. The atelier does not take a deposit for a date it cannot hold.

Can you copy a design I saw online?

For a family piece or a museum motif, yes, with changes. For another designer's current work, no. The atelier will draw you something in the same spirit and tell you honestly where it differs.

Do you work with menswear?

Sherwanis, bandhgalas and stoles. The frame work is the same; the density is lower, because a groom has to be able to sit down.

Is the metal real gold or silver?

Silver-gilt wire over a copper core, which is what zardozi has used for two centuries. Pure gold wire exists and costs about forty times as much; the atelier will quote it if asked, and will advise against it for a garment that will be worn once.

Can the piece be cleaned?

Not washed. Dry-cleaned only by a specialist who knows metal embroidery, and only if the piece is badly soiled. Most pieces should simply be aired, brushed with a soft brush, and stored flat in muslin.

Do you take international orders?

Yes, with two fittings replaced by a video call and a calico toile posted out. About a third of the atelier's bridal work is now overseas, mostly to families in the Gulf, London and Toronto.
