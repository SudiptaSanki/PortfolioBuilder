वैदिक मन्त्रोच्चार

Vedic chanting, taught in Kothrud, Pune — sound first, text second, svara always.

I am Vasudha Kulkarni. For nineteen years I have taught students to chant the Veda the way it was taught to me in Satara: by ear, by breath, one line at a time. This September my students and I lead the morning chanting at the Kothrud Ganeshotsav, and Saturday 4 September 2027 is the date half my classroom is quietly working towards.

One rule before anything else. Nobody in my class chants from Roman letters for three years and calls it learning — that teaches you to read English quickly, not to chant. The first month here is sound alone. There is no text on the desk until your ear can hear where a svara rises and where it falls.

Sound before text — the foundation month

Four weeks, every beginner, no exceptions and no skipping because you "already know the words". Breath, the long vowels, the three svaras, and the shapes of the Devanāgarī letters. Students who arrive chanting from memory still take it: it is where I learn how your voice actually works.

स्वर · The three tones, and a bench to hear them

क

Udātta उदात्त — the raised tone

The voice steps up and stays level. In the printed text it carries no mark in most editions — which is exactly why it has to be learned by ear, not by eye.

ख

Anudātta अनुदात्त — the lowered tone

The voice drops below the home pitch. Marked with a line beneath the syllable. Beginners flatten it; the foundation month exists largely to fix that.

ग

Svarita स्वरित — the falling tone

The voice lands from above and glides down through the home pitch. A chant is a line drawn by these three tones together. If the line is wrong, the words can be perfect and the chant is still wrong — which is why we learn the line before the words.

The reference bench

My harmonium's Sa is tuned to 136 Hz. Your own Sa may sit higher or lower — at your first lesson we find it, and it becomes yours. Until then, borrow mine. Nothing on this bench plays until you tap it, and every tone stops itself after two seconds.

Silent. The bench is off until you tap a tone.
