Leaf 1 · the three svaras

अग्निमीळे पुरोहितं यज्ञस्य देवमृत्विजम्

You cannot read a Veda. You can only be taught it.

The accents are not written in the book; they are carried in the voice of the person who taught you. I teach the three svaras first — the raised, the low and the falling — because everything else in the recitation is built on them.

Hear the three before you try to make them

The vyāhṛtis — भूर् भुवः स्वः — are where every student begins, because they carry all three svaras in nine syllables. Press a syllable to hear it alone, or recite the whole line and watch the contour.

Bhūr · udātta

The contour of the line above: three levels, and one falling syllable every third beat.

Udātta sits a fourth above the base: at 196 Hz that is 261 Hz. The svarita falls from there to just above the base.

The first of the three vyāhṛtis. It is raised, and it sets the height the rest of the line returns to. Say it on the same breath as the oṃ before it, without a gap.

How to practise it

  • Hum the base note first, then lift a fourth and hold it for two breaths.
  • Do not push from the throat; the lift comes from the breath, not the jaw.
  • If you cannot hear the difference between the two, you are not ready to chant the line.

The six ways

The same line, six ways of saying it

A student who learns only the continuous text can recite it and cannot check it. The other ways are how the tradition keeps the words from drifting.

  1. 01

    Saṃhitā

    The joined text, as it is recited. Sandhi is applied and the words run into one another.

  2. 02

    Pada

    Word by word, with the sandhi undone. The first check a student learns: if the words do not come apart, they were not learned.

  3. 03

    Krama

    Word one with two, two with three, and so on to the end. It doubles the length and catches a dropped syllable at once.

  4. 04

    Jaṭā

    One-two, two-one, one-two, then two-three, three-two, two-three. Tongue and memory together.

  5. 05

    Ghana

    The hardest of the common ways: one-two, two-one, one-two-three, three-two-one, one-two-three. Very few students go this far, and there is no shame in stopping at krama.

  6. 06

    Padapāṭha, kept pure

    The same words chanted without sandhi and without accent, as a reference form. It sounds mechanical. That is the point.
