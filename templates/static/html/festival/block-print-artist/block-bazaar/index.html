The blocks in the cabinet

Four hundred and twelve blocks, cut from sheesham by two carvers who work in the room above the table. These are the five that never leave it.

Booti

The small flower, four to eight petals depending on the block. The first block every printer learns, because a mistake cannot hide in it.

The dye house behind the table

Indigo is a vat, not a paint. The leaves are fermented with lime and jaggery for three weeks until the liquor turns copper, and the cloth goes in green and comes out blue when the air hits it. A vat can be worked for months and dies in a day if it is fed wrong.

Madder root is boiled with harda and alum for red; iron filings soured in lime give the black that Bagru is known for; pomegranate rind gives the yellow that turns olive with iron. No azo dyes, no discharge printing, no screen.

Cloth is washed in the river at Bagru before printing and again after, which is why the colours sit in the fibre instead of on it. Expect a half shade of variation between two pieces. That is the dye, not a fault.
