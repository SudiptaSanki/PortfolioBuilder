The rejects

Of 14,800 metres printed in 2026 we rejected 890 — six per cent. That is not unusual for hand printing, and it is priced into the per-metre rate rather than passed on as a surprise.

420 metres, July. The indigo vat went off overnight after a week of heat. The cloth came out a flat grey-blue and could not be re-dyed because the first dip had already fixed. The vat was emptied, rebuilt over nine days, and the Kyoto order slipped by a fortnight.

210 metres, March. Misregistration on a jaali repeat — the background block drifted by two millimetres across a twenty-metre table. It reads as a wobble at three metres and is obvious at thirty centimetres. Rejected by us before the customer saw it, reprinted at our cost.

260 metres, September. A new printer pushed the outline block too hard on damp cloth. The edges bled. He reprinted the batch himself over four days and has printed nothing but filling blocks since.

How a price is built

Per metre, not per piece. A single-colour indigo yardage is ₹1,150 a metre in cotton mul; each additional block or mordant adds ₹180 to ₹260. Minimum ten metres, because setting a table for less wastes half a day.

A new block costs ₹4,500 to ₹9,000 depending on size and detail, and belongs to the workshop unless the customer pays a further ₹3,000 for exclusivity. Cutting takes four to six days; the carver will not be hurried by a deadline.
