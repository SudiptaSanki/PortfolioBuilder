Catalogue of work

Six pieces that left the bench.

A working catalogue, not a stock list: each piece was made once, for a person or a place. Everything can be remade in principle, but no two pieces are identical, because no two pieces of wood are.

The baptismal bowl

Cut from a single storm-fallen trunk in Beit Jala, hollowed to a 9 mm wall, and left with one dark heart-line running across the floor of the bowl. The priest asked for the line to stay.

Domestic2024 · Haifa

The table from a roof

Three boards from a nineteenth-century roof beam in the Old City, joined with butterfly keys and no screws. The nail holes were filled with black wax and left visible; the restaurant owner asked for them to be pointed out to guests.

2.4 m
Wood

Forty years, one board

A serving board for a couple's fortieth anniversary, with the date carved at the centre and the initials of their four children around the rim. The wood came from the olive tree in the garden of the house they built in 1986.

A 1930s bowl, split along the pith, brought in by a granddaughter who remembered it on her grandmother's table. Repaired with four brass staples and no replacement wood; the split stays visible, as it should.

Sacred2025 · Pittsburgh

Nine mezuzot, one house

A set of nine cases for a synagogue's school wing, each hollowed from a single branch and carved with a shin on the face. The scrolls were supplied by the congregation's sofer, who rejected one case because the opening was two millimetres tight.

9

10–16 cm
Finish

Six spoons for a kitchen

A set for a cook who asked for six spoons that were not identical: one for stirring, one for tasting, one with a long handle for a deep pot, one small enough for a child, and two that simply felt right in the hand.
