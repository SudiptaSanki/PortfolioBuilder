The palm atlas

Choose a line, and a hand

Five lines, two hands. The working hand is the one you write with, and shows what you have made of the life; the receiving hand is the other, and shows what you came in with. A reading compares the two — one hand alone is a sentence without its other half.

Reading the working hand (right).

Which hand

Line C

Hriday rekhathe heart line

Runs
from the edge of the palm below the little finger, across the upper palm, towards the index and middle fingers
Classical reading
The old texts read this as the line of feeling and of the heart's weather: how a person gives affection, how they take it, and whether the two are the same. A curve that ends high, between the index and middle fingers, is read as a person who loves with judgement; one that ends low, near the middle finger alone, as one who loves with appetite.
In this hand
In the working hand it is read as the shape feeling has taken in your life: what you have learned to do with it, and what you have decided not to say.

The heart line does not tell you whether a marriage will last, and I will not pretend it does. It describes a manner of loving, not an outcome.

A question worth taking home: when did you last say the thing you actually felt, rather than the version of it that would be easier to hear?

The sitting

Forty-five minutes, in this order

I do not read both hands at once and I do not begin with the lines. The first ten minutes are for the question you came with, which is usually not the one you wrote down.

  1. 01

    The question

    You tell me why you came. I write it down, in front of you, in a small notebook. Nothing about your health or your death is discussed, and if the question is medical I will say so and send you to a doctor.

  2. 02

    The two hands

    Both palms, in daylight, on a cloth. The working hand first, then the receiving, then the comparison, which is where most of the reading actually happens.

  3. 03

    The note

    At the end I write you a note of four or five lines — not a chart, not a diagram. It is the part people keep. Several have come back years later with the note in their bag.

The limits

What a reading will not do

Palmistry is a cultural practice with a long memory, not a science and not a diagnosis. I hold to that distinction in the room, because the hour is only useful if nobody in it is being frightened.

  • No health, no pregnancy, no death. Not because I am squeamish, but because a line on a hand cannot know these things and fear is not a service.
  • No dates. Not for marriage, not for money, not for a job. A date is a guess dressed as knowledge.
  • No third parties. I read the hand of the person in front of me, not an absent husband's or a daughter's. If you want to understand someone else, bring them.
  • No filing. I keep no photographs, no copies, and no record beyond the note you take with you.

If you are in distress, this is not the room. Please speak to a doctor or a counsellor; I will happily be the second thing you do that week.

The room

Above the spice shop on Bazaar Road

Up one flight, past the sacks of cardamom, a room with a north window, two chairs, a table with a cloth, and a brass lamp that is not lit in the afternoon. The fan is loud between eleven and two; after that it is the street.

Sittings are in Malayalam or English, whichever you prefer, and I will not switch without asking. Tea is offered and is usually refused; I make it anyway.
