2025Personal

The architect who stopped drawing

Forty-one, precise, told me at the door she did not believe any of this. The heart line ended between the index and middle fingers, and the working hand had a callus the receiving hand did not. I asked when she had stopped drawing by hand. She sat back down for another twenty minutes.

2024Couple

Two hands, one table

A couple in their fifties, married twenty-nine years, who each wanted separately to know whether the other was happy. Their heart lines were nearly identical and their fate lines were not. I read them together and said almost nothing for the first ten minutes.

2023Personal

The fisherman's son

Twenty-six, about to take over his father's boat, and wanted to know whether he should. The fate line rose straight from the wrist, unbroken, which in the old books means a life that finds its shape early. I told him what it meant and also that the books had never met his father.

2021Teaching

Twelve students, one hour

A literature class at the college, on metaphor. I taught them to describe a hand without predicting anything, and then we read the descriptions aloud. The best one was four words long: "This palm is a kitchen."

2022Festival

The tent at Kochi-Muziris

Twenty sittings in a day, fifteen minutes each, at a long table under a tent. It is not a reading; it is a demonstration of one. I said so at the start of every hour, and the people who stayed were the ones who wanted that.

2019Personal

The man who asked for a date

He wanted a month. The fate line had a cross on it, which the old books read as an interruption, and I said that and no more. He pushed for a date three times; on the third I gave him back his money. He wrote to me two years later to say the interruption had been a job, and that he had wanted a number to blame.

2017Family

Two sisters, same lines

They came to prove the practice wrong, with a bet between them. Their hands were almost identical: same heart line, same head line, same short fate line. They were delighted. I asked whether they had ever made the same decision twice, and the elder sister stopped laughing.

2012Personal

The note that came back

A woman in her thirties, a note of four lines, and eleven years of nothing. Then a letter: she had kept the note in her purse through a divorce and two moves, and wanted to say that the fourth line had been right. I had forgotten the sitting entirely.

2024Personal

The nurse who did not want her future

She asked me to read only the life and head lines, and to leave the rest. I did. It was the most honest request I have had in twenty years: she worked in palliative care and knew exactly how much of a future she wanted named.
