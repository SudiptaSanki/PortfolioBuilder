The working table

Plan the egg before the first bath.

A pysanka is written in reverse. Whatever you wax now keeps the colour it has now, so the whole egg has to be composed before it touches dye. Set a motif, choose three baths in light-to-dark order, and the table will write the recipe with you.

    Your working recipe

      Four moves, one egg

      There is no sketching in pencil and no erasing. The kistka holds about thirty seconds of wax, so the hand learns to stop and refill without leaving a pool.

      1. 01

        Wash and divide

        The shell is wiped with vinegar, then divided with a light graphite line the wax will cover. Eight axes for a star, six for wheat. The pencil is a scaffold, never part of the design.

        20 min
      2. 02

        Wax the first white

        Beeswax at 118 °C through a copper funnel. Whatever the wax covers now stays eggshell-cream for good. This is the line that has to be right, because it is the one you cannot take back.

        35 min
      3. 03

        Bathe, wax, bathe

        Onion skin, then madder root, then indigo. Between baths the egg is blotted, never rubbed. Each wax line locks in the colour beneath it, so a dark line drawn late sits on a light field.

        1 h 10
      4. 04

        Melt the wax away

        Hold the egg beside a candle flame until the wax turns glossy, then wipe. The pattern arrives all at once, in reverse, which is the only magic trick I know.

        15 min

      What a single egg asks for

      One egg takes three evenings. A set of six takes a fortnight, because the dyes need their own pots and the wax needs to cool between passes. Dates are held with a deposit of a third.
