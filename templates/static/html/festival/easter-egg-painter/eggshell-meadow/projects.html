Selected eggs · 2019–2026

Six shells, six
reasons to write.

Every egg below was made for a person, a table or a grave. I have kept the working notes for each one: the shell, the baths, the hours, and the thing I got wrong the first time. Prices are shown only where the piece is repeatable.

Wedding pair · 2024

Two eggs for a wedding in Kosmach

A bride from Kosmach asked for the star her grandmother used and a wheat ear for the groom's family, who farm above the village. The pair had to be dyed in the same pots on the same afternoon so the reds would match; they are never quite identical, which is the point.

Triptych · 2023

Rye field for Aunt Halyna

Three eggs for three sisters, each a different bath order so the wheat changes colour as it moves across the table. Halyna kept the middle egg, the madder one, because it was the one that cracked at the base and had to be blown and sealed instead of kept whole.

Parish set · 2022

Forty eggs for the Kolomyia parish

The parish asked for forty eggs for the Easter baskets, all one pattern, all hen, all affordable. I dyed them in batches of ten and waxed the star by hand each time, which is how a set stays alive instead of becoming a print run. Three were stolen from the church porch in the first week.

Memorial · 2025

The lost village

One egg for a family who asked not to be named. The spiral is the old mark for a life that continues; the crosses are the four houses that are no longer standing. It is the only egg I have made in walnut and madder without any white left on the shell. They took it to the cemetery at Velykden.

Goose, whole

Commission set · 2026

Velykden, six for one table

A set of six for a family who wanted one egg for each member, motifs chosen by asking what each person remembered from childhood rather than by assigning symbols. The child asked for a sun; the grandfather asked for the green that used to come from buckthorn and is almost never seen now.
