Method

The egg is a diary
written in wax.

Pysanky are not painted. A line of hot beeswax protects the shell, the shell goes into a dye bath, and the colour underneath the wax survives. Three or four baths later the pattern is complete but invisible, buried under wax, until the flame takes it off. Everything you see was decided in the first hour.

Kolomyia, Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast Working since 2009 Fifth-generation in the family

A borrowed kistka, 2009

My grandmother kept her kistka in a tin that once held tea. When I was nineteen she handed it to me without ceremony and said the only useful thing she ever said about the work: write slowly, the wax is listening. That first spring I made forty eggs and burned thirty of them.

The studio is the same room now, though the tin has been replaced by a copper one from Kosmach. The window faces north so the dyes look true, and there is a bucket of goose eggs under the bench from April to June, each one blown and dried for a fortnight before it is worth touching.

What the dyes actually do

Traditional pysanka colour has a grammar. Yellow comes first because it is the lightest bath and the wax laid over it keeps the shell cream. Red follows, then dark blue or brown, then black if the design is a mourning one. A Hutsul egg will often run onion, madder, indigo, walnut; a Pokuttian egg may stop at two baths and let the white do the work.

I use aniline dyes for commissions because they are consistent, and natural baths for the spring eggs I keep. Madder root gives a red that leans brown in the second year. Indigo is the only bath I still cannot predict on a humid day. The recipe table on the studio page is the same arithmetic I use at the bench.

Rules the studio keeps

  • 01
    No acrylic, no varnish. A pysanka is finished when the wax is gone. If it needs a coat to look right, it was not right.
  • 02
    Whole or blown, never cracked. Eggs for hanging are blown through a single hole at the base and sealed with wax. Eggs for the hand stay whole and are weighed before and after.
  • 03
    The motif has to belong to someone. I will not put a random star on a wedding egg. Tell me the family, the village, the reason, and we will find the mark that fits.
  • 04
    Nothing leaves before it is dry. A pysanka sits ten days in the dark before it is packed. Rushing the drying is how a red turns orange in July.

Years, briefly

  1. 2009

    First spring

    Forty hen eggs, one borrowed kistka, thirty cracked shells. The four that survived are still in the family cupboard.

  2. 2013

    Kolomyia museum

    Two eggs accepted for the regional spring show: a Hutsul wedding pair and a small rye-field egg that took eleven evenings.

  3. 2018

    The meadow commission

    A family from Lviv asked for sixty eggs for a wedding table. I dyed through two pots of madder and learned to keep a written bath log.

  4. 2022

    Teaching

    Four workshops a season, eight people at most. The first rule taught is how to hold the kistka without gripping it like a pen.

  5. 2026

    This list

    The spring list opens on the first Sunday after the thaw. Commissions are booked by letter, not by form alone.

What is on the table
