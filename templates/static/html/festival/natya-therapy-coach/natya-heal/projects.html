Casebook

Six rooms, and what moved in them.

Case notes are written with consent and with identifying details changed. Outcomes are described in the client's own words where possible, not mine. Filter by setting.

Setting
SchoolCase 04 · 2025

The boy who could not sit still

Government higher secondary, Vyasarpadi · 11 sessions

Referred by a history teacher for “disruption”. No diagnosis, no counselling available in the school. We worked standing, at the back of the room, for eleven weeks: raudra first because it was the only rasa he trusted, then veera, then shanta for one minute at a time.

Outcome“I can stay in the class now. I still get angry. I just know where to put it.”
ClinicCase 02 · 2024

Twenty minutes, one hand

Burns unit, Kilpauk · 6 sessions

A woman of forty-one with burns across the left arm and shoulder, six weeks post-graft. She could not look at the arm and would not speak about it. We worked with the right hand only, using shanta and karuna, and never approached the injured side. The physiotherapist joined the last two sessions to carry the work into movement.

Outcome“I touched it with the other hand for the first time. I did not cry. I did not look away.”
EldersCase 06 · 2026

The chair that does not need a memory

Elders' day centre, Mylapore · weekly, ongoing

Half the group has a dementia diagnosis. The work is not recall but sequence: the same eight gestures, the same order, every week, with the breath between them. The repetition is the point. Carers report that the hour after the session is the calmest of the day.

Outcome“She cannot tell me what she did. She hums the rhythm on the way home.”
TrainingCase 08 · 2026

Teaching the alphabet, not the item

Counsellor training, Chennai & Coimbatore · 3 cohorts

A two-day training for school counsellors who want a non-verbal option in the room. We cover breath, three rasas they can use without a dance background, and the limits of the work: what to refer, what not to touch, how to stop. Assessment is a recorded practice session, reviewed with the group.

OutcomeThirty-one counsellors trained; nine now run weekly groups in their schools.
SchoolCase 01 · 2023

Panic between periods

Private school, Adyar · 9 sessions

A sixteen-year-old with panic attacks that arrived in corridors and not in classrooms. We practised bhayanaka deliberately, with an exit visible and a bell in her hand, until the feeling could be entered for five breaths and left. The school moved her locker to a quieter corridor; both things helped.

OutcomePanic episodes down from four a week to fewer than one, at six-month follow-up.
ClinicCase 05 · 2025

The carer who had no time to be one

Individual sessions, Alwarpet · 14 sessions

A woman of thirty-eight caring for a parent with Parkinson's, referred by her own GP for exhaustion. The first four sessions involved no movement at all — just tea, breath, and permission to sit. When we began, hasya came first, because she had not laughed in eleven months and it frightened her.

Outcome“I sleep through the night now, most nights. I also told my brother no.”
