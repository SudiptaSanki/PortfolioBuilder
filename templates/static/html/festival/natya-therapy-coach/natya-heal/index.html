Grief does not need a sentence. It needs a gesture that can hold it.

I teach people to move what they cannot say. Not dance lessons, and not talk therapy with music on: a structured practice drawn from the rasa theory of Bharatanatyam, used with adolescents, carers, school counsellors and people in the middle of a hard year.

The rasa card

Compose a movement protocol

Choose a rasa, the place in the body where it sits, and the length you have. The card that comes out is the shape of a real session: an entry, a sequence, a breath ratio, and the one thing not to do. It is silent by design — this practice is done in the room, with a body, not through a screen.

Case note · movement NH—01

Karuna at the chest

The rasa of grief, held where most people hold it.

Breath
4 : 6
Time
25 min
Level
seated
  1. Sit on the floor with the spine tall and the hands resting on the sternum.
  2. Inhale for four; on the exhale, let the chest sink a centimetre and stay there.
  3. Lift the right hand into alapadma and turn it slowly outward, as if offering something.
  4. Hold the turn for one full breath, then let the hand fall open.
  5. Finish with six rounds of 4:6 breathing and sit for one minute without moving.

Do not ask the person to name the loss. The gesture is doing the naming. Keep the room warm and stop at the first sign of numbness in the hands.

How it works

Three doors into the same room

Natya therapy borrows from a performance tradition but asks for no performance. There is no audience, no mirror, and no correction of form. What we borrow is the grammar: a rasa to begin from, a breath to regulate with, and a gesture to finish in.

  1. 01

    Rasa, not mood

    A mood is weather; a rasa is a structure that can be entered deliberately. Naming the rasa gives the session a direction and gives the person permission to feel one thing at a time.

  2. 02

    Breath before movement

    Nothing begins until the exhale is longer than the inhale. A body in fight-or-flight cannot make meaning from gesture; it can only rehearse the alarm.

  3. 03

    A gesture to end on

    Every session closes with a hand position held for three breaths and released. The release matters more than the hold — it teaches the nervous system that an emotion can be put down.
