Programmes · what flew and what did not

Four lines, one of them lost

Flight results are the only numbers that matter here. The mission that lost its tether after 41 days is documented in the same detail as the one that worked, because the failure is the reason the next four survived.

4 of 4 shown

01

Kite 2

Deorbit demonstrator · 420 km · 2024
Flew · deorbited

A 5.0 km aluminium line on a 180 kg satellite, deployed in three stages over eleven hours. The line generated 360 V at 0.42 A on the first sunlit pass, which was 18 % below the model and traced to a contactor impedance higher than the chamber value.

It lowered perigee from 420 km to 180 km in 148 days and re-entered on the 151st. The interesting number is not the deorbit: it is that the line survived 2,900 thermal cycles without a measurable change in resistance.

02

Nordlys 1

Technology demonstrator · 600 km · 2022
Line severed

A 2.4 km line that worked for 41 days, then went open-circuit in a single telemetry frame with no precursor in the current trace. The line had been hit by something about a millimetre across; the cut face showed a melted lip and a short fray, consistent with a hypervelocity impact rather than a wear failure.

The spacecraft could not deorbit itself and was left in a 600 km orbit with an estimated 240-year lifetime. That outcome is why every Longline design since carries two cutters, a restartable deployment brake, and a residual-propulsion check we insist the customer passes before we ship.

03

Constellation deorbit study

12 satellites · 900 km · 2025
Study delivered

A study for a 12-satellite constellation at 900 km, where the 25-year rule is not met by drag alone. Each satellite would carry a 6 km line and a 40 W contactor, and deorbit in 14 months instead of 90 years.

The mass penalty is the number that decides these programmes: 11.2 kg per satellite including the spool, the contactor and the cutters, against 34 kg of propellant for the same Δv. The study was accepted; the constellation has not been funded.

04

Partial elevator concept

20 km · closed 2023
Closed

A 20 km line hanging below a station, used to raise payloads without propellant. The mechanics worked on paper and the first 200 m deployment test worked on the bench. The failure analysis did not: the cut probability over twenty years of operation came out at 0.3 per year for a line that could not be replaced, and no customer would accept that number on a crewed platform.

We published the result rather than the concept. Two of the three materials we tested for that study are now the baseline for our deorbit lines.
