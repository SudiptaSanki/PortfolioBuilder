STUDY FILE 01 · DEPLOYMENT STUDY

The window was wrong

CLIENT
Constellation operator (name withheld)
SYSTEM
Electrodynamic deorbit tether
SPACECRAFT / LINE
180 kg smallsat · 2.5 km bare line
ORBIT
800 km sun-synchronous
SCOPE
Deployment study, full campaign
STATUS
Delivered · recommendation accepted · deployed 14 months later, clean

THE BRIEF

Analyse the deployment of a 2.5 km electrodynamic deorbit tether from a 180 kg smallsat, against a fixed launch-and-deploy slot the client had already committed to commercially. The slot fell in a forecast window of heightened solar activity, which raises atmospheric density at 800 km and changes both the drag environment and the electrodynamic current the bare line would collect.

THE DECISIONS

The deployment simulation — bead model, deployment rates swept across the client’s planned profile — found a rate band in which libration growth outruns the deployer’s damping, and the band sat squarely inside the planned profile. Separately, the raised-drag environment in the chosen window shortened the time the partially deployed system would spend in its least stable configuration. The recommendation was written plainly: do not deploy in this window. Delay one quarter, halve the deployment rate through the unstable band, and add a tip damper that cost 1.9 kg the client did not want to spend.

THE OUTCOME

The client delayed. The study — whose main content was a recommendation against the client’s own schedule — was the entire deliverable, and it was paid for without argument, because the alternative was priced in the same report: a deployed-but-rotating system is not a deorbit device, it is two uncontrolled objects. Fourteen months later the system deployed on the revised profile, without anomaly. The damper flew. The launch slot did not matter in the end; the rate band would have.

“Jeroen told us our launch slot was the problem, in writing, with the unstable band printed next to our own deployment profile. Nobody enjoys hearing that. The board read the alternative — a rotating 180 kg satellite on 2.5 km of line — and the delay approved itself. When we did deploy, it was the dullest week of the programme. Dull is what you want.”

STUDY FILE 02 · INDEPENDENT DESIGN REVIEW

The resonance in the blind spot

CLIENT
Research agency (name withheld)
SYSTEM
Momentum-exchange demonstrator, study phase
SPACECRAFT / LINE
Demonstrator pair · 4 km line
ORBIT
600 km, mid-inclination
SCOPE
Independent review of client’s dynamics model
STATUS
Delivered · finding accepted · fix verified on rebuilt model

THE BRIEF

Review the dynamics model behind a momentum-exchange demonstrator’s design case. The client’s model — a 12-bead lumped-mass simulation, competently built — showed comfortable libration margins at every deployed length. The review board wanted a second pair of eyes before the design froze, because the margins looked, in their words, “suspiciously calm”.

THE DECISIONS

The calm was an artefact. The client’s model carried no bending stiffness, which is a defensible simplification for steady state and a blinding one during deployment: rebuilt with bending terms, the model showed the first transverse (skip-rope) mode coupling into in-plane libration at 68% deployment, at exactly the deployment rate the programme planned to use. The fix cost no hardware redesign: a revised rate profile through the coupled band, and a passive damper specification at the tip mass. Both were verified on the rebuilt 200-bead model, and the resonance lengths were printed in the review as numbers to avoid, not as a paragraph to admire.

THE OUTCOME

The finding was accepted without dispute — the client’s own team reproduced it within a week once they knew where to look, which is how an independent review is supposed to end. The design froze with the revised profile. The deliverable was not a better model; it was the knowledge of which lengths and rates the real system must never sit at.

STUDY FILE 03 · DEORBIT SIZING STUDY

Where the simple relation stops

CLIENT
Satellite operator (name withheld)
SYSTEM
Electrodynamic deorbit tether
SPACECRAFT / LINE
600 kg satellite · lengths to 30 km assessed
ORBIT
800 km sun-synchronous
SCOPE
Sizing study with assumption audit
STATUS
Delivered · adopted as the programme’s reference design

THE BRIEF

Size a deorbit tether for a 600 kg satellite at end of life, for the operator’s disposal-compliance case. The client’s opening position was a 30 km line, chosen because a longer tether deorbits faster and the compliance deadline was uncomfortable.

THE DECISIONS

The sizing ran on the same relation printed on this site’s front page — and the study’s main work was marking where that relation stops describing the system. First: an electrodynamic tether’s deorbit thrust comes from current collection against the ionosphere, not from gravity-gradient tension, so the tension number was never the governing one — it sizes the load path, not the disposal timeline. Second, the client’s own growth slide — a 100 km follow-on system, in steel wire — put the line’s mass at 23% of the tip mass at their orbit: the near-massless assumption the simple formula stands on fails outright there, in the sizer’s own terms, and every downstream number would have inherited the error. The study recommended a 12 km UHMWPE-class system, with the thrust model, the contactor assumptions and the disposal timeline printed beside it.

THE OUTCOME

The 12 km design was adopted as the programme’s reference. The compliance case took two months longer to close than the 30 km fiction would have — and it closed on numbers that survive audit, which is the entire point of a compliance case. The assumption flags were delivered in the report, not in an appendix the reader was expected to skip.
