02 / Motherboard & Bus DDR5 · PCIe 5.0 · M.2 Gen5

The bus is the contract

A motherboard is not a picture of a computer, it is a schedule: every device on the board is arguing for a slice of the same memory bus, and the board is the arbitration you can photograph.

I design across those boundaries — RTL, rails, firmware and the thermal log that tells you what actually happened.

03 / CPU & Silicon Die 16 cores · 96 MB L3 · 3 nm

A die is a budget of heat and time

Close the distance and the cooler goes transparent: eight cores under a soldered heat spreader, a 96 MB L3 slice around them, and a memory controller that decides whether the array is ever fed.

Thermal density, not clock speed, is what ends an inference run. The interesting engineering is in the last two millimetres of silicon and the first two centimetres of copper.

04 / Liquid Cooling Loop 360 mm radiator · 2.4 L/min

Heat is the schedule

A 360 mm radiator, a pump reservoir and two metres of tubing are a latency budget written in coolant. Every litre per minute buys headroom the scheduler can spend later.

Watch the loop under AI surge: the flow rate climbs, the coolant warms, and the fan curve follows a second behind it — the same control law a rack of accelerators runs at 400 Hz.

05 / GPU & AI Tensor Core Triple fan · 24 GB GDDR7

Inference has a floor, and it is bandwidth

Weights arrive from the host over PCIe, land in 24 GB of GDDR7, and move through a tensor matrix that is faster than the memory feeding it. Press AI Surge and watch the packets crowd the link.

Tensor cores are the glamorous part. The floor is the memory hierarchy around them — which is why every accelerator I design starts with the smallest buffer that still keeps the array busy.
