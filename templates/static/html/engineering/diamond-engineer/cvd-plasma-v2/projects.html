Growth-run log · Sheet 02 — run files

Three runs, filed as they happened.

Every engagement in this practice closes as a run file: the brief as received, the decisions as taken, the outcome as measured — including the run that failed its specification and the job whose deliverable was a report saying the material was not good enough. Client names are withheld where an NDA asks for it; roles, sectors and numbers are as filed.

RUN 187 · HEAT SPREADERS

The smaller sale

Delivered — cheaper grade, on the evidence
ClientLaser-systems OEM (name under NDA)
MaterialPolycrystalline CVD, thermal grade
Part24 plates · 15 × 15 mm · 0.40 mm
OutcomeQualified first pass · per-part cost −58%

The brief

A laser-diode array programme arrived with a finished drawing: single-crystal electronic-grade spreaders, the highest-k material on Table T1, specified by a thermal engineer who had — reasonably — reached for the best number on the page.

The decisions

Before any growth, the practice characterised the client's actual stack for one €620 batch. The arithmetic: at 0.40 mm over 225 mm², single-crystal gives Rth = 0.00085 K/W and thermal grade gives 0.00119 K/W — a difference of 0.34 mK/W, against roughly 8 mK/W of joint and interface resistance in their assembly. The expensive grade was buying a 4% improvement in a budget it did not dominate.

The outcome

Thermal grade was grown, lapped and delivered with its measured k on the report. Junction temperatures across the qualification batch landed within 1.5 °C of the run sheet's prediction. Per-part cost fell 58% against the original drawing. The client kept the cheaper specification for production.

“Marta talked us out of the expensive plate. Her characterisation of our actual stack showed the thermal grade met the budget with margin — she sold us the cheaper run and printed the working. The junction temperatures landed where her sheet said they would.”

Thermal engineering lead, laser-systems OEM — client name withheld under NDA; role and sector as stated.
RUN 203 · OPTICAL WINDOW

The window that failed, twice filed

First run failed spec — regrown, no run-day charge
ClientThermal-imaging photonics firm
MaterialPolycrystalline CVD, IR grade
PartWindow Ø 40 mm · 1.0 mm · uncoated
OutcomeRun 1: 61.2% · Regrow: 68.4% · 3 weeks late

The brief

An uncoated window for the 8–12 µm band. The specification was a band-average transmission of at least 67% — close to what bare diamond surfaces allow, since Fresnel reflection at two faces caps an uncoated window near 70% before any absorption is counted.

The decisions

The first run measured 61.2%: an absorption feature near 1130 cm⁻¹ — nitrogen-related — was eating the middle of the band. The file records the failure without varnish, and the regrow clause was invoked as written: same part, changed recipe — nitrogen in the feed gas reduced, growth rate lowered — at no run-day charge to the client.

The outcome

The regrown window measured 68.4% band-average and was delivered with its full transmission curve. It was also delivered three weeks late, and the file says that too: a regrow is free in reactor days, not in calendar. The client has since booked two further windows with the longer lead time written into the order.

RUN 211 · QUANTUM SUBSTRATE

Not yet good enough — and that was the deliverable

Deliverable: a report that says not yet
ClientUniversity quantum-photonics group
MaterialSingle-crystal CVD, electronic grade
PartPlates 3 × 3 mm · 0.50 mm · NV-host grade
OutcomeReport issued · follow-up run booked

The brief

A research group needed host plates for nitrogen-vacancy emitter work, with a written threshold for substitutional nitrogen and strain. They asked for plates. The practice proposed characterising a first growth before committing their grant to a full batch.

The decisions

Characterisation — Raman, photoluminescence and birefringence mapping — showed nitrogen above the group's threshold and strain high enough to split their emitter lines. Rather than grow the batch anyway, the engagement was converted, with the group's agreement, into a report: the measured values, the threshold they missed, and the specified changes — a higher-purity gas train, substrate offcut selection, a slower growth rate.

The outcome

The report was the deliverable, invoiced at the characterisation and report rates — a small fraction of the batch price. The group took it to their consortium review as evidence for a gas-train upgrade, and a follow-up run under the changed recipe is booked. Nobody bought plates that would not have worked.
