Three reactors, one metrology room, and a rule about unmeasured film.

Carbon Lattice has been growing diamond since 2013, when the first reactor was a rebuilt 2.45 GHz cavity bought at auction. We ship about 1,400 films a year and reject roughly one in nine.

Why we reject a ninth of what we grow

A diamond film can look perfect and be useless. The failure is usually invisible: a 4 cm⁻¹ widening of the Raman line, a sp² fraction that crept from 0.8% to 2.5% because the chamber had a slow air leak, or a grain boundary network that stops heat at exactly the interface the customer cares about. So nothing leaves the building without a Raman spectrum, a thickness map and a thermal measurement on the same coupon, and the coupon is cut from the film it certifies.

That rule costs us material and time. It is also why our polycrystalline spreaders are specified by thermal engineers who have been burned by someone else's "1,900 W/m·K" claim measured on a different sample.

The sequence, start to finish

  • Seeding. A 5 nm nanodiamond suspension, spun on and sonicated, giving 1010 cm⁻² nucleation sites. On single-crystal seeds we skip this and rely on the polished, off-axis surface.
  • Growth. 40–600 hours depending on thickness, at 0.4–9 µm/h, with the substrate temperature held to ±8 °C by a two-colour pyrometer feeding the microwave power.
  • Cool-down. Six hours, ramped, because a 500 µm film on a 4″ seed will crack if the thermal gradient across the stack exceeds 40 °C.
  • Release and polish. Laser cut, then mechanical lapping to Ra 3 nm for optical work; thermal grades are left as-grown, because roughness helps the interface.
  • Metrology. Raman at three points, white-light interferometry for thickness, and a laser flash measurement for κ on a witness coupon.
The plasma does not care what your drawing says. It grows what the surface energy allows, and it will grow it at the edge of the holder first.

What we are bad at

Large-area single crystal. Our best is a 12 × 12 mm plate, and the dislocation density rises by a factor of five at the corners. Anything above 15 mm goes to a heteroepitaxial supplier, and we say so in the first meeting rather than the third.

We are also slow on small runs. A 40-hour growth slot is the same setup cost as a 400-hour one, so a single 5 µm coating is genuinely expensive here — we will quote it, and we will tell you if a competitor's price means they are skipping the metrology.

Thirteen years of runs

  1. 2013 — 2015

    Auction cavity

    A rebuilt 2.45 GHz reactor in a rented basement. First 50 mm film in 2014, with a Raman line you would not ship today.

  2. 2016 — 2019

    Metrology first

    Raman, interferometry and laser flash brought in-house. The reject rate went from 4% to 18%, then settled at 11% as the recipes improved.

  3. 2020 — 2023

    Thermal grade

    100 mm polycrystalline spreaders, 1,800 W/m·K guaranteed, and the first quantum customer asking for nitrogen-vacancy control.

  4. 2024 — now

    Single-crystal push

    Step-flow on 12 mm plates and a 915 MHz reactor for 150 mm coatings — still in qualification, still not something we will promise.
