What the plasma is actually doing

Atomic hydrogen is the whole trick

Diamond and graphite differ by 0.02 eV per atom. In a furnace you get graphite every time. In a hydrogen plasma, atomic H preferentially etches the sp² bonds and leaves the sp³ surface terminated — so the crystal grows one metastable layer at a time.

01

Activation

The microwave field accelerates electrons until they ionise the gas; the ball you see is a steady state of ionisation and recombination. Electron density around 1011 cm⁻³ at 100 Torr is enough to break H₂ into H and CH₄ into CH₃.

02

Transport

Methyl radicals cross a 10 mm boundary layer to the surface. Most arrive and leave again. The growth rate is set by the small fraction that finds a dangling bond — which is why power and pressure matter more than methane flow beyond 3%.

03

Etch

Atomic hydrogen removes about one carbon in ten that lands. Without that loss term you would grow a black, graphitic film at three times the rate. The loss is the quality control, and it is the one thing a recipe cannot buy.

Thermal spreaders
