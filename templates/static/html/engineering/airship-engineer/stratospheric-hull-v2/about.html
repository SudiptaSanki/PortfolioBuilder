About · Method · The honest page

An engineer for the part of the airship that holds the sky out.

Dr Katrin Vogel — structures engineer by training, envelope and lift engineer by conviction. This page is the story, the method, and then the part most practices put in a footnote: what is actually true about stratospheric airships in this decade.

The story

I spent my first decade on aircraft primary structures in Hamburg — fuselage frames, and above all pressure bulkheads: the wall that holds the cabin's air in and the sky out, ten thousand cycles a year. A pressurised hull is a promise you make to physics and then keep, measurably, for thirty years. It is the best possible schooling for airships, because an envelope is the same promise made of film instead of aluminium, at a hundredth of the pressure and a thousand times the area.

In 2013 I joined a stratospheric demonstrator programme as its structures lead, and watched a competent team — good people, real funding — spend two years discovering, in the most expensive order possible, the lessons that a one-week lift budget would have taught in sequence. The envelope was fine. The altitude was fantasy, the night energy was unbudgeted, and the laminate was ageing faster than the schedule. None of it required a wind tunnel to know. It required a table and the nerve to read it aloud.

Since 2016 I have run this practice from Friedrichshafen — the town where the Zeppelin works stood, which keeps a person honest about airships — doing the reading-aloud as a service: lift and mass budgets, envelope materials qualification, energy balance, and independent review for the funders who were not in the room. Twenty-three budgets signed. Four programmes told, in writing, to descend, enlarge or stop. I count the second number as the credential.

How a study runs

M.01

Mission profile first

Altitude, season, latitude, payload duty cycle — fixed in writing before any sizing. Most studies die at this stage, which is exactly where dying is cheap.

M.02

The lift & mass budget

Built from the printed atmosphere table with every allowance — envelope, propulsion, energy, reserve — argued line by line. The client sees every deduction, including the fatal ones.

M.03

Materials & energy in parallel

The envelope's ageing clocks (UV, ozone, permeation) and the hour-by-hour energy audit run alongside the budget — audited across the worst week of the year, never the average day.

M.04

The written verdict

Proceed, descend, enlarge, or stop — with the numbers attached and my name under them. The report is the deliverable, and it is priced the same whichever way it lands.

Engagement terms

Advisory only. Studies from this practice are engineering advice. Certification is the manufacturer's road through its own authorities; no study here is, or substitutes for, a certification basis.

Scoped in writing. Every engagement starts with a one-page scope: question, inputs, deliverable, fee. If the question changes, the scope is re-issued — never silently stretched.

The honest page

Five things that are true about this trade whether or not they appear in a funding deck.

01 · Prototypes, not a fleet

No operational stratospheric airship fleet exists. What exists are demonstrators, research platforms and balloons — flown bravely, lost occasionally, and none of them a product you can order. A programme plan that assumes a mature vehicle is a plan with fiction in the critical path.

02 · The stratosphere is not calm — it is merely thin

The winter polar vortex, the quasi-biennial oscillation and sudden stratospheric warmings mean winds at 20 km can exceed an airship's propulsion for weeks at a time. There is a minimum-wind layer up there; it moves with season and hemisphere. Station-keeping is the unsolved problem of this trade, and it appears on page one of every study I sign.

03 · Leakage is a clock, not a risk

Helium permeates every envelope film ever manufactured. The engineering question is never whether but how fast — and the replenishment plan, its mass and its cost belong in the lift budget from the first draft, not in an operations appendix nobody reads.

04 · UV is a clock too

Above the ozone layer's protection, sunlight dismantles polymers on a schedule. Envelope service life is a rating with stated conditions and inspection intervals — the way Case File 02 was settled — and any laminate sold without an ageing curve is being sold on its first-day strength, which is the one day it will never see again.

05 · If the budget says no, the report says no

Four programmes have paid this practice in full to be told to descend, enlarge or stop. That is not a failure rate; it is the service. A consultant whose conclusions lean toward the client's hopes is a second marketing department with a calculator.

06 · Balloons are the honest competition

For many stratospheric jobs — short campaigns, recoverable payloads, no station-keeping — a balloon is cheaper, available and proven. Where a balloon does the mission, my studies say so, and two of the twenty-three budgets on this site's record end with exactly that recommendation.
