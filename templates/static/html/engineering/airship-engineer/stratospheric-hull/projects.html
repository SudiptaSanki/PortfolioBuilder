HW-04 · first flight 2023

Aurora

30,000 m³ · 20 km
Atacama, Chile

Mission

A 90-day stratospheric platform for a hyperspectral imager over the Atacama, where the cloud statistics are the best on the planet and the diurnal temperature swing is the worst. The customer needed 450 kg of payload with 3 kW of power and a pointing stability better than 0.05°.

Hull

Superpressure envelope, 96 m × 24 m, 210 g/m² laminate with a 6% ballonet for the day swing. Helium fill at 61% of envelope volume so the hull reaches its 600 Pa limit exactly at station. Fins sized for a 12 m/s crosswind at 20 km, which is the 95th percentile for the site.

Result

61 days aloft over two flights, 1.43% helium loss over the first 30 days — inside the 1.5% budget. The day swing reached 1,180 Pa before the ballonet took it, and the hull showed no measurable seam creep at recovery. The lesson was the power system, not the envelope: the CIGS array lost 9% of its output to a delamination at the busbar.

HW-02 · first flight 2021

Lumen

60,000 m³ · 20 km
Tarapacá, Chile

Mission

A telecom demonstration: a 380 kg payload of LTE base station and backhaul terminal at 20 km, with the coverage argument depending on staying up for 30 days without a descent. That requirement, not the payload, set the hull size.

Hull

139 m × 35 m, 195 g/m² laminate, zero-pressure with a forward ballonet of 9,000 m³. Two 6 kW propulsion pods on the gondola. The ballonet is the whole design: it lets the hull shed 800 Pa of day pressure without venting helium, which is what makes a 30-day hold possible at all.

Result

34 days aloft. The ballonet worked; the blower did not — a 12-hour failure on day 19 cost 400 m of altitude before recovery. Coverage tests met the link budget at 96% of the modelled footprint, and the programme moved to a second hull with a redundant blower.

HW-07 · first flight 2024

Kestrel

12,000 m³ · 18 km
Almería, Spain

Mission

A cargo demonstrator for remote mine sites: 120 kg of spares to a site 300 km from the nearest road, landing within 500 m of a marked pad. The economic case needs 40 flights a year from one envelope, which makes turnaround the design driver.

Hull

72 m × 18 m, 230 g/m² laminate — heavier than Aurora because the hull is handled on the ground twice a week. A removable 4 m cargo hatch in the lower skin, ballonet inside, and a vent valve sized to dump 60 Pa/s on descent.

Result

Six flights in eleven weeks, best turnaround 41 hours. The hatch seal leaked 0.9 kg of helium per flight, which is 3% of gas mass and enough to need a top-up every third flight. Redesigned for the next hull; the mass penalty of the heavier laminate was worth it.

HW-13 · first flight 2025

Nordlys

22,000 m³ · 19 km
Svalbard, Norway

Mission

An ice-thickness radar campaign over the Arctic in polar night, when there is no solar power at all for eight weeks. The envelope has to lift 260 kg of radar and 260 kg of batteries, and stay above 18 km for 40 days without sunlight.

Hull

88 m × 22 m with a 260 g/m² laminate and an oversized ballonet, because at −70 °C the gas shrinks 22% and the hull has to breathe inward without wrinkling. Fins are heated at the leading edge; ice on a fin changes the trim more than it changes the drag.

Result

40 days, 19 km mean altitude, 2.1% helium loss — the highest of any Höhenwerk hull, traced to a seam batch that failed its peel test after cycling but was already in the envelope. The batch is now quarantined and every seam carries a coupon tag.

HW-11 · first flight 2022

Sirocco

8,000 m³ · 12 km
Riyadh province

Mission

A boundary-layer dust campaign: 80 kg of particle counters flown in a slow vertical profile between 8 and 12 km, where the dust layer sits over the Arabian plateau. The science needs repeated short flights, not endurance.

Hull

62 m × 16 m, zero-pressure, 200 g/m² laminate, no ballonet — the diurnal swing is handled by venting, because the flights are daylight-only and 6 hours long. Cheapest hull we have built per cubic metre: €186.

Result

23 flights in one season, 14 of them usable. The failure mode was ground handling: two hulls abraded at the nose on the same concrete pad before we put down a mat. The dust data itself was good enough for two papers and a revised regional model.

HW-01 · 2016, rebuilt 2024

Filament

300 m³ · 0 km
Konstanz test cell

Mission

The original envelope, kept as a pressure rig. Every new laminate batch is flown in Filament's shell — a 300 m³ sphere inflated to 1.5× its design differential pressure and held for 30 days with strain gauges on 48 points.

Hull

Spherical, 8.3 m diameter, 300 m³, with a bolted test flange and interchangeable coupons. It is not a flight article and never will be: the seams are deliberately the weak point, so a batch failure does not destroy the rig.

Result

41 batches qualified since 2018, four rejected. The rig found the seam-tape problem that later showed up on Nordlys, six months before the flight — which is the entire argument for keeping a test cell that costs more per year than it earns.
