ENTRY 05 · THE ENGINEER

I came to interferometry from the arguing end of physics: a national measurement institute, where a number is not finished until its uncertainty budget survives people whose hobby is finding the missing term.

From there, nine years on lithography-adjacent benches, where displacement measurement is not a specialism but the floor the whole industry stands on — and where I learned the trade’s open secret: the instrument is rarely the problem. The floor is. The air is. The hour of day is. Most “faults” I have ever been called to were buildings, faithfully measured.

Since 2019 I have practised independently from Delft, building and calibrating interferometric systems for labs and manufacturers who need a figure they can defend in a review meeting. The practice is small on purpose: one bench, one engineer, and a short register of services I can stand behind. I have never met a vibration problem that a more expensive laser could fix — and a practice that remembers that stays honest about what it sells.

ENTRY 06

How an engagement runs

FOUR STAGES · SURVEY BEFORE INSTRUMENT
01
The requirement, in measurand terms

What changes, by how much, how fast, and against what limit? If the requirement cannot be written as a measurand with a threshold, we write it together before anything is specified. Half of all overspecified instruments are born in this conversation, and die in it too.

02
The environmental survey

Floor spectra at several hours, thermal drift across a working day, air-path audit. Fixed fee, credited against a build. The survey report is yours whether or not a build follows — and it is written to be shown to other suppliers.

03
The build, characterised as it grows

The system is assembled and measured at each stage, so the final budget is a summary of records, not a hope written at the end. If the survey said the room would limit us, the design answers the room first.

04
Calibration & handover

Traceable calibration, the full uncertainty budget printed and signed, and your people trained on the alignment — including the part where I show them what the system looks like when it is lying, and how to catch it.

ENTRY 07

The honesty page

PRINTED IN EVERY REPORT
WHY IT EXISTS

Every report this practice issues carries these four statements on its first page. They have cost work twice. They have never cost a client a review.

  • A fringe is not a fact until the drift is bounded. An interferometer reports change against its own recent past. Until the thermal and mechanical drift over the measurement window is measured and printed, the number is a story the light is telling.
  • Every figure ships with its conditions. Hour, floor state, temperature, bandwidth. A precision figure without them is advertising, and it will be treated as such by the first competent reviewer who reads it — better that it never leaves this bench.
  • The budget keeps all its terms. Terms are not removed because they are large or inconvenient. If a client needs a smaller number, the honest route is a better measurement, and I will happily sell that instead.
  • The cheaper instrument wins when it meets the requirement. The requirement is the specification, not the technology. Where an accelerometer, a dial gauge or a ruler meets it, the report says so, in writing, with the comparison attached.
ENTRY 08

Engagement terms

SHORT, BECAUSE THEY ARE READ
Starting point

Every engagement starts with the environmental survey (fixed €1,800) unless your room is already characterised — in which case, send the spectra and we start at the build.

Quoting

Builds are quoted in days at the printed rates against the survey. The quote names its assumptions; if an assumption breaks, we re-quote in the open, not in the invoice.

Deliverables

The system, its calibration record, its full uncertainty budget, and training. Reports are written to be handed to reviewers without me in the room.

Fees & results

No success fees, ever. A measurement whose fee depends on its outcome is not a measurement. The invoice is the same whichever way the number lands.

Confidentiality

Client names, parts and processes stay confidential; that is why the case files print roles and measurands instead of names. Methods and budgets are publishable — they are the credential.

Availability

Two build slots per quarter, booked in order. Surveys and calibrations run between builds; the register on the log page shows the current state.
