CB-M3 · 2024

Mirror segment bench

1.2 m aperture
Space telescope programme

Requirement

Measure the figure of eighteen hexagonal mirror segments, 1.2 m across, to λ/100 at 632.8 nm, with the part held in a mount that has to survive launch vibration. Each segment had to be certified at 20 °C and at −10 °C after thermal cycling.

Build

A vertical-axis Fizeau with a 1.3 m reference flat on a separate foundation, 13-frame acquisition, and a 36-term Zernike reduction. The segment sits on three whiffletree points; the mount's own distortion is measured with the segment replaced by a λ/20 flat.

Result

λ/118 figure at 20 °C, λ/104 at −10 °C, repeatability 2.1 nm RMS across three operators. The first segment failed acceptance at λ/64 because of a 40 nm polishing ridge — the bench found it, and the optician re-polished in three weeks.

CB-E2 · 2022

Lidar etalon

25 mm Fabry-Pérot
Two flight units + spare

Requirement

A solid etalon for a wind lidar at 355 nm: finesse above 150, thermal stability of 2 MHz between −10 and +40 °C, and a transmission peak within 1% of design after a launch environment of 14 g RMS.

Build

Fused-silica spacers, dielectric mirrors with 0.4% loss per pass, and a thermal enclosure with a 20 mK controller. Each unit was measured on the Airy bench at three temperatures before and after vibration.

Result

Finesse 180 at 355 nm, stability 1.6 MHz, peak transmission 94%. All three units passed vibration with no measurable change. The second unit was rejected at first because a spacer had a 2 µm wedge; that is one of the reasons the bench exists.

CB-W1 · 2021

Coating gauge

Low-coherence Michelson
14 units in two plants

Requirement

Measure optical coating thickness from 2 to 40 µm on a production line, in under 5 seconds, without touching the part, at ±3 nm. The parts are curved, so a conventional interferometer cannot hold a fringe.

Build

A 1550 nm superluminescent source with a 30 µm coherence length, a fibre Michelson, and a scanning reference arm on a voice coil. The thickness is the centroid of the coherence envelope, not a fringe count — which is why it tolerates a curved surface.

Result

±2.4 nm on 40 µm, 2.8 s per part, and a drift of 1.1 nm over an 8-hour shift that is corrected by an internal reference every 30 minutes. Fourteen installed; the first three had a thermal design flaw that took a year to find.

CB-C1 · 2025

Cryogenic bench

4 K interferometer
Dilution fridge, Leiden

Requirement

Measure the figure of a 50 mm mirror inside a dilution fridge at 4 K, to λ/50, while the fridge cycles between 300 K and 4 K seven times. The measurement has to distinguish thermal distortion from mounting distortion.

Build

A fibre-coupled Michelson with the reference arm outside the cryostat on a thermally compensated path, and a phase-shifter that works at 4 K. The whole optical head is 38 mm across, which is the hard part.

Result

λ/62 at 4 K, repeatability 4.8 nm, and a thermal distortion of 31 nm PV measured across the cycling — the figure the customer actually needed. The first design failed because the fibre feed moved 90 nm between cycles; a monolithic mount fixed it.

CB-E1 · 2019

Wavemeter cavity

Confocal Fabry-Pérot
Laser stabilisation service

Requirement

A 150 mm confocal cavity for a laser-stabilisation laboratory: FSR 500 MHz, finesse above 300, and a drift below 1 MHz per hour with the cavity in vacuum.

Build

Ultra-low-expansion spacers, superpolished mirrors at 0.15% loss, and a vacuum can with a getter. The cavity sits on three Peltier-controlled posts, with the error signal taken from a sideband lock.

Result

Finesse 340, FSR 500.2 MHz, drift 0.4 MHz/h after 48 hours of pumping. This bench is now used to calibrate the laser for every other measurement in the lab, which makes it the most important instrument we own that no customer ever sees.

CB-X1 · 2020

Inline flatness

Production interferometer
Cancelled at commissioning

Requirement

A hardened interferometer for a glass line, measuring flatness of 300 mm panels every 20 seconds, 24 hours a day, in a workshop with 3 µm of airborne dust and a 6 °C daily temperature swing.

Build

A common-path design to kill vibration sensitivity, a 532 nm source, and a sealed optical path. Everything about it was reasonable except the environment.

Result

Cancelled. Thermal drift of the reference moved the measurement by 80 nm over a shift, and no amount of referencing fixed it because the panel and the reference were at different temperatures. The honest conclusion is that a production line needs a different technique, and the customer went to a capacitance gauge.
