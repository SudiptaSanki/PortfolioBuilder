Why the fringes move

A nanometre is 1.6 fringes

In a Michelson, moving one mirror by d changes the path by 2d. At 632.8 nm that is one fringe every 316.4 nm of mirror travel — which is why a flexure stage and a 24-bit phase-shifter are worth more than a better camera.

01

Phase-shifting, not counting

A single fringe image tells you the shape modulo a wavelength. Four images at 0, π/2, π and 3π/2 give the phase at every pixel, so a 1,000 nm step becomes a continuous surface rather than a set of contours. Our standard acquisition is 13 frames, 5-phase, with a least-squares unwrap.

02

Coherence is a budget

A 1 MHz DFB laser has a coherence length near 95 m; a cheap multimode diode at 200 MHz has 0.5 m. If your path difference approaches the coherence length, the fringes fade and no amount of averaging brings them back. Check the number before you blame the mount.

03

Finesse is a loss figure

Fabry-Pérot finesse is set by mirror reflectivity — until scatter and absorption take over. A 99.5% mirror pair gives a theoretical finesse of 628; our coated etalons reach 180 because the coating loses 0.4% per pass. The curve on the second tab shows exactly that ceiling.
