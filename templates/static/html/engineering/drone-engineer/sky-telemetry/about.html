How I Work

The engineer is the least interesting part. The routine is the product.

I came to this work from the bench side: years of building and repairing research drones taught me that airframes fail loudly and links fail silently. A cracked arm is found in the workshop. A telemetry link that gives up for nine seconds at the far corner is found by a customer, in a report, months later — or it is found in the log, the same evening, by a routine that was looking for it. Everything I sell exists to move discoveries from the first kind of finding to the second.

Since 2019 I have worked independently from Delft with survey and inspection operators, university laboratories and drone startups across northern Europe: ground stations that run unattended, telemetry that survives the far corner, log pipelines that turn a season of flights into evidence, and crews trained to run all of it without me. My training is in electrical engineering; my habits are from flight lines — measure first, write it down, and never trust a number nobody can reproduce.

I work in the open ecosystems on purpose: PX4 and ArduPilot autopilots, MAVLink telemetry, QGroundControl and Mission Planner on the ground, ULog and DataFlash logs as the record. Open formats mean your data outlives any vendor — including me.

The Working Page

How a flight actually runs

Whether it is a nine-minute inspection or a forty-minute survey leg, a well-run flight has the same five beats. Most of my work is making beats one and five happen on their own.

01

Pre-flight — the flight is decided here

Airframe checks, compass and estimator health, battery state, and the link margin measured at the planned far corner of the mission — not assumed from yesterday. If the margin is thin, the mission changes on the ground, where changes are cheap.

02

Arming — the failsafes are already set

Lost-link behaviour, battery reserve and geofence limits were configured and bench-tested before the flight day. Arming is a confirmation, not a configuration session.

03

The flight — the ground station witnesses

The crew flies the mission; the station watches the links, the reserves and the plan, and calls deviations early. Telemetry dips are noted with a timestamp, not discovered later.

04

Landing — the log comes off before the batteries

The complete on-board log is pulled at the bench and checked for integrity the same hour. A flight without a log is treated as a flight that did not happen — because, evidentially, it didn’t.

05

Log review — the second flight

Vibration, current draw, estimator behaviour and link quality are read against the fleet’s own history. This is where nicked propellers, tired batteries and tired antennas are found — on a screen, in the warm, before they are found in the air.

Honest Limits

What I will tell you on the first call

  • Weather grounds drones. Unlike a satellite pass, a flight can simply be rained off: wind and rain limits are real, they are in the ops pack, and no link budget fixes them. Plan the season, not the day.
  • Radio rules are regional. 915 MHz telemetry is licence-free in the Americas and not in Europe, where 868 MHz does the same job under duty-cycle limits. Your radios must be legal where you fly — I specify for your region, and I will not help make an illegal fit quieter.
  • BVLOS is an authorisation, not a feature. Beyond-visual-line-of-sight work needs approvals that depend on your operation and your regulator. I build the technical file — links, failsafes, logs — inside your authorisation, never around it.
  • Brochure range is a fair-weather number. Every range figure on this site is a working line-of-sight figure with margin. If a vendor promises you three times that, ask for their budget and watch the meeting change.

Terms

Engagement terms

  • Written scope before price. Every engagement starts with a short written scope and a firm figure or a capped day-rate — never an open meter.
  • Your data stays yours. Logs, pipelines and documentation are delivered in open formats, with the keys. No lock-in is a feature I charge for once, not rent you pay forever.
  • Handover is part of the work. Builds end with training and documentation your own crew grades, as in Case File 03 — not with my departure.
  • Availability. Two engagements at a time, booked roughly six weeks out. Link audits and reviews can usually start sooner.
  • Base. Delft, Netherlands. Bench work here; field work across northern Europe, travel at cost agreed in advance.
