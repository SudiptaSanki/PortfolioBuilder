S6-class bay family

Commercial station · 2024–2026
In production

A three-metre bay with four CFRP M55J longerons, titanium nodes and aluminium end frames, sized to carry a 2,400 kg payload at the outboard interface. The bay had to pass the vehicle's 9.4 g quasi-static case and still keep a 2 mm line-of-sight budget through a 240 K eclipse cycle.

The first build failed acceptance sine at 34 Hz: a node clevis was bearing on its bolt shank rather than its shoulder, which cost 6 % of local stiffness and moved a panel mode into the sweep. Fixed with a 0.4 mm shim and a revised preload table, now standard in every joint sheet.

14 m antenna mast

Earth observation · 2023
Flying

A coiled longeron mast that deploys from 0.9 m to 14 m and carries a 22 kg SAR antenna. The pointing budget allowed 4 mm of tip movement under a 5 °C gradient, which rules out aluminium; the mast is CFRP with Invar fittings only where a coefficient mismatch is helping rather than hurting.

Deployment was the risk, not stiffness. The first deployment test on the ground stalled at 11.2 m because a longeron guide was 0.15 mm under nominal; in orbit there is no second attempt, so the mechanism was rebuilt with a 3 mm clearance and a redundant release.

Payload balcony

External platform · 2025
Delivered

A two-bay frame that bolts to a standard external payload interface and gives an instrument 1.1 m³ of clear volume with a 300 kg capacity. The interfaces were fixed by someone else's drawing, so most of the design work was in the transition: keeping the load path short while leaving a radiator unobstructed.

It is the least glamorous thing we have built and the one with the fewest problems, because nothing in it was new.

40 m extendable boom

Concept study · 2022
Not flown

A 40 m boom for a magnetometer, built from 1.2 m collapsible bays on a cable drive. It looked good on paper: 62 kg, 0.03 N/mm tip stiffness, and a first mode comfortably above the controller band at 0.11 Hz.

The qualification unit measured 0.04 Hz. Cable stretch and joint clearance in the deployed stack cost two-thirds of the modelled stiffness, and no reasonable amount of local stiffening recovered it. The study was closed rather than rescued, and the two candidate instruments moved to a shorter rigid mast on a different mission.
