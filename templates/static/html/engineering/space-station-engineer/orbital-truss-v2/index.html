BS-101 · The signature instrument

The Module Sizer

Size a pressurised barrel the way the first week of a real study does: hoop stress first, then the factor, then the mass — with every line of the arithmetic printed where you can check it.

Printed method — the sheet’s own arithmetic
  1. Volume  V = π r² L — the barrel only; end domes are volume the outfitters argue about later.
  2. Hoop wall  t = p · r / σallow — thin-wall pressure stress. This is the floor of the design.
  3. Shell area  A = 2πrL + 2πr² — barrel plate plus an end-closure allowance.
  4. Design wall  tdesign = 1.5 × t — the practice’s preliminary factor: weld lands, joints, mass growth.
  5. Shell mass  m = A × tdesign × ρ.

Workings are computed unrounded and printed rounded — the last digit may not re-multiply exactly. Alloy allowables below are this practice’s preliminary-design values (weld-knocked-down, room temperature), not handbook minima.

Margin note — what the ×1.5 buys, and what it does not

The factor covers weld lands, joint doublers and early mass growth. It excludes micrometeoroid shielding — often two to four times the pressure wall’s mass on a crewed module — end-dome detail design, docking mechanisms, hatches and all secondary structure. Treat this mass as the floor of a mass budget, never the budget.

What moves the price

  • Load cases first. A module with 40 defined cases is a different study from one with 12 — the case list is agreed before the fee is.
  • Review depth. Support to a client PDR costs less than carrying the structure through CDR.
  • Interface count. Every docking port, hatch and feedthrough is a local design problem of its own.
  • Schedule. A study that must land before a programme gate costs more than one that can breathe.
