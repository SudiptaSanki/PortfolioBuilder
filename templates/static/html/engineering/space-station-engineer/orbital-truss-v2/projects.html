Study 07 · BS-207

Node module — the 400 kg lesson

Rev C · Closed 2025
Client typeCommercial station programme
StructureNode module, Ø 4.2 m, four ports
Driving caseLaunch — not pressure
Mass effect−400 kg, preliminary
StatusClosed — client proceeded to PDR

The brief

A commercial station developer brought in a node module for an independent structural review ahead of PDR. The module had been sized — as most first passes are — on the pressure case: hoop stress set the barrel, and everything else inherited the margins. The client’s question was narrow: is it ready?

The decisions

The review ran the full load set instead of the customary subset: 46 cases across launch quasi-static and acoustic environments, on-orbit pressure and thermal, berthing and reboost. Launch drove the design — the ring frames and the longeron hardpoints were carrying launch loads the pressure-sized barrel had never been asked about, while two barrel bays were thicker than any case required because the shielding stack already provided the stiffness. The barrel came down in gauge in those bays; the ring frames grew, deliberately, where the launch case actually lived.

The outcome

Preliminary structural mass fell from 2,940 kg to 2,540 kg — 400 kg returned to a programme that needed it for payload — and the module went to PDR with a load set the client’s own reviewers could interrogate line by line. The redesign was the deliverable; nothing about the original geometry survived except its diameter.

Study 11 · BS-211

Longeron truss — the configuration that failed

Rev B · Closed 2024
Client typeStation logistics developer
Structure18 m truss, two docking ports
Driving caseDocking, off-axis capture
Preferred configFailed — replaced
StatusClosed — alternative adopted

The brief

A logistics developer had a preferred truss configuration — symmetric, elegant, and already drawn into their investor material. The study’s job was to confirm it against the programme’s load set, docking cases included.

The decisions

The symmetric truss passed pressure-adjacent and launch cases comfortably. It failed the one case the client had not run: an off-axis docking capture at the aft port, which drove a negative margin at the aft node fitting under the agreed margin policy. The study reported the failure as its headline, not its footnote, and priced the fix in the same document: move the aft port 1.8 m inboard, add one diagonal bay, accept 38 kg. The alternative passed every case in the set.

The outcome

The client adopted the alternative. The investor drawing was reissued — a cheaper conversation in the study phase than at CDR, and a much cheaper one than on orbit. The failed configuration stays in the study file, margins and all: a programme that hides its failed options will eventually re-buy one.

Study 14 · BS-214

Habitat layout — governed by the shelter

Rev A · Closed 2025
Client typeHabitat developer, four crew
StructureCrew module, 180-day missions
Governing constraintRadiation shelter volume
Sleep quartersInside the shelter
StatusClosed — layout adopted

The brief

A habitat developer asked for a layout study of a four-crew module for 180-day missions, assuming — as most layout studies do — that floor area and rack volume would govern the internal architecture.

The decisions

They didn’t. The governing constraint was the storm shelter: a refuge volume wrapped in the mass the mission already carries — water, food and waste stowage — sized for the crew to ride out a solar particle event. Once the shelter’s 6.8 m³ was placed, everything else arranged itself around it. The study put the sleep quarters inside the shelter volume, so the crew’s eight most stationary hours are also their most shielded ones; the galley and hygiene facilities moved outboard, where shielding buys nothing.

The outcome

The layout was adopted with one honest cost, printed in the study rather than discovered later: the module grew 0.4 m in length to hold the shelter and its stowage without stealing rack volume. Shielding that is already on the manifest is the cheapest shielding there is — but it is not free, and the study said so.
