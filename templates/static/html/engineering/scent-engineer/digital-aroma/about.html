Method

Six steps, one caveat,
no perfume poetry

A scent brief is a specification with feelings attached. We keep the feelings and write the specification. Here is the route from a sentence in a meeting to a cartridge that doses the same way on its four hundredth hour.

StudioSillage, founded 2021. Two chemists, one perfumer, one device engineer, one panel coordinator who decides when we are fooling ourselves.

InstrumentsGC-MS with a sniff port, dynamic headspace on Tenax TA, an olfactometer, two 12-channel dosing rigs, a 12 m³ scent booth held at 45 % RH.

StandardsIFRA 51st amendment, ISO 16128 for natural content, and our own repeatability spec: ±4 % of target mass, batch to batch.

We do not begin with a fragrance. We begin with an air sample, a photograph of the place, and a question about who is going to stand in it.

  1. Capture the air, not the idea

    Dynamic headspace onto Tenax TA for forty minutes, then thermal desorption into the GC-MS. A room gives us between 140 and 320 volatile peaks. A ripe fig gives us 60, of which four carry the fruit and one carries the leaf.

  2. Sniff the column

    Two trained noses mark every peak they can detect at the sniff port, and how it changes across the run. This is the step that separates a reconstruction from a chemical inventory: most of the mass is usually odourless, and the material that defines the place is often at four parts per billion.

  3. Reconstitute, then break it

    We build the accord from the marked peaks, then remove materials one at a time and taste the result on a smelling strip at 15 minutes, 2 hours and 6 hours. What survives removal is the skeleton. What does not was decoration.

  4. Check the receptors, not the adjectives

    For device work we test the accord against a receptor panel — 24 human olfactory receptors expressed in a cell line, each with a known response profile. It tells us whether the mixture engages what we think it engages, and whether two materials are competing for the same receptor at the dose we chose.

  5. Dose it on the device

    Every formula is re-tuned on the hardware. A piezo pump delivers a different mass at 12 °C than at 24 °C, and a cartridge that sits idle for a week needs a purge cycle before the first visitor. Onset, decay and carry-over are measured, not assumed.

  6. Panel, then publish the number

    Twelve untrained noses, a three-alternative forced choice against the reference, and a result we are willing to print. If a claim cannot survive that, it does not go on the site — including the claims we would have liked to make.

The caveat: a scent is not a picture. Two people standing in the same room, one metre apart, are not breathing the same mixture, and no device we build changes that.

What we will not do

We turn down three kinds of work. Pheromone claims, because the human evidence does not support them and the marketing does not need us. Scented safety signals, because a smell is a poor alarm and a worse alarm under stress. And food-adjacent scent in a room where people cannot leave, which is a ventilation problem wearing a fragrance brief.

We also publish negative results. The hospital wayfinding pilot on our work page did not shorten anyone's walk, and it is on the page with the same weight as the projects that worked.

Who is doing the work

Selin Aydın read chemistry at Boğaziçi and spent six years in flavour before starting Sillage, which means she is unimpressed by a top note that only works in a lab. Marc Delaunay runs the Grasse bench and the sniff port, and has a documented ability to name a material at 0.3 ppb in a room that smells of coffee. Ömer Kaya builds the cartridges and the rigs that test them; he came from medical nebulisers and treats every nozzle as a dose-critical part.

The studio takes four briefs a quarter. Two of them are usually small and strange, and those are the ones we remember.
