Aurora-3

Pera Museum, Istanbul · 2025 · In service

A six-channel smell cartridge for a room-scale VR installation about the Bosphorus at night. The brief asked for “salt, diesel, and the inside of a fig”. We captured the ferry dock at Karaköy at 22:40, found diesel at 11 ppb and cut it to 3, because the truth of a dock is not the same as the memory of one.

The hard part was the fig: real fig leaf is dominated by six green materials that oxidise inside a sealed cartridge. We stabilised the accord with a nitrogen blanket and a PTFE-lined chamber, and dropped onset from 640 ms to 180 ms by pre-heating the nozzle to 34 °C.

Cedar & Static

Four cartridges for a survival horror title · 2024 · Delivered

A peripheral scent kit that sits under a monitor and fires on in-game events. Six states — pine floor, wet plaster, burnt wiring, cold metal, stale tobacco, and the one the studio called “the basement”. Each had to be recognisable in under a second at arm's length without becoming unbearable over a three-hour session.

Channel 3 (wet plaster) grew a geosmin bloom after two weeks of disuse. The fix was not a different material but a different seal: the nozzle was drawing room air back into the chamber between firings. A duckbill valve and a five-second purge on wake removed the problem entirely.

Ward 4 wayfinding

Teaching hospital, 1,240 patients · 2024 · Negative result

The hypothesis was modest: a vanilla and melon-skin scent at 0.02 ppm in the air, placed at two corridor junctions, might help patients find the phlebotomy queue without asking. We ran it for eleven weeks against a scent-free control corridor, with the same signage and the same staff.

Wayfinding time did not change. Asking at the desk did not change. What did change was the number of comments about the corridor smelling “like a hotel”, which the facilities team read as a complaint. The scent was removed in week twelve. The finding — that a smell without a visual anchor adds nothing — shaped every environmental project we have taken since.

Harbour

Car interior concept · 2023 · Cancelled

“The smell of rain on hot concrete, when you open the door after a summer storm.” A good brief. We built it from geosmin, a petrichor accord and a trace of hot-dust pyrazine, and it smelled correct on a strip within four attempts.

It failed in the cabin. The material set could not meet the interior VOC standard at the dose the effect needed: the two materials doing the work were exactly the two the standard limited. We could have delivered a weaker version and called the project done. The client agreed that a faint version would read as a wet carpet, so we cancelled at the end of the capture phase and returned the unused budget.
