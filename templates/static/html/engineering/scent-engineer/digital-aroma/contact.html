Brief

Tell us what the room
should do to people

Not what it should smell like. That is our job, and it is the wrong question to start from. Tell us who is in the room, for how long, and what has to change while they are there.

Nothing is sent until you send it yourself.

We answer every brief within four working days. If we cannot take the work, we say so in the first reply rather than after a call.


      
What a good brief has

      
The number of people who will breathe the air, and for how long. The temperature they will be at. Whether the scent can be removed or must be constant. And the one smell you are terrified of.


      
After you write

      
We read the brief twice: once for the smell, once for the constraint. The reply is either a capture plan with a price, or a plain no. If we take it, the first thing we ask for is a sample of the air, not a mood board. If we decline, we usually say who else might do it.


      

        

          
Lead time

          
Six to ten weeks for a device formula, twelve or more if we start from an air sample.

        

        

          
Minimum project

          
A single capture week. We would rather sell you one useful week than a year of guessing.

        

        

          
Not a fit

          
Pheromone claims, scented alarms, and any room people cannot leave.
