Sheet 03 · The practice

Nine years inside the brochure, then out

Operating Point is the independent practice of Dr Yusuf Adeyemi — electrochemistry doctorate, nine years in a stack manufacturer's test department, independent since 2021 and based in Teesside, where the hydrogen work actually is.

The story

My doctorate was in fuel-cell diagnostics — finding out why cells die, in what order, and what the voltage decay is trying to say. I then spent nine years inside a stack manufacturer, in test and validation, watching where brochure numbers come from.

They come from the far right of the polarisation curve, at minute one, on the best cell of the batch, at a temperature the vehicle will never hold. Nothing about that is fraudulent — the curve is real. But a stack bought on that point is bought on the one condition it will never see again, and I kept meeting the engineers who had to live with the gap.

So the practice exists to close it: measure the duty cycle, size at the point the machine actually visits, prove it on a bench, and design to end of life. I have never met a duty cycle that matched the brochure. I have met several that were better than it — quieter, flatter, cheaper to serve. You only find out by logging.

How an engagement runs

  1. 01Duty cycle firstLogging before sizing, always. If you have data, we start there; if you don't, a logging package is the first deliverable and the cheapest engineering on the programme.
  2. 02Size at a stated pointEvery rating I issue carries its conditions — current density, temperature, pressure, and where on the curve it sits. A number without conditions is returned to sender.
  3. 03Bench before beliefPolarisation and durability validation at a partner laboratory, against the duty cycle we measured — not a generic cycle. The raw data belongs to the client, all of it, including the runs that embarrass the design.
  4. 04Design to end of lifeDegradation is budgeted at 5–10 µV per hour per cell and the stack is specified at its end-of-life voltage. A design that only works on its birthday is not a design.

Engagement terms

  • Feasibility studies are a fixed fee of £6,500, conclusion included — “not yet” bills the same as “build it”.
  • Design work runs at the printed day rates; the estimate is fixed in writing after the duty-cycle stage and does not drift.
  • Bench time is booked through a partner laboratory and charged at the printed bench-day rate; the practice owns no bench and marks nothing up.
  • No vendor commissions. The practice takes no payment, discount or hospitality from stack or component suppliers. If a supplier relationship exists, it is declared on the first page of the report.
  • Reports are the client's property, including the data behind them. The practice keeps anonymised lessons, never your numbers.

The honesty pageRead before the brochure

Peak efficiency is a point on a curve — the gentlest one, at the lowest power. Quoting it as “the efficiency” is quoting the best second of a machine's life as its character.

Degradation is real. Cells lose voltage from the first hour. Any design that doesn't state its degradation budget and end-of-life point is a beginning-of-life design, and it will be someone else's problem.

Grey hydrogen changes the subject. Run a fuel cell on hydrogen cracked from unabated natural gas and you have built a gas appliance with extra steps — sometimes still worth doing, never worth mislabelling.

“Zero emission” is a claim about a source, not a device. On this site it never appears without the hydrogen's origin attached — delivered, electrolysed, or reformed, stated.

Standing conditions

Every efficiency is quoted at a stated operating point. “Zero emission” appears only with a hydrogen source attached. Degradation is budgeted, never hidden. No vendor commissions are taken.
