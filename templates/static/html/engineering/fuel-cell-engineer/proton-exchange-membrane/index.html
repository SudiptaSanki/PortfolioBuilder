Where the volts go

Three losses, three different fixes

Every PEM curve has the same shape because it has the same three mechanisms. Telling them apart is what decides whether you buy a better catalyst, a thinner membrane or a bigger compressor.

01 · activation

The steep start

At low current, voltage falls logarithmically because the oxygen reduction reaction is slow. A Tafel slope near 60–70 mV/decade is good; 90 means the catalyst is wet, poisoned or old. This is the only loss a better catalyst fixes.

02 · ohmic

The straight middle

Once the reaction runs, the curve becomes a line and its slope is the resistance. A 15 µm membrane contributes about 0.017 Ω·cm²; the interfaces and bipolar plates add 0.045. Halving the membrane thickness buys back roughly 8 mV at 1 A/cm².

03 · transport

The knee at the end

At high current the oxygen cannot reach the catalyst fast enough and the curve dives. Flooding, a dry cathode or a compressed GDL all look the same on a curve — and all three are diagnosed from the impedance spectrum, not from the polarization data alone.
