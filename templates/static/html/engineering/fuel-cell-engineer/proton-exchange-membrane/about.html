Most fuel cell problems are duty-cycle problems in disguise.

Fourteen engineers, two test cells and a rule: no stack leaves the building without a measured polarization curve and 200 hours on the dyno. We specify about nine stacks a year and turn down the ones where the numbers do not close.

Why we start with the load profile

A truck that idles for six hours and then climbs a hill needs a very different stack from a ferry that runs at 60% load for eleven hours. The first needs low crossover and a small active area; the second needs efficiency at a single point and a lot of heat rejection. Both would be quoted the same rated power by a datasheet, and one of them would be wrong.

So the first week of a project is a duty-cycle analysis: a histogram of power against time, the ambient envelope, the freeze-start requirement and the lifetime target. The stack size falls out of that, and the polarization bench on the front page is where we test whether a proposed membrane and catalyst can actually deliver it.

The MEA is the product

We do not make membranes or catalysts. We buy PFSA film from two suppliers, catalyst-coated membranes from one, and gas diffusion media from three — then we qualify every incoming batch on a 50 cm² single cell before it enters a stack. A batch that gives 30 mV less at 1 A/cm² is rejected, even if the certificate says it is in specification.

The stack itself is a compression problem as much as an electrochemical one. Our fixtures hold 0.8–1.2 MPa across a 300 cm² active area to within 5%, because a 10% compression error at the edge costs more voltage than a whole catalyst generation.

Efficiency is a number with a current density attached to it. Take the attachment away and the number means nothing.

What we test

  • Polarization from 0.02 to 2.0 A/cm², ascending and descending, to catch hysteresis from water management.
  • Electrochemical impedance at five current densities — the only way to separate ohmic from transport loss.
  • Hydrogen crossover by linear sweep voltammetry on every MEA batch.
  • 200 hours of dynamic cycling on the dyno, including three freeze starts from −30 °C.
  • Post-test teardown of one stack in twenty: catalyst layer cross-sections and membrane thinning.

What we are honest about

Field efficiency is 2–4 points below the bench because of air-side pressure drop and a colder coolant loop. We report both numbers, always, and we tell customers which one the warranty is written against.
