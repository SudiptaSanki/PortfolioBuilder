MW-240 · 2024

Long-haul truck

240 kW gross · 330 cells
Two-year field trial, 14 trucks

Duty

Line-haul trucks averaging 65 km/h: 60% of operating time between 90 and 150 kW, with a 40-minute idle every four hours. The requirement was 240 kW for a 6% grade at 40 t, and 54% efficiency at the 110 kW cruise point.

Specification

330 cells of 300 cm², 15 µm reinforced PFSA, 0.4/0.1 mg Pt/cm², graphite composite plates at 2.2 bar absolute. Freeze start from −30 °C in 90 seconds using a resistive plate heater and a dry-cathode shutdown purge.

Result

0.673 V per cell at 1.35 A/cm² on the bench, 0.648 V in the field at 30,000 km — a 1.9% loss, inside the 3% target. Degradation 4.1 µV/h per cell, which projects to 12% over 25,000 hours. The miss was freeze start: 118 seconds in the coldest truck, not 90.

MW-200M · 2021

Coastal ferry

200 kW · 260 cells
Class-approved, 2 vessels

Duty

A 12-hour shift with 22 crossings: 70% of the time at a steady 140 kW, the rest at manoeuvring load. The duty point is narrow, which made efficiency at one point more valuable than peakpower.

Specification

260 cells of 400 cm², 18 µm membrane, 0.35/0.08 mg Pt/cm², 2.4 bar. Marine classification required a gas-tight enclosure, hydrogen detection in every compartment and a documented 10⁻⁵ failure rate per hour.

Result

0.706 V at the 0.95 A/cm² duty point, 56% LHV, matching the model within 8 mV. After 9,800 hours the stack holds 0.688 V — 2.5% degradation, better than the 4% guarantee. The classification file ran to 1,400 pages and took longer than the stack.

MW-30S · 2018

District heat

30 kW · 90 cells
12,000 h continuous

Duty

Baseload operation for a district-heating plant: 30 kW electrical, 34 kW thermal, running continuously except for monthly maintenance. Efficiency mattered more than power density, and heat was a product rather than a problem.

Specification

90 cells of 300 cm², 25 µm membrane, 0.4/0.15 mg Pt/cm², 1.2 bar. Deliberately conservative: thick membrane, low pressure, and a coolant loop that keeps the stack at 65 °C rather than chasing 80.

Result

58% electrical LHV with 34 kW of usable heat, 12,000 hours and 3.6% degradation — the best long-run result we have. The conservative membrane choice cost 12 mV at the duty point and bought back four years of life.

MW-12A · 2025

High-altitude UAV

12 kW · 60 cells
Dry cathode air, 25,000 ft

Duty

A surveillance aircraft at 25,000 ft: 12 kW for climb, 6 kW cruise, ambient pressure 0.37 bar, ambient temperature −40 °C. The cathode feed is ram air, so the stack sees dry gas at low pressure and must not dry out.

Specification

60 cells of 150 cm², 12 µm membrane, 0.5/0.2 mg Pt/cm², and a humidifier bypass that recirculates cathode exhaust at low power. Thin membrane for resistance, high catalyst loading for kinetics at low pressure.

Result

0.62 V per cell at 1.1 A/cm² in the altitude chamber, 12% below sea-level performance — the pressure term, as predicted. Two of three test flights lost power in descent because the recirculation valve iced; the fix is a heated seat, not a new MEA.

MW-80C · 2022

City bus

80 kW · 170 cells
Rejected at durability

Duty

A city bus cycle: 40 stops an hour, 80 kW on acceleration, 400 A of regen on braking, and a depot charge overnight. The requirement was 20,000 hours with no more than 10% degradation.

Specification

170 cells of 250 cm², 15 µm membrane, 0.3/0.1 mg Pt/cm² — a low-loading catalyst chosen to hit the cost target. The trade was 14 mV at the duty point against the reference MEA.

Result

Failed the durability test at 11,800 hours with 13% loss. Teardown found catalyst migration at the cathode and a membrane that had thinned 30% under the regen cycling. The stack is in this list because the customer's second programme used the higher loading and passed at 22,000 hours.

MW-1K · 2016

Telecom backup

1 kW · 24 cells
First delivered product

Duty

Backup power for a telecom mast: 1 kW for 8 hours, 40 starts a year, and months of standby at −20 °C in a cabinet. The duty is almost entirely start-stop and freeze tolerance.

Specification

24 cells of 100 cm², 25 µm membrane, 0.4/0.2 mg Pt/cm², dead-ended anode with a purge every 30 seconds. Simple, over-built, and cheap to service in a field where nobody wants to service it.

Result

1.02 kW at 0.66 V per cell, 41% efficiency including the parasitic load of the cabinet heater — the honest number for a backup unit at −20 °C. Eighty-two units shipped, four returned, all for the same solenoid valve.
