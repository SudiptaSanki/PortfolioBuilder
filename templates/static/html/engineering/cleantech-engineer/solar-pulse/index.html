About this model. The reference profile is a south-facing, ~35° tilt, unshaded array in central England: 992 kWh per kWp per year, in line with standard PVGIS-class estimates for the region (a Leeds roof reads a few percent lower; a Cornish one higher). Orientation factors are the standard guidance shape — south-east/south-west ≈ 0.96, flat ≈ 0.87, east–west split ≈ 0.83 of optimal — stated as regional guidance, not as a promise about your roof. Shading, which this explorer cannot see, is handled at survey and can matter more than orientation. A kWp is the array's rated peak output under standard test conditions; it is a rating, not a forecast.

  • Installed cost band: £1,350–£1,750 per kWp (mid £1,550) — the current market range for a straightforward domestic installation, stated so you can hold any quote against it. Scaffolding, slate roofs and long cable runs sit above it.
  • Tariffs: electricity bought at 24.5p/kWh; exported electricity paid at 15p/kWh. Both move. The estimate uses today's figures and says so.
  • Excluded, deliberately: panel degradation (~0.5%/yr), inverter replacement around year 12–15, maintenance, and any change in your usage. A design pack models all four; this sheet is a first pass.

What moves the price

  • Roof complexity — several planes, dormers or a split array take longer to model honestly.
  • Size — above 10 kWp the export-limit and connection paperwork grows.
  • Battery inclusion — storage sizing needs your half-hourly data, not a guess.
  • Survey findings — structural questions are referred to a structural engineer, at their fee, named in advance.
  • Speed — a report inside a week costs 25% more, and the wait list is told.
