FR.40 — ENQUIRY

One path in. It is answered by the engineer.

Say the vessel, its depth rating, and when it next comes out of the water. That is enough to start.

A name is needed — the report has to be addressed to someone.
A working email is needed, in full.
A few lines on the vessel and the problem — the rating and the next docking date above all.

Demonstration form — on this portfolio site it transmits nothing and stores nothing. The reference it generates is local to your browser. For a real enquiry, use the email beside it.

DH-2026-000

Thank you — your enquiry is logged under this reference. On the live practice site this is the point at which the details reach the engineer's desk; replies come within two working days, faster when a boat is sitting in a dock.

Prefer email?

andrew@deephull.example

The .example domain is deliberate: this is a demonstration portfolio for a fictional practice, so the address is illustrative too. The form and the address are the two paths in — there is no third, hidden one.

What to include

  • The vessel's type and depth rating
  • When it last docked, and when it next comes out
  • What drawings and gauging records you hold
  • The worry, in your own words — “a number we don't trust” is a perfectly good brief

What happens next

A reply from the engineer, not an assistant. If the work fits, a fixed-fee proposal per stage follows — the rates are on the overview page, so the proposal should hold no surprises. If it does not fit, the reply says who it might fit better.

Deep Hull is the portfolio of Andrew Fell, a fictional pressure hull engineer; the practice, clients and case files are illustrative and no real vessel is identified. The engineering — the formulae, the rule practice, the failure modes — is real, and is stated as carefully as it would be in a survey report.

No sound on this site, deliberately: a sonar ping is decoration, not information, and it failed the need-test. Photographs are credited in CREDITS.md and captioned as reference photographs throughout.
