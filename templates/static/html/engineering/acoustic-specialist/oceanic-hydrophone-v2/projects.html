Case file 01 · Baseline survey · PAM-01 + PAM-04

Half the brochure range

A consent array drawn for a 25 km detection range that the water, measured honestly, put at about 8 km on the nights that mattered.

Client
Offshore wind developer, pre-consent
Site
Lease area 34 km offshore; shipping lane on the northern boundary
Kit
8 autonomous recorders + 2 real-time buoys
Duration
Two seasons — winter and summer deployments
Deliverable
Baseline report with a measured detection-range annex
Status
Complete — array design adopted before consent submission

The ask

The developer's environmental statement rested on a desktop study: a manufacturer's brochure source level and a quiet-sea assumption put reliable detection of low-frequency whale calls at 25 km, and the monitoring array had been drawn on that basis — six stations, wide spacing, tidy budget.

What was done

The sound field was measured instead of assumed: ambient logging across both seasons — the lane put winter noise at the busy planning band most nights — plus in-situ propagation runs with a calibrated source. Then the detection model was re-run on the measured figures: the same arithmetic as the propagation sheet on the home page, with the site's own numbers in it.

The outcome

Reliable detection of fin-whale-class calls ran to about 8 km in lane conditions — a third of the desktop figure on bad nights, and 25 km only in the quietest conditions of the year. The array was redrawn to 11 stations on 8 km spacing before submission, and the regulator's reviewer accepted the annex without a range challenge. The developer paid for a smaller claim and a bigger array. Both were cheaper than a consent condition imposed afterwards.

Case file 02 · Construction monitoring · PAM-02

The stop-work call

Eleven weeks of pile-driving watched in real time, one detection inside the exclusion zone — and a stop that was called, costed and published.

Client
Port authority, expansion programme
Site
Harbour basin; 750 m exclusion zone around the piling rig
Kit
4-hydrophone real-time array; watch room on the quay
Duration
11 weeks of piling windows
Deliverable
Live watch + mitigation log, published by the client
Status
Complete — one stop called in 11 weeks

The ask

The piling consent required marine-mammal mitigation: soft-starts, a 750 m exclusion zone watched in real time, and — written into the contract before mobilisation, at this practice's insistence — the PAM operator's authority to stop the hammer on a confirmed detection inside the zone.

What was done

Two watches around the clock during piling windows. Every detection logged with a range estimate and a confidence grade; the rule printed on the watch-room wall: a confirmed detection inside the zone stops work, and only the analyst on watch restarts it — not the programme manager, not the contractor, not the tide table.

The outcome

On day 23, a porpoise-class click train was localised inside the zone during soft-start. The stop was called at 09:41; piling resumed at 13:07, after the zone had been clear for the full printed clearance period — 3 hours 26 minutes of programme time, costed in the log the client chose to publish. It was the only stop in eleven weeks, and the log says that too. The client's testimonial for this file is on the home page.

Case file 03 · Long-term dataset · PAM-03

The pattern was the finding

Three years of recordings, one question the data could not answer — and one it could, which turned out to matter more.

Client
Marine research institute — open dataset
Site
Single deep mooring on a ferry lane, 210 m depth
Kit
1 archival recorder, serviced six-monthly
Duration
3 years continuous
Deliverable
Analysis report + documented open dataset
Status
Complete — dataset public

The ask

The institute wanted a species trend: were fin whale calls on this lane rising or falling over three years? It is the question long-term moorings are usually sold on, and it deserved a straight answer rather than a hopeful one.

What was done

A full re-analysis with the detector validated against hand-checked subsamples — its false-positive rate is printed in the report — and then the step that changed the answer: the detection series was laid against the ferry timetable and the traffic record instead of the calendar alone.

The outcome

No defensible species trend. The data cannot carry one, and the report says so in its first paragraph. What it can carry: detection probability fell by half on nights the new ferry service ran its added 02:00 crossing, and recovered when the crossing moved. The finding was a timetable, not a whale count — and it became the institute's evidence in a routing discussion the following year. A negative result with a mechanism in it is worth more than a trend with none.
