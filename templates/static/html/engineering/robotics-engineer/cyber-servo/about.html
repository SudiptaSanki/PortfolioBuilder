Sheet 03 — About

The engineer behind the bench

I trained as a mechatronics engineer in Munich and spent eight years in the motion laboratories of automotive suppliers — the rooms where prototype axes are allowed to misbehave before they are allowed near a product. The work taught me the trade's central habit: a servo axis never lies, but it only answers the question the trace asks. Most "mystery faults" are unmeasured machines.

Independent since 2019, I now split my time three ways: tuning and commissioning for automation firms whose lines have drifted out of tolerance; sizing and first-motion support for product teams and research spinouts building prototypes; and retrofit audits for plants whose drive lines have been discontinued out from under them. Two engagements at a time, never more — tuning is attention work.

The through-line is measurement discipline. I do not tune by ear, by folklore, or by copying the gains from the last machine. Every engagement starts with the machine's own data and ends with a log that stays with the machine.

Method — How tuning actually proceeds

Four rules, in order, every time

M.1

Measure first

Before a single parameter changes, the axis is logged as it is: following error across real production cycles, a step response, and a frequency sweep to find the structural resonances. Half of all tuning jobs are diagnosed at this stage — the fault is in the mechanics, the sizing or the profile, and no gain will fix it.

M.2

One change at a time

Gains are derived from the measured inertia and resonance, then adjusted one at a time with a trace after each change. Change three things at once and you have learned nothing, however good the result looks — and the next engineer inherits a superstition instead of a procedure.

M.3

Tune at the worst payload

A tune that is beautiful with the lightest part and unstable with the heaviest is not a tune. Verification runs at maximum payload, maximum reach and production cycle rate, and the numbers quoted in the case log are measured there.

M.4

Leave a log the next engineer can read

Every engagement closes with the before/after traces, the final parameter set with the reasoning attached, and the sizing or resonance data the tune rests on. The parameter set belongs to the client — your gains are yours, including the right to hand them to someone else.

Bench — Tools & rigs

What the measurements rest on

Drive-integrated tracing at the drive's own sampling rate — the following error, current and velocity as the controller itself sees them, which is the only version of events the loop can act on.

A frequency-response kit — accelerometer, instrumented excitation through the drive's own test signals — because a resonance you have not measured is a resonance you will tune into, not out of. Case 01's 38 Hz was found this way.

A bench rig with a torque transducer and interchangeable inertias, for prototype work: motors are proven against the sizing sheet on the bench before they are proven expensively on the machine.

Feedback literacy. The encoder sets the floor under every tune: a 17-bit device resolves 131,072 counts per revolution — about 0.0027° per count — and a 23-bit device resolves 8,388,608. If a specification demands settling finer than the feedback can see, that is a sizing conversation, not a tuning one.

Safety — Around moving machinery

Stated plainly

Tuning happens in reduced-speed mode with the enabling device in hand, inside the cell's safeguarding, after a written risk assessment for the session. First motion of any new axis happens at a fraction of rated speed, in an empty cell, with the stop circuit proven before the motor is energised — not after.

I do not defeat guards, bridge safety circuits, or tune a live production axis at full energy to save an hour. If a schedule requires that, the schedule is the thing that changes. An emergency stop is a safety device, not a pause button, and it is tested, not assumed.

These are not disclaimers; they are the working conditions, and they are part of why the audit is two days and not one.

Terms — Engagement terms

How an engagement runs

  • Audit first. Every tuning or retrofit engagement starts with the fixed-price motion audit (€1,900, credited in full if the work follows). It protects us both from quoting blind.
  • Written scope. Axes, payload range, cycle target and acceptance measurement are agreed in writing before work starts; the acceptance test is a trace, not an impression.
  • Two engagements at a time. Booking is honest about the queue — currently booking audits for March.
  • Your parameters are yours. Final gain sets, sizing sheets and logs are delivered in full and belong to the client.
  • Travel. On site across the EU at cost; audits further afield can run on logged traces and video, with the limits of that stated up front.
