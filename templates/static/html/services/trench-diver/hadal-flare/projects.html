Shifts and surveys · 2021 to 2026

Six jobs, and how deep each one actually went.

A saturation stint is 21 days at storage depth with two divers in the bell at a time. A lander survey is three weeks on a ship watching a machine do the diving. Both are listed here with the depth, the duration and what the client got.

190 m

Gannet manifold replacement, northern North Sea

Fourteen excursions to replace a valve manifold on a subsea wellhead, in a 21-day stint at 190 m storage. Visibility under 2 m for nine of the fourteen, with the hot-water hose re-routed around the bell frame on day six after it chafed against a structural member.

The manifold came up leak-tested. The hose went back to the supplier with a photograph and a strongly worded note, which is the most useful thing a diver can do with a near miss.

2025 · 14 excursions
Bell supervisor on 6
Bottom time 30 min per dive
Client: subsea contractor

240 m

Pipeline tie-in, west of Shetland

Two divers, four excursions, 26 minutes on the bottom each. Gas density was the limiting number, not depth: the mix crossed 7.2 g/L on two dives and the supervisor called both short. The tie-in was finished on the fourth excursion with a revised gas blend and a shorter work sequence.

This is the deepest working excursion in my log, and the one I use when someone tells me depth limits are about courage.

2024 · 4 excursions
Deepest 243 m
Bottom time 26 min
Client: pipeline operator

1,180 m

Lander survey, Rockall Trough

Three free-fall landers, 14 deployments over 19 days, with a baited camera frame and a sediment trap. Forty hours of usable footage and one grenadier that took up residence in the sample box between deployments.

No divers. My job was the deployment plan, the ballast calculations and the recovery schedule around a very unreliable weather window.

2024 · 14 deployments
Deepest 1,204 m
19 days on station
Client: marine institute

2,760 m

Cold-seep transect, Porcupine Seabight

ROV work from a survey vessel, 11 days, mapping seep fauna along a 6 km transect with a downward camera and a push core. The ROV pilots did the flying; I ran the dive plan, the deck and the sample chain of custody, which is the part that makes the data publishable.

2023 · 11 days
6 km transect
42 cores
Client: university group

150 m

Chamber and bell drills, Bergen

A training rotation rather than a job: two weeks running bell and chamber drills for a new dive team, including a simulated lost-bell recovery and a hyperbaric evacuation exercise. The most valuable day of the year, every year, and the one clients never want to pay for.

2023 · 2 weeks
9 divers trained
4 drill scenarios
Client: dive contractor

90 m

Intake inspection, Norwegian fjord

A short air dive job that turned into a survey: a cooling-water intake blocked by kelp and a collapsed grid. Six dives over three days, one of them in 4 °C water with 30 cm visibility, cutting the grid free with a hydraulic shears and a lot of patience.

2021 · 6 dives
Air, surface supplied
Water 4 °C
Client: power station

What a shift needs before it can be planned

A task list, a depth profile, a gas supply, and a chamber within the evacuation time. The task list is usually the thing that is missing, and it is the only one that determines how long anyone is in the water.
