Lyon · The autumn collection is in the case

The tasting comes first.

I am Colette Marchand. From a narrow shop off the rue Mercière in Lyon, I make single-origin bars and bonbons in six-kilo batches — and every October the Harvest Case opens the autumn season, six flavours built from the orchard glut. Nothing here asks to be believed before it is tasted: the counter keeps an open bar of everything on the shelf, cut fresh each morning. Read the notes, taste the square, then decide.

The shelf · November

Bars, written like a menu

A percentage is a recipe, not a rank. What tells you whether a bar is for you is where the beans grew and what they taste of — so that is what the label leads with.

Kilombero 75%

Tanzania · Single origin · Dark

Red fruit first — raspberry, almost — then brown sugar, and a finish like the top of a crème brûlée. The bar I hand to people who say they do not like dark chocolate, because it argues gently.

Manabí 70%

Ecuador · Single origin · Dark

Jasmine on the nose — actually there, not a copywriter's flower — then toasted hazelnut and a long honeyed end. The quietest bar on the shelf and the one the regulars reorder without discussing it.

Sambirano 68% Sold out

Madagascar · Single origin · Dark

Raspberry and citrus peel, bright as sorbet. The November tempering sold through in nine days; the next beans are already in the roaster's book and the bar returns in December. I would rather print an empty line than fill it with a different valley wearing this name.

The case · Bonbons

Twelve flavours, three weeks of life

My ganaches are made with fresh cream and no preservatives, so a bonbon lives three weeks from the day it is enrobed — and the date is written on the box, not hidden from you. That short life is why the batches are small and why the planner below counts in days, honestly.

The Case of Twelve

Bonbons · The standing case

Salted caramel; espresso with cardamom; pear and brown butter; hazelnut praline; blackcurrant; and a vanilla-bean white chocolate that starts arguments — two of each. The box to start with, and the one weddings order by the hundred.

Friday evenings · 19:00

Tastings, seated and argued

Ten seats around the counter. Five bars in ascending darkness, two bonbons, one white chocolate served blind to settle the slander, and water and bread to reset the palate. You leave with the notes you wrote, not the ones I read at you. €38 a seat, chocolate included; book by email or at the counter.
