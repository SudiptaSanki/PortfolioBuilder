Case File 01 — The First Calibre

Calibre AV-31 · 2015–2019

Four years from the first drawing to the first three watches. A calibre is not launched; it is survived.

The brief

After eleven years restoring other makers' watches, Vogt set his own examination: a hand-wound calibre of his own design, three hands, that he could make at one bench in honest numbers — and whose making he could print without embarrassment. The brief fixed the honesty rules before the first wheel was drawn: bought-in groups would be decided in advance, named, and never quietly absorbed into the word “in-house.”

The decisions

2015 was drawings: a 30 mm plate, 21,600 A/h, the train laid out for wheels he could cut on his own machines. 2016 produced two mainplates that were scrapped — the second for a jewel seat drilled a tenth of a millimetre proud, kept on the workshop wall as the standing lesson. 2017 ran the first train. 2018 was the hard year: the rate spread across positions would not close inside −2/+6, and the fault was traced to the hairspring supply; the spring source was changed, the year largely repeated, and the episode is why the hairspring line on the plate names its origin without flinching.

The outcome

2019: three watches, Nos. 1–3, completed and sold to the first three names on a list kept in a notebook. AV-31 has been in production since, at the printed rate of fourteen a year. Its plate — 109 components, 47 made here, 62 bought in — is Table 1 on The Watches, unchanged since the day it was first printed.

Case File 02 — Watch No. 47, a Commission

Calibre AV-24 · Commissioned 2023

The buyer chose where choice was real. The calibre did not change for him; the record shows what did.

The brief

Henrik Dahl, a collector in Copenhagen, asked for a watch he could wind every morning of his retirement: small seconds, a dial he could read without glasses, and his father's initials cut into the caseback. He also asked — his words — to be told “what the watchmaker didn't make.” The answer is printed on the plate he was sent before he paid any deposit.

The decisions

The small-seconds requirement chose the calibre: AV-24, the slower 18,000 A/h movement. The dial finish came from the dial atelier's range — bought in, as dials here are. The engraving was cut by hand after casing. Fine regulation was requested and charged on its printed line; the watch then spent its extra bench days being brought inside the fine class of Table 3. Nothing else about the movement was altered for the commission, because a calibre that changes per buyer has no record worth printing.

The outcome

Delivered in 2023 with its making record and rate card. Measured at delivery across five positions: range −1 / +6 s per day, mean +2 — inside the fine class it was sold under, and printed here exactly as it stands on the card. The price was the printed lines and nothing else: €21,900 + €450 + €380 = €22,730. The owner's words about the watch are on The Watches, attached to this file, where testimonials belong.

Case File 03 — Watch No. 12, Returned

Calibre AV-31 · Delivered 2020 · Serviced 2026

Six years on the wrist, measured against its own birth record. This is what the records are for.

The brief

No. 12 came back in 2026 for its first full service, six years after delivery — inside the printed 5–7 year interval, at its later end. The owner reported nothing wrong beyond the watch “feeling fast.” The birth record from 2020 was pulled from the file before the case was opened.

The decisions

Measured as received, the watch had drifted: its positional range had widened from −1/+5 at delivery to +4/+11, mean up from +2 to +8 s per day, amplitude down — the ordinary signature of oils at the end of their life, not of damage. The service was therefore the standard one, charged on the printed line: full strip, clean, fresh oils, and the mainspring replaced as a matter of course — a bought-in part, replaced with a bought-in part, and recorded as such. No component was “improved”; a service restores the watch to its record, it does not revise it.

The outcome

Re-regulated after service to a measured range of −1 / +4, mean +1 — inside its delivery card, a second slower than its birth mean. Both records went back to the owner side by side, and they are printed side by side here: the drift is the evidence that the interval on the rate card is a measurement, not a sales line.
