Sending a watch by post

  • Use a tracked, insured service — Royal Mail Special Delivery covers up to £2,500. For a watch worth more, use a specialist insured courier or bring it in by hand.
  • Wrap the watch head in soft tissue inside a small rigid box, then pad that box inside a larger one. Watches die in jiffy bags.
  • Don’t send the presentation box or spare straps unless I ask — they add bulk and are the first thing a post room loses.
  • Don’t write “watch” or the maker’s name anywhere on the outside of the parcel.
  • Put your name, return address and a note of the fault inside the box.
  • I confirm arrival by email the day the parcel lands, and the watch is insured on the premises from that moment.

What happens next

  • Reply within two working days, with the next bench slot.
  • Assessment when the watch arrives: timing as found, strip inspection, written estimate. £45, deducted from any work.
  • Your decision. No work starts — and nothing beyond the assessment is charged — until you agree the estimate.
  • The bench. Typically 4–6 weeks, with the timing figures and the old parts returned with the watch.
