Fees · the shape of the price

What moves the price, in order

First, the scope — and the scope is set by the interval and the timing machine, not by what the bench would like to sell. A regulation visit is £60 and is the right answer more often than the trade admits. Second, the family — a chronograph's full service is £640 because its work is all-or-nothing and its wear allowance is real. Third, the wear parts — each one a named line in Table P1, fitted on evidence. Nothing else moves the price. There is no charge for the book, the entry, the stamp, or the truth about your watch's value.

Turnaround, printed like everything else: regulation 1–2 weeks · mainspring & seals 3–4 weeks · full service 6–9 weeks. The bench takes watches in the order their assessments are entered. The rules behind these figures — intervals, wear parts, the value rule, and the watches this bench will not touch — are written out in full on the bench & the rules.

The fiction, stated

Kaspar Aebischer and the Mainspring Atelier are fictional, created for this template; any resemblance to a real bench is the resemblance of an honest trade practised anywhere. The figures in the tables are the template's stated data, and the planner's arithmetic is worked from them in the open.
