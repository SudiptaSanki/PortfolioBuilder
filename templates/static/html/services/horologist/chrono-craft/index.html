01 — the escapement

The only part that makes a watch a watch

A mechanical watch stores energy in a mainspring and releases it in equal packets. The escapement is what decides the size of the packet: the balance swings, the pallet fork lets one tooth of the escape wheel pass, and the train advances by a fixed amount. Everything else in the movement exists to deliver that impulse cleanly.

The panel beside this text is running the same geometry as the calibre on my bench. At 21,600 beats per hour the balance turns three times a second and the escape wheel steps twelve times; at 28,800 it steps sixteen. A higher beat rate usually buys stability at the cost of power, which is why the torque curve underneath matters as much as the beat.

A watch does not keep time because it is accurate. It keeps time because the errors cancel.

05 — the path

What happens to your watch

  1. i

    Intake photographs, a timing reading and a written estimate before anything is opened.

  2. ii

    Strip and clean. Every part photographed on a tray, and anything worn is listed.

  3. iii

    Reassemble, lubricate with six oils, and set the beat and the rate on the machine.

  4. iv

    Six positions, then a week on the wrist of a colleague to catch what the machine misses.

  5. v

    Returned with the old parts, the timing sheets and a two-year warranty.
