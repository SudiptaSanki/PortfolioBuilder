CC-1147

Omega calibre 321

1968 · chronograph

Water ingress through a worn pusher gasket had corroded the chronograph bridge and seized the column wheel. The bridge was beyond saving, so a new one was made to the original profile, anglaged and finished to match; the column wheel was re-cut and the pushers re-gasketed.

The old bridge was returned to the owner with the rest of the replaced parts. It is the only record of how the original was finished.

CC-1129

Longines calibre 12.68Z

1954 · hand-wound

A family watch with a snapped balance staff and a ratchet wheel from an earlier repair that did not belong to the calibre. The staff was turned between centres from blued steel; the ratchet wheel was re-made to the original tooth form and the click spring replaced.

The dial was left alone apart from a dry clean. Two lume plots are missing and will stay missing.

CC-1102

Smiths calibre 0104

1962 · hand-wound

One of the last British-made movements, brought in with a cracked jewel and a dial that had been cleaned with the wrong solvent, taking the print with it. The jewel was replaced and the dial conserved — stabilised, not refinished — so the remaining print stays as it is.

A replacement dial exists and was offered. The owner chose to keep the original, which is almost always the right call.

CC-1088

English pocket watch, maker unknown

c. 1880 · fusee pocket

A fusee pocket watch with a broken chain and a balance that had been re-staffed with the wrong staff, giving a rate that wandered by forty seconds a day. A new chain was fitted, the staff replaced with one of the right length, and the fusee re-set.

The case is hallmarked 1879 and the movement is unsigned. The repair record now lives inside the case back, on acid-free paper.

CC-1064

Zenith El Primero 3019

1971 · automatic chronograph

A high-beat chronograph that had been serviced with the wrong lubricant on the escape wheel, giving a lazy amplitude and a chronograph that lost the seconds when started. Full strip, correct oils, a new oscillating weight bearing and a re-set chronograph bridge.

The 3019 runs at 36,000 vph. Anything less than 290° at full wind means something is wrong, and it was.

CC-1041

Seiko 6139-6002

1973 · automatic chronograph

A well-used 6139 with a worn barrel arbor and a vertical clutch that had been adjusted with the wrong tool, leaving a burr on the chronograph bridge. New arbor made, bridge dressed, and the whole movement cleaned and regulated in six positions.

These are robust movements and worth saving. The case was left unpolished; the dings are the watch’s history.
