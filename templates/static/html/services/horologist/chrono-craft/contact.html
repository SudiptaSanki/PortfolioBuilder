Questions

    
Before you hand it over

    

      

        How often does a mechanical watch need a service?
        
Every five to seven years for a watch worn daily, or when the amplitude at full wind drops below about 250°. A watch that sits in a drawer still needs one eventually: the oils oxidise whether or not the movement runs.

      

      

        Will you polish the case?
        
Only if you ask, and only lightly. Polishing removes metal, softens chamfers and rounds lug edges, and on a vintage watch it removes value. My default is a clean and a new gasket, nothing more.

      

      

        Can you get original parts?
        
For current calibres, usually yes through the manufacturer’s parts account. For older movements, often not, and then the part is made here or sourced from a documented donor. Either way you will be told which it is on the estimate.

      

      

        What if the estimate changes?
        
It happens, usually when a second fault is found under the first. Work stops and you get a call and a photograph before anything else is ordered. No additional work is done without a fresh yes.

      

      

        Do you guarantee the watch is waterproof?
        
I pressure-test to the rating on the case, but a forty-year-old case should not be treated as waterproof afterwards. If you swim, say so at intake and I will test to the higher figure and change every gasket.
