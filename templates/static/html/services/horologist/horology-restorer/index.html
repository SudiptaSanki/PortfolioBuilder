Tourbillon & high-complication restoration — Geneva

Restoration is an argument with the future about what the past is allowed to keep.

This bench restores significant watches — tourbillons, repeaters, grand complications — for collectors and museums. It works under one rule, printed here and kept in every treatment record: originality outranks shine. Cases are not polished. Dials are not refinished. A part is replaced only when the original can no longer carry its load, and then the replacement is documented and the original returned with the watch.

§ 01

The register — condition on arrival

Every engagement is entered here, graded before it is priced

A watch is graded on arrival, before any fee is discussed. The grade — set out in Table G below — decides what may be done to it, and the mapping in Table M is not negotiable at the bench: a higher fee has never yet bought a higher class of intervention than the grade allows. Recent entries:

§ 02

The reckoning — what intervention costs

Printed tables first; the planner only reads them aloud

Four tables govern every quote this atelier gives. They are printed here in full, and the Scope Planner below does nothing but apply them — select a grade and a complication, and it returns the class the ethics assign, the hours, the fee, and the rate the record supports. Where the tables refuse, the planner refuses.

Table G — Condition grades
GradeWhat is wrong
I — TiredRunning. Lubricants dried, honest wear only. No corrosion, nothing missing, no previous bad work.
II — WoundedStopped. Corrosion on the steelwork. Nothing missing. Earlier work, where present, was competent.
III — BrokenFabric missing or fractured — a wheel, a cage part, a gong. Corrosion. The movement cannot run as it stands.
IV — UntouchedComplete and demonstrably undisturbed. The value of the watch is its originality.
Previous bad work is graded by its damage, not its intention: over-polishing alone is Grade II, noted in the report; wrong or missing parts make the watch Grade III.
Table M — The mapping: grade → class
GradeClass assigned, and why
IRestore — light. Running order restored; nothing replaced that wear has not already condemned.
IIRestore — full. Corrosion arrested, train rebuilt to running order, original fabric kept wherever it can carry its load.
IIIRestore — full, with a Reconstruct element. The missing fabric is made on the bench, to the original's pattern, and disclosed as new work in the record.
IVConserve only. Stabilise, document, hand back. Restoration is refused — the reasoning is on the ethics page.

What moves the price — in order

  • 1Complication first. A repeater's striking train is a second movement inside the first; a tourbillon cage is a watch within the watch. Table H is priced accordingly.
  • 2Missing fabric second. Fabrication is hours at the lathe and the bench, not a parts order — that is the Reconstruct element, and it is why it is printed as its own line.
  • 3Previous bad work third. Undoing harm takes longer than the harm took. It is graded by its damage (Table G) and charged as the grade it creates.
  • §The standing terms. Assessment & report CHF 450, credited in full if the watch proceeds. Deposit on treatment: 30% of the low end of the quoted range. The treatment record — bound, with photographs — is included in every fee. Nothing on this page is “price on enquiry”.

Every treated watch leaves with its record: photographs before, during and after; the parts list, naming what was cleaned, what was fabricated and what — rarely — was replaced; and the measured rate. The three case files that follow this report in the index are published in that same form.

A watch you are unsure about is exactly the watch to have graded. The grade comes before the price — and sometimes the grade says: leave it alone.
