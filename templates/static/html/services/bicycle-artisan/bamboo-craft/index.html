Why bamboo

A natural composite with one honest weakness

Bamboo is strong along the fibre, variable between culms, and hopeless in shear at a joint. The frame is designed around that: the cane carries bending, the wrapped lug carries everything else.

A bamboo culm is cellulose fibre in a lignin matrix, and almost all of that fibre runs lengthwise, which is exactly the direction a bicycle tube is loaded. Density sits around 0.6–0.8 g/cm³, comparable with some aluminium alloys, while the tensile strength of the outer wall reaches 148–389 MPa depending on species, age and moisture content. We buy Tonkin cane (Arundinaria amabilis) in 3 m lengths, select by wall thickness and node spacing, then heat-treat and seal it.

The weakness is the joint. A culm splits under point load and creeps under sustained moisture. Each joint is therefore mitred to a fish-mouth, sleeved into a wrapped carbon lug, and bonded with a toughened epoxy, with a 45 mm overlap. The lug carries shear and clamping load; the cane carries the bending moment. That division is why the frame can weigh 2.1 kg and still pass a fatigue test at 1,200 N for 240,000 cycles.

It is also why the frame can be repaired. Cut out a damaged tube, scarf in a new culm, rewrap the lug. A carbon monocoque goes in the bin; this does not.

Fourteen weeks, in four stages

01

Fit

Height, inseam, arm span, shoulder width, preferred bar and saddle. We compare against your current bike and its measurements.

Mitre & wrap

Weeks 3–8
