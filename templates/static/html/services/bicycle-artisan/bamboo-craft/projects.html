Built work

Five frames, five riders, five sets of numbers.

Each entry is drawn from the finished frame's log book: geometry as built, rider measurements, and the riding it was made for. Names are the riders' own.

No. 58 · 2025 · gravel

The Holloway

For a rider who wanted the front end calm on washboard and the back end stiff enough to sprint out of a farm gate. The 71.5° head angle and 50 mm clearance do the calm part; the 430 mm chainstay and a short seat mast do the rest.

2,180 gbare frame
71.5° / 72.5°head / seat
430 mmchainstay
9,400 kmridden to date
172 cmrider height

No. 46 · 2023 · road

The Severn

Built for a rider coming off a carbon race frame who wanted to keep the position but lose the road buzz. Same stack and reach as his old bike, half a degree slacker, and 410 mm chainstays so it still turns.

1,980 gbare frame
73.0° / 73.5°head / seat
410 mmchainstay
14,200 kmridden to date
181 cmrider height

No. 51 · 2024 · track

Herne Hill

A velodrome frame with no brakes, no derailleur hanger and a 74° head angle. The rear triangle is shorter than the bamboo usually allows; the seat stays are sleeved to keep the joint within its shear limit.

1,940 gbare frame
74.0° / 74.0°head / seat
380 mmchainstay
2,100 kmtrack only
176 cmrider height

No. 37 · 2021 · touring

The Tamar

A loaded touring frame for a rider who spends three weeks a year on the Rhine and the rest commuting. Longer chainstays, a 71° head angle and a fork built for a front rack. It carries 18 kg without shimmy.

2,300 gbare frame
71.0° / 72.0°head / seat
445 mmchainstay
21,600 kmridden to date
165 cmrider height

No. 60 · 2026 · randonneur

The Quantock

A 200 km audax frame for a shorter rider: 650b wheels to keep the proportions right, a 73° head angle and a taller head tube. The smallest frame on this list and the hardest to fit a pump to.

2,050 gbare frame
73.0° / 73.0°head / seat
415 mmchainstay
3,800 kmridden to date
