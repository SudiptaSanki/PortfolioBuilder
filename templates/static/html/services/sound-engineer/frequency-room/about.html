ABOUT — THE ENGINEER

The engineer, and how the work runs.

Jonas Weber. A master’s degree in acoustics, then six years on studio-build crews — floating floors, isolated shells, silenced ventilation ducts — learning the unglamorous truth that a studio is a building before it is a sound, and that most “gear problems” are room problems wearing a price tag.

Frequency Room began in 2016 as the thing I kept being asked for mid-build: come and tell us what the room is doing before we spend on equipment. It turned out to be the whole job. 214 rooms measured since (practice records): control rooms, podcast boxes, listening rooms, rehearsal spaces, two village halls, and one swimming-pool plant room that remains the loudest space I have ever stood in.

I work alone, on site, with a calibrated measurement microphone and the same arithmetic this site does in the open. If a room doesn’t need me, the survey says so and the survey is the whole bill.

METHOD

Measure → model → treat → re-measure

STEP 1

Measure

Half a day in the room. Swept-sine measurements at the seat and around it, RT60 by octave band, and background noise with the building doing its worst — ventilation on, traffic up. You receive the raw figures, not a summary of them.

STEP 2

Model

The measurements set against the room’s computed modes — the same f = c ÷ 2L arithmetic as the home page, done for your actual dimensions and seat. Where model and measurement agree, we trust them. Where they don’t, we find out why before designing anything.

STEP 3

Treat & place

Placement first — it costs nothing and often buys the most. Then absorption where the reflections are, trapping where the modes are, diffusion where a surface should scatter rather than soak. Every item priced from the printed rate card before it is fitted.

STEP 4

Re-measure

Same microphone positions, same software, same seat. The report closes with before and after side by side — including anything that didn’t improve. That line stays in. It is the line the next client reads first.

HONESTY

What measurements can’t promise

  • A measurement is one seat, one day, one microphone. Move the seat 30 cm and the bass changes. Every report states where the microphone stood and why, and the numbers belong to that seat.
  • RT60 targets are conventions for a use, not laws of nature. A control room at 0.30 s and a live room at 0.80 s can both be right. The brief decides; the formula just keeps the brief honest.
  • Treatment is not isolation. Panels shape the sound inside a room. Stopping sound arriving from outside is building work — mass, seals, structure — and is quoted as such, or declined, in writing, before money moves.
  • Ears differ, and so does taste. Some clients prefer a livelier room than the target. We tune to the target, then listen, then adjust. The listening is part of the job, not a failure of the numbers.
  • Diminishing returns are real. The first £1,000 of treatment in a bare room transforms it; the fifth £1,000 polishes it. I will tell you when you have reached the polishing stage, because that is when you should stop.
  • I take no commission from panel, speaker or cable suppliers, and I sell no cables. The rate card on the home page is the whole of my interest in what you buy.
