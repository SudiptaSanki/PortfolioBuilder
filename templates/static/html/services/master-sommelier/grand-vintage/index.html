Bin 04

Left-bank Bordeaux, 2005

£145 a bottle
Provenance
Bought in 2023 from the cellar of a Kent collector who bought the wine on release through a London merchant. Invoices seen and copied. One private cellar from new until 2023, then my bonded store.
Condition
Fill: base of neck. Capsule clean, lightly scuffed at the rim. The cork has not been drawn — I do not pull corks to inspect, so the level and the capsule are what I stand behind. Label cellar-dusty, one corner lifted.
Drinking window
Drink now to 2032 — my judgement. The tannin has resolved and the fruit is still ahead of the tertiary notes; this is the plateau, not the climb.
At the table
Roast lamb, or a hard sheep's cheese after. It does not need rich food; it needs quiet food.
Held
12 bottles held. Sold singly or by the case.

Reference table — fill levels & condition terms, as used in every sheet above

TermWhat it meansWhat it does to the price here
Into neckThe wine fills up into the neck of the bottle.Full price. The best sign a bottle of this age can give.
Base of neckThe wine sits at the widest point, where the neck meets the shoulder.Full price. Normal and expected from about ten years of age.
Top shoulderThe wine sits at the top of the shoulder's curve.A small reduction. Normal from fifteen to twenty years.
Upper shoulderThe wine has fallen partway down the shoulder.A real reduction, and the drinking window narrows.
Mid-shoulderThe wine sits at the middle of the shoulder.Sold as drink-now and priced for the risk. Some bottles are tired; some are lovely.
Low shoulderThe wine has fallen below the shoulder's midpoint.Not sold here. At that level, the risk belongs to whoever already owns the bottle.
Burgundy & sloping bottlesLevels are given in centimetres below the cork, because a sloping shoulder makes shoulder terms meaningless.1–2 cm is sound at ten to fifteen years. 4 cm or more is a warning, and is priced as one.

A fill level is evidence, not a verdict: it shows how much wine has left the bottle through the cork over the years, which is the best outward sign of how the bottle has lived. It cannot tell you the wine is good. Nothing can, short of opening it — which is why the terms on the merchant page include a credit for any bottle that is corked or tired beyond its description.

Prices & terms

What things cost, and what moves the price.

Bottle prices

Every price includes VAT and duty and is the price in the register — the price on the sheet is the price at the till. No bottle in this book is priced by auction fever; each is priced off its level, its provenance and the last comparable parcel I handled.

Cases

Six or twelve bottles — any multiple of six, mixed freely across bins — take 10% off the bottle total. Part-cases pay bottle price. The calculator above does this arithmetic in the open.

Delivery

£18 to UK mainland addresses, free at £300 after discount. Bottles travel in moulded pulp packaging and leave on a Monday to Wednesday, so nothing spends a weekend in a depot.

Selling a cellar

I buy outright, or sell on commission at 15% of the achieved price, with an itemised account of every bottle — including the ones I downgrade. The first conversation and the first review of a list cost nothing.

Restaurants

Wine direction — list building, buying and staff training — at £450 a day, or a season retainer from £1,800. I never take margin from both sides of a bottle: if a merchant pays me, the restaurant is told, in writing.

What moves the price

Condition first — the same wine at base of neck and at mid-shoulder is two different bottles. Provenance second. Then scarcity, then format. The famous name on the label moves the price least in this cellar, which is rather the point.

Grand Vintage and Clara Whitfield are a fictional practice, created for this portfolio template. The wines are described generically — region and vintage only — and no bottle shown or listed is offered for real sale. Photographs are reference photographs, credited in the folder's CREDITS.md, and never depict the practice's own cellar or stock. This site makes no sound. Sales, in the fiction, are to over-18s only.
