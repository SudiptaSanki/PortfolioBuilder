Pens made one at a time · Nibs ground to the hand that will hold them

A pen should write the way its owner thinks.

A little pressure, and the line widens. That is the whole of my work: turning the body, grinding the nib, and tuning the two to one particular hand — yours, with its angle, its pressure and its habits, measured from a page of your ordinary writing.

The book today: 25 names — 13 pens to make, 12 nibs to tune. A bespoke commission entered this week reaches the bench in week 12 and is finished, on the printed figures below, in week 17. The queue is never hidden; it is the price of one pair of hands.

The Sampleswhat a grind actually lays down

A grind is a promise about a line. These are the bench's standard samples, drawn at four times life size, each with the figures the planner below works from. If a sample and a delivered pen disagree, the sample is right and the pen comes back — the adjustment policy on the bench page says how.

The hairline

Needlepoint · 0.15 mm · feather hand

Upstrokes, written at the lightest touch the hand can manage. A needlepoint lives or dies here: any scratch at this pressure and it goes back to the stone.

The shade

Broad · 0.60 → 1.00 mm · full flex

Downstrokes under full pressure. The tines open, the line widens, and — the part catalogues leave out — the tines must close again at the bottom of the stroke, every time, or the nib is being spent, not used.

The oval

Flex test · 0.20 → 0.85 mm · even, or back to the stone

The figure-eight every flex tune must pass before it leaves. Written in both directions, it shows uneven tines at a glance: one side shades early, the other late. Even, or back to the stone — there is no third verdict.

The drill

Fine · 0.25 → 0.45 mm · writing speed

Connected strokes at ordinary writing speed — the line most owners actually live in, somewhere between the hairline and the shade. A pen tuned only for slow, careful strokes is tuned for a hand nobody has.

    The Reckoningwhat a pen costs, and what moves it

    What moves the price, in the order it moves it: the nib's metal and its grind first, then the body material, then any vintage nib to be sourced and fitted, then size. What never moves it: how celebrated the handwriting is. The fees are printed as a table, and the reckoning below reads that table and no other.
