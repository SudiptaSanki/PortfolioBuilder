§ 03 Grinds

What a grind changes

A regrind reshapes the tipping only; the nib keeps its alloy and its feed. The broader the grind you start from, the more line variation you can see; the finer the 14k nib, the more flex you can ask of it.

    Extra fine

    0.30–0.35 mm. For small hands and cheap paper, where a broader nib would bleed. Feedback is audible on smooth stock.

§ 04 Fitting

Five questions before the first cut

Most nibs are ground for nobody in particular. A fitting begins with the hand: which hand you write with, the angle you hold the pen at, how hard you press, how fast you move, and what the paper does under the ink. The answers decide the grind, the flow and the feed.

A left hand that pushes rather than pulls needs a different radius on the inner edge. A writer who prints needs a flatter foot than one who joins every letter. Someone who writes on a clipboard at 70° cannot use an architect cut ground for a desk.
