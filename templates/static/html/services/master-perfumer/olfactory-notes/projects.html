CASE FILES

Three commissions, told the way they happened.

Each file follows the same discipline: the brief as given, the decisions as taken — including the round where the client's favourite direction was killed, and the reformulation nobody wanted but the rules required — and the outcome, stated plainly.

CASE FILE 01 · BRAND DEVELOPMENT · CANDLES

The candle that survived round two

THE BRIEF

Northlight Home, a two-person candle brand, wanted a winter candle: woodsmoke and warmth, without the acrid note that made their first attempt smell, in their words, "like a bonfire in a cupboard." It had to work in soy wax at production scale — a fragrance that performs on a blotter and dies in wax is a failed fragrance.

THE DECISIONS

Round one built the smoke from cade and birch tar at low dose over cedarwood — correct on paper, harsh in wax. Round two chased the client's favourite direction, a sweet spiced-woods accord; it smelled wonderful cold and vanished when lit, because sweetness carries almost no hot throw. I killed it in the round-two notes, in writing, with the burn-test results attached — and rebuilt around guaiac wood and a restrained smoke accord, with clove kept low enough to season rather than shout. Round three tuned the dose to the wax; round four was a production trial, not a new idea.

THE OUTCOME

The candle launched that winter and is still in production two winters later. The founder's verdict is on the home page. The formula, its allergen calculation and its stability record transferred to the client on final payment, as brand formulas here always do.

CASE FILE 02 · BESPOKE COMMISSION · PERSONAL PARFUM

The heart, replaced

THE BRIEF

A private client commissioned a personal parfum from a memory: a greenhouse after rain — wet green air, warm glass, soil. One wearer, one formula, no interest in what anyone else would think of it. That is the easiest brief to love and the hardest to finish, because "greenhouse" is a place, not a note.

THE DECISIONS

Iteration one built the heart on Damask rose — the client's first instinct, and mine. On the blotter it was lovely; on skin it turned the composition into a garden rose perfume with a green opening, and the greenhouse was gone. Iteration two replaced the heart entirely: orris for the cool, damp air, violet leaf for the green, a trace of galbanum for the sap. The bergamot opening stayed; the base moved from cedarwood to vetiver, which smells of soil in a way cedar never will. Iteration three was restraint — two materials removed, nothing added.

THE OUTCOME

Delivered as a parfum at 22% — the client chose the extrait strength after wearing both, knowing it would sit closer and last longer. The formula is archived under her name; she has reordered the same 50 ml twice. A bespoke formula is never resold, adapted or shown to another client. It is hers, and the archive copy exists so that it stays hers accurately.

CASE FILE 03 · REFORMULATION · PERSONAL CARE

The oakmoss letter

THE BRIEF

A small personal-care brand's body oil was built, as many good things are, on oakmoss — the deep, earthy base that holds a green formula together. Then the permitted level for oakmoss's key allergenic constituents was cut again under the industry restrictions every perfumer works within. The existing formula could no longer be sold as it stood.

THE DECISIONS

The client heard it from me first, in a letter, before their compliance adviser raised it: what had changed, what it meant for their stock, and what I proposed. The proposal was not "less oakmoss" — a diluted version of the idea is just a weaker perfume. The moss accord was rebuilt from the ground up: vetiver for the earth, cedarwood for the dryness, and a modern synthetic moss base for the green depth, dosed to sit inside the new limits with room to spare. Two rounds of wear tests against the original, on skin and in the oil base, before anyone signed anything.

THE OUTCOME

The brand relaunched with the change disclosed on its own product page — what changed, and why — which their customers rewarded rather than punished. The reformulation, its reasoning and its new allergen calculation are documented in the file, so the next restriction, whenever it comes, starts from knowledge rather than archaeology.
