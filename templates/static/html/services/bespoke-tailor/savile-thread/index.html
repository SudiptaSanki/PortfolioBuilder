The draft: what twenty-eight measurements actually decide

Before any cloth is cut, the measurements become a paper pattern — the draft — drawn for one body only. This is a coat front, simplified. Choose a point on the draft, or a line in the measure book, and see what that figure is for.

15½″

Neck & scruff

Taken round the neck where the collar will sit, with two fingers inside the tape. It sets the collar height and the scruff — the small of cloth below it. A collar cut from a wrong neck figure either strangles or stands away from the shirt, and no later fitting can fully rescue it.

Ten to twelve weeks, five stages

The timetable is not ceremony. Each stage exists because the stage after it cannot be done honestly without it — a baste fitting catches in an hour what no amount of measuring can foresee.

  1. Consultation & clothWeek 0

    Forty-five minutes: what the garment is for, how it will be worn, and cloth — chosen from the bunches by weight, weave and how it behaves, not by the photograph in the book. Half the price is taken here, as a deposit.

  2. Measure & draftWeeks 0–1

    Twenty-eight measurements and a set of posture notes become a paper pattern drafted for this body alone. The cloth is cut only after the draft is checked against the measure book a second time.

  3. Baste fittingWeeks 3–4

    The garment arrives basted together with white thread, one sleeve only, no buttonholes. It is worn, chalked, pinned and then taken apart again — this is where balance and posture are truly settled.

  4. Forward fittingWeeks 7–8

    Made up in earnest: sleeves in, pockets placed, collar set. What remains is refinement — a quarter inch here, the button point there — and the decision on details that cannot be judged flat: cuff length, break, button stance.

  5. Finish & final fittingWeeks 10–12

    Buttonholes are cut and sewn by hand only now, when the button point is proven — a worked buttonhole cannot be moved. Final press, a last fitting, and the balance is paid. The pattern is filed under your name for good.

What moves the price

  • Cloth. The standard bunches are included. A limited or vintage length can add £200–£600; I will always show you the cloth's price per metre before you choose it.
  • A second pair of trousers — £650, and the single best value in tailoring: trousers wear at twice the rate of a coat.
  • Extra fittings beyond the three included — £60 each, though a fourth is rarely needed and never sold to you as a necessity.
  • Time. I take two rush commissions a year. They cost 25% more, because they cost the other clients their place in the queue.
