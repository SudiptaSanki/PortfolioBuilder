What is on the floor

A five-axis trunnion mill with a 20,000 rpm HSK-E40 spindle is the centre of the shop. It cuts the cases and the brackets; the fourth set-up is usually the one that decides whether the part is good. Beside it, a three-axis mill for second operations and fixtures, a CNC lathe for crown tubes and bezel blanks, and a wire EDM for the odd pocket that cannot be milled.

The finishing bench is in the same room on purpose. If blasting and brushing happened down the corridor, the finish on the report would be a claim rather than a measurement.

Why 20 °C matters

Titanium expands about 8.6 µm per metre per kelvin. On a 39 mm case, a 5 °C swing moves the diameter by roughly 1.7 µm — most of a ±0.008 mm band. So the shop is held at 20 °C ±1 °C, the CMM sits in the same air, and parts are left on the granite for an hour before inspection. It is not comfort; it is part of the tolerance.

Metrology, and what a report actually contains

Every first run ships with a first-article inspection report: each dimensioned feature, its nominal, its tolerance, the measured value and the instrument. It is dull reading, which is the point. For production runs there is a sampling plan agreed at quote — typically the first part and one in ten after that, with a full CMM layout at the start of each batch.

Where a drawing is ambiguous, the shop does not choose the convenient interpretation. It sends a query with a marked-up PDF and waits for an answer. That habit has cost us two jobs and saved us four.

  • GD&T read to ASME Y14.5-2018, with datum structure agreed before CAM.
  • Surface finish measured with a skidless instrument, not judged by eye.
  • Every part photographed on the granite with its serial before packing.
  • Certificates of conformity issued against the purchase order, not the drawing.

Capacity and lead times

Around 60 % of the trunnion time is booked four weeks ahead. A single prototype case is typically three to five weeks from a clean drawing; a batch of twenty, six to eight. Aerospace work with full FAIR paperwork adds a week.

The shop does not take work it cannot inspect. If a feature needs a measurement we cannot make in-house, it is subcontracted with the report attached, and the subcontractor is named on the quote.
