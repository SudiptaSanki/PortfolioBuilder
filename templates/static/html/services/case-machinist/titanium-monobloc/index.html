Three materials, and why not more

The shop cuts what it can measure and finish properly. Titanium, 904L and tantalum cover watch cases and most instrument work; anything else is quoted only after a test cut.

Stock held
Grade 5: 12 bar
904L: 4 bar

Ti-6Al-4V

Grade 5 titanium

The default for watch cases and brackets. 4.43 g/cm³, 900 MPa tensile. Hard on tools, kind to the skin, and it will not take a polish the way steel does.

Cutting
carbide, 45 m/min
Coolant
high pressure, 70 bar

904L

Super-austenitic stainless

For cases that need a deeper lustre than titanium can hold. 8.0 g/cm³, and it work-hardens under a dull tool, so the tooling is changed on a count rather than on a squeal.

Cutting
carbide, 120 m/min
Finish
mirror to Ra 0.025

Tantalum

Refractory, 16.6 g/cm³

For limited editions and instrument weights. Dense, dull, and it takes a satin finish that wears like stone. Small parts only — the stock cost rules out anything larger.

05

From RFQ to shipped box

Typical lead time
3–6 weeks

  1. 01

    DFM review

    Drawing checked for datum structure, tool access and features that will need a fourth set-up. One page of notes, two days.

  2. 02

    CAM and stock

    Toolpaths verified in simulation against the stock model. Material called off; test cut on a coupon for anything unfamiliar.

  3. 03

    Cut

    Four to six set-ups on the trunnion, with in-process probing after each. Coolant checked twice a shift.

  4. 04

    Finish

    Bead blast, brushing or hand-lapped chamfers, done in-house so the Ra on the report is the Ra on the part.

  5. 05

    Inspect and ship

    CMM report, first-article report, certificate of conformance and photographs, boxed with the part in its cradle.
