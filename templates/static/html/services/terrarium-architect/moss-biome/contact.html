02Care

Questions that come up after a month

The glass is foggy all the time. Is it dying?

Probably not. Fog that never lifts means too much water in the loop. Take the lid off for two to four hours, wipe the inside of the glass with a clean cloth, and reseal. If it fogs again within a day, the drainage layer is too shallow and the jar should come back to the studio for a rebuild.

White mould on the substrate — what now?

White, thread-like growth at the base is usually a saprophytic fungus feeding on dead material. Remove it with a cotton bud, reduce the water, and add springtails if the vessel can take them. Grey or black fuzz on the moss itself is different: that is usually the start of rot and the affected patch should come out.

Can I use tap water?

No. Rainwater or distilled water only. Tap water leaves a white ring on the glass and adds salts that the closed loop cannot flush away.

How often should I open it?

A balanced jar needs opening perhaps four times a year for a look and a trim. If you are opening it weekly, the balance is wrong somewhere and it is worth bringing in.

What if I kill it?

Bring it back. A refresh starts at £45 and reuses your glass, which is usually the expensive part. If it fails within three months of purchase, the refresh is free.

03Maintenance

Looking after a collection

For offices, restaurants and plant shops with more than five vessels, we offer a quarterly visit: waterline check, trim, glass clean, and a replacement of anything that has failed. It takes about an hour for ten jars and costs £90 per visit within Greater Manchester.

It is not a subscription anyone needs for a single jar on a shelf. If that is what you have, the care table on the studio page is enough.
