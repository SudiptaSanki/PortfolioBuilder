Built vessels

Five jars, and what each one is for.

Every vessel below is built, settled and running. Volumes are glass volume, not planted volume, and the age is time since sealing.

01Sealed jar

Vessel 44 · March 2025

The long jar

A tall six-litre vessel for a north-facing shelf, built around a single piece of cork bark and a fern-moss slope. The headspace is deliberately generous: it takes longer to fog and gives the moss room to dry between cycles.

6.0 Lvolume
20 mosealed

02Paludarium

Vessel 39 · October 2023

The shallow water

A 24-litre paludarium with a water section at one end and a moss bank at the other. Not sealed: it has a mesh vent in the lid, because standing water and a closed lid make a smell. Springtails live in the bank and keep the mould down.

24 Lvolume
3 yrrunning
8 Lwater section
82%RH
ventedlid
03Wall panel

Vessel 51 · June 2024

The wall

An 800 × 600 mm framed moss panel for a stairwell with no floor space. Stainless frame, 60 mm of substrate held behind mesh, and a capillary mat fed by a 4-litre reservoir at the top. Not sealed: it is a living wall in miniature, and it is watered every ten days.

0.48 m²planted area
60 mmsubstrate depth
10 dayswatering cycle
4 Lreservoir
1.9 yrrunning
04Bottle garden

Vessel 33 · November 2021

The demijohn

A 1.2-litre bottle garden in a reclaimed demijohn, corked and sealed with wax. The smallest and oldest vessel on this list, and the one that taught me the most: a narrow neck means the substrate must go in dry and the moss must be pushed in with a bent spoon handle.

1.2 Lvolume
5 yrsealed
38 mmneck opening
3moss species

05Cabinet

Vessel 47 · 2025

The propagation cabinet

A 90-litre glass cabinet with two shelves, a humidity tray and a 12-hour LED bar, built for a plant shop that wanted to propagate its own moss stock. Not a sealed ecosystem: it is a controlled one, and it grows replacements for every jar on this page.
