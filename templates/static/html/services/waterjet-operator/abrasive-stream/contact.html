SHEET 02 · FILE PREP

Make the DXF easy to quote

UNITS MM
KERF COMPENSATION OFF

A clean file saves a day. We do not charge for programming, but we do have to stop and ask when a drawing is ambiguous, and that is where lead times go.

  • Units. Draw in millimetres at 1:1. A drawing at 1:2 scaled in the DXF is the most common problem we see.
  • Kerf. Send the true profile with no kerf compensation. We offset the toolpath to your nominated side.
  • Layers. One layer for cut, one for engrave (if any), one for dimensions. Delete construction lines.
  • Holes. Nominal size; we hold ±0.05 mm on holes above 3 mm. Below that, drill.
  • Tolerance. Put it on the drawing. If you do not, you get ±0.13 mm and an edge at Q3.
  • Material. Say whether you are supplying it. If we supply, we will quote from stock and hold the certs.

SHEET 03 · QUESTIONS

Asked on the phone most weeks

HONEST ANSWERS
NO SALES VOICE

Can you cut my part to ±0.001 in?

On thin material, with the tilting head, a slow fine cut and a lot of patience, we can approach ±0.025 mm. On 25 mm titanium, no. The honest answer depends on thickness and feature size, which is why the quote is per part rather than per shop.

Will the edge need finishing?

At Q3 the edge is a matte sandblast texture, Ra 6.3 µm, with a light burr on the exit side. A hand deburr or tumble is usually enough. Bearing and sealing surfaces are machined afterwards.

Do you supply material?

Yes, for most metals and stone. We do not stock glass or carbon, and we will tell you which merchant to use. Material is quoted at cost plus 15% and the mill certs come with it.

Can you cut 150 mm steel?

Yes, slowly, at a taper of 1–2° and with a tolerance closer to ±0.5 mm. If the part needs a parallel edge at that thickness, waterjet is the wrong process and we will say so.

Do you do small one-offs for individuals?

Yes. The minimum order is £45, which covers a small part in mild steel or aluminium including programming.

Can I watch the cut?

Yes, by arrangement. It is worth seeing once: the head moves slower than people expect and the sound is a hiss rather than a roar.
