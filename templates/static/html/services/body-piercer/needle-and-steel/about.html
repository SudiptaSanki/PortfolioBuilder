The Studio · Needle & Steel

Mara Quinn,
piercer

I started behind the counter of a London studio in 2008, sterilising tools and watching how the good piercers worked. My apprenticeship ran 2010 to 2012 under a senior piercer who made me learn the autoclave before the needle — the right order, and I still teach it that way. Two studios and some thousands of piercings later, I opened Needle & Steel in Brighton in 2016: one room, one piercer, every chart on the wall. The studio is registered with Brighton & Hove City Council and inspected by Environmental Health, and I work to the UK Association of Professional Piercers’ published standards for jewellery materials and sterilisation.

Materials

What goes in a fresh piercing, and what does not

Implant-grade titanium — ASTM F136

The metal of surgical implants, and the studio standard for every fresh piercing. It contains no nickel to speak of — nickel sensitivity is the most common jewellery reaction I see, and it is avoidable from day one. Every piece is mill-certificated: the studio can tell you the standard each batch was made to, not just the word “titanium” printed on a packet.

Solid 14k gold from named body-jewellery makers is the other fresh-piercing option — solid, not plated, with a titanium post where the piece design needs one.

“Surgical steel” & stall alloys

“Surgical steel” is a marketing phrase, not a standard — most of it is 316L stainless, which carries enough nickel to trouble sensitive skin, and none of it tells you the polish quality a healing channel actually feels. Jewellery from market stalls and festival traders is worse: unnamed alloys, plated coatings that flake into a healing piercing, and butterfly backs that harbour bacteria and cannot be cleaned properly.

That is why outside jewellery never goes into a fresh piercing here — and why I will happily check a healed piercing’s jewellery for free before I’ll fit a piece whose material nobody can name.

The aftercare card — the one pinned by the till

  • Clean twice a day with sterile saline spray (0.9% sodium chloride, nothing added). Spray, let it soften anything crusted, pat dry with clean gauze. That is the whole routine.
  • Do not twist or rotate the jewellery. That advice is a leftover from the gun era. A healing channel is not a hinge; turning the jewellery tears the new skin growing inside it.
  • No tea-tree oil, no surgical spirit, no “piercing solution” cocktails. Anything strong enough to sting is strong enough to set healing back. If a product promises to heal you faster, it is selling, not helping.
  • Do not sleep on healing cartilage. Pressure is the number one cause of angle drift and irritation bumps in ears. A travel pillow with the ear in the hole is the studio-standard trick.
  • No swimming — sea, pool, lake or hot tub — while a piercing heals. Brighton makes this the hardest rule to keep in summer, which is exactly why timing matters (see Chart 02 on the home page).
  • Come in, don’t search. Redness that spreads, pain that grows after the first week, or a bump that will not settle: check-ups are free, and ten minutes in person beats a week of forum advice.
Refusals

What I will not do

Printed, so nobody has to guess
  • Anyone under 18Every piercing in this studio is 18+ with photo ID — passport, driving licence or PASS card. No parental consent changes this, no “they look older” changes this, and if the ID is not in your hand on the day, the appointment moves. It protects you, it protects the studio, and it is not negotiable.
  • Piercing gunsThere is no gun in the building and there never will be. They cannot be sterilised between clients and they tear rather than pierce.
  • Your own jewellery in a fresh piercingStall-bought, gifted or found jewellery goes nowhere near a healing channel. Bring it in once you are healed and I will check the material for free.
  • Anyone who has been drinkingAlcohol thins the blood, ruins judgement about placement, and voids consent as far as I am concerned. Come back tomorrow; the piercing will keep.
  • Anatomy that will not hold itSome piercings need tissue a particular body does not have — a navel without a lip, ears too shallow for an industrial. I will say so at the consultation and chart what your anatomy will carry beautifully instead. A piercing that migrates out in six months helps nobody.
