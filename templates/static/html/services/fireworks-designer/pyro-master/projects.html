Six shows from the last two seasons

Each entry is a fired show with the cue sheet closed: durations, shell counts, the largest shell and the site constraint that shaped it. Client names are withheld where the event is private; venues are named where they are public.

26-0802 Aug 2026
Bristol Harbour

Harbour Festival finale

Two barges, nine minutes and a bespoke score written for the show. The close is twenty-six cues a minute: a wall of 3-inch chrysanthemums under 8-inch kamuro, then a 12-inch gold willow as the last note. Fallout over water, agreed with the harbour master and the fire authority.

26-0621 Jun 2026
Westonbirt

Midsummer concert close

An orchestral prom finishing with the 1812. Low-level fountains and comets along the lake edge, six 6-inch peonies on the cannon, and a single 8-inch willow over the water on the last bar. The conductor's click track drove the firing system; the show ran to the symphony, not to a stopwatch.

25-1105 Nov 2025
Frome

Bonfire night, town field

A tight site with houses on two sides and a 4-inch ceiling. Racks angled away from the terraces, a shortened fallout plan agreed with the fire officer, and a low-noise product list for the first ten minutes so the town's dogs had warning. Twenty-two minutes, 960 shells.

25-0913 Sep 2025
Chew Valley

A wedding over water

Six minutes fired from a pontoon on the lake, timed to a first dance and a father's speech. No sound in the sky for the first ninety seconds: a single line of strobe shells at the far end of the water, then the first peony on the chorus. 240 shells, 5-inch ceiling.

25-0726 Jul 2025
Bath

A university graduation ball

An 8-minute show over a cricket ground with a 6-inch ceiling and a strict 23:00 curfew. The music was a live band, so the show was fired on visual cues from the stage manager plus a timecode backup. The last shell broke at 22:58, two minutes inside the curfew.

24-1231 Dec 2024
Bristol

New Year over the docks

A midnight show fired from two rooftops with the harbour below. Eight minutes, 1,200 shells, and a 10-inch finale that was cut to 8-inch when the wind picked up at 23:30. The fallback sheet had been written the week before; the swap took four minutes on the firing line.

What it costs

Budget bands, honestly

Firework displays are priced by the shell, the crew, the site work and the paperwork. The shell is usually the smallest part of the bill. These bands are for a display designed and fired by us, including the site visit, the permit application and the sweep.

  • Small, 5–8 min, 3–4 in£2,500–£4,500
  • Mid, 8–12 min, 5–6 in£4,500–£9,000
  • Large, 10–15 min, 8 in£9,000–£18,000
  • Harbour or 12 in finalefrom £24,000
  • Weddings and privatefrom £2,200
  • Travelat cost beyond 50 miles

A site that needs traffic management, barges or a temporary magazine adds cost that has nothing to do with the fireworks. It is quoted separately and never buried in the shell price.
