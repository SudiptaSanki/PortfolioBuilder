The practice, in cases

How a small practice gets better, in the open

A doctor's portfolio cannot show patient cases — nor should it. What it can show is the practice itself: the problems we noticed in how care is organised, what we changed, and what the numbers did. All figures are the practice's own, published the same way as our waits and fees.

No patients appear on this page. Every story below is about rotas, phone systems and screening administration. Anything clinical stays where it belongs: in the consulting room and the patient's own record.
Case 01 · AccessStarted September 2023Measured over 12 monthsStatus Permanent — late clinics continue

Evenings for people who work

The brief

One appointment in five was booked and not attended — and the pattern was not carelessness. The missed slots belonged overwhelmingly to working-age patients who had been offered 10:40 on a Tuesday, taken it because it was all there was, and then been unable to leave work.

What we did

We moved two clinics a week to the evening — Tuesdays and Thursdays now run to 19:30 — and held those slots for patients in work or education until 48 hours before. A text reminder the day before, with a one-tap way to release the slot, replaced the old letter.

The outcome

Evening slots were 92% booked within a month and have stayed that way. Missed appointments across the practice fell from 19% to 7% over the year — which is eleven appointments a week given back to people who need them, at no extra cost to anyone.

Case 02 · ResultsStarted March 2024Measured over 6 monthsStatus Permanent — now on the fee table

Results without the phone queue

The brief

Reception counted over 300 calls a week that were some version of “are my results back?” Each one took a receptionist away from the desk, most ended with “not yet”, and patients were ringing daily because nobody could tell them when to ring once.

What we did

Every test now leaves the practice with a booked results call attached: a ten-minute telephone appointment, at a set time, with the doctor who ordered the test. Normal results also go by text the day they are reviewed, with the doctor's own comment — never a bare “normal”.

The outcome

Inbound “any results?” calls fell 38% in six months, and the phone queue at 8:00 am shortened with them. Just as important: no result is filed unseen. Every result is read by the doctor who ordered it before the patient hears a word — that rule is written into the system, not left to memory.

Case 03 · PreventionStarted January 2025Measured over two screening roundsStatus Continuing — next round spring 2027

The screening catch-up

The brief

Our bowel screening kit return rate sat at 54%, below the city average — and the unreturned kits clustered in exactly the streets where the test matters most. The kits were arriving; they were going into drawers, because the instructions are genuinely off-putting and nobody had explained them face to face.

What we did

We joined the local screening programme's catch-up properly: Saturday clinics where our nurse walks through the kit step by step, reminder letters rewritten in plain English and checked by the patient panel, and a phone call — not a second letter — to anyone whose kit was still out after a month.

The outcome

Return rates rose from 54% to 71% across two rounds. The Saturday explanation clinics were the single biggest factor: twenty minutes with a nurse and the actual kit in your hands does what three leaflets cannot.
