A window is surveyed before it is touched

The order does not change. A rose is read in daylight, drawn in elevation, and numbered the way the Corpus Vitrearum numbers it, so the record outlives the treatment.

  1. Survey

    Scaffold access, raking-light photography and a panel-by-panel condition drawing. Glass loss, lead fatigue, ferramenta corrosion and mortar failure are mapped separately, because they fail separately.

    Three to five days on site · 1:10 elevation drawings

  2. Stabilise

    Loose paint is consolidated in situ where the panel can stay. Where it cannot, the panel is lifted, supported face-down in a lined crate, and carried to the studio at Rue aux Ours.

    Crates foam-lined to panel profile · Two-person lifts

  3. Treat

    Edge-bonding for fissures, copper-foil repairs at the saddle, and a full re-lead only when the came has lost its section. Grisaille and silver stain are never re-fired without a test panel.

    Reversible adhesives · Lead came matched to original width

  4. Return

    Ventilated isothermal glazing goes in front of the medieval panel, with the air gap measured and monitored. The window is photographed again and the report is lodged before the scaffold comes down.

    Isothermal glazing · Datalogger left for twelve months
