Four windows, four different answers

Two roses were re-leaded, one needed only its ironwork, and one was surveyed and left alone. Each record lists what was found, what was done, and what was deliberately not done.

Elevation, west rose. Twenty-four medallions in two rings; the outer ring was re-glazed in 1889.

Louviers, Normandy

West rose, Église Saint-Martin

The window had not been opened since 1908. The survey found lead at the end of its life across the whole outer ring and a ferramenta bar deflected by 14 mm, pressing on the glass at four points. The inner ring was sound and stayed in its opening for the first four months, while the outer ring was lifted, documented and re-leaded.

All original glass was retained, including the 1889 border, which was recorded as a later addition rather than removed. Ventilated isothermal glazing was installed 60 mm in front of the medieval panel, with a datalogger left for twelve months.

Elevation, north rose. Eight lobes; the ferramenta had been replaced in 1954 with a heavier section than the original.

Avignon, Vaucluse

North rose, Chapelle des Pénitents

This rose is small and was in better condition than its ironwork suggested. Corrosion had expanded the 1954 saddle bars, and three of them were pressing into the glass. The glass itself showed only two fissures, both stable.

We replaced the ferramenta in stainless steel to the original, lighter profile, released the pressure points, and bonded the two fissures. The lead was left. The window was back in its opening in seven weeks, and no panel left the building.

Elevation, south transept rose. The grisaille lining is original; the paint had lost adhesion across nine figures.

Caen, Calvados

South transept rose, Ancienne Abbaye Saint-Pierre

A thirteenth-century rose with an unusually complete grisaille lining. The paint was lifting in nine figures, and the previous intervention — a 1970s resin applied over the whole panel — had yellowed and was holding dirt against the glass.

We removed the resin under magnification, consolidated the lifting paint with a reversible adhesive, and left the losses as losses. The surviving grisaille is legible; inventing the missing lines would have made the window a modern drawing of a medieval one.

Elevation, east rose. Surveyed in 2024; recommended for re-inspection in 2034, with no treatment in the interval.

Besançon, Doubs

East rose, Chapelle du Séminaire

A sixteenth-century rose, rebuilt in the nineteenth century after a fire, with a mix of medieval and 1860s glass. The fabric committee expected a re-leading quotation. The survey did not support one.

The lead is sound, the ferramenta is clean, the mortar is draining, and the interior environment is stable. We produced the elevation drawing, the panel register and a ten-year re-inspection date, and recommended that the budget be spent on the roof above the chapel instead. That is the report we were paid for.

Surveyed, not treated

Some windows need a record more than they need a workshop. These were surveyed, drawn and left in place, with a re-inspection date lodged with the building:

  • Église Saint-Rémi, Dieppe — north rose, c. 1280. Re-inspect 2031.
  • Chapelle Saint-Michel, Mont-Saint-Michel — two lancet roses, fifteenth century. Re-inspect 2029.
  • Église Notre-Dame-du-Val, Caen — west rose, c. 1310. Re-inspect 2033.

Full condition reports for any of these are available to the fabric committee, the diocesan architect or a successor conservator.
