The workshop · About the maker

One bench makes everything you saw

There is no separation in this company between the people who build the puppets and the people who perform them. That is deliberate: a mechanism designed by its operator gets fixed at the drawing board, not in a corridor at the half.

Ruth Ellery — maker & director

I trained in theatre design, then spent two seasons as an apprentice in a marionette workshop in Prague, where the rule was that you did not touch a performance puppet until you could restring one in the dark. I founded the Ellery workshop in Norwich in 2011 with a bench, a bandsaw and one commission; the company now tours four productions and takes two commission slots a year.

My specialism is mechanisms that survive touring — joints, cords and controls designed to be repaired at a venue with a hex key and a head torch. The Harbour Fox case file on the Productions page is the clearest statement of how I work: weight on the rod, not the wrist; every wearing part replaceable; a printed bench file with every figure.

The company is me, two performer-makers, and a stage manager who also drives, rigs and keeps the bench files honest. When you book a show or commission a puppet, the person who answers the email is one of the people who will be in your building.

The short version, for programmes

60-word biography (use freely): Ruth Ellery is a puppet maker and director based in Norwich. Trained in theatre design and apprenticed in Prague, she founded Ellery Puppet Theatre in 2011. The company tours four productions across the UK and Europe — tabletop, shadow and string — and builds commissioned puppets for other companies, all from one workshop where the makers and the performers are the same people.

Press & programme enquiries go to the same bookings address as everything else; there is one inbox and it is answered within two working days. Production photographs for press use are supplied on booking — reference photography credits are listed in this site's CREDITS file.

Teaching: two workshop formats (half and full day, up to 30 participants) cover rod-puppet making for beginners and mechanism clinics for companies maintaining their own figures.

Commissions

From script to first rehearsal

  1. Conversation & script

    We read the script or treatment and talk through what the puppet must do — actions before appearance. Free, and it ends with a written fee quote.

  2. Design drawings

    Scale drawings and a mechanism sketch for each figure. One round of revisions included.

  3. Maquette & approval

    A half-scale working maquette of the principal figure. This is the approval point: 50% deposit is due here, and nothing full-size is cut before you sign the maquette off.

  4. Build

    Full build at the bench, with photograph updates at carcass, mechanism and paint stages. Typical total: 3–10 weeks by type, per the table above.

  5. Rehearsal & handover

    A movement session with your operators is included — a puppet is only finished when the person playing it can make it breathe. Bench file handed over with the figure.

Care & repair

Keeping figures on the road

  • Restringing — marionettes from £60, including cord map check against the bench file.
  • Repaint & surface repair — from £85, matched to the recorded paint mixes where a bench file exists.
  • Mechanism overhaul — joints, rods and controls stripped, re-bushed and re-set, from £120.
  • Annual service for touring companies — a bench day per production: every figure checked, cords replaced on schedule rather than on failure, £300 per day plus parts.
  • Storage, honestly — figures travel and store best hung or laid in their own boxes, out of sunlight, at room humidity. We will tell you if your store is going to eat your puppets, and what to change.

Repairs are taken year-round, not only in commission slots. Send photographs of the damage with the enquiry — most quotes are possible from pictures.

The rider, in substance

Sheet 05 · Summary — full sheet per show on the Programme page
  • Space & blackout — per the show's tech sheet: footprint, clear height and blackout are contractual, attached to the booking as an appendix.
  • Sound playback — the company brings the playback laptop and operator. The venue provides the PA for rooms over 150 seats; under that, our own PA travels with the show at no extra charge. Schools need provide nothing.
  • Power — two 13A sockets at the stage edge for playback and work light. Nothing else: the shows use no other electrics.
  • Get-in schedule (Salt Road, typical) — 10:00 access & unload · 10:30 set build · 12:00 focus & playback check · 13:30 company break · 14:15 doors for a 14:30 matinee. The sheet for your show gives its own timings to the quarter hour.
  • Venue crew — one technician for studio shows, two for The Clockmaker's Daughter, none for schools. Front of house remains the venue's.
  • Company — we tour with our own stage manager, who calls the show and is your single point of contact on the day.
