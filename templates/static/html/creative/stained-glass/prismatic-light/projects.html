New commission · House

The Herron Hill Stairwell Window

A half-landing window for a 1908 Pittsburgh house, replacing the clear glass that had stood in the opening since the original fanlight was lost in the 1970s.

The brief

The landing was the darkest room in the house and the window faced a neighbour's brick wall six feet away. The owners wanted the light kept, the wall hidden, and a design that belonged to a 1908 house rather than to a catalogue.

Decisions

A symmetrical pattern drawn from the surviving 1908 glass in the house's own front door. Opalescent glass at eye level for privacy; clear, heavily textured quarries at the top to borrow every bit of sky. Mouth-blown antique glass in amber and green, because machine glass read flat in that north light.

Outcome

The landing is now the brightest turn in the house and the wall is gone from view. The panel went in over one day, set into the repaired original sash, and the owners report the hall lights stay off until evening.

Setting
Private house, Pittsburgh
Glass
Mouth-blown antique; opalescent

Restoration · Church

St. Brigid's Chancel Window — releaded

The parish's 1928 chancel window, taken down in sections, releaded in matched-profile came, and reset behind vented protective glazing.

The brief

The window bowed an inch and a half out of plane, its cement had fallen out along the bottom third, and rain was reaching the sill. Two firms had quoted the parish for a replacement. The buildings committee asked a narrower question: can the old glass be saved?

Decisions

Yes — the paint was sound and only four pieces were cracked. Before anything moved, the window was photographed piece by piece and a full rubbing taken. It came down in three sections; 212 pieces were cleaned and reset in new lead of the original profile. The four replacements were cut from mouth-blown glass and painted to match, and are recorded as replacements in the parish archive. A vented protective glaze went on the outside so weather meets it first, and the panel stays dry.

Outcome

The window hangs flat and weather-tight, with 96% of its 1928 glass still in it, and was back in place for the parish centenary. The committee's verdict is on the home page. The next releading should not be due until the window is two hundred years old.

Setting
Church, Pittsburgh
Glass
Original 1928 glass, 96% retained
Size
48 × 132 in · 44 sq ft
Completed
2023 · 14 weeks

New commission · Workplace

The Kiln Yard Screen

A floor-to-near-ceiling screen dividing a ceramics studio's office from its kiln room — contemporary work, drawn from the client's own materials.

The brief

The studio needed the office separated from kiln heat and dust without losing the daylight that comes over the kiln room's high windows — and without a partition that pretended the building was something other than a workshop.

Decisions

An abstract grid with no picture in it at all. The palette was taken from the client's glaze test tiles — the screen is, in effect, their glaze library at architectural scale. Opalescent glass low down, where the office needs privacy; antique glass above, where the light is. The panel is leaded in sections inside a steel frame, so any section can come out for repair without disturbing the rest.

Outcome

The office is cooler, quieter and private; the kiln room keeps its light. On firing nights the screen is the warmest-coloured wall in the building, and it has become the studio's informal sign — visitors are directed to "the glass wall".

Setting
Ceramics studio, Pittsburgh
Glass
Antique & opalescent
Size
30 × 84 in · 17.5 sq ft
Completed
2024 · 10 weeks
