Projects, in full

Most commissions arrive as a short email and a long hope. These three are written out whole, because the decisions — the hand, the support, where the gold goes — are the part a photograph cannot show.

The Hartley Vows

A wedding leaf · North Yorkshire · 2025

The brief

Clare and Daniel Hartley were marrying in a small North Yorkshire church and had written their own vows — 186 words. They wanted the words themselves, not a print of them: one leaf they could read from at the ceremony and then frame for the house. The date was fixed, eleven weeks out, and the leaf had to survive being held, read aloud, and passed hand to hand on the day.

The decisions

Chancery italic, because vows are spoken, personal text — the hand of letters, not of monuments — and because it stays legible at arm's length under church light. Cotton paper rather than vellum: it takes handling better, lies flatter in a frame, and costs the couple a third less. One raised gesso initial in 23.5 ct gold on the first word, and no other gold — a wedding leaf should not compete with the day. The painted accents were matched to the bouquet, cornflowers and poppies, which put ultramarine and vermilion in the margins and nowhere else.

The outcome

The leaf was read from at the ceremony, delivered the week before the wedding, and framed behind museum glass by the couple's local framer using the studio's guidance sheet. A year on, it hangs in their kitchen — the clients' own account of it is on the home page, attached to this piece. The studio has since written two more vows leaves from referrals at that wedding.

Text
The couple's own vows, 186 words
Script
Chancery italic
Materials
Iron-gall ink, raised gesso initial in 23.5 ct gold, ultramarine, vermilion, cotton paper
Dimensions
420 × 297 mm
Turnaround
Seven weeks, enquiry to delivery

The Missing Leaf

A Book of Hours, completed honestly · 2024

The brief

A collector's Book of Hours — Flemish, late fifteenth century — lacked its Magnificat leaf, almost certainly cut out long ago for the miniature it carried. He did not want a fake, and he did not want a gap. He wanted a new leaf that would sit quietly in the book, do the text's work, and declare itself to anyone who looked properly.

The decisions

The surviving leaves set every term: textura quadrata matched stroke for stroke to the book's own scribe, vellum chosen and toned to sit with five-hundred-year-old skins, the border painted in the book's own vocabulary of ivy-leaf and gold dots. The gilding is raised gesso, as the original's was. And on the verso, in a modern hand and modern ink, the leaf is signed and dated — a recreation that can never be detached and sold as medieval. That signature was the studio's condition for taking the work, and the collector agreed before the deposit was paid.

The outcome

The leaf is in the book, which the collector now shows complete for the first time in living memory — with the new leaf pointed out, not hidden. He has written that the verso inscription is the part he shows people first. The studio takes this kind of work only on those terms, and says so in every first reply.

Text
Magnificat (Luke 1:46–55)
Script
Textura quadrata, matched to the book's scribe
Materials
Calfskin vellum, raised gesso gilding, ultramarine, vermilion
Dimensions
180 × 125 mm, the book's own trim size
Turnaround
Nine weeks, including matching studies

The Guild Charter

A ceremonial document for a 250th year · 2024

The brief

A city guild, marking 250 years since its charter, wanted the charter's text written out as a ceremonial document: read aloud by the Master at the anniversary dinner, signed by the officers at the table, and framed in the guild hall afterwards. It had to read clearly across a candlelit room and still reward a close look the next morning.

The decisions

Inscriptional capitals for the preamble — the lines that carry the guild's name and grant — with the body in a large italic, a pairing taken from early-modern charters. Vellum, because the document is handled once a year and must outlast everyone at the table. Space was ruled and left blank, deliberately, for the signatures and the seal: a document that will be signed must be designed around its signing. The rubrication is vermilion throughout, and the gold is shell gold only — flat, fine lines that read as richness at a distance without shouting.

The outcome

The charter was read at the dinner, signed by eleven officers, and now hangs in the guild hall in a frame built to the studio's specification. The guild's sister guild in a neighbouring city has commissioned its own charter for its anniversary next year — the second institutional commission to come directly from the first being seen in use.

Text
Charter of incorporation, anniversary redaction
Script
Inscriptional capitals, italic body
Materials
Vellum, iron-gall ink, vermilion rubrication, shell gold
Dimensions
560 × 420 mm
Turnaround
Eight weeks, enquiry to the dinner
