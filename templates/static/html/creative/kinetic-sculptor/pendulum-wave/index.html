01

Forty pendulums, one release

A coupled field is not a wave in water. It is a row of pendulums with graduated lengths, released together. Because each period follows T = 2π√(L/g), the line of bobs drifts out of phase, reads as a travelling wave, closes into a standing shape, then scatters — and comes back. Change the length step and count below; the traces are computed from the simulated positions, not played from a video.

Three installations

All 38 works

Phase Field III

Zürich, CH · atrium · 6.4 m × 3.1 m

Thirty-two brass bobs on Dyneema, released by a single gate. The length step is tuned so the field passes through three standing shapes per beat.

Case study

Rotor Wall

Rotterdam, NL · façade · 9.8 m × 7.2 m

Forty-eight anodised discs on a stainless spine, each on a sealed POM bearing. Moves at 5 km/h; parks itself above 17.4 m/s.

Case study

Drift Line

Copenhagen, DK · harbour pool · 12.0 m

A floating line of five rods, baffled so boat wake reads as slow breathing rather than slap. Anodes renewed on a twenty-year schedule.

05

From site to second release

Typical programme
6–14 months

  1. 01

    Site

    Wind zone, mounting structure, sightlines and the hour of day the piece will be seen. Two visits, one survey.

  2. 02

    Simulate

    Motion study in Python; the piece is tuned for its beat period before anything is drawn.

  3. 03

    Maquette

    1:10 model in brass and steel, released and filmed for two weeks to check the read at distance.

  4. 04

    Engineer

    FE analysis, weld schedule, bearing selection and an anchor layout signed by a structural engineer.

  5. 05

    Fabricate & install

    Cut and finished in Reinach, trial-assembled, then installed over three to five nights.
