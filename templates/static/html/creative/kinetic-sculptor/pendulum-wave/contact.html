Before the first meeting

      
Five questions
from institutions

    

    

      

        Who signs off the structure?
        
Büro Halt in Basel checks and stamps the calculation pack; on projects outside Switzerland the local engineer of record reviews and countersigns it. The client receives both documents, plus the weld schedule and anchor layout as built.

      

      

        Do we need planning permission?
        
Usually yes for anything on a façade or in a public space, and often for interior work in a listed building. The studio prepares the drawing set for the application and has taken eleven pieces through Swiss and Dutch committees. It adds six to sixteen weeks, so it sits in the programme from the start.

      

      

        What does maintenance cost?
        
Between CHF 1,200 and CHF 4,800 a year depending on exposure, covering two inspections, bearing checks and fastener torque. Outdoor works also need the annual dye-penetrant test, around CHF 900. Maintenance is quoted separately and is a condition of the warranty.

      

      

        Can the piece be silent and unpowered?
        
Yes. Atrium fields are released by hand and use no power at all, which is why they suit libraries and hospitals. Façade pieces are wind-driven; only the geared works need a 24 V supply, and those include a manual release for power cuts.

      

      

        What is the smallest commission?
        
Around CHF 25,000 for an interior piece up to 4 m. Below that the engineering and documentation dominate the budget and the studio will usually suggest a smaller, denser work rather than a cut-down version of a large one.
