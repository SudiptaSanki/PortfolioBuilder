Field Record № 01 — The Pendulum Wave

Wind is a material.

The Printed Field

One relation runs the whole rail

A pendulum’s period is set by its length and nothing else. That single fact is the field, the planner and the price list — all three read from this table.

The relation, with its working

For a pendulum swinging through a small angle, the period is

T = 2π√(L ÷ g),  so  L = g × (T ÷ 2π)², with g = 9.81 m/s².

Worked once, for the slowest pendulum: T = 2.00 s. T ÷ 2π = 0.31831. Squared: 0.10132. Times g: 0.994 m — the seconds pendulum, near enough a metre, which is why clockmakers loved it before they had any right to.

Each pendulum on the rail completes exactly one more swing than its neighbour inside the 60-second cycle: 30, 31, 32 … 44. When every count comes whole again, the field re-phases. The patterns in between — the double row, the braid, the brief chaos at half-cycle — are not programmed. They are what the arithmetic looks like.

The relation holds for small swings; the field is released at 12°, where the error stays under half a percent, and the rods are cut to the table, not tuned by ear afterwards.

    The survey comes first

    Every commission starts with twelve months of the site’s wind — from the nearest station record, checked against a season of the studio’s own anemometer on the site itself. Buildings lie about wind; a year of numbers does not.

    The survey ends in one of three answers: the site can carry the piece asked for; the site can carry a different piece, named; or the site is too still, and the answer is no. The third answer has been given four times and is a case file on the Projects page, because a refusal with its data printed is worth more than a testimonial.

    Fees — The Shape of a Price

    What moves the price, in order

    Scale first — rod length and count. Foundations second: a field stands on its footings longer than on its bearings, and engineered footings in poor ground cost more than the steel above them. Finish last. The site wind survey is €1,200, deducted in full from any commission over €25,000. Nothing is quoted before the survey: a price given without the site’s wind is a guess wearing a suit.
