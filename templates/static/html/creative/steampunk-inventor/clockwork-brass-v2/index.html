The fleet · four machines that earn their keep

Every sticker tells the truth

What winds it, how long it runs, and which of its gears are pure decoration — printed on the plate, not whispered afterwards.

Machine No. 1 · Class A · Table automaton · 2023

The Pearl Oyster

A brass oyster the size of a dinner plate. Over forty seconds the shell opens, a pearl on a stem rises, turns once to catch the light, and the shell closes on it. Built for close looking and quiet rooms.

Power truth: mainspring, key-wound — 5 minutes per wind, demonstrated in turns, one operator.

Confession: nine gears on the back plate. Three do nothing and are proud of it.

Machine No. 2 · Class C · Grand automaton · 2024

Madame Cog, the Writing Automaton

She dips a real pen in real ink and writes one of twenty-four phrases on your card — your name worked into the sentence if you book her for a wedding or a launch. The phrases live on changeable cams. You may inspect them. Hirers do.

Power truth: twin mainsprings — 20 minutes per wind, two operators to wind and mind her.

Confession: 214 gears in the train. Thirty-seven form the great “cogitation wheel” over her desk. It spins free. It thinks nothing.

Machine No. 3 · Class B · Cabinet machine · 2022

The Brass Band in a Box

A four-piece band in a walnut cabinet. The music is honest: a pinned-barrel organ in the base plays it. The figures mime — their arms are cam-driven and strike nothing at all. Except the drummer. The drummer's stick lands.

Power truth: falling weights, raised by hand — 40 minutes per raise, one operator.

Confession: the trumpet player's cheeks puff. There is no air in him. There never was.

Machine No. 4 · Class D · Steam spectacle · 2019

The Firedrake

A dragon the length of a family car. It rears, beats its wings and breathes real steam — because it runs on it. Boiler at 3 bar, inspected and certified every year, attended for the whole show by its own boiler hand. Outdoors, or in a hall vented like one.

Power truth: live steam — 80 minutes per firing, 45 minutes to steam up before doors, three operators.

Confession: none. A steam machine does not get a joke on its plate. It gets a pressure certificate.

      What moves the price

      Three things. Nothing else.

      First, the class of machine — a table automaton is £450 a day; the dragon is £2,600 and earns it. Second, operators — my machines travel with my people, never without, and the planner above counts them honestly. Third, transport — 90p a mile, there and back. No percentage of your budget. No “production fee”. No surprise line at the bottom of the invoice.
