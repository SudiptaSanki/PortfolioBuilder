Plate X — questions

      
What people ask before sending a clock

    

    

      

        Is my clock worth restoring?
        
Sometimes it is not, and I will say so. A mass-produced 1930s mantel clock usually costs more to restore than it is worth, but it may still be worth restoring for the family that owns it. The estimate separates “keep it running” from “conserve it as an object”, and you can choose either.

      

      

        Can you make a missing part?
        
Yes, and it will be marked. Wheels, pinions, levers, hands and case parts are all made here; dials are engraved by a specialist in Clerkenwell. If a part is missing and there is no evidence of its shape, I will tell you what is a guess and what is not.

      

      

        Do you work on electric clocks or watches?
        
No watches, and no electric movements. The bench is set up for brass clocks and automata from about 1750 to 1950. For a watch I will give you the name of two people who do that work properly.

      

      

        How long will a commission take?
        
An automaton is four to ten months, a longcase six to nine, a dial five to seven. The longest part is always the train design, not the making. I take three commissions a year so that each one gets a bench, not a corner of one.

      

      

        Will you work from my design?
        
Yes, if the design can be made to run. Send drawings and I will return them marked up with what the train needs — wheel counts, centres, cam profiles. If the design cannot work, I will say why in one page rather than three.
