C3 · The Practice

One designer, one document set, no showroom

Matte Lumen is the practice of Ingrid Halvorsen. It sells specification and site time — not fittings — which is why this page is a plan and two tables rather than a gallery of other people's interiors.

The designer

Ingrid Halvorsen trained as an architect at the Aarhus School of Architecture and spent six years in the lighting team of a large Copenhagen practice, working on hotels and museum galleries — the two building types where light is specified hardest, because it is measured against guests who complain and objects that fade. She has practised independently since 2019 and is a professional member of the International Association of Lighting Designers (IALD).

The practice is deliberately small: one designer, a controls engineer and an electrician team she has worked with for years, brought in per project. Forty-six schemes since 2019 — houses, flats, one restaurant, one hotel — is the output of that size, and the register on the Specification page is the honest sample of it.

Two working rules explain most of the site. First: a scheme is its documents. If the levels live only in the designer's head, the client has bought a performance, not a lighting design — so every scheme leaves with printed schedules. Second: independence. The practice takes no commission, discount or hospitality from fixture suppliers, and trade discounts are passed to the client in full. A specification that earns from what it specifies is advertising with drawings.

Most commissions arrive through architects and previous clients. The survey — half a day in the actual rooms, meter and ladder included — is how every engagement starts, whether the project is one room or eighteen suites.

§ 02 · The document set

What a specification contains

Seven documents make up a full package. Your electrician prices from them, your insurer files two of them, and the scene schedule is the one that stays in the kitchen drawer.

§ 03 · The numbers rooms are designed to

A lux guide, because "bright" is not a specification

Maintained illuminance — the level on the surface that matters, after the lamps have aged a little. These are the working values used across the practice, in line with CIBSE and IES guidance for homes.

Design illuminance — residential
PlaceLevelNote
Living room, ambient100–150 lxEvening scenes sit far below this — 20–50 lx is where the room feels warm.
Reading chair300 lxLocal light at the page, from a lamp or a spot — never by raising the whole room.
Dining table150–200 lxMeasured at the table surface; faces lit indirectly, not from directly above.
Kitchen worktop300–500 lxNo shadow of your own hands on the board matters more than the number.
Bathroom mirror300–500 lx verticalLight on the face from both sides; a single downlight makes shadows, not light.
Stairs & hall100 lxThe night path circuit runs at 5–10 lx — enough to walk by, not enough to wake by.
Artwork — oils & acrylics150–200 lxWith UV-free sources and the level verified by meter at the surface.

§ 04 · Commissioning notes

Hire the design, buy the fittings, keep the documents

Who buys what

The practice specifies; you or your contractor buy. That separation is the point — a designer who profits from the fittings will specify more of them. Order one spare of each fitting type while the line is current, and record the driver references in the fixture schedule: in fifteen years the drivers are what you will replace, and the schedule is where you will look.

When to bring in a designer

Before first fix — before ceilings close and cable routes are decided. A lighting designer at that stage costs a survey and a fee; the same designer afterwards costs that plus making good. The survey covers daylight, ceiling construction, existing wiring and the furniture plan, and it ends with a written brief and a fixed fee proposal, so the decision is made on paper either way.

Keeping it working

Good LED fittings are rated L80B10 at 50,000 hours — decades in a home. What ages first is taste: rooms get redecorated around fixed light. The defences are boring and effective — keep the printed scene schedule, choose fittings with replaceable drivers, and re-aim after any redecoration. An annual check on collection lighting is part of the Collector's Flat arrangement and is offered on every scheme.
