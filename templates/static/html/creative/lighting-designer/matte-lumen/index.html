§ 01 · The scene player

Four scenes, one room, every level printed

This is the living room of the Harbour Apartment (register № 01), drawn as an elevation. Press a scene and the drawing does what the room does: each circuit fades to its scheduled level, and the scene line underneath prints the same figures that appear on the schedule handed to the client. The master fader is the slider this template always promised — here it is wired to a room instead of a background colour.

The keypad

Four scenes, set on site at dusk and printed on the schedule. Levels are percentages of each circuit's full output.

Colour follows level, as a filament lamp does: the deeper a circuit dims, the warmer it runs — 2700 K down toward 2000 K.

How to read the lux figure: a simple additive model — each circuit's full-level contribution at the reference plane (pendants measured at the table, the rest ambient), scaled by its level. It teaches the shape of a scene; it is not a calculation. A real room is calculated point by point in DIALux and the summary is part of the specification.

What moves the price

  • Room and circuit count — the honest driver, stated per project
  • Listed or protected fabric, which rules out chasing and adds survey time
  • Control platform: wireless costs less to install, wired costs less to maintain
  • Site visits beyond the two included, and travel outside Greater Copenhagen at cost
  • Fixtures are bought by you or your contractor. We take no supplier commission and pass trade discounts to the client in full — the specification has to be independent to be worth paying for
§ 04 · Method

Six stages, in the order the building needs them

  1. STAGE 01

    Survey & measure

    Half a day in the rooms: dimensions, ceiling construction, existing wiring, furniture plan, and the daylight each room already gets — most schemes fail by ignoring the free light.

  2. STAGE 02

    Concept — layers & scenes

    The rooms described as layers (ambient, task, accent) and as scenes (the four or five states each room actually lives in), agreed with you before a single fitting is named.

  3. STAGE 03

    Specification

    Fixture schedule with beam angles, colour temperature and rendering figures; circuit schedule; reflected ceiling plans; lux calculations summarised room by room.

  4. STAGE 04

    Tender & install support

    Your electrician prices from the schedule, not from a mood board. Queries answered, substitutions checked against the spec — a fitting is only equal if its beam and colour figures are.

  5. STAGE 05

    Focus & aim

    On site at dusk, ladder and meter in hand: every adjustable fitting aimed, every beam checked against the wall it was drawn for. This is the visit most schemes never get.

  6. STAGE 06

    Scene-setting & handover

    Scenes set with you in the room, then printed: the scene schedule, the control files and the fixture list stay with the house, so the lighting can be re-set by anyone, years from now.
