Case 01 · Wall 041

The Heron Returns — a wall that floods

A council arts programme wanted the greyest underpass in the ward to carry the river that runs under it. The wall's problem was not artistic. It was wet.

The brief

Paint 70 m² of cast concrete in a pedestrian underpass beneath a railway embankment, Bristol, for a council arts and place programme. Subject: the river wildlife that passes through the ward. Budget fixed by grant; the wall had to be finished before the winter programme launch, and the underpass could not be closed to pedestrians.

The decisions

The survey moisture meter read 18–22% across the lower two metres — the wall floods to about knee height most winters. Standard acrylic on damp concrete blisters within a season, so the design was painted in a silicate mineral system, which bonds chemically with masonry and lets vapour pass instead of trapping it. The wall was fungicidal-washed and left to dry for nine days; the heron was drawn at full wall height so its legs stand in the flood line — the water, when it comes, completes the picture instead of ruining it. Work ran in half-width sections behind barriers so the route stayed open.

The outcome

Finished in 11 days on site, inside the grant budget. The underpass has flooded twice since; at the 24-month inspection the paint film was intact, with no blistering and one graffiti tag removed from the coated plinth in twenty minutes. The programme officer's testimonial is on the home page, attached to this wall.

Dimensions
22 × 3.2 m · 70 m²
Surface
Cast concrete, 18–22% moisture
Paint system
Silicate mineral, 2 coats + coated plinth
Permission
Council programme · 2024

Case 02 · Wall 044

A Gable for the Hartleys — the render was the job

A couple in Bath wanted their blank house gable painted with the orchard that used to stand behind it. The painting took five days. The wall took three weeks.

The brief

A 45 m² pebbledash gable, visible from the street and the neighbour's garden, to carry an orchard scene — apple trees, a ladder, the family's black cat in the branches. Private commission, paid in stages: survey, then prep, then paint, each signed off before the next began.

The decisions

The tap test found roughly two-fifths of the render hollow — it sounded like a drum and would have taken the paint with it when it let go. The hollow sections were cut out and re-rendered by a plasterer the clients engaged on the survey's recommendation, then the whole wall was stabilised and primed. Access was a tower scaffold, not a lift, because the side path is 900 mm wide. The neighbour received a letter and a print of the design before work started — consent from the wall's owner is the legal part; courtesy to the street is what makes it welcome.

The outcome

Painted in exterior acrylic over the repaired render, finished with a clear coat at the weather line. Total elapsed time five weeks, of which the painting was five days. The clients' staged payments meant nobody was ever owed more than one stage's money — the arrangement the studio now recommends for every private wall.

Dimensions
7.5 × 6 m · 45 m²
Surface
Pebbledash, 40% hollow, re-rendered
Paint system
Stabilising primer + acrylic, 3 coats
Permission
Private commission · 2025

Case 03 · Wall 029

The Listening Wall — painting a listed building without painting it

A Grade II listed warehouse, a festival that wanted a mural on it, and a conservation officer who was — correctly — not going to allow paint on 1840s brick.

The brief

A music festival's legacy commission: a 54 m² artwork for the gable of a listed warehouse it uses as a venue, to stay up for at least five years. The building is Grade II listed; any material change to its fabric needs listed building consent, and paint on historic brickwork is effectively irreversible.

The decisions

The mural was painted in the studio, on 18 mm marine-ply panels, sealed on all faces, and the panels were fixed to the wall through the mortar joints at existing fixing points — no new holes in brick, nothing bonded to the face. From the street it reads as a painted wall; from the scaffold it is furniture. The method statement, panel drawings and a removal plan went to the conservation officer before the festival committed its budget, and the survey of the fixing points was done from ropes to keep the pavement open.

The outcome

Listed building consent was granted on the reversibility argument: the whole work can be unbolted in a day and the wall left exactly as found. The panels went up in two days, and two spare panels from the same paint batch are stored by the venue against damage. It is the studio's standard answer now whenever a wall is worth more than the painting.

Dimensions
13.5 × 4 m · 54 m² on 12 panels
Surface
Grade II brick — untouched
Paint system
Acrylic on sealed marine ply
Permission
Listed building consent · 2023
