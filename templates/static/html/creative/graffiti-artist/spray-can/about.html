Working With Me · 03

The painter, and the terms

Ten years of walls, and the rules that accumulated with them. Everything on this page is also in the written agreement every client signs — there is no second, smaller print.

From theatre flats to other people's walls

Danny Reyes painted scenery for touring theatre for six years — flats, cloths and backdrops built to be looked at for two hours and struck by midnight. The first mural commission came in 2016, from a youth centre in Bristol that wanted its sports-hall wall painted with the members, and it taught the difference: a wall is looked at for years, by people who never asked for it. That is why this practice runs on consent forms and moisture meters as much as on spray cans.

Since then: 47 walls — council programmes, schools, breweries, one railway underpass that floods, one listed warehouse that was never touched. The work is spray and brush together: spray for the big fields of colour, brush for anything a passer-by will stand close to. Every job is surveyed before it is priced, and every price names its surface, its access and its paint system, so a client can compare it against any other quote line by line.

The name RAZE is a joke the practice takes seriously: the fastest way to remove a mural is to paint it badly on the wrong wall. Paint it on the right wall, on the right surface, with the owner's signature in the file, and nobody needs to.

The Paint Systems

What it is painted with decides how long it lasts

Four systems cover nearly every wall. The survey picks the system; the client is told which one, and why, before the price is agreed.

Coatings: a permanent anti-graffiti coat (£18/m²) lets tagging be pressure-washed off; a sacrificial coat is cheaper and melts away with the tag, then is re-applied — the right choice for schools and playgrounds. Uncoated walls in tag-heavy areas are repainted at the day rate, and the quote says so up front.

The Rules

Permissions, access, and aftercare

Permission is the first material

Work starts only with the wall owner's written consent in the file — a signature from the freeholder, the council's programme officer, or the school trust. Painting a wall without it is criminal damage, whatever the artistic merit, and no deadline changes that. Tenants need their landlord's signature, not just their own.

Listed buildings and conservation areas

Paint on a listed building needs listed building consent, and on historic fabric the honest answer is usually panels: painted in the studio, fixed reversibly, consented as furniture rather than as a material change. The studio writes the method statement and removal plan; the client files it with their conservation officer.

Access and safety

Every job has a written risk assessment and method statement. Lifts are operated under an IPAF licence, towers built to PASMA standard, and work over a pavement runs behind barriers with a banksman — or from ropes where a lift would close the street. Public liability cover is £10m; certificates travel with the quote.

Maintenance and touch-ups

Every wall is photographed at handover and re-photographed free for three years, so fading or damage is caught on evidence, not memory. Touch-ups and tag removal on coated walls are charged at the day rate with a half-day minimum. Coatings are renewed at about five years, priced per square metre from the original job ticket — the colours are on file by name.

What will not be painted

  • Any wall without the owner's written consent — no exceptions, no "just one small piece"
  • Work designed to look independent that is actually an undisclosed advertisement
  • Imagery that targets the people who live next to the wall
  • A surface the survey says will fail, repainted anyway to meet a launch date
