The cold chain is the craft

Anyone can carve ice in a freezer. The work is keeping it at temperature from the moment the block is harvested to the moment the last guest leaves.

A bench, a freezer room and a truck

Our shop on Winter Street has four block freezers, a carving room held at −10 °C, and a loading bay that opens onto the truck. Nothing is carved warm. Warm ice tears, clouds, and loses the edge that makes a chisel mark read at twenty metres.

We freeze our own blocks rather than buying them. It takes twelve days per block, with the water circulated and degassed so the ice comes out clear. A bought block is cheaper and faster; it is also full of the bubbles that turn a face into a fog when it is lit.

Blocks are harvested with a chainsaw, squared on the band saw, and either carved at the bench or welded into a larger assembly with slush and clear water. A welded seam is invisible; a glued one shows as a white line under stage light.

What the room does to the work

Ice at 0 °C loses its surface to any air above freezing. In a 21 °C ballroom with still air, a vertical face recedes by roughly a millimetre an hour. Put the same piece under a warm wash of light, or beside a door that opens onto a Minnesota February, and that rate changes in both directions — which is why we ask for the room before we quote the piece.

Detail goes first. A face cut 6 mm proud will soften within a few hours; a wall 200 mm thick will still be standing at midnight. We design for both numbers and we tell you which one you are buying.

Every indoor commission includes a drip tray, a subfloor if the venue needs one, and a member of our crew on melt watch for the first two hours. If the piece starts to fail, we would rather cut it back quietly than have a guest watch it go.

What we will not do

We will not promise a carved piece will last an outdoor afternoon in sun, whatever the forecast. We will not deliver above −8 °C, because the piece arrives soft and every edge is lost. We will not use dry ice or acrylic to fake a result we cannot make in ice, and we will not put a sculpture on a floor that cannot take the load, even if the client has already printed the invitation.
