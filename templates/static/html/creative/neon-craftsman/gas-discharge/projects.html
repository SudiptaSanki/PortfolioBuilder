Four tubes with their numbers

Footage, gas, transformer and the reason the tube was bent that way. A sign is a drawing that has to survive weather and voltage.

Williamsburg, Brooklyn

Wythe Bar, storefront script

A single-line script across a 3.4 m brick front, bent from one continuous tube so there is no joint to go dark. The wall was curved, so the pattern was drawn on site against a chalk line rather than on paper.

Neon at 15 mm bore, mounted on standoffs 40 mm off the brick. Two electrode pairs, because the run is over three metres.

Long Island City, Queens

Ridgeline Hotel, roof mark

A 2.1 m mark on a parapet, seen from the bridge approach. Argon with a mercury droplet gives a blue that survives the sodium streetlight wash better than neon red, which is why the client chose it against our first suggestion.

The transformer had to sit on the roof in a weatherproof enclosure with a fused disconnect, and the tube is on a fire-rated frame with a 50 mm air gap.

Bushwick, Brooklyn

Night Orchard, gallery ring

A 1.8 m ring of xenon for a gallery corridor, hung from a ceiling grid and dimmed to 40%. Xenon runs cooler in colour than argon and holds a bluish white that photographed well against the concrete.

Xenon needs more voltage per foot than the other three gases, so the ring runs on a 9 kV transformer even though the footage is modest.

Park Slope, Brooklyn

Repair, 1962 arrow sign

A dry cleaner's arrow sign, bent in 1962 and dark at the tail for a decade. We re-gassed two sections, replaced one electrode and kept the original glass, which had a heavier wall than anything we would bend today.

The original transformer was still serviceable but not listed to current standards, so it was replaced and the old one returned to the owner with the repair card.

What a sign costs

A single-line storefront tube starts around $1,800 and runs to $6,000 for a complex multi-colour piece, before installation. Installation in the five boroughs is quoted after a site visit, because the wall, the circuit and the lift decide it more than the tube does. Repairs start at $350.

We take a deposit for the glass and the gas, and the balance on the day the sign goes up. If the sign is going on a landmarked building, start earlier: the permit is usually the long pole.
