The tube is only half the sign

Glass, gas, pressure and the transformer that drives them. Get one of the four wrong and the sign either will not strike or will not last the year.

One bench, one pump, one bombardier

The workshop is a former garage on Wythe Avenue with a ribbon burner down one wall, a bending table with paper patterns pinned to it, and a pump station at the back. Tubes are bent by eye against the drawing, because a pattern is a plan and glass does not read plans.

Bombarding is the part clients never see. The finished tube goes onto the pump, is heated with a hand torch while the vacuum pulls, and a high voltage is run through it to drive the last impurities out of the glass wall. Only then is it filled and sealed. A tube that skips this step goes dark at the ends within a year.

After filling, every tube is aged for a day on the bench before it is packed. If it is going to fail, we would rather it failed here than above a doorway.

Gas, pressure and voltage

The figures below are the ones we work to for a 12 mm bore. Pressure goes up as the tube gets shorter and down as it gets longer, and the voltage follows the footage, not the shape.

Fill gas characteristics
Neon (Ne)Red-orange · 10–27 torr · about 346 V per foot
Argon (Ar)Pale lavender, blue with mercury · 6–12 torr · about 281 V per foot
Krypton (Kr)Silvery white · 8–14 torr · about 400 V per foot
Xenon (Xe)Bluish white · 8–14 torr · about 455 V per foot
Transformers3,000–15,000 V, 20–60 mA, constant current, UL 2161 listed
ElectrodesIron sleeve, sealed at the glass; one pair per tube, two on runs over 3 m
Bore8–20 mm; 12 mm is the house standard for signage

The parts that can hurt you

  • Secondary voltage on a sign transformer is lethal. Every installation is dead-tested before it is energised and the transformer is on a switched, labelled circuit.
  • Mercury is used only in argon tubes, a droplet per tube. Old tubes are returned to us and processed as hazardous waste, never put in a skip.
  • Tubes are mounted on fire-rated standoffs with an air gap; never directly on combustible cladding.
  • Glass under vacuum implodes inward. Carry tubes vertically, in a sleeve, and never lift one by the electrode.

What we will not do

We will not put a tube on a wall that cannot take the transformer. We will not copy another shop's tube from a photograph, because the bend is the drawing and a photograph is not a dimension. We will not promise a colour match between a new tube and a twenty-year-old one — the phosphor and the gas have both changed.
