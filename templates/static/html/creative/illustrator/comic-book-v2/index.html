Page Two

One page, four passes

The same panel at every stage of its making. This is the evidence page: what you are buying, pass by pass, before any contract is signed.

Pass 1 · Thumbnail

Thumbnail

Drawn at postage-stamp size, in minutes.

Composition, reading order and page turns are decided here — where the eye lands first, what the splash withholds. Nothing precious; everything structural.

Pass 2 · Pencils

Pencils

The drawing, finished and checkable.

Anatomy, perspective and acting are settled in pencil, where changes are still cheap. The editor approves this pass before a single line is inked.

Pass 3 · Inks

Inks

Brush and pen over the pencils.

Line weight does the lighting: what is black, what is white, where the night sits. Ink is the point of no return, which is why pencils are approved first.

Pass 4 · Colour

Colour

Flat colour, one accent, no tricks.

Colour is kept on a short leash: dusk blues, paper cream, and the courier's red scarf — the one warm note the whole book is allowed, so it always means her.

      The Fee Shape

      What moves the price

      Page count first. A book is priced by the page, so the page count is the price. Scope second: pencils only (£90) against full art (£230) is the biggest single lever, and choosing it means placing inks and colour with hands that will be credited.

      What does not move the price: genre, page size within standard formats, or how famous the writer is. Crowd scenes and research-heavy periods are absorbed by the pace table, not surcharged — the table was built on books that had both.

      The Rule of the Studio

      One book at a time

      The studio draws one book at a time, in the order booked. That is why the booking line at the top of this page is a real date and not a mood — and why the planner above would rather refuse your deadline in public than miss it in private.

      — the opinion this studio is known for: a late book helps nobody, least of all the person who drew it.
