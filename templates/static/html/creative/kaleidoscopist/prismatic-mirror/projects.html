Rooms that close on themselves

Four commissions, each with the angle, the chamber and the light that made it work. Instruments are listed at the end with the same numbers.

Kunsthal, Rotterdam

The Twelve-Fold Chamber

A walk-in room, 4.2 m across, with twelve mirror panels set at fifteen degrees to one another and a single viewing aperture at the centre. Visitors stand inside the fold rather than looking through a tube.

The hard part was the seam: at this size, a tenth of a degree of error shows as a bright line across the ceiling. We aligned the panels on site over three nights, using a laser and a lot of patience, and sealed the joints with a black gasket so nothing reflects between them.

De Appel, Amsterdam

Corridor of Small Suns

Three suspended discs in a 9 m corridor, each a shallow chamber with a warm light behind. Visitors walk beneath them and the pattern opens and closes overhead as they pass.

Because the corridor had a glass wall, the discs had to be lit from inside and blacked at the rim, or the daylight would have washed the chamber out. We used 2700 K at low output and a baffle behind each disc.

Stadsbibliotheek, Utrecht

Aperture for a Library

A permanent instrument set into a reading-room wall: a 640 mm aperture with a sixteen-fold chamber behind it, lit from the side so the pattern is visible without glare on the page.

Sixteen folds at this depth make a pattern that reads almost as glass, which suited a room where people sit for hours. The chamber is sealed and the object cell can be changed by the library twice a year.

Private collection, Ghent

The Glass Beekeeper

A single instrument in brass and blackened steel, made for a collector who wanted a chamber that could be held in one hand and would not tire the wrist during a long viewing.

Twelve folds, a 180 mm body, and an object cell filled with cut amber glass and a single piece of gold leaf. The cell rotates on a thread so the pattern can be wound slowly rather than shaken.
