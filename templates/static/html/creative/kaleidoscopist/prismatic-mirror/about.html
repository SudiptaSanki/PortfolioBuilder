The instrument is a pair of mirrors, not a tube of glass

Everything in this studio comes down to an angle and a surface. The rest — brass, velvet, light — is there to keep the angle from moving.

How a chamber is built

Two mirrors are cut to length, bevelled so their reflective faces meet without a gap, and set into a brass cradle at a fixed angle. The angle decides the fold count: fifteen degrees for twelve folds, eleven and a quarter for sixteen. The pair is aligned on an optical bench until the reflections close without a visible seam, then locked.

The object chamber sits at the far end, behind a glass cell. Objects are loose — glass shards, seeds, cut foil — so the image never settles. A chamber that is too shallow reads as a pattern; one deep enough to have parallax reads as a space. That is the difference between a toy and an instrument.

First-surface mirrors are non-negotiable. Ordinary mirror glass has its silver behind a pane, which gives a second reflection a few millimetres out of register. In a twelve-fold instrument that ghost repeats twelve times and the whole field goes soft.

Ada Kessler

Ada trained as an optical instrument maker in Jena before moving to Rotterdam in 2009. She spent six years restoring nineteenth-century kaleidoscopes for collectors, which is where the obsession with closure comes from: an old instrument either closes perfectly or it does not, and no amount of new velvet hides the seam.

The studio has been at Scheepmakershaven since 2016. It is one room, one bench, one optical table, and a small anechoic corner for testing light sources without reflections.

Commissions are taken directly from institutions, architects and private collectors. There is no gallery representation and no catalogue; each instrument is drawn for its site.

Handling and care

  • Never open the mirror cradle. The angle is set under tension and cannot be re-found by eye.
  • Dust the outer glass with a dry optical cloth. Solvent on the mirror edge will wick under the coating.
  • Keep the object cell out of direct sun; the shards fade and the cell can cloud.
  • Rooms are aligned on site at installation. If a panel is moved, call the studio before re-hanging it.
  • Instruments travel in a lined case with the cradle blocked. Keep the case.

What we will not do

We will not make a decorative mirror wall and call it a kaleidoscope. We will not use acrylic mirror in a room, because the surface is never flat enough to close. We will not sell an instrument we have not bench-tested, and we will not promise a specific pattern — the pattern is what the chamber does on the day, not a design.
