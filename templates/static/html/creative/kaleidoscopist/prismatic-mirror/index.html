What the mirror does, in plain terms

Two mirrors, one angle
The number of repeats is set by the angle between two first-surface mirrors. Fifteen degrees gives twelve folds; twenty-two and a half gives eight.
Front-surface, always
Ordinary mirror glass has the silver behind the glass, which produces a second, offset reflection. Optical mirrors are aluminised on the face.
The chamber decides
Objects sit at the far end in a glass cell. Move them a millimetre and the whole field changes. That is the instrument, not the decoration.

The angle is the whole instrument

A kaleidoscope is not a tube with pretty glass in it. It is a pair of mirrors held at an angle precise enough that the reflections close on themselves. Half a degree out and the folds never meet; the eye finds the seam immediately.

We cut, bevel and align every mirror pair in the workshop, then check the closure on an optical bench before the instrument is cased.
