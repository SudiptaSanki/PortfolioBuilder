LOG 002 · SHOT LOG — THREE CASE FILES

Brief, decisions, outcome. Failures included.

A case file that only lists successes is a brochure. Each file below records the decision that cost something, the incident that hurt, and the number the shot actually finalled at — because that is what a producer is buying evidence of.

CASE FILE 01 · STREAMING SERIES · FINALLED 2026

Cinderfall, Season 2 — a bridge comes down in 118 shots

Shots

118 (31 hero)

Peak sim

212M particles

Cache written

88 TB, checkpointed

Hero shot finalled at

v047

The brief

A suspension bridge fails progressively across a nine-week schedule: cable snaps, deck torsion, a full collapse into a river gorge, and the dust that outlives the structure by a full minute of screen time. The show's own team had the destruction models; Particle Field was hired for everything that moves after the geometry breaks — dust, debris, spray and atmosphere across 118 shots.

The decisions

Coupled solvers, one cache. The dust was not a separate effect layered over the debris: the RBD fracture drove the pyro source directly, so dust bursts inherit the debris' timing exactly. It cost two extra wedge rounds to art-direct, and saved an estimated three weeks of per-shot hand-matching.

Checkpoint everything. After a near-miss in week 3, every cache was written with a checkpoint every 20 frames and a checksum on completion. Storage rose by roughly 15%. The producer approved the cost in writing; the incident below is why.

The incident — 03:40, last Tuesday of finals

A farm node died mid-cache on the hero collapse shot, 11 hours into its final pass. The job restarted from the frame-380 checkpoint: 40 minutes lost, zero frames of approved work lost, dailies held at 09:00 as scheduled. The same failure on an uncheckpointed pipeline would have restarted the pass from frame 1 and put delivery a day out. The full incident note is quoted by the show's producer on the overview page.

The outcome

All 118 shots finalled inside the nine weeks, the hero at v047 — high, and honestly reported: 31 of those versions were lighting-driven re-caches after the grade changed, not failed sims. Final delivery: 4K EXR, ACES, with sim sheets archived for the Season 3 team.

CASE FILE 02 · FEATURE FILM · FINALLED 2025

The Longest Gale — a storm the audience had to believe

Shots

64

Peak sim

390M FLIP particles

Cache written

61 TB

Delivery

4K EXR · ACES 1.3

The brief

A North Atlantic storm sequence for a feature: a trawler taking green water over the bow for six minutes of screen time. The director's note was one sentence — “the sea is the antagonist; it cannot look like a screensaver.” Plates were shot in a tank at partial scale, which set the central problem of the job.

The decisions

Simulate at 0.7 scale, on purpose. Physically true 1:1 spray looked wrong against the tank plates — real droplets at full scale read as “CG rain”. The sims were run at the plates' effective scale so droplet size, hang time and sheet breakup matched the photography. The cheat is documented in the sim sheet; matching the plate is the job, and physics is the starting point, not the deliverable.

Foam as its own pass. Foam and spray were separated into two particle systems with different lifespans, letting the director art-direct “more violence in the foam” without re-running the body of the wave. It halved the iteration cost on 40 of the 64 shots.

The outcome

64 shots finalled; the sequence's longest single cache ran 31 hours across the farm without a failure — the checkpointing discipline from Cinderfall was already standard practice here. The film's VFX coverage singled out the storm as “the sequence that sells the third act”, which is quoted here from the review the client forwarded, not invented for this page.

CASE FILE 03 · COMMERCIAL · FINALLED 2026

Aurora Bank, “Ignite” — six shots, eleven days, one changed mind

Shots

6

Peak sim

48M particles

Versions on hero

14

Schedule

11 days, held

The brief

A 30-second spot ending on a spark burst that resolves into the bank's mark. Agency boards were precise about the first 90% and vague about the resolve — which is where commercials usually go to die. The wedge package was sold first, specifically to lock the resolve before the schedule did.

The decisions

Sim against the plate, not against taste. The burst was tuned against a licensed practical spark element (above) until the art director failed a blind A/B twice running. Every later note could then be judged against a fixed reference instead of a mood.

The changed mind, priced honestly. At v09 the client replaced the end-frame mark. Because the burst's final 20 frames were a separate, small cache, the change cost one evening of re-sim on those frames — quoted as a change at the agreed Class B rate and approved before work started, not absorbed and not inflated.

The outcome

Six shots finalled in eleven days, hero at v14, aired on schedule. The agency has since booked the same workflow — wedge first, plate-matched — for two further spots, which is the outcome a commercial case file is actually for.
