The working tool

Plan the set before the night

A DJ's sets are other people's recordings, so this site plays none of them — no fake player, no thirty-second clips. What a promoter can judge is the planning. Pick a slot and an energy arc; the builder assembles a timed set plan from the crate printed in full below, with the key and tempo of every join worked out. Every figure is computed from that printed data — check it against the table.

    How to read a join: keys are given in Camelot notation. Same key, a relative key, or one step round the wheel blends clean; keys further apart are played end-to-end with no blend — the honest move, and the one a vinyl DJ actually makes. Tempo steps are kept small because a 1210's pitch fader only travels ±8%.

    Tap tempo — the other working tool

    Found a record in a shop with no BPM written on the sleeve? Tap the pad in time with it. If you want to hear the tempo back, press Play click — the click is a plain metronome generated in your browser, started only by that press. It is a tool, not a track: this page contains no recordings.

    The register

    Four sets, four jobs

    Promoters don't book "a DJ set"; they book a job — open the room, lift it, hold the peak, or land it. These are the four standing sets, with the figures a booker actually asks for.

    First Warm

    The warm-up as a craft in itself. Doors open, coats still on, bar queue longer than the floor. Soul and Philly strings at conversation-plus volume, building a floor the headliner inherits instead of having to dig out.

    Format: vinyl only
    Tempo: 98–112 BPM
    Length: 2 hours
    Job: club warm-up
    Played: monthly

    The Long Blend

    Peak-time, mixed long. Disco 12-inches with the extended intros left in, keys matched round the Camelot wheel, tempo walked up two or three BPM at a time so the lift is felt, not noticed.

    Format: vinyl only
    Tempo: 110–132 BPM
    Length: 90 minutes
    Job: peak time
    Played: quarterly, guest

    Slow Close

    The last hour, played down properly. Mid-tempo soul, lights coming up a fraction at a time, and a final record the room knows is the final record. No encore games, no sudden house lights.

    Format: vinyl only
    Tempo: 88–106 BPM
    Length: 60 minutes
    Job: wind-down
    Played: Sunday social, monthly

    All Night, One Crate

    The residency set: the whole arc in one pair of hands, doors to close. Three crates, one story — first hour for arrivals, the lift at eleven, the peak held without shouting, and the slow close.

    Format: vinyl only
    Tempo: 88–132 BPM
    Length: 5 hours
    Job: full night
    Played: first Friday, monthly

    How a booking runs

    From enquiry to last record

    1. 01

      The brief

      Date, room, crowd, and where the night has to land. Fifteen minutes on the phone saves a wrong crate.

    2. 02

      The crate pull

      Forty-eight hours before, the night's records are pulled and put in playing order — the plan the builder above demonstrates.

    3. 03

      Soundcheck

      Sixty minutes before doors: pitch calibration checked, booth monitor set, the venue's limiter found and respected.

    4. 04

      The night

      Requests on cards, the plan followed loosely and the room followed closely. The last record is chosen by the floor, not the clock.
