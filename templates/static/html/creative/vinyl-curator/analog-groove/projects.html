Case studies

Three nights, told properly

A portfolio of mixes proves nothing — anybody can record a good hour at home. What a promoter is buying is judgement in a room that is going wrong, or growing. These are the three nights that say the most, with the decisions left in.

Ninety minutes that became five hours

Job: warm-up, basement club, Manchester · Capacity: 300 · Format: vinyl only · Booked: 9:00–10:30pm · Actually played: 9:00pm–2:05am

The brief

Warm the room for a touring headliner: ninety minutes of first-hour records, floor ready by half ten, then hand over. The crate was pulled for exactly that — soul, Philly strings, nothing above energy three.

What happened, and the decisions

At 9:40 the promoter appeared at the booth: the headliner's flight was cancelled, the replacement was driving from Glasgow, ETA unknown. The choices were to peak early and burn out, or to stretch. Frankie stretched. The warm-up arc was re-planned on the fly — energy held at two and three for two full hours, the four biggest records in the crate kept back, requests taken early because a room that feels heard stays. Tempo never crossed 118 BPM until the replacement walked in at 1:40am, at which point the lift took twenty minutes instead of an hour, because the floor was already warm and already his.

Outcome: the room held at roughly seventy percent of capacity through what should have been a walk-out, the headliner inherited a floor instead of a rebuild, and the guest slot became the monthly residency Frankie still holds. The lesson is printed on the crate lid to this day: never peak on a rumour.

One deck down at the all-nighter

Job: guest set, Northern Soul all-nighter, Leeds · Hours: 9pm–3am · Format: original 45s · Failure: deck two's motor, 11:20pm

The brief

A guest hour in the middle of an all-nighter — the strictest room in the country. Original-pressing soul 45s, no reissues, no boots, and a crowd that has been dancing since before the guest was born.

What happened, and the decisions

Twenty minutes in, deck two's motor gave up mid-blend. On vinyl there is no backup laptop to fall back on, so the technique changed instead: records played end-to-end, the way the original all-nighters were played before two-deck mixing existed — each record announced by its run-out, the next cued on the one working deck during the applause that northern rooms give a finished record. During the midnight break the venue's spare deck — a belt-drive with wandering pitch — was fitted, and the last hour was mixed on it by riding the pitch fader through every blend, the way you ride a clutch on a hill.

Outcome: the floor never emptied; three dancers asked at the end why the middle hour had felt "more like the old days", which is the end-to-end style's whole point. The booking was renewed for the following season — and a spare stylus and a tested backup deck are now written into the rider, in ink, because of this night.

Twelve Fridays: building a room

Job: residency season, Subterra, Manchester · 12 consecutive Fridays · Capacity: 350 · Attendance: 80 in week one, sell-out from week nine

The brief

A new basement room with a good system and no crowd. The venue didn't need a name; it needed a habit — the same night, built slowly, until Friday meant something.

The decisions

The arc never changed, so regulars could learn the night: soul for the first hour while the room filled, funk as the engine room, the lift at eleven, peak held without shouting, and the same last record every week — "What's Going On", lights up a fraction, no encore. Request cards went on the tables and actually fed the crate: any record requested three times got bought, and the crate grew forty records over the season in the room's own taste. What was refused matters too: no guest DJs parachuted in, no theme nights, no early peaking for a hen-party booking in week four — the night protected its shape.

Outcome: sell-outs from week nine to twelve, the residency renewed for a second season, and the testimonial on the home page — from Danny Osei, the booker who took the chance on week one.
