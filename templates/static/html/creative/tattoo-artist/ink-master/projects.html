Case notes

Judged healed, or not judged at all

A tattoo on the day it is finished is swollen, red and shining — a photograph of it proves almost nothing. These are the studio's case notes on three finished pieces: what was asked, what was decided and why, and how each one stands now that it has healed and settled. Healed photographs are kept in the studio book and shown at consultation; the drawings here are the original designs the work was tattooed from.

Case 01 · Cover-up

The moth over the armband

A tribal armband from 2004, faded to blue-black, wrapped the whole upper arm. The client had worn long sleeves for ten years and wanted it gone — and did not want laser removal, with its cost, its sessions, and no guarantee the ghost of it would clear.

Design of recordCase 01

The brief

Cover the band completely, in black, with something the client would choose today — a design with its own reasons, not a darker version of the same mistake. No laser first. The arm had to read as a moth at conversational distance, in a short sleeve, at work.

Decisions

A cover-up works where the new design's darkest areas sit exactly on the old design's darkest lines, so the moth was drawn at 20 cm wide — sized by the band, not by taste — with the wings' solid blacks mapped onto the band's heaviest strokes. The old design's gaps became the wing pattern's negative space. The work was staged over three sittings so each healed layer could be checked for show-through before more black was committed; a cover-up judged fresh is a cover-up guessed at.

Outcome

Healed, the band is unreadable at arm's length; one 1 cm patch where the old ink sat highest needed the free touch-up at month four. Photographed healed at fourteen months, the blacks have settled even. In the client's words: "nobody has ever guessed what is underneath."

Blackwork cover-up
Placement / size
Upper arm, full wrap · 20 × 14 cm

3 sittings · 9 hours in total

Case 02 · Black & grey

A heron for a fisherman's daughter

A returning client wanted a heron down her back for her father, who fished the Wye for forty years. A back is the largest canvas the studio works on, and the least forgiving: it moves, it stretches, and its owner almost never sees it.

The brief

One bird, full back, nothing else — no landscape, no lettering, no date. It had to sit quietly under clothing and hold its own the two months a year it is seen. The client is fair-skinned and wanted the grey soft, closer to pencil than to print.

Decisions

The heron was drawn to the spine's centreline with its neck following the back's natural curve, so the bird moves when she bends instead of cracking across it. The grey wash was mixed in four dilutions and heals lighter than it looks fresh — on fair skin, black & grey tattooed at photograph darkness heals heavy. Sittings were spaced four weeks apart, outlines first, so the client lived with the drawing's bones before any shading was committed.

Outcome

Five sittings over five months. Photographed healed at two years, the wash has held at the values intended — the palest dilution reads as atmosphere rather than fading, which is the difference between planning for the heal and hoping for it.

Style
Black & grey, four-step wash
Placement / size
Full back, centred on the spine · 34 cm tall
Sittings
5 sittings · 16.5 hours in total

Case 03 · Fine line, over a scar

The scar becomes the stem's shadow

A surgical scar, five years healed, ran diagonally across the client's forearm. She did not want it buried under a heavy piece; she wanted it worked with — "it is part of the arm's history, I just want it to stop being the first thing I see."

Design of recordCase 03

The brief

A fine-line botanical piece over and around the scar — stems, leaves, small flowers — that treats the scar as part of the composition. The client had been refused by one studio and quoted a heavy blackout by another; both answers were wrong for what she asked.

Decisions

Scar tissue is not ordinary skin: it is denser, takes ink unevenly, and can blow out under a heavy hand. The scar was checked first — fully healed, pale and flat, five years old, so workable. The design runs the stems alongside the scar and sets the leaves over it, so its slight texture reads as the plant's shadow rather than a fault. A lighter needle grouping was used over the scarred section, and the piece was split into two shorter sittings so the scarred area could be judged healed before the rest was finished around it.

Outcome

The scarred section held at roughly ninety per cent on the first pass — the expected figure, given honestly at consultation — and the remainder was settled in the free touch-up. Photographed healed at eight months, the scar is visible if you look for it and unremarkable if you don't, which is what was asked for.

Style
Fine line botanical
Placement / size
Inner forearm · 16 cm tall
Sittings
2 sittings · 3.5 hours in total
Healed record
Finished Sep 2025 · photographed healed at 8 months

Working over scars is assessed case by case at consultation: the scar must be fully healed — pale, flat and at least a year old — and some scars should not be tattooed at all. That answer, when it is the answer, is given straight and costs nothing.
