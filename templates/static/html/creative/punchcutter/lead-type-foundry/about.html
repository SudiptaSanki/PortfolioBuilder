One bench, two hands, and a lens

Ashcroft & Matrix is a punchcutting shop, not a drawing studio. Every face we sell began as a steel punch cut under a loupe, was struck into copper, justified by hand, and cast from a mould older than either of us.

01 — Method

How a letter becomes metal

The drawing is the easy part. A punch is cut from a soft steel blank with a graver, then hardened, then struck into a copper planchet to leave a matrix. The matrix is justified: the letter is struck again and again, each strike moved a hair, until the impression has the depth and the sidebearings a compositor can set without swearing.

Our counterpunches are cut first, for the counters of the a, b, d, e, g, o, p and q. Cutting the inside of a letter with a graver is slow and uneven; striking it from a hardened counterpunch gives the counter a clean wall and a consistent depth across the fount.

A punch is judged at 20× and then again at reading distance. Both have to agree before the matrix is struck.

What a matrix has to survive

A single copper matrix may be struck thousands of times before it mushrooms. We keep a strike count on every matrix in the case and retire one at the first sign of a spread foot. Replacement matrices are struck from the original punch, which is why we never lend a punch out of the shop.

Type is cast to 0.918 in — 23.32 mm — in hand moulds, with a jet trimmed and the foot rubbed down on a stone. Sorts are counted by weight into a case, and a fount leaves with a printed tally of what is in it.

02 — The shop

What is on the bench

Two benches, a hardening furnace, a proof press and a Heidelberg windmill that came from a jobbing printer in Bethnal Green in 2011. The pantograph is used only for enlarging drawings, never for cutting outlines: a pantograph traces a shape, and a shape is not a letter.

  • Punches in the shop1,214 steel, 312 counter
  • Matrices held412 copper, 58 nickel
  • Proof pressAlbion, 1854, rebuilt 2018

Materials

Type metal is a lead-antimony-tin alloy, mixed in the shop to 62/24/14 for text faces and a harder 58/28/14 for display. We buy antimony from a single refiner and keep the assay sheets. No cadmium, no arsenic, and no alloy we cannot name on the invoice.

Ink is a stiff letterpress black with a linseed base, mixed to a working tack of 8 to 12 at 25 °C. Paper is bought in mill-sealed packs and acclimatised for a fortnight before it is printed. A paper that has travelled in a cold van will not take an impression evenly for a week.

03 — Dates

Seventeen years, four presses

Hedley Ashcroft cut his first punch in 1998, in a garage in Sheffield, using a graver borrowed from a die-sinker. The foundry took its present name when Marta Matrix joined in 2009 and brought the matrix archive of a closing London trade house with her.

  1. 2009

    The shop opens in a former watchmaker's workshop on Clerkenwell Close. One bench, one Albion, 60 matrices bought at auction.

  2. 2011

    Heidelberg arrives. Edition printing begins: invitations, keepsakes and small books, set from the faces being cut.

  3. 2015

    Ragstone Text commissioned by a poetry press in Newcastle that wanted a roman with a shorter descender. The face is still the shop's best seller.

  4. 2019

    Marta's archive catalogued. 212 matrices from the West Street trade house are cleaned, proofed and entered into the case.

  5. 2023

    Testament Titling cut for a stone-carving studio in Bath. Capitals only, cut wide on the body so the spacing survives at 96 pt.

  6. 2026

    Six retail faces, eleven private. The shop takes four commissions a year and refuses the rest, which is the only way the proofs stay honest.

04 — Limits

What we will not do

We do not cut a revival and call it an original. If a face is drawn from a historical source, the source is named on the specimen, with the library and the shelf mark.

We do not trace a scan. A shape traced from a photograph has the shape of a letter and none of its structure; it will not hold at 8 pt and it cannot be spaced.

We do not license a retail face for use as a template or a generator, and we do not sell a face outright. Type is licensed, never sold, and the licence names the use.

We do not print an edition from a face we have not proofed on the customer's own stock. A proof on our paper is not a promise about yours.

We do not take a commission we cannot cast. If the size is outside our moulds, we say so at the brief, not at delivery.

We do not keep a customer's copy in the shop after the job is delivered. Drawings and proofs go back with the type, or into the archive with written permission.
