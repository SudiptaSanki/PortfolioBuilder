How much light will the panel let through?

Every commission begins with arithmetic before it becomes a drawing. Set the opening, the tessera and the grout, and the panel tells you its piece count, its glass area and the daylight it will transmit.

The window is a drawing that holds light

A stained glass design begins as a full-size cartoon in charcoal and gouache, not as a picture. The drawing has to work at the scale of the building: a face at thirty feet is not a face, it is a few pieces of glass that suggest one. Every line in the cartoon becomes a piece of lead, and every piece of lead becomes a dark line in the finished window, so the drawing is really a plan for where the darkness goes.

I choose the glass before the colours are fixed. Mouth-blown glass is not flat: it has a direction, a seed and a slight variation in thickness that changes how the light comes through. The same nominal ruby can read as wine in a north window and as flame in a west one. The cartoon is adjusted to the glass, not the other way round.

Cutting is done on the light table, with the pattern glued to the glass and the cutter run against a straight edge or freehand for curves. Each piece is then fitted to the cartoon, the lead came is stretched and cut, and the whole panel is assembled dry before a single joint is soldered.

The last step is the least visible and the most consequential: the lead is soldered on both sides, the joints are puttied, and the panel is braced so that it will not sag. A window that is beautiful and badly braced will be beautiful for about fifteen years.

I

Draw for the distance

A window is read from the floor, the street or the pew. Detail that only works at arm’s length is decoration, not design.

II

Glass first, colour second

The batch, the seed and the direction of the sheet decide the colour. I never specify a colour from a swatch book.

III

Lead is a line, not a gap

The lead carries the drawing. Thick came for structure, thin for detail, and never a joint that exists only because the glass was awkward.

IV

Restore what is there

Original glass is kept wherever it is sound. A restoration that replaces everything is a new window wearing an old stone frame.

“A window is finished when the room stops needing the electric light at four in the afternoon.” That is the test.
