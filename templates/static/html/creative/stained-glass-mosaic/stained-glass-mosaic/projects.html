Windows · 2023 – 2026

Six openings, and what the light asked for

Each entry lists the building, the opening and the palette, then the decision that shaped the window. The drawings are at the studio and can be seen by appointment.

North rose, chapel of St Clair

Lyon · 2024 · restoration

A storm in 2023 loosened the saddle bars and cracked eleven quarries. The rose was dismantled, documented, and rebuilt with the original glass where sound and matched glass where not.

Decision: the client wanted the 1907 colour restored. We matched from a photograph rather than from a modern sheet, which meant a slightly duller ruby than the supplier’s catalogue — and the correct one.

Size2.4 m diameter
GlassAntique + flashed ruby
Lead6 mm, re-leaded
Year2024

Reading room, Evanston Library

Illinois · 2025 · new commission

A south elevation of eight tall openings behind the reading tables. The brief was explicit: colour from the street, no glare on the pages, and nothing ecclesiastical.

Decision: a quarry grid in amber and pale blue at 3 mm, with the density increasing toward the head of each opening so the light falls away from the tables. The pattern is doing the work, not a picture.

Size8 × 2.1 m
GlassCathedral amber
Lead4 mm, copper foiled
Year2025

Stair window, Lake Michigan house

Michigan · 2026 · private commission

A single opening over a landing, seen for four seconds on the way up and much longer on the way down. The owners wanted green that changed with the weather, and no figures.

Decision: three greens and a single line of lead that reads as a horizon from the bottom of the stair and as a wave from the top. The gold disc is placed so that at 4 pm in June it lands on the landing wall.

Size1.1 × 2.6 m
GlassAntique greens, one gold
Lead6 mm, hand-bent
Year2026

Chapel of the Ascension

Lyon · 2023 · new commission

A small chapel with a west window and a congregation of forty, mostly at evening mass. The subject is Pentecost, which in glass usually means too many flames and no air.

Decision: three large tongues of fire, one line of landscape, and a gold disc that reads as the sun behind them. At 7 pm in winter the whole chapel turns red for about ten minutes, which the priest described as “the entire point”.

Size1.9 × 3.4 m
GlassFlashed ruby, gold
Lead6 mm, painted
Year2023

Ark light, Anshe Sholom

Chicago · 2025 · new commission

A window above the ark in a 1920s synagogue, replacing a plain amber light that had been installed in the 1970s. The congregation asked for light that would not compete with the ark itself.

Decision: deep lapis ground, geometric stars rather than a figure, and gold only where the daylight can reach it. The lower panel is ruby so that the ark reads as the brightest thing in the room.

Size1.4 × 2.8 m
GlassLapis, gold pink
Lead5 mm, copper foiled
Year2025

Église Saint-Bénigne, Dijon

Burgundy · 2024 · restoration

A 1904 window with painted medallions that had been badly repaired after the war. Three medallions were replaced with plain glass in 1947 and the lead had failed along the sill.

Decision: re-lead the whole window, keep every original piece including the 1947 repairs, and replace only what could not be saved. The 1947 glass is now documented as part of the window’s history rather than as a fault.

Size2.2 × 4.1 m
GlassOriginal, 1904
LeadFull re-lead
Year2024
