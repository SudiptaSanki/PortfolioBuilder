The survey sheet

Read the site before you plant it.

The survey comes before any drawing. Soil is tested, not guessed — pH, structure, compaction, contamination where the site's history asks for it. Water is audited: what falls on the site, where it runs, what can be kept. Shade is mapped across the day and the year, because a bed that is sunny in the brochure can be dark from November to March.

Only then does the planting plan get drawn — and it is drawn in classes with densities, not in drifts of colour. That is deliberate. A class can be counted, priced, watered and replaced. A mood cannot.

Every scheme leaves this office with three documents: the plan, the planting schedule, and the maintenance plan with the caretaker's real hours written on it. If the third document can't be signed, the first two get redrawn smaller. That rule has cost this practice work, and saved it more.

The arithmetic we plant by

Losses are budgeted before they happen.

Planting fails — some of it, every time, on every site. The honest response is not to promise otherwise; it is to print the expected losses in advance, order against them, and replace inside them. These are the practice's standard rates. The planner below reads this table — change the areas and the arithmetic follows the printed numbers, line by line.
