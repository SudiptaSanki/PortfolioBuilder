Ellery Ground / Studio

The practice

Three people, one drawing board, a lot of soil tests.

We are a small studio because the work is small: a courtyard, a school entrance, the strip between a building and the street. The scale is where planting can still change how a place feels and performs.

Why the ground comes first

Nadia Ellery founded the studio in 2014 after eight years in commercial landscape practice, most of it spent specifying planting that no contractor could build and no maintenance budget could keep. Ellery Ground was set up to stay from site walk to the second summer, so that the drawing and the ground stay in the same conversation.

Every project begins with a hole in the ground. We dig an infiltration pit, test the soil texture by hand, and read the existing canopy. That hour of work decides more about the design than any mood board: whether water will leave, whether roots will establish, and how much shade the site already has.

A garden that needs a pump, a drip line and a weekly visit is not a climate strategy. It is a subscription. Nadia Ellery, founding principal

How we work

  • Fixed design fees. Quoted after the site walk, never as a percentage of construction, so a cheaper build does not make the design worse value.
  • One contractor list, three names. We tender to contractors we have seen plant well, and we stay through setting out.
  • Plant sourcing in the room. Species are confirmed with a nursery before the plan is issued. If a plant is not available in the quantity the plan needs, the plan changes.
  • Two-year establishment care. Months 3, 12 and 24 on site. If a plant fails, we replace it and record why.
  • No synthetic fertiliser, no potable irrigation after year two. Both are written into the specification, not left to goodwill.

Where the carbon goes

Landscape work has an awkward footprint: concrete edging, imported topsoil, and stone moved hundreds of kilometres. We count the main items on every project and publish the total in the project notes. On Alder Court, removing 240 m² of asphalt and reusing it as sub-base saved an estimated 7.4 t CO₂e against a conventional rebuild, while the 412 m² of planting is estimated to sequester 1.1 t CO₂e a year once established.

What we will not do

We turn down work that cannot meet its own water and maintenance promise. In practice that means:

  • No lawn in a courtyard that has no irrigation budget for it.
  • No plant palette that depends on a species the regional nurseries cannot supply.
  • No rain garden without a tested infiltration rate and a drawn overflow route.
  • No tree in a planting pit under 6 m³ of soil volume.
  • No scheme that leaves maintenance to a stranger with a mower.

Working with us

Most clients come after a builder, an architect, or a school business manager has already asked the same question: can the outside do more of the work? The answer is usually yes, but the design has to start before the slab is poured. If construction is already underway, we can still help with the soil and the planting, though not with the levels.
