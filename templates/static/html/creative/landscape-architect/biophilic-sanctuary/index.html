01  Approach

Three moves before any plant is chosen

A

Shade the glass, not the view

We plot the solar window of the façade we are asked to cool, then place canopy where it intercepts June sun and clears December sun. A 6 m tree at 4 m from south glass does more than external blinds and asks nothing of the grid.

B

Slow the water, then sink it

Every scheme starts with a level survey and an infiltration test. Rain gardens are sized on the 25 mm storm, pond 150 mm deep, and sit at least 600 mm above the seasonal high water table. Overflow is designed, not discovered.

C

Feed the soil first

Compacted subsoil is the reason most planted courtyards fail by year four. We specify 300 mm of engineered topsoil, no synthetic fertiliser, and a mulch regime that builds fungal networks instead of dependence on irrigation.

02  Palette

Plants chosen by where the water sits

Selected work · 2022

Alder Court

Twelve townhouses around a gravel car park, with a south façade that hit 34 °C on the inside face each July. We removed 240 m² of asphalt, dished the centre into a rain garden, and planted eight Oregon white oaks to shade the glass within seven years.
