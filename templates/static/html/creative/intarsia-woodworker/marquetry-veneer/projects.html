Projects

Three commissions, told properly

A portfolio of pictures alone tells you nothing about how the work goes. Each of these is written up the way it happened — what was asked, what was decided at the bench, and what the client ended up with.

Eightfold Star

Parquetry floor medallion for an entrance hall, Bath — 2024

The brief

A Georgian townhouse whose hall floor had been patched with plain boards after a leak. The owners wanted the repair to be the point of the room: a single medallion, geometric, in keeping with the house, that would stand up to boots, dogs and a bicycle.

Decisions at the bench

The star and its square frame were packet-cut from European oak and walnut so the pattern repeats exactly; the centre was assembled piece by piece in sycamore and ebony where the eye lands first. Ebony was kept to the smallest pieces — it is dense, expensive, and swallows light if overused. The ground was made 22 mm thick rather than the usual veneer-on-board, because a floor medallion is walked on, and the finish is hard-wax oil for the same reason: it can be spot-repaired in an afternoon, where French polish cannot.

Outcome

Installed over two days, levelled flush with the surrounding boards. Eighteen months on, the owners report the hall has become the room visitors ask about — and the oil finish has already survived one muddy winter and been refreshed once, in place, in under an hour.

Species
European oak, walnut, sycamore, ebony
Technique
Packet-cut parquetry; piece-by-piece centre
Dimensions
1200 × 1200 mm medallion, 22 mm ground
Finish
Hard-wax oil
Making time
9 weeks, plus 2 days installing

The Damascus Box

Keepsake box in the Damascene tradition — 2023

The brief

A 60th birthday present for a man who had spent his working life in the Levant. His wife asked for a box for his desk — for letters, a watch, the small kept things — in the geometric inlay tradition of Damascus, but made here, and made to be handled daily.

Decisions at the bench

The lid picture was cut piece by piece by the window method, each shape sawn into the space its neighbour would leave. Holly was shaded in hot sand to give the pattern its soft shadow side, and the pale inlay is mother-of-pearl and boxwood rather than the plastic substitutes common in tourist work — they cost more and age better. Inside, the box is lined in cedar and suede rather than marquetry: a keepsake box is opened more often than it is admired from across the room.

Outcome

Delivered with a treatment note listing every species used, so any future repair can match them. The client's words on receiving it are on the home page. The box is in daily use, which is the outcome the design was aimed at.

Species
Walnut, ebony, boxwood, holly, mother-of-pearl
Technique
Piece by piece (window method); hot-sand shading
Dimensions
320 × 210 × 110 mm
Finish
French polish; cedar and suede lining
Making time
7 weeks

Regency Card Table, c. 1820

Restoration of an antique rosewood card table — 2024

The brief

The table had stood in a sunny bay for forty years. One half of the top was bleached almost grey, veneer had lifted along the crossbanding, a section of boxwood stringing was missing, and a later varnish had gone cloudy over all of it. The family wanted it usable again — not made new.

Decisions at the bench

The later varnish was softened and lifted without disturbing the original shellac beneath. Lifted veneer was re-laid with hot hide glue — the same adhesive the table was made with, and reversible, which matters for the next restorer. The missing stringing was re-cut in boxwood and toned to sit beside the old, not to impersonate it. The bleached half could not be un-bleached; instead the whole top was carefully evened and the finish rebuilt so the difference reads as age, not damage.

Outcome

The table went home with a written treatment report recording every replaced piece — eleven sections of stringing, two veneer losses — so nothing about the repair is hidden from a future owner or valuer. It is back in daily use as a card and writing table.

Species
Rosewood (original), boxwood stringing (replaced sections)
Technique
Re-laying in hide glue; re-cut losses; finish revival
Dimensions
Top 915 × 457 mm, opening to a square baize
Finish
Original shellac revived, waxed
Making time
5 weeks, including assessment
