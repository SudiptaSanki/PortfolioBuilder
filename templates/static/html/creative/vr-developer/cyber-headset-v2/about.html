About · The studio

An engine programmer who went indie and took the profiler with him.

01The story

I’m Dario Ricci. For nine years I wrote engine and rendering code for other people’s games, the last four of them on VR titles where my whole job was the frame: finding the missing milliseconds at 2 a.m. before a certification deadline. I was good at it and it taught me the trade’s open secret — performance is never rescued at the end; it is decided at the beginning, in what the team agrees to spend.

Ricci/Frame is the studio I founded in Berlin in 2021 to work that way on purpose: small cyberpunk VR titles of my own, contract builds for publishers and arcade operators, and rescues for studios whose games are too good to ship at 59 fps. One lead developer, a bench of trusted contractors for art and audio, and a profiler that is treated as a member of the team.

The cyberpunk setting is not an aesthetic accident. A neon street is the hardest honest test a standalone headset can be given — emissive signage, wet reflections, crowds, weather. If your engine can hold 11.1 ms in the rain on a neon street, it can hold it anywhere. So that is where I build.

03The honesty page

Sheet H1 · Policy

Comfort policy — motion sickness is a defect

A player who feels ill has not “failed to adapt”. The build has failed them. Every title I ship, mine or yours, carries:

  • No artificial acceleration without an alternative — teleport locomotion always offered alongside smooth movement.
  • A comfort vignette on by default during smooth movement and turning, adjustable and removable.
  • Snap turn at 30°/45° alongside smooth turn.
  • Seated and standing play, height recalibration in one button, never a menu dive.
  • The camera is never taken from the player — no forced pans, no cutscene head movement, no exceptions for drama.
  • Comfort settings listed per title, on the store page and in the register — a player can check before buying, not after feeling sick.

A title that misses its frame target on a shipping platform is filed as a comfort defect and patched before new content. That is what happened with Static Bloom (Case File 02), and the register still says so.

Image & platform policy

Every image is labelled. An in-headset capture says so, with the headset named. A rendered still says “rendered still” — marketing renders are legitimate and lying about them is not. A photograph of the real world says “reference photograph” and names its photographer. The drawn scene in this site’s viewport is labelled a rendered still, because that is what it is.

Platforms are never blended. A standalone figure and a PCVR figure measure different machines; averaging them produces a number that describes nothing anyone can buy. Where a title ships on both, both numbers print, separately — as Void Runners’ 120 fps PCVR figure stands apart from every standalone figure on this site.

Figures are p95, measured in-headset, on shipping builds — not best-case, not editor builds, not a desktop monitor pretending to be a headset.
