Case files

Kept the way the bench keeps them

Every drop and every commission gets a file: what was asked, what was decided, what happened — including the delay, in the file where it belongs. Three files are printed here in full. They are the shop's references; a buyer deciding whether to trust a group buy should read the third one first.

Case File 01 — Field Notes, from design to delivery

The ask. A keycap set with the manners of good stationery: cream stock, sage ink, a rust-red that behaves like a editor's pencil. Buyers wanted a set that would sit on a work desk without shouting — and legends that stayed legible at arm's length.

Decisions

Dye-sub PBT over double-shot: the sage the design needed sits between mouldable colours, and dye-sub holds it exactly. Legends were drawn at a heavier weight than the first proof — at real desk distance the fine cut of the original drawing thinned out. Two colour-match rounds were budgeted from the start, because printed colour on PBT is a negotiation, not a setting.

Outcome — including the delay

The first match round failed. The sage came back reading grey under daylight, and the set would have shipped wrong to be on time. The second round added five weeks. Buyers were emailed on Fri 17 Oct 2025 — inside the original window, before anyone had to ask — with the failed sample described, the new range given, and the refund offer restated. Eleven buyers took the refund; the rest waited. The run shipped the week of Mon 16 Feb 2026, and the extras — 23 base kits — went on the index only after the last order left the bench. They are still there, counted.

Case File 02 — A full build for 60,000 words a week

The ask. Marcus Yuen writes insurance claims documentation — roughly sixty thousand words a week, by his own count — and came to the bench with tired fingers and a loud board. He wanted the lightest honest actuation we stock, every switch treated, and proof of the result rather than a description of it.

The bench work, itemised

Vault 65 kit. Sixty-eight Harbour Fog switches, each opened once: stem rails and bottom housing brushed with 205g0, 0.125 mm films fitted, springs swapped to 55 g and bag-lubed with 105 oil. Stabilisers clipped, lubed and tuned on the board. Firmware flashed with his layout. Then the part most shops skip: the board sat on the typing-test desk for 24 hours and was typed on, by two people, before it was packed.

Outcome — the measured result

After tuning, the batch was re-measured on the same gauge: 47 g ±3 g actuation, against 48 g ±4 g stock. Tuning did not make the switches lighter — it made them consistent, and the build sheet says so in those words. The sheet lists all 68 switches, the spring weight fitted and the re-measured figures; it went in the box. Total below is the kit at its index price plus the planner's own lines for this layout and switch — the planner on the drop sheet reproduces it exactly.

Case File 03 — Night Post, the delayed group buy

The ask. A tactile switch with a rounded bump and a quiet bottom-out, for people who type at night in shared houses. The group buy ran in Aug 2026 and sold out its run. Then production started, and the file stops being a success story — which is why it is printed in full.

What slipped

In week 3 of production the stem mould cracked. A cracked mould does not make slightly worse switches; it makes scrap, so the run stopped. A replacement mould had to be cut — six weeks of tooling on its own — and when the run was ready to re-enter the factory schedule, its slot was gone: two larger orders had taken the line. Small batches queue behind big ones. That sentence is on the honesty page because it is the whole truth of group-buy manufacturing, and Drop 06 is where this shop learned to print it in advance instead of explaining it afterwards.

What buyers were told, and when

The shop knew on Fri 5 Sep 2026. The notice went out Mon 14 Sep — day 9, inside the printed 10-day promise — with the cause, the lost slot, and a revised range instead of a new promise date. A second update followed on Fri 23 Oct with the mould finished and the run re-queued. The refund offer stands until production restarts; eight buyers have taken it, and their switches return to the run count, not to a waitlist sale.

The extras, handled as printed

There are no Night Post extras and there will be none to sell until every group-buy order has shipped. Any switches left after dispatch will be counted, listed on the index as extras, and priced at the group-buy price — leftovers are not a second launch.

The shop

Keycap Vault is the drop sheet of Sorin Switchworks — small-batch keycaps, switches and built boards, run by Ilya Sorin from a bench on SE Morrison Street, Portland, Oregon. Everything sold here moves in numbered drops or from counted extras.

Sound ruling

This site is silent, and so are our product pages. A sound test is a recording of a board you are not buying, in a room you are not in, through a microphone you do not own. We print measurements instead — see Table B1.
