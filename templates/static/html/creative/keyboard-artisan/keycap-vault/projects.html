KV-24

Kiln Blue, R3 Cherry

2026

A split-ergo builder in Ghent wanted the letter row to read as one continuous gradient across two halves of a board. Three-layer pour: dense base, translucent mid, clear top with the legend suspended under 0.8 mm of resin.

What went wrong: the first pour trapped a bubble on the underside of the legend, visible exactly where the thumb sits. Fixed by vacuuming the legend insert separately before the second pour.

KV-26

Oxblood Cameo

2026

A carved boat prow, lifted from a photograph of the client’s grandfather’s fishing boat and eased until the thumb rests on it without finding a hard edge. One-shot pour, so no two caps in the set share a swirl pattern.

What went wrong: the first master was too sharp at the gunwale and the resin chipped during demoulding on two caps. The edge was filleted to 0.6 mm and a fifth cap was cast as a spare.

KV-28

Ash Glaze blanks

2026

A typing school in Tokyo replaced 40 worn OEM caps on their teaching boards. Matte blanks with a 0.4 mm texture pass, cast in four pots of ten so the glaze stayed consistent across the order.

What went wrong: pot three cured 3 °C cooler and came out one shade pale. Those ten caps were sold to the school at cost as spares and the set was completed from pot five.

KV-22

Ember restock

2026

The house colourway, re-run for a 30-cap group buy after the 2025 mould wore at the stem boss. New insert, same chip: a warm terracotta that reads brown under tungsten and orange in daylight.

What went wrong: nothing structural. The pigment settled faster than expected, so pot two needed a longer stir and the first two caps were kept back as bench samples.

KV-25

Slate Inlay

2026

A Berlin architecture studio wanted the escape row to match their model-making card: warm grey shell with a bone-white inlay line. Double pour with a 0.9 mm machined channel, filled after the shell had cured.

What went wrong: the first inlay wicked 0.2 mm up the wall. The channel was deepened and the second attempt held a clean edge across all 16 caps.

KV-29

Salt Flat

2026

A photographer in Reykjavík asked for a cap that felt like the crust of a salt pan: shallow ridges, no gloss, edges that catch the light without reflecting it. Ten caps, each hand-finished after cure so the ridge pattern differs slightly.

What went wrong: the first texture pass was too deep and collected dust in a week. Depth was reduced from 0.8 mm to 0.35 mm and sealed with a matte lacquer.

Pricing

What a batch costs

The sculpt fee is paid once per design. The mould belongs to the design, not the order, so a re-run in a new colourway costs the per-cap rate only.

Deposits are 50 % and refundable until the master is cut. If a stem fit fails twice, the deposit comes back in full and you keep the failed caps to cut up and inspect.
