The bench

A keycap is a tolerance part wearing a costume

Keycap Vault is one person, one pressure pot and a wall of moulds in a former mill in Batley. I cast resin caps for people who already know which switch they like and want the board to look like theirs.

Bench opened 2019, after six years of injection-mould tooling

Casting Pressure pot, 40 psi, 18-hour cure

Output 40 caps a week, one colourway per pot

Compatibility Cherry MX mount only. No Alps, no Topre.

How the bench started

I spent six years setting injection-mould tools for a plastics shop in Bradford, which is where I learned the part of keycap work that most artisan photos skip: the first article, the shrink compensation, and the argument you have with yourself about whether 0.03 mm matters. It does. It is the difference between a cap that seats and one that cracks a stem after a month of typing.

The first Vault cap was a replacement for a cracked escape key on a 1990s board that belonged to a friend’s father. I carved it in jeweller’s wax, cast it in a two-part silicone mould, and it fit on the third attempt. That cap is still on the board. Everything since has been an attempt to get to the third attempt faster.

Commissions are still single-person work. I sculpt, mould, cast, ream and pack every order myself. If you ask for 200 caps, I will tell you no and point you to an injection moulder, because a pressure pot is not a production line.

Shore D and shrink figures are the supplier’s datasheet values at 23 °C. Every mould is compensated for the shrink of the resin assigned to it, which is why a mould is retired when I change resin supplier.

QC protocol

Six checks, every cap, logged by hand

A cap that fails any check is re-cast. The log ships with the order, and the spare stem is kept for two years.

  1. 01

    Socket cross

    Go/no-go pin at 4.10 mm +0.05. A cap that will not take the pin is reamed once; twice means the insert moved and the batch is suspect.

    4.10 +0.05
  2. 02

    Seat and release

    Fitted to three switches from different production years, pressed twenty times, then pulled by hand. It must seat without force and release without a tool.

    20 cycles
  3. 03

    Skirt flatness

    Checked on a granite plate with a 0.02 mm feeler. A skirt that rocks will catch the neighbouring key at full travel.

    0.06 mm
  4. 04

    Wall thickness

    Measured at four points with a ball anvil. Thin walls sound hollow and crack at the stem boss under a heavy spring.

    1.50 ±0.10
  5. 05

    Neighbour clearance

    Mounted in a 3×3 grid at 19.05 mm pitch and pressed through the full 4 mm stroke. Nothing may bind.

    19.05 pitch
  6. 06

    Colour match

    Compared against the approved chip under D50 light. A batch that drifts more than two ΔE is offered at a discount or re-cast.

    ΔE ≤ 2.0

What this bench will not do

Being clear about the edges saves us both a month.

  • Full keycap sets in injection-moulded plastic. That is a different trade with a five-figure tool cost.
  • Shine-through legends on backlit boards. The resin is opaque where the legend sits.
  • Function-row or novelty caps for boards with non-standard stabiliser spacing unless you send the plate drawing.
  • Rush orders under three weeks. The cure is 18 hours and the resin does not negotiate.
  • Any cap for a switch other than Cherry MX and its clones. Send a switch and I will test it before quoting.
