Restorations · 2024 – 2026

Six signs, six decisions about what to keep

Each of these came in dead or partly missing. What follows is the condition, the work, and the one compromise that had to be made — because there is always one.

2026 · RELIT

Greyhound Bus Terminal

A double-faced roof sign that had been dark since 1998. The porcelain was intact under forty years of grime; the tubes were gone except for two broken returns, and the can had rusted through at the bottom edge.

We rebuilt 41 tubes from the original shop drawing, which the terminal’s retired manager found in a filing cabinet. The arrow was the difficult piece: six bends in a single 12 mm tube, and the original had used a splice we could not reproduce safely.

Gas
Argon + Hg
Core
15 kV / 30 mA
Tubes
41 new
Compromise
one splice, moved
2026 · RELIT

Star Barber Shop

A projecting sign with a ruby star that had been replaced in the 1980s with a fluorescent panel. The bracket and can were original; the face had been drilled for the panel, which left four holes through the porcelain.

The star was re-bent in 10 mm neon to the 1953 outline, taken from a photograph of the shop in a 1961 newspaper advertisement. The holes were filled and touched in rather than repainted.

2025 · IN SERVICE

Carolina Theatre marquee

The marquee itself was in daily use; the 1937 chase circuit was not. Ninety-six lamp sockets had been bypassed over the decades, and the neon border had three tubes flickering on a failing core.

We restored the chase as a sequence of four circuits rather than one, so a single lamp failure no longer takes out the row. Three border tubes were re-bent on site, thirty feet up, because removing the soffit would have closed the theatre for a week.

2025 · RELIT

Hendersonville Feed & Seed

A gold-white helium script that had been re-gassed badly in 1998 and had glowed a sickly pink ever since. The glass was original; the pressure was wrong and the electrodes had been replaced with the wrong size.

We pumped it down, re-electroded both ends, and refilled to the correct pressure for 10 mm helium. The colour came back to the warm gold the owner remembered from childhood, which he described, accurately, as “the colour of a porch light”.

Helium
Glass

Electrodes
2 replaced
Compromise
none
2024 · RELIT

Blue Ridge Motor Lodge

Four crates and one photograph. The porcelain face was intact, every tube was broken or missing, and the transformer had been replaced twice with whatever the hardware store had in stock. The final owner had wired the sign to a doorbell button.

All 41 tubes were re-bent to the 1958 layout. A rebuilt 9 kV core went into the original can, with a proper timer and a disconnect. The doorbell button is in a drawer in the office, kept because the owner liked it.

Argon + Hg
Core
9 kV / 30 mA
Tubes
41 new
Compromise
doorbell retired
2024 · RELIT

Earl’s Diner cup

The cup had been repaired three times with clear silicone and one tube had been replaced in white plastic. The CO₂ fill was original and still worked, which is unusual; the electrodes were finished.

We kept the original tube where it could be saved, re-bent the rest in the same diameter, and replaced both electrodes. The plastic replacement went in a frame on the diner wall, because the owner’s father had fitted it and the family wanted it kept.

Gas
CO₂
Core
6 kV / 30 mA
Tubes
2 new, 1 kept
Compromise
plastic framed
