§ 01

The builder

I’m Dan Whitaker. I started as a carpenter’s apprentice in Maine at seventeen, spent twelve years framing other people’s houses, and built my first cabin — my own, 22 square metres, on a hillside with no power and a long driveway — in 2016. It taught me the two lessons this practice runs on.

The first: size everything for the worst month. A solar array figured on the annual average is a generator subscription in disguise; a roof figured for the snow load in the next town down the hill is a collapse with a date on it. The second: the unglamorous work decides everything. Footings, drainage, flashing, the fall on a pipe. Nobody photographs a footing. Every cabin I’ve been called to rescue failed at one.

Since 2019 the practice is me, two carpenters and an apprentice, working from a workshop in Craftsbury Common. We build four to six cabins a year, slowly on purpose, and we keep a register of every one — where it stands, what it runs on, what it cost, what winter did to it. Electrical work and any septic work are done by licensed Vermont trades; the rest is ours, and we sign it.

Twenty-three cabins built or rebuilt since 2016. The plans, prices and the sizing calculator are on the home page; the three builds that explain us best are in the case studies.

Timber — what goes where, and why

Species are chosen per job, not per photograph. Durability classes run 1 (very durable) to 5 (not durable) for heartwood in ground-adjacent use; movement is how much the board will cup, twist and shrink on you after it’s fixed.

§ 04

Upkeep — what a cabin asks of you

A timber cabin is not maintenance-free, and anyone who sells you one as if it were is selling you a rescue job for 2035. This is the actual list — it comes printed in every owner’s manual we hand over.

SPRING

After the thaw

Walk the piers and check the cabin still sits level after frost heave. Clear roof debris and gutters. Check rodent screens and the cistern’s first-flush diverter. Run the generator under load for twenty minutes.

SUMMER

Wood & water

Re-oil or re-stain painted and oiled finishes every two to three summers, south face first — it takes the weather. Change the sediment filter. Check deck boards and the bottom cladding board, the one that lives in the splash zone.

AUTUMN

Before it turns

Sweep the stove flue — every year, no exceptions. Stack next winter’s wood under cover (a cord is 128 cubic feet; buy by the measure, not the pickup load). Drain exterior taps, check roof seams and screws, and run the battery health check while the sun is still decent.

WINTER

Snow discipline

Rake the roof after more than 45 cm of fresh snow on lower pitches. Brush the array clear — ground-mounts earn their keep here. Keep drifts off vents and the flue, and keep the battery room above freezing.

Owners get a service visit from us after the first winter, included in the price — levels, seals, systems, and the list above walked through on the actual building. After that, most owners do it themselves in a weekend a season, which is rather the point of a small cabin.
