Bill № 03 — The Casebook

How the work
gets built

A comedian's portfolio cannot print the act — material is the stock-in-trade, and it stays in the room. What it can show is the work around the act: how an hour is built, how a clean set is engineered, how a night is held together. Three engagements, told properly.

Case 01 — The festival hour

“Second Helpings” — an hour built across fourteen previews

The brief

A first full hour for the festival circuit: sixty minutes on family, debt and turning forty, premiering in August with a tour to follow. The club twenty was strong; an hour is a different discipline — it has to be an argument, not a playlist.

The decisions

The original opener died at preview three and was cut without sentiment. The family material moved from closer to opener, where its warmth buys patience for the harder debt section. Two callbacks were planted at minutes 6 and 19 and paid off at 41 and 54 — the architecture that makes an hour feel built rather than long. Nine minutes were cut across the fourteen previews; nothing that survived did so by habit.

The outcome

The hour ran 26 dates, sold out its final festival week, and now tours as the headline format. Every preview audience was told exactly what they were watching — work in progress, priced as such — which is why the show arrived finished instead of merely old.

Case 02 — The corporate rewrite

A clean twenty-five for 300 insurance staff — without losing the room

The brief

A Christmas party for a national insurer: 300 staff, the board present, dinner finished at nine. Requirements in writing — no profanity, nothing about named individuals, and “roast us, but safely” from the chief executive, who had been burned by a previous act.

The decisions

The club set was audited line by line against the agreed definition of clean; roughly 40% of it failed the audit and was replaced, not diluted — clean material written as clean material lands harder than club jokes with the words filed off. Six minutes were written bespoke about the firm from a briefing call (the new HQ, the legendary IT migration, the car park), and a kill-list of topics was agreed in writing before the night.

The outcome

The set ran 25 minutes to the venue's schedule and the room stayed. The client rebooked for the following Christmas before the act had left the building, and two guests booked their own company nights off the back of it. The review below is theirs.

Case 03 — The residency

Compère at The Brickhouse — holding a new-act night together for two years

The brief

A monthly new-material night: eight acts a bill, half of them in their first year, in a room that had a reputation for eating beginners. The promoter did not need a star host; he needed the night itself to survive its weakest act.

The decisions

The running order is built on energy, not billing — the strongest newcomer goes second, never last-but-one. House rules are set in the first three minutes (phones, talkers, where the bar run happens), because a room told the rules early polices itself. A standing five minutes of proven material sits in reserve for the nights an act dies or the raffle overruns — used eleven times in twenty-four shows, and never acknowledged as a rescue.

The outcome

The night doubled its audience inside a year and the residency has been renewed twice. Beginners now ask for the room by name, which is the metric a new-act night lives by. The promoter's verdict is on the front page of this site.
