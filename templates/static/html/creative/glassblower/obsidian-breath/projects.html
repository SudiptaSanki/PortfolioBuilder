Firing records, not a lookbook

Every commission leaves the shop with a card: the schedule, the kiln, the day, and the weight. These are four of them, with the numbers that decided the shape.

Reykjavík, Iceland

Three vessels for the altar

A parish on Skólavörðustígur asked for three pouring vessels for the altar, in black, to be used rather than displayed. That meant a heavier foot than our gallery pieces and a lip that could be wiped without cutting a cloth.

All three were blown from the same gather weight so they read as a set, and all three spent nine days in the kiln because the feet were built up to 18 mm. The parish has used them every Sunday since.

Oslo, Norway

Twenty-four for a lobby

A hotel on the harbour wanted a single gesture across a twelve-metre reception wall: black vessels at three heights, lit from below, with nothing hung between them. We made twenty-four in four firings and rejected seven that did not hold the same tone.

The lighting was the difficult part. Black glass absorbs almost everything, so the vessels are lit from beneath through a 2700K strip and read as silhouettes rather than as colour. The installation drawings are included with the commission.

Edition of twelve

Black sand edition

Twelve low bowls for a gallery in Copenhagen, numbered and signed on the foot. The brief was a vessel that could hold fruit, ash or nothing, and that would look like a hole in the table when the light was low.

Each bowl carries a slightly different inclusion of obsidian frit in the base — the only place the actual volcanic glass appears. The frit is sealed under 4 mm of clear, so it never touches food or water.

Reykjavík, Iceland

Cast panel with obsidian frit

A museum commission for a small gallery about volcanic glass: one panel, 900 by 1800 mm, cast in a sand mould with 6 kg of crushed obsidian suspended in the pour. The obsidian is the only material in the panel that is not made in the shop.

Casting a panel this thick means a fourteen-day schedule with two holds, and a risk that the block cracks in the first 48 hours. It did not, and the firing card records every ramp. The panel is mounted on a steel frame with a 40 mm air gap behind.
