The shop is hot eleven months a year

Furnace 1 has not been cold since 2016. Everything else in the studio — the kiln, the benches, the crates — is arranged around keeping it that way.

Obsidian is a glass. It is not a furnace glass

You cannot gather obsidian. It is a natural glass formed when lava cools too fast to crystallise, and if you heat it in a tank it devitrifies — it turns to stone in the furnace and the batch is lost. Any studio claiming to blow molten obsidian is either not blowing it or not telling you what is in the pipe.

What we blow is a reduced soda-lime coloured with iron from crushed basalt, which gives the same green-black in daylight and the same dull black in the shop. Where obsidian itself belongs is in cast work: we grind it to frit and include it in panels and paperweights, where it can be melted under a controlled schedule without being held at working temperature.

We put this on the page because collectors ask, and because the difference matters when you are buying a vessel that is meant to hold water and light for a century.

Two furnaces, one kiln, one glory hole

The shop is built around a single continuous tank. Colour changes are slow and expensive, so we work in black, amber and clear for months at a time rather than switching by the day.

Where the black comes from

The colour is iron, not dye. Basalt from the Reykjanes peninsula is crushed, sieved and added to the batch at around 2%, and the furnace is run slightly reducing so the iron sits as Fe²⁺ rather than Fe³⁺. That is what makes the glass black in thick sections and green where the wall thins.

Reduction is not perfectly repeatable. Two gathers from the same tank on the same day can differ by a shade, and a vessel blown in February does not match one blown in September. We photograph every piece in north daylight and publish the photograph with the firing card, so a buyer sees the actual colour rather than a swatch.

Ash glazes are applied cold, on the finished vessel, and fired at 720°C for a second time. The ash is from the 2021 Fagradalsfjall eruption, milled and sieved to 90 microns.

What we will not promise

We will not promise an exact colour match to an earlier piece. We will not promise a delivery date before the kiln has finished, because a vessel that cools a day early is a vessel that may fail in your house. And we will not ship a piece without a crate: every vessel travels in a foam-lined ply case cut to its profile, and the crate is included in the price.

If a piece arrives damaged, photograph the crate before you open it and write to us the same day. We insure every shipment and we would rather replace a vessel than argue about one.
