Three commissions, told in full

A brief, the decisions it forced, and what came of them. The prices and measurements are the ones in the workshop book — a portfolio that hides them is asking you to trust it blind.

The Holm Dining Table

No. 23 · Aarhus · 2025
Timber
European oak, one tree for the top
Joinery
Wedged mortise & tenon, drawbore pegs, breadboard ends
Dimensions
240 × 100 × 74 cm · 320 cm extended
Finish
Soap flakes, three coats
Time at the bench
11 weeks
Price
68,000 kr, delivered and fitted

The brief

A family in Aarhus: four people at the table on a Tuesday, twelve at Christmas, and a room that could not take a 3-metre table all year. The old table — a flat-pack veteran — had begun to rack at the joints, and they wanted its replacement to be the last table they bought. Their one firm request: nothing precious. Children, homework, and hot dishes would all happen on it.

Decisions

Two extension leaves that store inside the frame, so no leaf lives in a cupboard losing its colour at a different rate. Breadboard ends, pinned with room to move, so the wide top can swell and shrink across its width without splitting. A soap finish rather than oil or lacquer — in a house with small children, the finish that a parent can repair in ten minutes beats the finish that resists damage slightly longer but needs a professional when it fails.

Outcome

Delivered and levelled in the room in March 2025. At Christmas it seated twelve with the leaves in. In February the pen incident happened — a grandchild, a biro, a moment — and the mark was soaped out by the owners, following the half-page of instructions that travels with every table. The repair cost them ten minutes and no telephone call, which was the point of the finish.

The Skagen Chair

No. 24 · Workshop piece · 2025
Timber
Danish ash
Joinery
Turned spindles, wedged tenons, solid shaped seat
Dimensions
44 × 48 × 86 cm · seat 45 cm · 3.9 kg
Finish
Hardwax oil
Made
In batches of four, between commissions
Price
7,400 kr each

The brief

No client this time — a workshop piece, drawn for the maker's own family summer house near Skagen. The requirements were domestic and specific: light enough to carry outside one-handed with a coffee in the other, comfortable through a long lunch, and narrow enough to fit six around a table built for four.

Decisions

Ash, because it turns cleanly, bends in the hand's judgement of thickness, and is grown in Denmark. Seven spindles, each turned slightly oval so the back meets a spine with a flat, not an edge. The seat is solid ash hollowed by inshave — no upholstery to fade in coastal sun, no cord to re-weave. Wedged tenons throughout, so a loose leg in twenty years is a repair, not a funeral.

Outcome

The chair went into the register as a repeatable workshop piece. Four are in the workshop now; when they sell, another four are made in the gaps between commissions. Two of the first batch went back to Skagen. The rest are scattered from Copenhagen to Oslo, which is further than a chair usually travels and a fact the maker is quietly pleased about.

Chairs for Café Norden

No. 22 · Copenhagen · 2024
Timber
European oak, woven paper-cord seats
Joinery
Mortise & tenon, pinned; replaceable cord seats
Dimensions
50 × 52 × 80 cm · seat 46 cm
Finish
Hardwax oil, two coats
Time at the bench
9 weeks for twelve
Price
6,800 kr each, set of twelve

The brief

A café with forty covers and a chair problem: their bought-in chairs lasted about eighteen months before the joints loosened and the seats sagged, and the replacements never matched. They wanted twelve chairs that would still be in service in ten years, that guests would find comfortable for the length of a slow breakfast, and that could be stacked by the staff at closing time.

Decisions

Everything was specified one grade harder than a domestic chair: rails a few millimetres thicker, cord seats woven tighter and designed to be re-woven in the workshop in a day when they eventually wear, and hardwax oil instead of soap — a café cannot ask its staff to soap chairs monthly. The owner was given the offcuts from the batch, so any future repair matches the original timber exactly.

Outcome

Delivered in November 2024. At the one-year visit, all twelve were tight, the cord had polished rather than frayed, and two chairs had survived being stood on to change a light fitting — against advice, but the workshop notes it without complaint. The café has since ordered four more, to the same drawing, at the repeat price.
